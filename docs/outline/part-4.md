# Part Four outline: Fellow travellers (Chs 13–15)

_Detailed outline per `FRAMEWORK.md` §6, 2026-10-12. Notes, not prose. Old numbers 7–9. Every quote below was checked against its extract; ids are used in the ledger at the end._

## Part Four divider
- **Part Four: Fellow travellers**
- **Question:** Who else is on this road, and where did they arrive?
- **Answer (draft):** The road you have walked was not imported: it runs from the Qur'an and the Imams. Many walked it before you. Some lost their footing in ecstasy; the sober came back and went further. Shia masters keep the path without a Sufi chain of authority, because authority belongs to the Imam.
- **Chapters:** 13 Where it comes from · 14 Drunk and sober · 15 Sufis and Shia
- **Munajat (§109–110):** [P4] "My Lord, make me one of those whom You call and they respond; when You look at and they are thunderstruck by Your majesty." `light` p. 165. Arabic: held (`duas-munajat-shabaniyah` §109–110). _Note:_ Light's clause "when You look at and they" is ungrammatical (`reviews/outline-alignment.md` §4 preferred duas.org "swoons"). See conflicts.

### 13. Where it comes from
**Subheading:** The road was not brought in from outside: it grew from the Qur'an, the companions and the Imams, and its first travellers met fear, renunciation and love in that order, with the Imams ahead of them all.
**Conclusion:** ʿIrfan is native to Islam, and the Imams stand above those who first walked it. **Serves:** B4 **Old number:** 7
**Opener (≤150 words):** Thursday night, Duʿa Kumayl. The prayer carries the name of the companion to whom Imam ʿAli entrusted it (`duas_dua_kumayl`, description). When later travellers drew up the chains of their teachers, Kumayl stood near the top, with Salman and Uways, under ʿAli (`light` p. 5). The reader has walked the road (Part Three); now: who walked it first?
**Words you'll meet:** Sufi · zuhd · tawakkul · muhasaba
**Sections:**
1. ## Not brought in from outside [13.1]. The road was not imported; at most, some techniques were borrowed.
   - Western books looked elsewhere because they "cannot believe that Islam as a religion of law and jurisprudence can produce mystics" (L11 ¶3). The candidates were Christian monks, Neoplatonism and Buddhism (L11 ¶4–5, ¶12, ¶21; the same list in `light` p. 16).
   - Monks: the Qur'an separates the mercy placed in Jesus' followers from the monasticism they invented (L11 ¶6–11). Muslim mystics married, and the khanqah is not a monastery (L11 ¶6, ¶11).
   - Neoplatonism came in only when theory was systematised, centuries later; Buddhism differs in doctrine, so at most techniques were borrowed (L11 ¶20, ¶22–23).
   - Jurdaq's pitcher, in words: people look for the pool that filled the pitcher while standing by the sea (`light` p. 20).
   - quote [13a] "'irfan has derived its basic sources of inspiration from Islam itself and from nowhere else" (`light` p. 16). Arabic: n.a.
   - quote [13b] Q 57:27 "But as for monasticism, they innovated it—We had not prescribed it for them—only seeking Allah's pleasure." Arabic: held (quran.py; `.ayah words=`).
   - quote [13c] Nicholson: "Surely the seeds of mysticism are here." (`light` p. 21). Arabic: n.a.
2. ## The Qur'an, the companions and the Imams [13.2]. The roots are inside Islam from the first generation.
   - A young companion after the dawn prayer, worn by certainty, who saw paradise and hell; the Prophet's verdict (`light` p. 19; L11 ¶42–43). Call back to Ch 3: knowledge as light God places in the heart.
   - quote [13d] "This is a man whose heart has been illuminated with the light of faith by God" (`light` p. 19, from al-Kafi). Arabic: VERIFY.
   - Imam ʿAli as the source; the chains of Sunni and Shia mystics run to him through Salman, Uways and Kumayl (`light` pp. 4–5).
   - quote [13e] Tabatabaʾi on ʿAli: "His sayings on this subject are a treasure of knowledge." (`light` p. 4). Arabic: n.a.
   - Imam al-Sadiq as "one of the greatest teachers of early mysticism": Schimmel, the Sheikh's reference (L11 ¶45–46), voiced as his report. The evidence is held: Fudayl's lessons from al-Sadiq, Maʿruf's conversion at al-Rida's hands, Shaqiq and Imam Musa (`light` pp. 24–25).
3. ## Fear, renunciation, love, and the Imams above them [13.3]. The first seekers leaned to fear, renunciation or love, in that order, often near the Imams, who stand above them.
   - Three temperaments, not exclusive. Love became "the victorious side" (L11 ¶61–63; L12 ¶9). This is the order the reader met in Ch 11.
   - **Fear: Fudayl b. ʿIyad.** A highwayman who heard Q 57:16 while climbing a wall and answered "yes, it is time, my Lord" (L12 ¶19–21; `light` p. 25). He was known for weeping, and is a reporter trusted in al-Kafi and by Sunni critics (L12 ¶21–22). Call back to Ch 9: repentance is open to anyone.
   - quote [13f] Q 57:16 "Is it not time yet for those who have faith that their hearts should be humbled for Allah's remembrance". Arabic: held.
   - **Renunciation: Ibrahim b. Adham.** The prince of Balkh who learned from al-Sadiq when young (L12 ¶9–11; `light` p. 24). His three levels of zuhd, the highest where the world is too slight to speak of renouncing (L12 ¶12–16). Tawakkul: do your part, then rely; nobody should repeat the old desert trials (L11 ¶78–90).
   - quote [13g] Nahj 3.221 "By God, this world of yours is worth less in my eyes than the bones of a pig in the hand of a leper." (`nahj` p. 760). Arabic: VERIFY. ʿAli's words already hold the seeker's highest level.
   - **Love: Rabiʿa.** Repentance as a gift from God; the torch and the water (L12 ¶53–54; L11 ¶72–73; `light` p. 24 "amazing spiritual states"). Told as "a story told of her". She lives out the worship of the free, which is recalled in words because Nahj 3.222 belongs to Ch 11.
   - quote [13h] (lecture) "I want to throw fire in paradise and to pour water on to hell so that these 2 veils disappear" (L12 ¶54). Arabic: n.a.
   - **Imams above.** Their sayings were "an echo" of al-Baqir and al-Sadiq (L11 ¶58). Rabiʿa's verses do not compare with the Lovers' prayer (L11 ¶58–59; recall Ch 2). Love was missing among the early seekers, but not in the Imams (L12 ¶56). The Shaqiq story (box); "completely different in terms of their calibre" (L12 ¶37).
   - quote [13i] the early gnostics' visions contain "those elements of spiritual life which we find in the sayings, and teachings of Imam Ali and other Imams" (`light` p. 5). Arabic: n.a.
**Boxes (3):** Who's Who: Salman, Abu Dharr, Uways (L11 ¶35–41, Uways as hagiography the Sheikh does not vouch for, ¶39; `light` pp. 20, 23). · Story: Shaqiq and the young man on the road to Mecca (L12 ¶30–37; `light` p. 24): his heart is read, he prays all night at the Kaʿba, and he is found in a great house as Musa b. Jaʿfar. The well miracle is trimmed; [13j] Q 20:82 "Indeed I forgive those who repent, become faithful, act righteously, and follow guidance." Arabic: held. · Who's Who: Hasan al-Basri (side by side: "ambivalent", L11 ¶64; in ʿAli's chain, `light` pp. 5, 23), Maʿruf al-Karkhi (`light` p. 25), al-Muhasibi (L12 ¶3–5; `light` p. 26), Dhu'l-Nun (L12 ¶62–64; `light` p. 27).
**Try this:** Muhasaba: fix a time each night to review the day; seek forgiveness for faults, give thanks if none (`kernel` p. 68). The Sheikh calls it "still persistent in our mysticism" (L12 ¶4). Quote [13k] Imam al-Kazim: "He who does not take account of him own soul once every day is not one of us." (`kernel` p. 68; the extract reads "him own"; check the print). Arabic: VERIFY.
**Hand-off:** Where did those travellers arrive?
**Callbacks / forward pointers:** Ch 2 (Lovers' prayer), Ch 3 (light in the heart), Ch 9 (repentance), Ch 11 (love; the worship of the free). Forward: Ch 14 (Bayazid, Junayd), Ch 15 (chains).
**Cut / gone around:**
- Cut as asides: the "big Josh", Cambridge and "Russell" anecdotes (L11 ¶3, ¶24–26); the qiraʾat debate (¶26–33); Plato as a pupil of Dawud (¶19); the "harsh companions" (¶43); Salman's lifespan (¶36); the desert camp and warfront stories (¶80–92); the women-and-men remark (¶73).
- Reworded: "Sufism under complete Shia influence" (¶51) becomes "drew on the Imams' teaching" (`light` p. 5).
- Left out: the Hasan al-Basri and Imam Sajjad exchange (¶68–70, garbled); Shaqiq carries the point instead. The four-levels hadith (¶46–48) is not held (D15). Ahl al-Suffa: one line at most (lecture-only).
- Worded around: the Sheikh's list of borrowed techniques ("halwa, not speaking, long meditations", ¶22) clashes with Ch 10's Qur'anic roots, so the chapter says only "some techniques, not doctrine".
- Avoided: Junayd's dating (¶77).

### 14. Drunk and sober
**Subheading:** Some who reached the loss of self stayed lost in it; the sober came back to themselves and went further, and the theory their experience produced has critics whose concerns have answers.
**Conclusion:** Of those who reached fana, the sober went furthest, and the tradition's great theory, though contested, is neither pantheism nor licence. **Serves:** B4 **Old number:** 8
**Opener (≤150 words):** Recall the divider's Munajat line in words: we ask in Shaʿban to be among those God glances at, who are thunderstruck by His majesty. Some travellers were. What came after the swoon divided them. (Call back to Ch 12's fana and baqa.)
**Words you'll meet:** sukr · sahw (add to GLOSSARY) · shath · ishara · wahdat al-wujud
**Sections:**
1. ## Lost in God, or back with God [14.1]. Two ways of arriving at fana; the tradition ranked the return higher.
   - Intoxication means losing oneself in God (L13 ¶13–14). Bayazid first spoke openly of fana and baqa (`light` p. 25); his four days (L13 ¶16–18; `kernel` p. 73).
   - quote [14a] "I came forth from Bayazid-ness as a snake from its skin." (`light` p. 25). Arabic: n.a.
   - quote [14b] "I want not to want anything!" (`kernel` p. 73). Arabic: VERIFY (kernel Arabic unreliable).
   - Ecstatic sayings: [14c] (lecture) "Praise is to me, how great my majesty is" (L13 ¶20). There are two readings: God speaking through him as from the tree, or he no longer saw himself (L13 ¶20–22). Recall Ch 12's dangers.
   - quote [14d] Q 28:30 "Moses! Indeed I am Allah, the Lord of all the worlds!" Arabic: held.
   - Hallaj spoke openly and was executed; the ʿurafaʾ accuse him of disclosing secrets (L13 ¶15; `light` p. 27). [14e] Hafiz: "His crime was that he used to reveal secrets." (`light` p. 27). Arabic: n.a. (Persian verse, held translation).
   - Junayd: "one of the moderate mystics" (`light` p. 27). For him fana is not the goal; baqa is "a new life in God" (L13 ¶27, ¶29). The explorer who goes beyond the river (L13 ¶29). The intoxicated are "children" at the first steps (L13 ¶15).
   - quote [14f] "This state, loftier even than mahw and mahq, is called sahw." (`light` p. 57). Arabic: n.a.
   - Junayd's language of allusion: secrets are kept because people cannot bear them (L13 ¶30–32). [14g] "If Abu Dharr knew what was in Salman's heart, he would (considering him a heretic) have killed him." (`light` p. 23). Arabic: VERIFY.
2. ## A theory of what they saw [14.2]. Ibn ʿArabi gave the experience a theory, the unity of being, which is grasped by experience, not argument.
   - The Shaykh al-Akbar of Shia and Sunni mystics (L13 ¶33). He shaped Sadra, Khomeini and Tabatabaʾi (L13 ¶42–47); "wildly divergent views" about him (`light` p. 35).
   - quote [14h] "certainly the greatest mystic of Islam" (`light` p. 34). Arabic: n.a.
   - One existence, of which creatures are manifestations. "certainly I'm not god or a part of god" (L13 ¶63). It is not illusion: "more real than you think" (L13 ¶66). It is above reason, and Sadra's gradation came closest (L13 ¶62, ¶67). Call back to Ch 3: reason stops short.
   - quote [14i] Q 57:3 "He is the First and the Last, the Manifest and the Hidden". Arabic: held.
   - quote [14j] Nahj 1.1.1 "He is with all things but not by association. He is other than all things but not by detachment." (`nahj` p. 122). Arabic: VERIFY. This is a different sentence from Ch 3's 1.1.1 quote.
   - quote [14k] Khomeini: "the sea is not the waves, although the waves are the sea" (`light` p. 144). Arabic: n.a.
   - quote [14l] (lecture) "it's not for us to judge because it needs someone who has the experience" (L13 ¶43).
3. ## The critics' concerns, and the answers [14.3]. This is the prologue's promise. It is scoped as how the defenders answer, since no critical Tier B source is held.
   - _Not bound to Islam._ Some jurists and traditionists held the ʿurafaʾ's appeal to the Qur'an "a ruse" (`light` p. 13; L01 ¶6, recalled from Ch 1). Answer: Mutahhari allows mistakes but not bad faith; recall Ch 6. [14m] "I do not believe that the 'urafa' have had evil intentions towards Islam." (`light` p. 14). Arabic: n.a.
   - _Pantheism._ Answered by §2. Khomeini: [14n] "why did you blindly accuse them of unbelief and insult them?" (`forty_hadith` p. 345). Arabic: n.a. Sadra was called a heretic though he wrote against the Sufis (`forty_hadith` p. 402, in words).
   - _Ecstatic sayings and licence._ The tradition answered these itself, in §1 (Junayd).
   - _Ibn ʿArabi's Sunni loyalties and passages hostile to Shiʿa_ (L13 ¶47–53). Experience cannot settle Shia–Sunni questions; reason must (¶47–48). Visions take the shape of one's theology (¶53). "you cannot 100% accept someone or reject" (¶52). Side by side (D20): his chain runs to Gilani (`light` p. 34), while Amuli reads his Seal of the Saints as ʿAli (`inner_secrets` p. 99). The Sheikh judges the Sunni-side case "much much more powerful" (¶55).
**Boxes (2–3):** Who's Who: Hakim al-Tirmidhi and the Seal of the Saints, a hierarchy taken "to very extreme" levels (L13 ¶4–12, ¶9; side by side `inner_secrets` pp. 98–99; no saint numbers). · Word study: four views of existence (L13 ¶56–62). · Optional Who's Who: Attar and Rumi (`light` pp. 33, 36).
**Try this:** Say *lā ḥawla wa lā quwwata illā biʾllāh* with the meaning the lecture gives it: every strength one has is held from Him (L13 ¶63–64). Arabic: VERIFY (not in the duʿa extracts).
**Hand-off:** How did the road become orders, and where do Shia stand?
**Callbacks / forward pointers:** Ch 1 (jurists' objection), Ch 3, Ch 6, Ch 12 (fana, baqa, dangers). Forward: Ch 15 (orders), Ch 16 (baqa completed as return to people).
**Cut / gone around:**
- Reworded: Junayd as Bayazid's "student" (¶25, chronology) becomes "a generation later"; "sovereignty" (¶13, ¶28) becomes sahw, from `light` p. 57.
- Left out: the unclear attribution in ¶26.
- Cut: the Simorgh (¶23–25: "lover, beloved and love are one" is the drunk view; one line at most); "Shia ideas via extremists" (¶7); Plato and Plotinus (¶4–5); the Yasin dream and the dream-books (¶35–43; claims of inspiration in one line); Platonic "sum total" (¶61–62, unclear); the Bayazid–Sahifa link (¶17–18, garbled); the Gorbachev letter (¶51–52; one line at most).
- No detail: the Rajabiyyun passage (¶48) appears only as "passages hostile to Shiʿa".
**Target:** ~2,900 words

### 15. Sufis and Shia
**Subheading:** The Sufi orders and Shia masters walk one road from one source, ʿAli, but a Shia traveller takes a tested guide rather than a chain of authority, because authority belongs to the Imam.
**Conclusion:** Shia ʿirfan shares the path and its source with Sufism, not its institution of authority. **Serves:** B4 **Old number:** 9
**Opener (≤150 words):** Duʿa Tawassul on a Tuesday night (Mafatih p. 170): we call on the Prophet and then each Imam by name, in order, to the Imam of our time. A Sufi order also recites a chain, of its shaykhs back to the Prophet (L14 ¶5–7). Two lines; this chapter is about the difference.
**Words you'll meet:** tariqa · silsila · shaykh · samaʿ, khirqa (add both to GLOSSARY)
**Sections:**
1. ## What an order is [15.1]. A Sufi order is an institution: a founder's method, a chain to the Prophet, and total submission.
   - Leave "tariqa" untranslated (L14 ¶3–5). An elder's method of stations (¶6–7); a chain (¶7–8); total submission, "just like a dead person, before someone who performs ghusl" (¶21). Sub-orders, double membership, honorary licences (¶11–19). Samaʿ in one line (¶14–18).
   - The orders are late: no Sufis by that name in the first century (`light` pp. 22–23). The orders took shape centuries after the first seekers (L14 ¶7; `light` p. 5).
   - quote [15a] the system came to include "total obedience, liturgy, special robes, music and ecstasy" (`light` p. 6). Arabic: n.a.
   - Three portraits:
     - ʿAbd al-Qadir al-Gilani: a Hanbali preacher, "one of the most controversial figures" (L14 ¶27–30; `light` p. 31).
     - Shihab al-Din ʿUmar Suhrawardi: *ʿAwarif al-maʿarif*, the perpetual lā ilāha illā'llāh; not the Illuminationist (L14 ¶36, ¶56–59; `light` p. 34).
     - Baha al-Din Naqshband: silent dhikr, company rather than retreat, the shariʿa, no show of wonders (L14 ¶62–78; `light` p. 40).
2. ## One source [15.2]. Nearly every chain runs to ʿAli, so Sufi and Shia seekers share a source.
   - quote [15b] "all of these orders culminate in Hadrat Ali Ibn Abi Talib" (`kernel` p. 87). Arabic: n.a. Two or three of the 25 main orders are Shia (same page). The golden chain runs via Maʿruf to al-Rida (`light` p. 25, recall Ch 13). Most robe-chains pass through Junayd, who "lived soon after the time of the infallible Imams" (`inner_secrets` p. 13).
   - Even orders tracing descent to Abu Bakr take the chain through ʿAli; the Naqshbandiyya is the exception (L14 ¶36–37, ¶79).
   - Inherited inner knowledge passed heart to heart is the Shia idea of the Imam's inheritance (L14 ¶8–9).
   - quote [15c] the Qutb "does not actually exist anywhere outside the Shi'ite world" (`light` p. 6). Arabic: n.a.
3. ## A channel, not a method [15.3]. Set techniques are secondary because each soul receives grace in its own shape; what a seeker needs is the tested guide of Ch 7, not a lineage.
   - The orders are their techniques (L14 ¶59–60). Yet the social and the secluded, the loud and the silent, all became ʿurafaʾ: "no one single method" (¶65).
   - The channel and the vessel: grace "will take your own shape" (¶66–69). The Sheikh's verse (¶68): [15d] Q 11:119 "except those on whom your Lord has mercy—and that is why He created them". Arabic: held.
   - quote [15e] Amuli: the paths to Allah are "as many as the souls of creation" (`inner_secrets` p. 195). Arabic: VERIFY. This means within Islam, not any religion (L14 ¶70).
   - Where a technique breaks the law, the Shia masters refuse it. [15f] "it is not permissible in the sacred Shari 'ah to concentrate on a stone or piece of wood" (`kernel` p. 87, on the Naqshbandi method). Arabic: n.a.
   - The guide is recalled in words only (L14 ¶22–24; Ch 7 owns the test).
4. ## Authority belongs to the Imam [15.4]. There is no chain of authority beside the Imam and no blind obedience; a teacher, yes.
   - Imamah leaves no room for a rival silsila (L14 ¶6). Total submission is "an exaggeration" (¶25).
   - quote [15g] "our tariqah is that of the marhum Akhund which does not end in any of these silsilahs" (`kernel` p. 88). Arabic: n.a.
   - From Sadra and Fayd on, Shia masters stood outside the orders with [15h] "a perfect loyalty to the shari'ah" (`light` pp. 40–41). Arabic: n.a.
   - Side by side (D20): Shia masters used teacher and pledge. Amuli took the Sufi robe (`inner_secrets` p. 8); the Kernel's traveller pledges to "an aware shaykh" (`kernel` p. 60); Khomeini was Shahabadi's murid (`forty_hadith` p. 8). Yet Amuli: [15i] "My arriving at the Truth and my unveilings were not dependent on the above" (`inner_secrets` p. 14). Arabic: n.a.
   - quote [15j] "the Imam's company is necessary after the attainment of the goal (wusul) too" (`kernel` p. 81). Arabic: n.a. Ends here: the Imam goes with the traveller at every rank.
**Boxes (2):** Who's Who: al-Ghazali (`light` p. 31) and Fayd Kashani, who reworked the *Ihya* with the Imams' hadith as *al-Mahajja al-bayda* (L14 ¶41–45; `light` p. 40). · History: why the orders rose and then lost weight (L14 ¶19; `light` p. 40).
**Try this:** For a week, notice in which act you already keep (salah, the Qur'an, a duʿa) your heart is most present, and give that one a fixed time each day: "everyone should find his or her own suitable type of practice and worship" (L14 ¶70–71). Alternative: lā ilāha illā'llāh said silently, the hidden dhikr (L14 ¶57–58).
**Hand-off:** If authority belongs to the Imam, what does the completed journey look like in him?
**Callbacks / forward pointers:** Ch 6 (law), Ch 7 (the guide's test), Ch 10 (dhikr), Ch 13 (ʿAli, Maʿruf), Ch 14 (Junayd). Forward: Ch 16.
**Cut / gone around:**
- Cut (contentious, D39/D15): Naqshbandi hostility (¶62, ¶79); the shaytan cult and Ahmad Ghazali (¶46–54); the Ismailis and the Aga Khan (¶20); "Sufism a branch of Shiʿism" (¶10); Gilani "80% wrong" and the epidemic invocation (¶30–34); *Ihya* "full of rubbish" (¶37–38); Ghazan Khan (¶64); the wuquf-i qalbi hadith, which the Sheikh doubts (¶88–91); the Nizamiyya aside; the Khoja/pir box (removed).
- Gone around: the Ghazali brothers confusion (¶37) uses `light` p. 31 instead. "Seventh century" (AH or CE?) becomes "centuries later". "Syed Ali Kazmini" (¶22) is left unnamed. The promised "Shia tariqa" (¶25) is answered by [15g].
- Reordered: ARGUMENT's 9.3 and 9.4 are swapped so the chapter ends on the Imam (FRAMEWORK §2). The dependency holds.
**Target:** ~2,900 words
