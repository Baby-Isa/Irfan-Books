## 1. The book in one page

**Thesis.** ʿIrfan, the disciplined journey to know and love God, is worth every believer's pursuit: it travels through the self and never leaves the shariʿa; it is walked by a tested and ordered method; it is rooted in the Qur'an and the Imams; and it ends not in withdrawal but in return, to people with God, and to God Himself.

**Five slides.**

| Part | Reader's question | Answer in a line |
|---|---|---|
| One · The Call | Why travel? | The Qur'an invites more than the minimum, our prayers already ask for it, and what they ask for is a knowing we do not yet have. |
| Two · Getting ready | What do I need before I set out? | The road runs through your own self, the shariʿa is its boundary, and a tested guide walks beside you under the Imam. |
| Three · The Road | How is it walked? | In a known order: veils removed, starting with repentance and character, trained by measured disciplines, carried by love, to the loss of the false self. |
| Four · Fellow travellers | Who else is on this road, and where did they arrive? | It grew from the Qur'an and the Imams; some lost their footing in ecstasy, the sober went further; Shia masters keep it without a Sufi chain, because authority belongs to the Imam. |
| Five · The Destination | What is the road for? | Not the road but God: His friends come back to people, with Him; every soul goes back to Him; ʿirfan is going back willingly, now. |

**The seventeen subheadings, in order.**

1. The Qur'an invites us beyond the minimum to see God's signs; we miss them because something inside us needs repair; the repaired state is open to every believer, and its only aim is God, never powers.
2. The prayers we recite in Shaʿban, in Ramadan and at ʿArafah already ask for what the mystics seek, so the goal of ʿirfan is already on our lips.
3. We already know a great deal about God; the prayers ask to know Him, a different knowing that comes as light in a purified heart, so the path is purification, and study prepares for it.
4. That knowing happens in the soul, so the road to God runs through the self; and since God is already near, the journey lifts veils rather than covering distance.
5. The self is deeper than it looks: it lives in more than one world, its deeds give it a form, and what it finally becomes is in our hands.
6. So inward a journey is kept safe by the shariʿa: law, path and truth are one thing at three depths, so there is no inner path outside the law.
7. A guide helps, and Shia teachers who walked the road exist within living memory; but authority stays with the Imam, so a guide is trusted, never blindly obeyed, and tested by the shariʿa, not by wonders.
8. God is already near, so the road is the removal of veils, in four stages that come in a fixed order.
9. The first steps are repentance, open to anyone after any sin and taken at a steady pace, and good conduct towards people.
10. Silence, hunger, night prayer, seclusion and remembrance train the soul; each has a Qur'anic root and is taken in measured doses, never forced.
11. Fear and renunciation start the journey, but love carries it, the love the Imams voice in their prayers; in the end even love is left behind at the Beloved.
12. **Crescendo.** The first journey ends in fanaʾ: losing the false sense of self before God's glory, not becoming God; the traveller then comes back, sober, seeing only God.
13. The road was not brought in from outside: it grew from the Qur'an, the companions and the Imams, and its first travellers met fear, renunciation and love in that order, with the Imams ahead of them all.
14. Some who reached the loss of self stayed lost in it; the sober came back and went further; the theory their experience produced has critics, and their concerns have answers.
15. Sufi orders and Shia masters walk one road from one source, ʿAli, but a Shia traveller takes a tested guide, not a chain of authority, because authority belongs to the Imam.
16. The road does not end alone with God; it ends back among people, with God, as it did for the Prophet and the Imams.
17. Every soul goes back to God by giving back, in reverse, what it was given; ʿirfan is giving it back willingly, now, and hell is the same return refused.

**Why the shape works for an impatient reader.** Each Part ends on the question the reader is now asking and the next Part answers it: wanting it (One) forces "what do I need?"; having the kit (Two) forces "how is it walked?"; reaching the summit at Ch 12 forces "who else got here, and were they safe?"; knowing the company (Four) forces "what was it all for?". The crescendo sits at Ch 12 of 17, two-thirds through; Parts Four and Five are the descent, shorter in theory and longer in people.

### PART ONE · The Call

**Why travel?** The Qur'an invites more than the minimum. Our prayers already ask for it. And what they ask for is a knowing we do not yet have.
Chapters 1–3. Munajat §73–74: "My Lord, I was not strong enough to move away from Your disobedience, except when You awakened me to Your love."

**1. More than the minimum** (approved text; title change only)

- Opener: the wedding verse, Q 30:21, "There are indeed signs in that for a people who reflect."
- What the minimum leaves out — the wajibat save, but the Qur'an asks for more — Q 30:22–24.
- Signs most people miss — the signs are everywhere, unseen — Q 12:105.
- The fault is in the receiver — the inner machine has rusted — Q 7:179.
- Some do see — the Qur'an names those who perceive — Nahj 3.403.
- What they are given, and why it is open to all — Q 56:10–11, 16:97, 4:69.
- The aim is God, not powers — "These are shirk" (`kernel` pp. 28–29).
- Seeking, and nothing else — the conclusion — "is actually seeking and nothing else".
- Try this: read Q 30:21–24 slowly once a day for a week.
- Hand-off: If the aim is to know and love God, what does that longing sound like?

**2. You already know these words**

- Opener: Thursday night, Duʿa Kumayl; "And my heart enthralled by Your love!" (Kumayl §221). What are we asking for when we say words like these?
- Cut off from all else — the prayers' first request is severance in the heart, not withdrawal from people — Munajat §104, "grant me complete severance of my relations with everything else"; Abu Hamza §384; ʿArafah §783–784.
- To see, to love, to draw near — the prayers ask for Ch 1's aims — Munajat §92–93, "place me among Your friends in the position of one who hopes for an increase in Your love"; Munajat §82; ʿArafah §781; Abu Hamza §599; the Lovers, "granted gazing upon Thy face".
- A pupil reading above his level — we ask from need and are honoured to be allowed to — "like if you tell a primary school boy to read books of university" (L01 ¶17); ʿArafah §689; Abu Hamza §510. The Part One line is spoken to here: even the turning is God's doing.
- Prayers for ordinary nights — these are the Mafatih's familiar prayers on nights ordinary believers keep — Khomeini, "You are already familiar with the words used in Imams' supplications" (`light` p. 156). Access, not attainment.
- Words already on our lips — the conclusion, once.
- Try this: recite the Munajat in Shaʿban "with presence of heart"; outside Shaʿban, its last petitions (§82–111) once a week, from the Appendix.
- Hand-off: The prayers ask for knowledge of God. What kind of knowing is that?

**3. Knowing about God is not knowing Him**

- Opener: a Ramadan fast; no one learns hunger from a book. Fiqh, belief and history are knowledge *about*; the prayers ask for the other kind.
- Knowing about, and knowing — acquired knowledge defines and limits; knowledge by presence arrives — Khomeini: the ʿarif knows "through direct witnessing", the ʿalim "through philosophical proofs" (`forty_hadith` p. 542).
- Where reason stops — reason knows attributes, never an essence — "Soaring thoughts cannot reach him and deep minds cannot fathom him" (Nahj 1.1.1).
- Light in the heart — knowledge of God is light placed in a purified heart; nothing is withheld on His side — "Knowledge is not extensive learning. Rather, it is a light that God casts in the heart of whomever He wills" (`forty_hadith` p. 331); Q 24:35.
- The path is purification — study prepares the ground but cannot do the work — "You purify yourself and then wait for Allah to put the light in your heart" (L03 ¶20); Munajat §88, "One who follows Your path is enlightened"; three scholars side by side on study.
- Knowing by drawing near — the conclusion, once.
- Try this: in prostration, say "Glory belongs to my Lord, the Exalted, and Him do I praise" while believing it (`kernel` p. 56).
- Hand-off: If that knowing happens in the soul, what is the soul?

### PART TWO · Getting ready

**What do I need before I set out?** The road to God runs through your own self, which is deeper than it looks. The shariʿa is its boundary from the first step to the last. And a tested guide walks beside you, under the Imam, never in his place.
Chapters 4–7. Munajat §5–7: "I have come running to You and am standing before You imploring You in humility."

**4. Know yourself, know your Lord**

- Opener: in Shaʿban we say "I have been unjust to my soul for I have not looked after it" (Munajat §32) as a confession. What would looking after it mean?
- The instrument of all knowing — the soul is what we know with, so it comes first — Q 91:9–10, "one who purifies it is felicitous, and one who betrays it fails."
- Forgetting God, forgetting ourselves — the Qur'an ties the two losses; the tradition ties the two knowings — Q 59:19, "Do not be like those who forget Allah, so He makes them forget their own souls"; "Whoever knows his own self knows his Lord", Prophet and Imam ʿAli side by side.
- Near already: meeting, not grasping — meeting God is nearness, not comprehension; He is near, so the journey removes veils — "We are actually in his presence at the moment. However, he is not in our presence" (L04 ¶54).
- Try this: after the daily prayers, Q 59:18–24, as Shahabadi asked his pupils (`forty_hadith` p. 201).
- Hand-off: If the self is the door, how deep does it go?

**5. The soul and its worlds**

- Opener: a graveside; we picture barzakh as a waiting room elsewhere. "Exactly we don't go anywhere" (L05 ¶6). Epigraph Munajat §118–119, "if my disregard for preparations to meet You has put me to sleep, my knowledge of Your kind bounties has awakened me."
- Depths, not distances — the material, imaginal and intellectual worlds are one reality at three depths — "another dimension of the same world in which we are living" (L05 ¶3–5).
- A soul that crosses them — in sleep, death and contemplation — "sleep is the brother of death" (`lantern` p. 24).
- The form our deeds make — deeds shape an inner form we will meet — Khomeini, "another form and a different face of him… determined by the qualities of his soul" (`forty_hadith` p. 41).
- The self can change its state — one nafs, three states, movable by dhikr, fasting and night prayer — Q 13:28, "The hearts find rest in Allah's remembrance!"; "whatever we do with it, would remain with us forever" (L05 ¶56).
- Try this: before sleep, "examine yourself by your sleep" (`lantern` p. 24).
- Hand-off: What keeps so inward a work from going astray?

**6. No path outside the law**

- Opener: *Allahu akbar*, and "Suddenly, you think about did I lock my car?" (L06 ¶30). Epigraph Munajat §126–127, "who do not fail to show You their gratitude and do not take Your orders lightly."
- Shell, core and kernel — law, path and truth are one reality at three depths — the ship, the sea and the pearl (L06 ¶13–17); "Sharia is a shell tariqa is a core and haqiqa is the kernel" (L06 ¶23).
- The inner never cancels the outer — wonders without the law prove nothing; the first inner work is attention in prayer — Kubra: whoever walks on water yet abandons "even one injunction of God… they are liars" (L06 ¶17–20); Khomeini, `forty_hadith` p. 35.
- To the last breath — even the end of the road is lived under the law — Tabatabaʾi, "from the beginning of wayfaring to its final stage the wayfarer must abide by all the precepts of the luminous Shari'ah" (`kernel` p. 30); the four journeys in a sentence, pointer to Ch 16.
- Try this: one prayer a day at the start of its time, nothing scheduled around it (`forty_hadith` p. 375).
- Hand-off: The law is the boundary. Who walks beside the traveller?

**7. Finding a guide**

- Opener: Surah al-Kahf on a Friday; Musa asks a stranger, "May I follow you…?" (Q 18:66). Even a prophet sought a teacher.
- Why a companion — the road has turns a traveller cannot read alone; scholars weigh the need differently — "it comes time that you hate worship" (L14 ¶23–24); the Sheikh: law first, teacher later; Mutahhari: without one the traveller "is in danger of going astray" (`light` p. 10).
- A guide beside the Imam, not in his place — no chain of authority, no blind obedience — "We don't have such a thing as tariqa in Shia" (L14 ¶6–8); Tabatabaʾi: "The wayfarer cannot dispense with the company of the special teacher in any circumstance whatsoever" (`kernel` p. 80). Pointer to Ch 15.
- Teachers within living memory — Shia jurists who walked the road form a line to our time — Tabatabaʾi: "My preceptor was the late Haji Mirza Ali Agha Qazi" (`light` p. 106); "we belong to none of these orders" (p. 105).
- How to test a guide — by the shariʿa and by not wanting followers, never by wonders — not "extraordinary feats, knowledge of hidden things" (`kernel` p. 81); ends on ʿIsa: sit "With one the sight of whom reminds you of Allah" (`lantern` p. 13).
- Try this: this week, notice whose company reminds you of God, and seek it out.
- Hand-off: Once a guide is found, what does the road itself look like?

### PART THREE · The Road

**How is it walked?** In a known order. The traveller covers no distance but removes veils: repentance and good conduct first, then measured disciplines, carried by love, to the loss of the false self.
Chapters 8–12. Munajat §94: "My Lord, inspire me with a passionate love of remembering You so that I may keep on remembering You."

**8. The stages of the journey**

- Opener: we save for years to reach Makka, Najaf or Karbala; Abu Hamza names another journey, "He who travels towards You will easily reach his destination" (§46–48).
- Nearer than the jugular vein — travel means removing veils, not covering distance — Q 50:16; Munajat §80, "You Near One, Who is not far from one who is away from You."
- Four stages, in order — polishing by the law, emptying the heart, adornment by God, fanaʾ; none skipped — the law "should accompany a mystic until the end" (L07 ¶21); Q 6:122, "a light by which he walks among the people". Box: Ibn Sina's ladder.
- Try this: the Musabbihat before sleep; Qadi gave beginners this for a year.
- Hand-off: Where on it does one set foot first?

**9. Setting out**

- Opener: *astaghfirullah* after salah, by habit. Epigraph Munajat §64–65, "I have wasted my life committing the crime of forgetting You…"
- Anyone can begin — repentance is open after any sin; despair is the obstacle — Q 39:53, "do not despair of the mercy of Allah"; Munajat §100–101, "Your repentant bondman".
- Steady, not fast — paced; relapse is fought again; sooner is easier — "Too much enthusiasm in the beginning leads to too little interest in the end" (`light` p. 89).
- First, put things right with people — good conduct is the first condition — "the first and foremost… condition is to behave yourself with regards to people" (L08 ¶39); Nahj 1.191. Box: the young man told "You are rushing."
- Try this: the Dhu'l-Qaʿdah Sunday prayer as the Mafatih gives it (p. 356).
- Hand-off: What trains the soul once it has turned?

**10. The five disciplines**

- Opener: in Ramadan we keep most of them already; ʿirfan keeps a measure all year.
- Five disciplines, one aim — each loosens the soul's hold on the body and has a Qur'anic root — silence, Munajat §83, "a tongue the truth of which may be submitted to You"; hunger; night, Q 73:1–4; seclusion "of the heart rather than seclusion of the body" (L10 ¶42); remembrance, Q 2:152, "Remember Me and I will remember you."
- A medicine, measured to the patient — dosed to the person — "half an hour is enough" (L10 ¶23); liturgies "are just like a medicine which may suit some and may not suit others" (`light` p. 102); the Sheikh's private hour beside the masters' retreat (`kernel` p. 77).
- Try this: the Tasbih of al-Zahraʾ after each prayer, framed by Nahj 1.219.1, "God has made his remembrance the burnish for your hearts."
- Hand-off: What keeps a traveller going when discipline alone runs dry?

**11. Love, the engine**

- Opener: among Shaʿban's requests for pardon, a sentence no trader would say: "If You haul me into the hell, I will tell its inmates that I love You" (Munajat §59).
- Fear starts the journey; love carries it — "Fear is the branch of knowledge; hope is the branch of certainty, and love is the branch of gnosis" (`lantern` p. 58).
- Love as the Imams pray it — worship as the free, not as traders or slaves — Nahj 3.222, "the worship of the free", beside al-Sadiq's "because they love Him" (`light` p. 94); Kumayl §123–124, "How can I endure separation from You?"; the Lovers, "tasted the sweetness of Thy love".
- Even love is left at the door — love too is a means — "he realizes that love is different from the beloved" (`light` p. 95); Rabiʿa in one line. Box: "Ask your heart whether it is in search of God or whether it is in love with itself" (`forty_hadith` p. 89).
- Try this: read the Whispered Prayer of the Lovers once this week.
- Hand-off: Where does love carry the traveller in the end?

**12. Losing the self, finding God** (the crescendo)

- Opener: sujud, twice in every rakʿa, "the position of annihilation in Allah" (`journey` p. 88).
- What is lost is the false self — fanaʾ loses a false picture of the self; one neither ceases to exist nor becomes God — "out of the imaginary you to the real you" (L09 ¶9); union with God is "just a shirk or kufr" (L07 ¶60–61).
- Three degrees: in action, in attributes, in essence — each the truth of words the reader already says: *la hawla*, *la ilaha illa Allah*, *Huwa* — Q 18:44, 2:255, 112:1. Summit: the prologue's sentence continues, "and let our souls get suspended by the glory of Your sanctity" (Munajat §108).
- Coming back, sober — fanaʾ has dangers; the healthy end is baqaʾ, oneself again, seeing God and the world together — "That's the beginning, that's not the end" (L09 ¶20); "the eternal grace comes to his aid, bringing him to himself" (`forty_hadith` p. 515). Last line, Imam ʿAli: "I did not see a thing without beholding God with it, in it, before it, and after it" (`forty_hadith` p. 516).
- Try this: in Surah al-Ikhlas this week, stop on its first word, *Huwa*.
- Hand-off: Others have walked this far. Where did the road come from, and who walked it first?

### PART FOUR · Fellow travellers

**Who else is on this road, and where did they arrive?** The road you have walked was not imported: it runs from the Qur'an and the Imams. Some who walked it lost their footing in ecstasy; the sober came back and went further. Shia masters keep it without a Sufi chain of authority, because authority belongs to the Imam.
Chapters 13–15. Munajat §109–110, duas.org wording (FRAMEWORK §8.3): "appoint me of those who responded to You when You called them, and at whom You glanced so he swoons before Your Majesty."

**13. Where it comes from**

- Opener: Duʿa Kumayl; in the chains later travellers drew, Kumayl stands near the top under ʿAli. _Note: FRAMEWORK §8.4 asks for a different opener, since Chs 2 and 16 open on Kumayl; the part file has not changed it yet (decision 6)._
- Not brought in from outside — at most some techniques were borrowed — Western scholars "cannot believe that Islam as a religion of law and jurisprudence can produce mystics" (L11 ¶3); Q 57:27; Nicholson, "Surely the seeds of mysticism are here" (`light` p. 21).
- The Qur'an, the companions and the Imams — the roots are inside Islam from the first generation — the young companion after dawn prayer, "a man whose heart has been illuminated with the light of faith by God" (`light` p. 19).
- Fear, renunciation, love, and the Imams above them — Fudayl hears Q 57:16, "Is it not time yet…"; Ibrahim b. Adham, with Nahj 3.221, "the bones of a pig in the hand of a leper"; Rabiʿa's torch and water (L12 ¶54); the seekers' words "an echo" of al-Baqir and al-Sadiq.
- Try this: muhasaba each night; al-Kazim, "He who does not take account of his own soul once every day is not one of us" (`kernel` p. 68).
- Hand-off: Where did those travellers arrive?

**14. Drunk and sober**

- Opener: the divider line recalled in words; some were thunderstruck, and what came after the swoon divided them.
- Lost in God, or back with God — the tradition ranked the return higher — Bayazid, "I came forth from Bayazid-ness as a snake from its skin" (`light` p. 25); Hallaj, "His crime was that he used to reveal secrets" (p. 27); Junayd, "This state, loftier even than mahw and mahq, is called sahw" (p. 57).
- A theory of what they saw — Ibn ʿArabi's unity of being, grasped by experience, not argument — "certainly I'm not god or a part of god" (L13 ¶63); Khomeini, "the sea is not the waves, although the waves are the sea" (`light` p. 144).
- The critics' concerns, and the answers — scoped as how the defenders answer, since no critical source is held — "I do not believe that the 'urafa' have had evil intentions towards Islam" (`light` p. 14); Ibn ʿArabi's Sunni loyalties: reason, not experience, settles that.
- Try this: say *la hawla wa la quwwata illa billah* meaning that every strength is held from Him.
- Hand-off: How did the road become orders, and where do Shia stand?

**15. Sufis and Shia**

- Opener: Duʿa Tawassul on a Tuesday night, the Imams named in order; a Sufi order recites a chain too.
- What an order is — a founder's method, a chain, total submission — "total obedience, liturgy, special robes, music and ecstasy" (`light` p. 6); Gilani, Suhrawardi, Naqshband in three portraits.
- One source — nearly every chain runs to ʿAli — "all of these orders culminate in Hadrat Ali Ibn Abi Talib" (`kernel` p. 87).
- A channel, not a method — grace takes each soul's shape, so a seeker needs Ch 7's tested guide, not a lineage — paths "as many as the souls of creation" (`inner_secrets` p. 195); no technique that breaks the law.
- Authority belongs to the Imam — no chain beside him, no blind obedience; a teacher, yes — "our tariqah is that of the marhum Akhund which does not end in any of these silsilahs" (`kernel` p. 88). Ends: "the Imam's company is necessary after the attainment of the goal (wusul) too" (`kernel` p. 81).
- Try this: for a week, notice in which act your heart is most present, and give it a fixed daily time.
- Hand-off: If authority belongs to the Imam, what does the completed journey look like in him?

### PART FIVE · The Destination

**What is the road for?** Not for the road, but for God. Those who reach Him come back to people, with Him. Every soul goes back to Him; ʿirfan is going back willingly, now.
Chapters 16–17. Munajat §128–131: "My Lord, let me be attached to the Light of Your Majestic Glory, so that I may know You alone, be away from others, and have a heart fearful of You and an eye watchful of You."

**16. Back among people**

- Epigraph Munajat §111, "You whisper to them secretly and they work for You openly." Opener: Imam ʿAli leads Kumayl toward the cemetery outside Kufa; of the few who hold God's proof, "In this world they live in bodies whose spirits are linked to the highest abode" (Nahj 3.133).
- A quarter of the way — after fanaʾ the road goes on: the first two journeys — "just one quarter of the way" (L09 ¶75).
- The way back — the third and fourth journeys bring him back to people, with God — "The fourth journey is amongst the people while still united with God" (`light` p. 50); those who stop early find it "very difficult for them to mix with the people" (L09 ¶91).
- Who completes it — the Sheikh and Tabatabaʾi side by side, gently — "the office of prophethood and Imamate is something exclusive, but attainment to… wilayah is not at all exclusive" (`kernel` p. 20).
- From ʿArafah to Karbala — the Imams embody the return; Karbala as a reflection — Khomeini, "Just see his Supplication of the Day of Arafah and ponder over it" (`light` p. 138); ʿArafah §790–791, "What can one who finds You miss anything else?"; stop on the ʿAshura instruction to "express sympathy to each other" (Mafatih p. 401).
- Try this: in duʿa, pray first for other believers by name, then for yourself (`journey` p. 83).
- Hand-off: The friends of God return to people. Where does every soul return?

**17. The return to God**

- Epigraph Q 7:29, "Even as He brought you forth in the beginning, so will you return." Opener: Surah Yasin by a sickbed; "iron collars around their necks" (Q 36:8), which no one reads literally.
- Reading from the inside — inner meaning agrees with the outer and never cancels it — "a type of insightful look at the verses of the Quran" (L16 ¶3–8); Khomeini, "Islam does not allow anybody to interpret the Qur'an according to his personal opinion" (`light` p. 110).
- Given in order, returned in reverse — existence, knowledge, power, will; the return runs backwards — Tusi: "God, the Exalted, becomes his hearing and sight, power, knowledge and existence" (`forty_hadith` p. 517).
- Will, power, knowledge, existence — four surrenders: rida, tawakkul, taslim, fanaʾ — Q 2:131, "I submit to the Lord of all the worlds"; ʿArafah §670, "I am ignorant despite my knowledge".
- The return refused — hell is the same return undergone against one's will — the chain's length is "the length of the will that they have" (L16 ¶43); "God doesn't want to revenge" (¶47–48), yet the fire is real (Khomeini, `forty_hadith` pp. 137, 365); Munajat §120–121, "if Your severe punishment calls me to Hell, the abundance of Your reward invites me to Paradise."
- Going back now — ʿirfan is the willing return, made in this life — Q 2:156, "to Him do we indeed return", said now of oneself; end on Q 89:27–28, "O soul at peace! Return to your Lord, pleased and pleasing!"
- Try this: each day read a few verses "as though all of them have been revealed especially for him" (`journey` p. 46).
- Hand-off: So how does one begin, this week?

**Epilogue. First steps** (~1,800 words). Opener: the Sheikh's measure, half an hour is enough, a practice kept "for at least a year or so" (L10 ¶23–26). Then how to begin; this week; this month; this year; close on Munajat §124. One table. See section 4.

## 4. The "Try this" ladder

1. Read Q 30:21–24 slowly once a day for a week.
2. The Munajat in Shaʿban with presence of heart; outside Shaʿban, its last petitions once a week.
3. The prostration dhikr, believed.
4. Q 59:18–24 after the daily prayers.
5. Before sleep, examine yourself by your sleep.
6. One prayer a day at the start of its time; at *Allahu akbar*, know what you are saying.
7. Seek the company that reminds you of God.
8. The Musabbihat before sleep.
9. The Dhu'l-Qaʿdah Sunday prayer of repentance.
10. The Tasbih of al-Zahraʾ after each prayer, slowly.
11. Read the Whispered Prayer of the Lovers once.
12. Stop on *Huwa* in Surah al-Ikhlas.
13. Muhasaba each night.
14. Say *la hawla wa la quwwata illa billah* meaning it.
15. Find the act where your heart is most present; give it a fixed daily time.
16. Pray first for others by name.
17. Read a few verses a day as addressed to you.

**How the epilogue gathers them.** Principles first: gradual; a programme that suits you (`journey` p. 109); nothing dropped because it cannot be done fully (p. 161); the wajibat and a companion, in words. Then three rungs. _This week_, daily acts of minutes: Chs 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 16. _This month_, one practice held: Chs 2, 7, 10, 17. _This year_, one practice kept (L10 ¶25–26); Maliki's heavier programmes are the ceiling, not the start. Close on Munajat §124.

## 7. Fable's view

Each chapter has a reason to turn the page, and the hand-offs are real questions, not transitions. Four places need work before drafting.

**Ch 5 risks reading as a lecture on metaphysics.** Three worlds, an image body, layers of the soul, three states of the nafs: it is the most theory the reader meets before setting foot on the road, and it arrives before they know why they need it. Fix: keep the graveside and tonight's sleep in view throughout, cut "Depths, not distances" to a page, send the layers to their box, and make every section pay off in the fourth, "what it finally becomes is in our hands", the one point the reader must carry forward.

**Ch 13 is the cool-down after the crescendo, and it opens on the coldest material.** The reader has just reached the summit; the next page is orientalists, monks and Neoplatonism. That is where an impatient reader closes the book. Fix: open on a person (decision 6 helps), lead with the companions and the Imams, then the three travellers, and compress "Not brought in from outside" to a short closing section or a box. The chapter's answer is "it came from here", and people prove that better than a refutation does.

**Ch 14 §3 promises more than the sources allow.** The prologue says Chapter 14 "sets out their wider concerns, with the answers". The outline has only the defenders' answers, because no critical source is held. A reader who came for the objection will notice. Fix: either hold a critical source before drafting and give the concerns one honest paragraph in the critics' own terms, or soften the prologue line to "the answers given to them" and let the section be plainly a defence.

**Two "Try this" acts break the ladder's promise of minutes.** Ch 9's Dhu'l-Qaʿdah Sunday prayer is tied to one month and is a full rite; Ch 2's Munajat is tied to Shaʿban. Both are right as practices, but neither can be done this week by a reader who finishes the chapter in Rajab. Fix: give each chapter a daily version for this week (a hundred *astaghfirullah* after one prayer; the Munajat's last petitions from the Appendix, which the outline already offers) and keep the calendar-bound rite as the month or year rung.

One smaller point. The 15→16 hand-off, "what does the completed journey look like in him?", is the hinge of the book, turning from history back to the destination, and Ch 16 then opens with the four-journeys scheme before it reaches the Imam. The opener already has Nahj 3.133; let the first section stay with that scene and bring the scheme in afterwards, so Part Five opens on a face and not a table.
