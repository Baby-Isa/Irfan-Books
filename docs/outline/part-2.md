# Part Two: Getting ready — detailed outline (v3)

_Planning notes per FRAMEWORK §6. Ch 7 = old 10 plus a short old 9.3. Munajat English `light` pp. 163, 166; Arabic `duas_munajat_shabaniyah` by §._

## Part divider
- **Part Two: Getting ready**
- **Question:** What do I need before I set out?
- **Answer:** The road to God runs through your own self, which is deeper than it looks. The shariʿa is its boundary from the first step to the last. And a tested guide walks beside you, under the Imam, never in his place.
- **Chapters:** 4 Know yourself, know your Lord · 5 The soul and its worlds · 6 No path outside the law · 7 Finding a guide
- **Munajat [P2-M]:** "I have come running to You and am standing before You imploring You in humility" [@light, p. 163] — Arabic held: §5–7 (`فَقَدْ هَرَبْتُ إِلَيْكَ وَوَقَفْتُ بَيْنَ يَدَيْكَ مُسْتَكِيناً لَكَ مُتَضَرِّعاً إِلَيْكَ`).

### 4. Know yourself, know your Lord
**Subheading:** The knowing the prayers ask for happens in the soul, so the road to God runs through the self; and since God is already near, the journey lifts veils rather than covering distance.
**Conclusion:** the way to God runs through the self. **Serves:** B2 **Old number:** 4
**Opener:** In Shaʿban we say, "I have been unjust to my soul for I have not looked after it" [4-M]. We say it as a confession of sins. What would looking after it mean? The Sheikh begins all of ʿirfan's theory there. No epigraph.
**Words you'll meet:** *nafs* · *ruh* · *hijab* (pl. *hujub*) · *qurb* · *liqaʾ Allah* [add to GLOSSARY first]
**Sections:**
1. ## The instrument of all knowing [4.1] — the soul is what we know with, so it comes first.
   - The Sheikh opens theory with the soul, "the means to understand and to learn" (L04 ¶7). The soul knows itself by presence, the clearest case of Ch 2's second knowing (L04 ¶4–5, ¶49).
   - Q 91:7–10: the soul is fashioned with discernment; "the whole story of human being in this world is about human soul" (L04 ¶12). quote [4-Q91] "one who purifies it is felicitous, and one who betrays it fails." — Q 91:9–10
2. ## Forgetting God, forgetting ourselves [4.2] — the Qur'an ties the two losses together; the tradition ties the two knowings together.
   - quote [4-Q59] "Do not be like those who forget Allah, so He makes them forget their own souls." (Q 59:19). The Sheikh: most of us have forgotten our souls; remembering God turns attention back to the soul (L04 ¶13–14). Shomali: "forgetting Him causes us to forget ourselves in turn" [@self_knowledge, p. 8].
   - Q 6:20 (held), "Those who have ruined their souls will not have faith": to see the soul is to see its Maker (L04 ¶18–19).
   - The saying, "a two way relationship" (L04 ¶21). quote [4-H] "Whoever knows his own self knows his Lord" — `lantern` p. 9 (the Prophet) — Arabic: VERIFY (self_knowledge p. 9 Arabic garbled). Side by side (D20): Maliki Tabrizi gives it to Imam ʿAli: "Whoever has identified his self in reality has recognised his Creator" [@journey, p. 125]. Word it: "handed down from the Prophet, and from Imam ʿAli".
   - Tabatabaʾi: the seeker "must see Allah through the window of his own soul" [@light, p. 7].
3. ## Near already: meeting, not grasping [4.3] — meeting God means nearness, not comprehension; He is near, so the journey removes veils.
   - Q 6:31: "losers who deny the encounter with Allah". Meeting means "We remove the layers that cover our soul" and see it, a reflection of God, not God (L04 ¶41–44).
   - Limits: "You can get some understanding but knowing him completely … is impossible" (L04 ¶31, ¶33). Khomeini: liqaʾ Allah "is not that it is permissible to fathom the mystery of the Divine Essence" [@forty_hadith, p. 398].
   - Seed (D26, a few sentences): "We are actually in his presence at the moment. However, he is not in our presence." (L04 ¶54). The journey is inward, the lifting of veils (L07 ¶4; L08 ¶9 in a pointer only). Forward pointer to Ch 8. Do not quote Q 50:16 (Ch 8's).
**Boxes:** Word Study: "Whoever knows himself": two attributions, two readings (L04 ¶29–33). Who's Who: Mirza Muhammad ʿAli Shahabadi, Khomeini's teacher, "a murid with his murshid" [@forty_hadith, p. 8].
**Try this:** After the daily prayers, recite Q 59:18–24 and reflect on its meaning, as Shahabadi asked his pupils to: "Our Shaykh would ask us to recite these verses after daily prayers and to contemplate over their meaning" [@forty_hadith, p. 201].
**Hand-off:** If the self is the door, how deep does it go?
**Callbacks / pointers:** Ch 1 (the faculty: one clause, not re-argued), Ch 2 (knowledge by presence), Prologue's "veils of light", all in words. Points to Ch 5, Ch 8.
**Cut / gone around:** orientalists (¶10–11); Buddhists (¶26–27); Adam "in his image" (¶8); Cardinal story (¶62) and "Do not be ignorant of your soul" (¶52), unsourced; angels Q&A (¶34–39). By wording: ¶29 vs ¶33 → "some knowing, never complete"; ¶29 "eternal" → "outlasts the body"; ¶54 quoted as said.
**Target:** ~2,800 words.

### 5. The soul and its worlds
**Subheading:** The self is deeper than it looks: it lives in more than one world, its deeds give it a form, and what it finally becomes is in our hands.
**Conclusion:** the self is deeper than it appears, and its final shape is in our hands. **Serves:** B2 **Old number:** 5
**Opener:** At a graveside we say the dead are in *barzakh*, and picture a waiting room somewhere else. The Sheikh: "Exactly we don't go anywhere." (L05 ¶6). Tonight we will sleep, which the tradition calls death's brother. Epigraph [5-M]: "My Lord, if my disregard for preparations to meet You has put me to sleep, my knowledge of Your kind bounties has awakened me." [@light, p. 166] — Arabic held §118–119.
**Words you'll meet:** *ʿalam al-mulk* · *barzakh* / *ʿalam al-mithal* · *ʿalam al-ʿuqul* · *badan mithali* · *muraqaba*
**Sections:**
1. ## Depths, not distances [5.1] — the material, imaginal and intellectual worlds are one reality at three depths.
   - Mulk, mithal/barzakh (shape and colour, no matter), then ʿuqul; "It is another dimension of the same world in which we are living" (L05 ¶3–5; L04 ¶61–66). Kursi, ʿarsh and jabarut in one line only (L04 ¶85–86).
2. ## A soul that crosses them [5.2] — the soul spans the worlds and moves among them in sleep, death and contemplation.
   - Sleep: the soul touches the middle world; dreams that come true (L04 ¶64–68). "Know that sleep is the brother of death." [@lantern, p. 24].
   - Death: "the soul doesn't leave the body, the soul dumps the body" (L04 ¶69); the image body, with "shape and size without the mass" [@self_knowledge, p. 76]; L05 ¶6–9.
   - Contemplation: muraqaba turns attention from the body; "I don't mean that your soul comes out of the body. I just want attention." (L04 ¶70–76). Tabatabaʾi's experience, as the Sheikh reports it (L04 ¶72).
   - Angels have "a known place" (Q 37:164); the soul has no fixed station (L05 ¶7–8).
3. ## The form our deeds make [5.3] — deeds shape an inner form, deformed or luminous, that we will meet.
   - The image body is darkened by sin, brightened by piety (L05 ¶10–14); Q 78:18, "you will come in groups", with the hadith of people raised by their natures [@self_knowledge, p. 21].
   - Khomeini: "there is another form and a different face of him, which is heavenly and invisible, which is determined by the qualities of his soul and his inner nature" [@forty_hadith, p. 41].
   - Q 57:12, "their light moving swiftly in front of them" (the lecture's "56:12" corrected). Shomali: "Sometimes rewards or punishments are nothing other than the actions." [@self_knowledge, p. 77].
4. ## The self can change its state [5.4] — one nafs, three states, and it can move between them.
   - "we don't have different types of nufus": commanding (Q 12:53), self-blaming (Q 75:2), at peace (Q 89:27, named, not quoted) (L05 ¶46–50). Self-blame is a hopeful sign (¶47).
   - Amuli counts four: "commanding to evil", "reproachful", "inspired", "at rest" [@inner_secrets, p. 192] (side by side).
   - What moves it: dhikr (Q 13:28, "The hearts find rest in Allah's remembrance!"), fasting and night prayer as release from the body (L05 ¶51–53, ¶56–57).
   - Close: "whatever we do with it, would remain with us forever" (L05 ¶56).
**Boxes:** Story: Qadi sees himself: "I saw myself standing silent by my side", and finds the mole in the mirror [@kernel, p. 17]. Word Study: *barzakh* (L04 ¶62–63; `self_knowledge` p. 76). Box: "The layers of the soul, counted differently": nafs, qalb, ruh, sirr, khafi [@light, p. 58]; levels "sometimes into seven sections and sometimes into four" [@forty_hadith, p. 32]; L05 ¶31–45 condensed.
**Try this:** Before sleep, take the Lantern's intention: to "disengage the self from its appetites, and to examine yourself by your sleep" [@lantern, p. 24].
**Hand-off:** What keeps so inward a work from going astray?
**Callbacks / pointers:** Ch 4. Points to Ch 8 (nafs, qalb, ruh as stages), Ch 17.
**Cut / gone around:** Hasanzadeh's saying (L05 ¶16, not held); Nakhudaki, Reza Shah (¶38–42); Friday-night hadith, which the Sheikh says he does not fully understand (¶24–27); garbled "As-Sabr", "Ar-Ruh" (¶45) and four-fold formula (¶20); Plato (L04 ¶89); seers who see people as animals (¶10–13): carried only by Q 78:18 and held sources. "Recite and ascend" (L05 ¶29–30) left for Ch 17.
**Target:** ~3,000 words.

### 6. No path outside the law
**Subheading:** So inward a journey is kept safe by the shariʿa: law, path and truth are one thing at three depths, so there is no inner path outside the law.
**Conclusion:** there is no inner path outside the shariʿa. **Serves:** B2 **Old number:** 6
**Opener:** Standing for prayer, we say *Allahu akbar*, and "Suddenly, you think about did I lock my car?" (L06 ¶30). The rules are met; something else is missing. The chapter names both and shows they are one. Epigraph [6-M]: "who do not fail to show You their gratitude and do not take Your orders lightly" [@light, p. 166] — Arabic held §126–127.
**Words you'll meet:** *shariʿa* · *tariqa* · *haqiqa* · *ihsan* · *karamat*
**Sections:**
1. ## Shell, core and kernel [6.1] — law, path and truth are one reality at three depths.
   - Ship, sea, pearl: the pearl "cannot be found except by sailing through the sea, and you cannot sail without a ship" (L06 ¶13–17; credited by the Sheikh to Najm al-Din Kubra [VERIFY Fawaʾih]; not `inner_secrets` p. 50, which inverts it). Prologue's "the beginning of ʿirfan is the shariʿa" recalled in words only.
   - Mulla ʿAbd al-Samad Hamadani: "Sharia is a shell tariqa is a core and haqiqa is the kernel." (L06 ¶23). Mutahhari beside it: "the kernel of the kernel" [@light, p. 15] (footnote only).
   - In salah: serving, drawing near, arriving (khidma, qurb, wusul) (L06 ¶24). The saying "The shari`ah is my words, tariqah my actions, haqiqah my states" (L06 ¶13; [@inner_secrets, p. 32]) — Arabic: n.a.
   - Certainty in three degrees, the Paris image (L06 ¶43–44); Islam, iman, ihsan, with Q 49:14, "faith has not yet entered into your hearts" (L06 ¶45–48). Footnote (Amuli): "shari`ah is possible without tariqah although tariqah is not possible without shari`ah" [@inner_secrets, p. 48].
2. ## The inner never cancels the outer [6.2] — wonders without the law prove nothing, and the first inner work is attention in prayer.
   - Kubra's rule: whoever flies or walks on water yet abandons "even one injunction of God", "you should know that they are liars" (L06 ¶17–20). Karama versus sihr (¶17–18).
   - Bahari: jurisprudence is "an introductory means" to good character, and character to knowledge of tawhid (L06 ¶21–22).
   - Khomeini: "Without adhering to the apparent teachings of Islam it is not possible that the light of the Divine Knowledge could descend upon his heart" [@forty_hadith, p. 35]. Footnote (Amuli): "Whoever does not protect his spiritual state and path by the shari`ah will destroy that state" [@inner_secrets, p. 42].
   - Forty days of hunger are easy; "continuously practicing Sharia is difficult" (L06 ¶65–70), as the Sheikh's own caution (D15; see Cut).
   - Attention: "Attention is very elusive" (L06 ¶27–33). Khomeini: "How is it that the study of a book often absorbs man's entire attention, but prayer does not?" [@light, p. 135].
3. ## To the last breath [6.3] — even the end of the road is lived under the law, among people.
   - Tabatabaʾi: "from the beginning of wayfaring to its final stage the wayfarer must abide by all the precepts of the luminous Shari'ah"; the claim of release is "a big lie and slander" [@kernel, p. 30].
   - The Prophet, asked why he strove in worship though forgiven (Q 48:2): "Don't you want me to be God's grateful servant?" [@kernel, p. 30]. (Ties to the epigraph.)
   - The four journeys are previewed in a sentence or two, no more: to God, in God, back to creation, among creation (L09 ¶70–72). The last journey ends among people. Pointer to Ch 16, which walks them (FRAMEWORK §8.1).
   - (L09 ¶89 "of course, they follow the Sharia" and L09 ¶91 "would bring him down to the level of ordinary people": moved to Ch 16.)
**Boxes:** Word Study: *karamat*, *muʿjiza* and *sihr* (L06 ¶17–18). (The four-journeys table box is cut, FRAMEWORK §8.1.)
**Try this:** For one prayer a day this week, pray it at the start of its time with nothing scheduled around it, as Khomeini advises ("set aside a certain time out of his day and night hours exclusively for the sake of worship" [@forty_hadith, p. 375]); at *Allahu akbar*, "know what you are saying" (L06 ¶26).
**Hand-off:** The law is the boundary. Who walks beside the traveller?
**Callbacks / pointers:** Prologue's third principle, Ch 1 (powers), Ch 5 (states). Points to Ch 7, Ch 10, Ch 16. Prose names: Kubra, ʿAbd al-Samad Hamadani, Bahari, Khomeini, Tabatabaʾi; Mutahhari and Amuli in footnotes only.
**Cut / gone around:** L06 ¶20 "probably it is possible" (ARGUMENT 6.2 [QUERY]): left out; Kubra's rule as given, Khomeini p. 35 beside it. ¶19 (the infallible can fall to desire): out. ¶66–69 Tabatabaʾi's "forty days", well and jinn: not in the Kernel; treated as OMISSIONS L10 ¶44–45 (the Sheikh's own caution; jinn dropped). Asides cut: "Aziz" story (¶67), Corbin (¶11), "police force" (¶4–5), Hafiz (¶33). Bahari's "recently passed away" dropped (`light` p. 106: Akhund Hamadani's pupil). Musabbihat (¶70) left for Part Three.
**Target:** ~3,000 words.

### 7. Finding a guide
**Subheading:** A guide helps, and Shia teachers who walked the road exist within living memory; but authority stays with the Imam, so a guide is trusted, never blindly obeyed, and is tested by the shariʿa, not by wonders.
**Conclusion:** a guide helps, and must be tested. **Serves:** B2 **Old number:** 10 (+ short form of old 9.3)
**Opener:** On Fridays many of us read Surah al-Kahf. A prophet, Musa, asks a stranger: "May I follow you for the purpose that you teach me some of the probity you have been taught?" (Q 18:66). Even a prophet sought a teacher (L06 ¶37). No epigraph.
**Words you'll meet:** *shaykh* · *murid* · *silsila* · *wali* · *wusul*
**Sections:**
1. ## Why a companion [7.1, old 10.2] — the road has turns a traveller cannot read alone; scholars weigh the need differently.
   - The soul passes through strange states: "it comes time that you hate worship"; someone who "actually have a journey along the path" can say what it means (L14 ¶23–24).
   - Side by side (D20): the Sheikh: begin with the law; the teacher comes later: "First you have to comply completely with whatever Sharia tells" (L07 ¶14–15); orders' claim that you are lost without them is "something of an exaggeration" (L14 ¶25). Mutahhari: without a guide the traveller "is in danger of going astray" [@light, p. 10]; the verse "Leave not this stage without the company of Khidr" (Persian: n.a.).
2. ## A guide beside the Imam, not in his place [7.2, old 9.3 short] — for Shia, authority belongs to the Imam, so there is no chain of authority and no blind obedience.
   - "We don't have such a thing as tariqa in Shia": Imamah leaves no room for a silsila of authority (L14 ¶6–8). Shia "find an instructor for themselves", someone "you could entrust your soul" to (L14 ¶22).
   - Tabatabaʾi's two teachers: a general teacher, consulted as "the people of the Reminder" (Q 16:43), and the special teacher, "the Apostle of Allah and his true successors": "The wayfarer cannot dispense with the company of the special teacher in any circumstance whatsoever"; that company is "the inner company of the Imam with the wayfarer" [@kernel, p. 80].
   - Pointer to Ch 15 for orders, pledges and the full comparison. Do not use the ghusl image (L14 ¶21).
3. ## Teachers within living memory [7.3, old 10.1] — Shia teachers who walked the road form a line to our own time.
   - Akhund Mulla Husayn-Quli Hamadani; his pupils (Maliki Tabrizi, Karbalaʾi, Bahari); Qadi; Tabatabaʾi: "My preceptor was the late Haji Mirza Ali Agha Qazi who was a pupil of Agha Sayyid Ahmad Karbalai." [@light, p. 106]. Khomeini with Shahabadi (callback to Ch 4's box) [@forty_hadith, p. 8].
   - They were jurists outside the orders: "But we belong to none of these orders and follow the directions of the late Akhund" [@light, p. 105].
   - Hamadani's method was knowledge of the soul, "which is essential to the gnosis of the Lord" [@kernel, p. 87]: callback to Ch 4.
   - The Sheikh calls Tabatabaʾi a "symbol of Shiah mysticism" (L10 ¶44).
   - Prose names at most five (Hamadani, Qadi, Tabatabaʾi, Khomeini, Mutahhari); Karbalaʾi, Bahari, Maliki Tabrizi, Shahabadi and Shushtari go to the boxes or a list.
4. ## How to test a guide [7.4, old 10.3] — a true guide is known by the shariʿa and by not wanting followers; wonders prove nothing.
   - Feats are no proof: not "extraordinary feats, knowledge of hidden things, of private thoughts of persons" [@kernel, p. 81]; recall Ch 6's Kubra rule (L06 ¶17–22).
   - Long company is the only test [@kernel, p. 81]; "A thousand trap lies concealed in this wilderness" [@kernel, p. 83] (Persian: n.a.).
   - The charlatan: "They have distracted the attention of the creatures of God from Him, attracting them towards themselves" [@forty_hadith, p. 102].
   - Where no inquiry is possible, weigh what he says "in the light of the Book of God and the Sunnah of the Messenger of Allah and the practice of the Immaculate Imams" [@kernel, p. 83].
   - End here. ʿIsa: sit "With one the sight of whom reminds you of Allah, and whose speech increases you in knowledge, and whose actions make you desire the next world." [@lantern, p. 13].
**Boxes:** Story: the weaver who knocked on Sayyid ʿAli Shushtari's door, and the start of the chain [@light, pp. 105–106]. Who's Who: Mirza Jawad Maliki Tabrizi, fourteen years with Hamadani [@journey, p. 8].
**Try this:** This week, notice whose company reminds you of God, whose speech adds to what you know, and whose conduct makes you want the next world, and seek it out (`lantern` p. 13).
**Hand-off:** Once a guide is found, what does the road itself look like?
**Callbacks / pointers:** Ch 4 (Hamadani's method; Shahabadi), Ch 6 (the law as test). Points to Ch 8, Ch 15.
**Cut / gone around:** [QUERY: which Hamadani] resolved: L06 ¶23's Mulla ʿAbd al-Samad Hamadani (Ch 6) is not Akhund Mulla Husayn-Quli Hamadani; kept apart. L14 ¶22's garbled name ("Syed Ali Kazmini") unused; the point given as the Sheikh's. Hasanzadeh (L14 ¶23–24) and Bahjat: out (not held). Ismaili aside (L14 ¶20): cut. Of L10 ¶41–54 (Ch 10's), only ¶44 used.
**Target:** ~2,800 words.
