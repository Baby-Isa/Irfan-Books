## Prologue: An invitation (approved; ~1,250 words)
- **Epigraph:** Munajat §105–107, "Enlighten the eyes of our hearts… reach the Source of Grandeur" (`light` p. 165; Arabic held).
- **Sections:**
  - What is ʿirfan? (the prayer; the less-practised side; the word; Mutahhari `light` p. 12; the thesis)
  - Who this book is for
  - How the book is built (five Parts)
  - Where this book comes from
  - What you can expect (three principles: not powers; no sect or order beside the Imam; never outside the shariʿa)
- **Edits for the new structure (approved and applied, D41; Part One wording adjusted for the Ch 2/3 swap):**
  1. ¶ thesis: "…ʿIrfan travels through the self and never leaves the shariʿa. ~~It is rooted in the Qur'an and the Imams. It is walked by a tested and ordered method.~~ **It is walked by a tested and ordered method. It is rooted in the Qur'an and the Imams.** And it ends…"
  2. "How the book is built":
     - Part One, *The Call* (Chapters 1–3), asks why a believer should bother. It answers that the Qur'an invites more than the minimum, with the goal of ʿirfan already on our lips in our duʿas. **What those duʿas ask for is a knowing we do not yet have.**
     - Part Two, ***Getting Ready* (Chapters 4–7)**, describes the landscape a traveller passes through on the journey towards ʿirfan: the self. It also sets out the boundary that keeps the traveller safe, the shariʿa, **and the guide who walks alongside.**
     - **Part Three, *The Road* (Chapters 8–12), asks how the path is walked: through ordered stages, by repentance, discipline and love, to the loss of the false self.**
     - **Part Four, *Fellow Travellers* (Chapters 13–15), meets those who walked the road before us. It shows the road's roots in the Qur'an and the Imams, where its travellers arrived, and how Shia ʿirfan relates to Sufism.**
     - Part Five, ***The Destination* (Chapters 16–17), asks what the road is for.** The friends of God return to people, with God; every soul returns to God.
  3. "explored in Chapter 9" → "explored in Chapter 15".
  4. "Chapter 8 sets out their wider concerns" → "Chapter 14 sets out their wider concerns".

### 1. More than the minimum
_(Approved text, frozen at `32e44c9`. Title changed from "Seeking, not powers" (D41); the hand-off line and the Word Study pointer now name Ch 2. The prose is otherwise unchanged.)_
**Subheading:** The Qur'an invites us beyond the minimum to see God's signs; we miss them because something inside us needs repair; the repaired state is open to every believer, and its only aim is God, never powers.
**Conclusion:** ʿirfan is worth pursuing: it is the training of the soul to perceive and love God, open to every believer, and sought for God alone. **Serves:** B1.
**Opener:** the wedding verse, Q 30:21; epigraph "There are indeed signs in that for a people who reflect."
**Words you'll meet:** ʿirfan, maʿrifah, ʿarif/ʿurafaʾ, muqarrabun, awliyaʾ Allah, shirk.
**Sections:**
1. What the minimum leaves out [1.1]: the wajibat save (Shomali `self_knowledge` p. 17); Q 30:22–24 endings; Khomeini, "within the bounds of the Shari'ah" (`forty_hadith` p. 61).
2. Signs most people miss [1.1]: Q 12:105; Tabatabaʾi's red light (`light` pp. 6–7).
3. The fault is in the receiver [1.2]: Q 7:179; the inner machine "[rusted]"; `kernel` p. 4; "has a beginning and no end".
4. Some do see [1.3]: Q 8:2, 3:18, 35:28, 10:62; Nahj 3.403.
5. What they are given, and why it is open to all [1.3]: Q 56:10–11, 16:97, 10:62, 4:69; "we are not yet human beings".
6. The aim is God, not powers [1.4]: "These are shirk"; `kernel` pp. 28–29; Who's Who: Tabatabaʾi; `forty_hadith` p. 61 (Shahabadi); `light` p. 44.
7. Seeking, and nothing else [conclusion]: "is actually seeking and nothing else"; Word Study (`light` p. 12, `lantern` p. 9); `light` p. 4.
**Try this:** read Q 30:21–24 slowly once a day for a week (D28).
**Hand-off:** If the aim is to know and love God, what kind of knowing is that?

### 2. Knowing about God is not knowing Him
**Subheading:** We already know a great deal about God; the prayers ask to know Him, which is a different kind of knowing. It comes as light in a purified heart, so the path is purification, and study prepares for it.
**Conclusion:** knowing God differs in kind from knowing about God, and it is reached by purification, which study serves but cannot replace. **Serves:** B1. **Old number:** 3 ("Two kinds of knowing"); moved to Ch 2 (D41).
**Opener (≤150 words):** a Ramadan fast. No one has to be told they are hungry, or learn it from a book (L02 ¶31–32, ¶37). Then the gap, stated once and without presumption: the believer's knowledge of fiqh, belief and history is knowledge *about*; the "more" of Ch 1 is the other kind.
**Words you'll meet:** ʿilm, ʿilm al-husuli, ʿilm al-huduri, suluk, ʿaql.
**Sections:**
1. ## Knowing about, and knowing [2.1]. Point: acquired knowledge defines and limits; knowledge by presence arrives.
   - Acquired knowledge comes from outside, by the senses or reasoning; to define is to limit (hadd) (L02 ¶29, ¶36).
   - Knowledge by presence is with the soul already, like hunger; one arrives at it rather than understanding it (L02 ¶29–32, ¶37).
   - The Sheikh's caveat: religious texts use ʿilm and maʿrifah for one thing; the distinction is a working one (L02 ¶36; L03 ¶21).
   - Khomeini: the ʿarif knows God "through direct witnessing", the ʿalim "through philosophical proofs" (`forty_hadith` p. 542). Mutahhari: "experienced and witnessed" (`light` p. 41).
2. ## Where reason stops [2.2]. Point: reason knows attributes and stops short of any essence, above all God's.
   - Colour, size, function, but never what carries them; in the Sheikh's account even Ibn Sina granted this (L03 ¶25–27).
   - quote [N1.1] Imam ʿAli: "Soaring thoughts cannot reach him and deep minds cannot fathom him" (Nahj 1.1.1, `nahj` p. 122; Arabic VERIFY).
   - "We cannot know the essence of god… But we want to know him" through His attributes, a positive knowledge (L03 ¶33, ¶35, ¶38).
3. ## Light in the heart [2.3]. Point: knowledge of God is light God places in a purified heart; on His side nothing is withheld.
   - quote [H-light] "Knowledge is not extensive learning. Rather, it is a light that God casts in the heart of whomever He wills." (`forty_hadith` p. 331; cf. `lantern` p. 10; L03 ¶18; Arabic VERIFY).
   - quote [Q24:35] "Allah is the Light of the heavens and the earth" (Arabic held). The light is everywhere; the heart is closed (L03 ¶20–21).
   - Heart to heart: "not from mind to mouth but from heart", and for Shia it comes through the Imams (L03 ¶10–11). The thousand-doors hadith is not used (D39).
4. ## The path is purification [2.4]. Point: the path is suluk; study prepares the ground but cannot do the work.
   - fikr against suluk: "You purify yourself and then wait for Allah to put the light in your heart" (L03 ¶20). First, polish the soul by what the Prophet asked; "without this there is no maarifah" (L03 ¶47–49).
   - quote [M88] "One who follows Your path is enlightened" (`light` p. 165; duas §88, held).
   - Side by side on study (added, attributed):
     - Khomeini: mere knowledge can be "the greatest of veils", yet "one has to approach a veil in order to tear it into shreds" (`forty_hadith` p. 403).
     - Tabatabaʾi: the rules of the shariʿa first, from a jurist, then practice; "knowledge gives birth to action and action in turn brings knowledge" (`kernel` p. 56).
     - Mutahhari: the ʿarif's means are "the heart and the purification, disciplining and perfecting of the self" (`light` p. 42).
5. ## Knowing by drawing near [conclusion]. The conclusion, once.
**Boxes:** Word Study: ʿilm al-husuli and ʿilm al-huduri. Story: Ibn Sina and Abu Saʿid (the Sheikh's account, L03 ¶23–24; `light` p. 42). Who's Who: Mulla Sadra (L03 ¶28; `kernel` p. 7).
**Try this:** in prostration, say "Glory belongs to my Lord, the Exalted, and Him do I praise" while believing it, and notice one's lowliness and God's majesty (`kernel` p. 56).
**Hand-off:** Where, then, does an ordinary believer already stand on this path?
**Callbacks / forward pointers:** Ch 1's Word Study promised this chapter. Forward: Ch 3 (our prayers already ask for this knowing).
**Cut / gone around:**
- L02 ¶34 (garbled) is not used.
- Essence and attributes follow L03 ¶38 word for word.
- The L03 ¶12–17, ¶30–32 and ¶51–58 digressions.
- The asides.
**Target:** ~3,000 words.

### 3. You already know these words
**Subheading:** The prayers we recite in Shaʿban, in Ramadan and at ʿArafah already ask for what the mystics seek, so the goal of ʿirfan is not foreign or reserved for an elite: it is already on our lips.
**Conclusion:** the goal of ʿirfan is already on the believer's lips. **Serves:** B1. **Old number:** 2; moved to Ch 3 (D41) to close Part One on hope.
**Opener (≤150 words):** Thursday night, Duʿa Kumayl. Epigraph [K221] "And my heart enthralled by Your love!" Link back to Ch 2's closing question (where does an ordinary believer already stand?): the words we already say ask for exactly what Ch 2 described.
**Words you'll meet:** munajat, duʿa, Mafatih al-Jinan, inqitaʿ (severance; add to the glossary first).
**Sections:**
1. ## Cut off from all else [3.1]. Point: the prayers' first request is to be cut off, in the heart, from everything but God.
   - The Sheikh: "they have to cut off from everything first" (L01 ¶18, ¶20); it is a severance in the heart, not a withdrawal from family or society (L01 ¶25–26, paraphrased).
   - quote [M104] "grant me complete severance of my relations with everything else" (`light` p. 165; Arabic: duas §104, held).
   - quote [AH384] "O my Master, (please) expel love for the world out of my heart" (Abu Hamza §384; held).
   - quote [AR783] "It is You Who have removed love for others from the hearts of Your lovers / so that they have loved none save You" (ʿArafah §783–784; held).
2. ## To see, to love, to draw near [3.1]. Point: they then ask to see God with the heart, to love Him and to come near Him, the two aims Chapter 1 gave ʿirfan.
   - **Sight:** recall the prologue's line in words (no re-quote). quote [AR781] "It is You Who have lit the illuminations in the hearts of Your intimate servants" (ʿArafah §781; held). The Sheikh: the prayer asks the heart to see what the eye cannot (L01 ¶19).
   - **Love:** quote [M92] "place me among Your friends in the position of one who hopes for an increase in Your love" (`light` p. 165; duas §92–93, held). The Sheikh: maʿrifah and love, both ever increasing (L01 ¶23).
   - **Nearness:** quote [M82] "provide me with a heart, the passion of which may bring it near You" (`light` p. 165; duas §82, held). quote [AH599] "make me love meeting You" (Abu Hamza §599; held). The Lovers: [LV1] "granted gazing upon Thy face" (Sahifa p. 656; Arabic VERIFY) (L01 ¶27–29).
   - Mutahhari: these supplications "all contain the most sublime spiritual ideas" (`light` p. 20).
3. ## A pupil reading above his level [3.2]. Point: they ask from need and humility, and we are honoured to be allowed to say them.
   - The Sheikh's question: "it's like if you tell a primary school boy to read books of university" (L01 ¶17; quoted).
   - quote [AR689] "Here I am begging You by my need for You." (ʿArafah §689; held). quote [AH510] "I, Your servant, am standing at Your door owing to neediness" (Abu Hamza §510; held).
   - The Part One epigraph (§73–74) is spoken to here: even the turning towards God is God's doing ("You awakened me to Your love").
   - His answer: we are honoured to be allowed to recite them; God may grant to a sincere reciter what He granted the Imams (L01 ¶22, paraphrased).
4. ## Prayers for ordinary nights [3.3]. Point: these are the familiar prayers of the Mafatih, set on nights ordinary believers keep.
   - The Shaʿbaniyyah: the prayer of Imam ʿAli and the Imams in Shaʿban, "advisable to say… with presence of heart all the time" (Mafatih pp. 243, 245). Kumayl on mid-Shaʿban and Thursday evenings (p. 129); Abu Hamza on Ramadan nights (p. 282; `journey` p. 111); ʿArafah on its day (p. 374).
   - Khomeini: "You are already familiar with the words used in Imams' supplications" (`light` p. 156).
   - Side by side (D20): `journey` p. 43 ("a small number of believers") and `light` p. 157 ("those who are fit to do so") against the Sheikh's recommendation to everyone (L01 ¶22). The chapter claims access, not attainment.
5. ## Words already on our lips [conclusion]. The conclusion, once. The full prayer is in the Appendix.
**Boxes:** Word Study: munajat and duʿa. History: the Mafatih and the Sahifa (compiler and dates VERIFY). Try this.
**Try this (approved brief):**
- **This week:** read the last petitions of the Munajat Shaʿbaniyyah (§82–111, in the Appendix) once, slowly, with the English beside the Arabic, "with presence of heart" (Mafatih p. 245).
- **In Shaʿban (the longer rung):** follow the Sheikh's own advice and recite the whole prayer "as much as you can" (L01 ¶22).
**Hand-off:** These prayers keep asking God to work on the heart and the self. What is this self?
**Callbacks / forward pointers:** Ch 1's two aims (maʿrifah, love); the Appendix. Forward: Part Four (how far each believer goes).
**Cut / gone around:**
- L01 ¶24, ¶26, ¶30–31 asides.
- "heart burn…" (¶31) is not in the Lovers, so it is not used.
- The Sheikh's naming of Zayn al-ʿAbidin as a reciter of the Shaʿbaniyyah: the book follows the Mafatih.
- Nahj 3.403 is not repeated (it is in Ch 1).
**Target:** ~2,800 words.
