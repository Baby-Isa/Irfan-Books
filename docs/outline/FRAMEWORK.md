# Detailed-outline framework (v3 structure, proposed 2026-10-12)

_The shared brief for writing the detailed outline of every chapter. The user's direction (2026-10-12): the book follows the reader's journey (**why travel → getting ready → travelling → who else you meet on the road → the destination**), builds to a crescendo about two-thirds of the way through, and has no side quests before the climax. The question "isn't this Sufism?" is answered in a line in the prologue (already there) and fully later. Everything points the reader at the purpose of the journey. Plan every chapter now, quotes included, so that drafting early chapters never forces rewrites later._

## 1. The structure
| Part | Reader's question | Book point | Chapters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **One: The Call** | Why travel? | **B1** ʿIrfan is worth pursuing: the Qur'an invites more than the minimum, which is knowing God and not only knowing about Him; that knowing is reached by purifying the heart; and our own prayers already ask for it. | 1 More than the minimum · 2 Knowing about God is not knowing Him · 3 You already know these words |
| **Two: Getting ready** | What do I need before I set out? | **B2** The journey runs through the self, which is deeper than it looks; the shariʿa is its boundary; and a tested guide is its companion. | 4 Know yourself, know your Lord · 5 The soul and its worlds · 6 No path outside the law · 7 Finding a guide |
| **Three: The Road** | How is it walked? | **B3** It is walked in a known order: veils removed in stages, beginning with repentance and character, trained by measured disciplines, carried by love, and reaching the loss of the false self. | 8 The stages of the journey · 9 Setting out · 10 The five disciplines · 11 Love as the engine · 12 Losing the self, finding God (**crescendo**) |
| **Four: Fellow travellers** | Who else is on this road, and where did they arrive? | **B4** Others have walked it: it is rooted in the teachings of the Qur'an, the Prophet ﷺ, and the Imams from his Ahl al-Bayt; some travellers lost their footing in ecstasy while the sober reached the end; and Shia masters keep the path without a Sufi institution of authority, because authority belongs to the Imam. | 13 Where it comes from · 14 Drunk and sober · 15 Sufis and Shia |
| **Five: The Destination** | What is the road for? | **B5** Not the road but God: the friends of God return to people, with God; every soul returns to God; ʿirfan is making that return willingly, now. | 16 Back among people · 17 The return to God |

Prologue (approved) before Part One; Epilogue "First steps" after Part Five; Appendix: the full Munajat Shaʿbaniyyah (Arabic and English).

**Thesis (reordered):** ʿIrfan, the disciplined journey to know and love God, is worth every believer's pursuit: it travels through the self and never leaves the shariʿa; it is walked by a tested and ordered method; it is rooted in the teachings of the Qur'an, the Prophet ﷺ, and the Imams from his Ahl al-Bayt; and it ends not in withdrawal but in return, to people with God, and to God Himself.

**Part dividers:** each Part opens on its own page with the Part number and title, the reader's question, a two- or three-line answer (five lines at most), the chapter titles, and the Munajat line in §4 below.

## 6. Template for each chapter's detailed outline
Notes, not prose. Every claim carries its source (`LNN ¶a–b`, `Q S:A`, `[@key, p.]`).
```
### N. Title
**Subheading (one plain sentence, the slide's message):** …
**Conclusion:** …   **Serves:** Bn   **Old number:** …
**Opener (≤150 words, familiar ground):** scene … ; epigraph (quote id) if any
**Words you'll meet:** 3–5 terms (GLOSSARY spellings)
**Sections:**
1. ## Heading [N.x] — point note (one sentence)
   - key point … (source)
   - quote: [id] "exact short English" — source, loc — Arabic: held (file §N) / VERIFY / n.a.
2. …
**Boxes (≤3):** type: subject (source)
**Try this:** the act (source)
**Hand-off:** the question
**Callbacks / forward pointers:** what it recalls from earlier chapters (in words), what it points ahead to
**Cut / gone around (D15, D39):** lecture asides, garbled or doubtful passages and how they are handled (no questions for the Sheikh)
**Target:** ~N words
```
End each Part's file with a **quote ledger**: id | short text | source and location | Arabic status | chapter.section.

## 7. Rules that bind every outline
- Never write scripture, hadith, duʿa or Arabic from memory. Copy exact English from the held extract; copy Arabic only from `sources/extracts/duas-*.md` or check Qur'an with `python3 build/quran.py S:A`. Otherwise mark `[VERIFY]`.
- One use per quote across the book (§4, §5). A chapter may recall an earlier quote in words.
- No questions for the Sheikh (D39). Go around unclear passages.
- No jokes or the lecturer's asides; no rhetorical-question chains; short quotations (TONE 10).
- At most five historical figures in a chapter's prose; the rest in Who's Who boxes.
- Each chapter: one conclusion; sections in dependency order; headings map one-to-one onto points; ~2,600–3,200 words of body.
- Every chapter opens on familiar ground ("the inside of what you already do": salah, Ramadan, a majlis, a graveside, a duʿa) and its "Try this" is one sourced act.
