**Title:** The return to God
**Serves:** B5, Part Five "The Destination" (`docs/ARGUMENT.md`; `docs/ladders/00-book-chain.md` line 17). As the last chapter before the epilogue, its close also lands the book's thesis ("…it ends not in withdrawal but in return, to people with God, and to God Himself").

**Conclusion (one sentence the chapter proves):** Every soul returns to God by giving back what it was given, willingly or not; ʿirfan is making that return willingly, now. *(Book chain wording plus "by giving back what it was given"; proposed.)*

## Chapter points (ARGUMENT.md)
- **17.1** The Qur'an may be read from the inside, on condition that the inner meaning agrees with the outer and never cancels it.
- **17.2** The return reverses creation: existence, knowledge, power and will were given, and are given back in reverse order, to be given again by God (Tusi).
- **17.3** Hell is the same return, refused: the same giving back, undergone against one's will.

## Sections
1. **Reading from the inside** [17.1]
   - **Point:** The Qur'an's images of the next world carry an inner meaning, read only where it agrees with the outer meaning and never cancels it.
   - **Paragraph points:** 1. The collars of Q 36:8 are not literal; the light is covered for them (L16 ¶10–12) → 2. but not any meaning: not at a mystic's whim, not a scheme from outside (L16 ¶3–5; `light` p. 110) → 3. an "insightful look" whose inner meaning agrees with the outer, which stays (L16 ¶5–8, ¶15); the other guard (`journey` p. 54) → 4. the test on the hell verse: the seventy-cubit chain (Q 69:32) is both real and the chain of long ambitions (L16 ¶13–16, both halves of ¶15); the Qur'an itself pairs the blindness of here and there (Q 17:72 recalled, Q 7:38 cited; ¶16–17) → 5. therefore the point.
2. **Given in order, returned in reverse** [17.2]
   - **Point:** Every soul returns to God by the road it came, giving back in reverse what it was given.
   - **Paragraph points:** 1. Tusi's reading of hell and paradise keeps the material side (L16 ¶17) → 2. the return (*maʿad*) is the way we came, reversed (L16 ¶18–19; Q 7:29) → 3. we came by four gifts: 3a existence, 3b knowledge, 3c power, 3d will (L16 ¶20–23; Q 40:67, 16:78) → 4. we go back will first, then power, knowledge, existence (L16 ¶22–23) → 5. therefore the point.
3. **Will, power, knowledge, existence** [17.2]
   - **Point:** Each gift given back becomes a station on the way to God, and God gives each back without limit.
   - **Paragraph points:** 1. will → contentment (*rida*) (L16 ¶23–24; `forty_hadith` p. 207) → 2. which leaves the soul with all it wishes (Q 50:35; L16 ¶24–25, Ridwan and "the key to it is this contentment") → 3. power → reliance (*tawakkul*), not idleness (L16 ¶27–28; `forty_hadith` p. 204; Q 65:3); Ch 12's annihilation in action recalled with the difference (seen there, acted on here) → 4. knowledge → submission (*taslim*) (L16 ¶28–30; Q 2:131; ʿArafah §670) → 5. existence → annihilation (L16 ¶30; Ch 12) → 6. all four given back without limit (L16 ¶31–32; Q 50:34) → 7. Tusi's own words, as Ayatullah Khomeini quotes them, with his guard against union (`forty_hadith` p. 517) → 8. therefore the point: that is paradise.
4. **The return refused** [17.3]
   - **Point:** The return happens whether or not we make it: a believer who left it undone finishes it after death, with difficulty; a will that never yields meets the same four gifts as their opposites, which is hell, and it is not God's revenge. *(Fable: the earlier "Hell is the same return, undergone against one's will" covered only steps 3–6; ARGUMENT 17.3 keeps its approved wording until the user sees both.)*
   - **Paragraph points:** 1. the return happens anyway; a believer finishes it after death, with difficulty (L16 ¶39–41) → 2. helped by intercession (Q 66:8; L16 ¶34, ¶37–39; the Sheikh's "humiliate" reading stated as his) → 3. a will that never turns is overruled and held; the chain is its length (Q 23:71; L16 ¶42–43) → 4. the other gifts come back as their opposites (Q 87:13; L16 ¶45–47, ¶54–55) → 5. not God's revenge, yet the wrath real and deserved (L16 ¶47–48, ¶43) → 6. the fire is still true: one reality, a form in each world (L16 ¶49–54; story box: Yusuf and the king's dream, L04 ¶81–83) → 7. Ayatullah Khomeini agrees (`forty_hadith` p. 137) → 8. fear and hope together (Munajat §120–121) → 9. therefore the point.
5. **Going back now** [conclusion]
   - **Point:** ʿIrfan is making that return willingly, in this life.
   - **Paragraph points:** 1. Kubra: two journeys, involuntary and voluntary; the mystic's is the shortcut (L08 ¶6–7) → 2. it is shorter and easier (Ch 4, which carries the pair unnamed), those who took it have already arrived, and the Sheikh reads the Great Terror verse of them (L08 ¶8; Q 21:103; L16 ¶40) → 3. so ʿirfan is §3's return made now, by choice (chapter point) → 4. the words of Q 2:156, which the verse itself puts in the mouths of the living, said now of oneself → 5. the book's opening question answered in one paragraph (≤90 words; the thesis) → 6. Q 89:27–28, "pleased" (*radiya*) the word for one who has reached *rida* (`forty_hadith` p. 207); end on the verse, then the one-line hand-off, then Try this.

## Where scholars differ (D20, shown side by side and gently)
- Hell inner and outer: agreement. The Sheikh keeps the material fire with the inner reading; Ayatullah Khomeini keeps both fires (`forty_hadith` p. 137).
- Not raised in prose: Ayatullah Khomeini ranks *rida* above *tawakkul* (`forty_hadith` p. 207), whereas Tusi's order gives the will back first; the prose makes no ranking.
