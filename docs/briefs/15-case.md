**Prologue promise answered:** "Shia ʿirfan has no institution of authority beside the Imam. How this position differs from that of the Sufi orders is explored in Chapter Fifteen." (`book2/chapters/00-prologue.md`). §4 answers it; §1–§3 set up what the orders are and what Shia share with them.

## Chapter points
- **15.1** A Sufi order is a path that, centuries after the first seekers, became an institution of chain, method and obedience.
- **15.2** Almost every order runs its chain back to Imam ʿAli (as), so Sufi and Shia seekers draw on one source.
- **15.3** The path is shared, but no single method fits every soul, so a traveller needs a guide who has walked the road more than an order's technique.
- **15.4** Shia masters used teacher, robe and pledge, but gave none of them the authority that belongs to the Imam.

## Argument, in order
1. An order is a path made an institution: elder's method, chain to the Prophet ﷺ (L14 ¶3–7).
2. It asks total obedience, the corpse before the washer (L14 ¶20–21).
3. It is late: no Sufis by name in the first century (`light` p. 22; L14 ¶7).
4. It became "a vast and independent system" (`light` p. 6).
5. Its chains branched and licences multiplied (L14 ¶12–19).
   - **Interim conclusion A:** an order is held together by chain, method and obedience.
6. The chains culminate in Imam ʿAli (as) (`kernel` p. 87), even Suhrawardi's (`light` p. 34; `inner_secrets` p. 13; `light` p. 25); only the Naqshbandiyya differs (L14 ¶37, ¶79).
7. The chain and the pole are the Shia idea of the Imam (L14 ¶8–9; `light` p. 6).
   - **Interim conclusion B:** one source.
8. Shia share the dhikr and the books (L14 ¶57–58; `forty_hadith` p. 8).
9. The orders differ by technique (L14 ¶58–60, ¶64, ¶76–77), yet all produced ʿurafaʾ: no single method (¶65).
10. Each soul is a channel for grace, blocked by deeds, receiving it in its own shape (L14 ¶66–69; Q 11:119).
11. The ways are many inside Islam (`inner_secrets` p. 195; L14 ¶70) and inside the law (`kernel` pp. 86–87).
12. So a traveller needs someone who has walked the road (L14 ¶23–24, Hasanzadeh).
   - **Interim conclusion C:** shared path, no binding method, a guide.
13. Obedience to a chain is not shared: Ch 7 recalled; the orders' line not orderly (L14 ¶9).
14. Shia masters stood outside the orders (`light` pp. 40–41), though they used robe, pledge and teacher (`inner_secrets` pp. 8, 192; `kernel` pp. 60, 64; `forty_hadith` p. 8).
15. Those forms did not hold the source (`inner_secrets` p. 14).
   - **Conclusion:** Shia share the source and the path, but the authority belongs to the Imam.

## Where scholars differ (D20, shown side by side and gently)
- The Sheikh: Shia have no *tariqa* (Ch 7). Amuli took the Sufi robe and reports that the shaykhs agree a traveller needs "a shaykh of perfection"; Tabatabaʾi assumes a pledge to an aware shaykh; Ayatullah Khomeini was Shahabadi's murid. Resolved by Amuli's own words: his arrival did not depend on them (§4 steps 3–4).
- Amuli's line that the soul at rest accepts the guidance of "the Prophet, the Imam or the shaykh without any further argument" (`inner_secrets` p. 192) is left out, with the reason in the ladder; Stage 2 may restore it.
