# Chapter 3: Case brief

**Status:** draft v2 for Gate A (2026-10-12; user's decisions applied, D39)
**Title:** Two kinds of knowing
**Serves:** B1 (`docs/ARGUMENT.md` v2.1)

**Conclusion:** knowing God differs in kind from knowing about God, and it is reached by purification, which study serves but cannot replace.

## Chapter points (copied from ARGUMENT.md v2.1)
- **3.1** Acquired knowledge (ʿilm) defines and limits; knowledge by presence (maʿrifah) arrives. (L02 ¶29–32, ¶36–37)
- **3.2** Reason analyses attributes and stops short of any essence, above all God's. (L03 ¶25–28, ¶33, ¶35, ¶38)
- **3.3** Knowledge of God is light that God places in a purified heart, passed heart to heart, not mind to mind. (L03 ¶10, ¶18–21; added: `lantern` p. 10, `forty_hadith` p. 331)
- **3.4** Therefore the path is purification (suluk), not thought alone; study prepares the ground but cannot replace it. (L03 ¶19–20, ¶47–49; added: `forty_hadith` p. 403; `kernel` p. 56; `light` pp. 42, 146)

_Fidelity: 3.1–3.3 and the first half of 3.4 are the Sheikh's. **The second half of 3.4 is added:** the Sheikh contrasts study with suluk but gives study no positive role. "Study prepares the ground" is Khomeini's and Tabatabaʾi's view, and the prose will say so._

## Sections (each heading serves the point in brackets)
0. **Opener** (under 150 words, before "Words you'll meet")
   - A fast in Ramadan: no one has to be told they are hungry, or learn it from a book. That is the Sheikh's own example of the second kind of knowing (L02 ¶31–32, ¶37). The chapter's question follows: the prayers of Chapter 2 ask to know God; which kind of knowing do they mean?
1. **Knowing about, and knowing** [3.1]
   - **Acquired knowledge** (*ʿilm al-husuli*) comes from outside, by the senses or by reasoning. It works through concepts and definitions; to define is to set a limit (*hadd*). (L02 ¶29, ¶34, ¶36)
   - **Knowledge by presence** (*ʿilm al-huduri*) is not acquired. It is with the soul already, as hunger is; one does not understand it but arrives at it. (L02 ¶29–32, ¶37)
   - **The Sheikh's caveat:** religious texts use ʿilm and maʿrifah for the same thing. The distinction is a working one, drawn "for us". (L02 ¶36; L03 ¶21)
   - **Support:** Khomeini: the ʿarif knows God "through direct witnessing", the ʿalim "through philosophical proofs" (`forty_hadith` p. 542). Mutahhari: the philosopher's knowledge is intellectual, the ʿarif's is "experienced and witnessed" (`light` p. 41).
2. **Where reason stops** [3.2]
   - Reason knows a thing by its attributes (colour, size, function) and never reaches what carries them. In the Sheikh's account, even Ibn Sina granted that the reality of things is beyond human capacity. (L03 ¶25–27)
   - God has no colour or dimension, so analysis has nothing to hold. Imam ʿAli: "Soaring thoughts cannot reach him and deep minds cannot fathom him" (Nahj 1.1.1; L02 ¶38 recalls the same sermon).
   - Philosophy divides things into units; ʿirfan seeks God in His unity. In the Sheikh's words, "We cannot know the essence of god… But we want to know him", through His attributes: a positive knowledge, not only the negative "God is not this". (L03 ¶33, ¶35, ¶38; the chapter follows ¶38 exactly, so no query is needed)
3. **Light in the heart** [3.3]
   - The hadith the Sheikh quotes: "Knowledge is not extensive learning. Rather, it is a light that God casts in the heart of whomever He wills" (`forty_hadith` p. 331; `lantern` p. 10; L03 ¶18).
   - The light is not withheld: "Allah is the Light of the heavens and the earth" (Q 24:35). It is everywhere; the heart is closed. On God's side there is no hindrance. (L03 ¶20–21)
   - **Heart to heart:** the Sheikh's point, in his own words: this knowledge passes "not from mind to mouth but from heart", and for Shia it comes to us through the Imams. (L03 ¶10–11) The thousand-doors hadith he cites there is left out (no held source; D39).
4. **The path is purification** [3.4]
   - University or hawza knowledge comes by *fikr*: thinking, assembling, analysing. Knowledge of God comes by *suluk*, the path of purification: "You purify yourself and then wait for Allah to put the light in your heart." (L03 ¶20)
   - The first step is to polish the soul by what the Prophet asked of us; "without this there is no maarifah". (L03 ¶47–49)
   - **Side by side on study (D20, added):**
     - Khomeini puts little store by "mere knowledge", which can be "the greatest of veils", yet "one has to approach a veil in order to tear it into shreds"; the sciences are "seeds" of experience (`forty_hadith` p. 403).
     - Tabatabaʾi begins the traveller with the rules of the shariʿa learnt from a jurist, then practice, "for knowledge gives birth to action and action in turn brings knowledge" (`kernel` p. 56).
     - Mutahhari: the ʿarif's means are "the heart and the purification, disciplining and perfecting of the self" (`light` p. 42).
   - **Hence:** study prepares the ground, and purification does the work.
5. **Knowing by drawing near** [conclusion]
   - The conclusion is stated once.
   - Hand-off: "If that knowing happens in the soul, what is the soul?" (The Sheikh ends L03 ¶60 on the same question.)

## Where scholars differ (D20)
- **The role of study:** the Sheikh gives it none; Khomeini and Tabatabaʾi give it a preparing role (above). Shown side by side in section 4. The conclusion's clause "which study serves" is theirs, and is attributed.

## Threads
- **"The inside of what you already do":** the fast (opener), and salah (Try this).
- **"Try this":** Tabatabaʾi's practice in prostration. Say "Glory belongs to my Lord, the Exalted, and Him do I praise" while believing what it says, and notice one's own lowliness and God's majesty (`kernel` p. 56). It turns a phrase known *about* into a thing known *by presence*, inside a prayer the reader already says. (Alternative: the Sheikh's "Correct, rectify your soul and wait", L03 ¶49. It is sourced but is not a concrete act.)

**Boxes:**
- Word Study: *ʿilm al-husuli* and *ʿilm al-huduri*.
- Story: Ibn Sina and Abu Saʿid Abu'l-Khayr. The Sheikh's account, given as his ("the Sheikh tells how…"): Ibn Sina changed after the meeting and wrote asking to learn (L03 ¶23–24). Beside it, Mutahhari's note that Ibn Sina "developed mystic inclinations" late in life (`light` p. 42).
- Who's Who: Mulla Sadra, who moved philosophy from the Peripatetic school to the "transcendent" (L03 ¶28), with his own confession that his syllogisms "were barren" (`kernel` p. 7).
