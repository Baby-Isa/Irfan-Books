# Chapter 3: Case brief

**Status:** approved 2026-10-12 (user; D39–D41). Moved from Ch 2 to Ch 3 at the user's request (D41). It closes Part One on hope: the reader already stands at the start of the path, in words if not yet in deeds.
**Title:** You already know these words
**Serves:** B1 (structure v3, `docs/outline/FRAMEWORK.md`)
**Subheading:** The prayers we recite in Shaʿban, in Ramadan and on Thursday nights already ask for this knowing, and for love and nearness, so the goal of ʿirfan is not foreign or reserved for an elite: it is already on our lips.

**Conclusion:** the goal of ʿirfan is already on the believer's lips.

## Chapter points
- **3.1** The Imams' supplications ask for exactly what the mystic seeks: severance from all else, illumined hearts, love, and nearness to God. (L01 ¶18–23, ¶27–30)
- **3.2** They ask from the seeker's posture: need and humility, a pupil reaching above his level. (L01 ¶17, ¶22)
- **3.3** These are the prayers of the Mafatih, recited on nights ordinary believers keep, so the goal is neither foreign nor reserved for an elite. (L01 ¶17, ¶22; added: Mafatih pp. 129, 243, 245, 282, 374; `light` p. 156)

## Sections (each heading serves the point in brackets)
0. **Opener** (under 150 words, before "Words you'll meet")
   - Thursday night, Duʿa Kumayl, a prayer most of us know. Epigraph: "And my heart enthralled by Your love!" (Kumayl §221, Arabic held).
   - The first paragraph names the prayer and says the line asks for love of God. It links back to Ch 2's closing question (where does an ordinary believer already stand?): the words we already say ask for exactly what Ch 2 described.
1. **Cut off from all else** [3.1]
   - The prayers' first request is that the heart be cut off from everything but God. The Sheikh: "they have to cut off from everything first" (L01 ¶18, ¶20). It is a severance in the heart, not a withdrawal from family or society (L01 ¶25–26, paraphrased).
   - Quotes:
     - Munajat §104: "grant me complete severance of my relations with everything else" (`light` p. 165; Arabic duas §104).
     - Abu Hamza §384: "O my Master, (please) expel love for the world out of my heart" (held).
     - ʿArafah §783–784: "It is You Who have removed love for others from the hearts of Your lovers / so that they have loved none save You" (held). Attributed as "Duʿa ʿArafah, as given in the Mafatih" (D41).
2. **To see, to love, to draw near** [3.1]
   - **Sight:** the prologue's line on the eyes of the heart is recalled in words, not re-quoted. ʿArafah §781: "It is You Who have lit the illuminations in the hearts of Your intimate servants" (held; "as given in the Mafatih"). The Sheikh: the prayer asks the heart to see what the eye cannot (L01 ¶19).
   - **Love:** Munajat §92–93, "place me among Your friends in the position of one who hopes for an increase in Your love" (`light` p. 165; Arabic held). The Sheikh: maʿrifah and love, both ever increasing (L01 ¶23). This is Ch 2's knowing, asked for by name.
   - **Nearness:**
     - Munajat §82: "provide me with a heart, the passion of which may bring it near You" (`light` p. 165; Arabic held).
     - Abu Hamza §599: "make me love meeting You" (held).
     - The Lovers: "granted gazing upon Thy face" (Sahifa p. 656; `ar="[VERIFY]"`). (L01 ¶27–29)
   - Mutahhari: these supplications "all contain the most sublime spiritual ideas" (`light` p. 20).
3. **A pupil reading above his level** [3.2]
   - The Sheikh's question: "it's like if you tell a primary school boy to read books of university" (L01 ¶17; quoted).
   - The posture of the prayers themselves:
     - ʿArafah §689: "Here I am begging You by my need for You." (held; "as given in the Mafatih")
     - Abu Hamza §510: "I, Your servant, am standing at Your door owing to neediness" (held).
   - The Part One epigraph (Munajat §73–74, "…except when You awakened me to Your love") is spoken to here: even the turning towards God is His doing.
   - His answer: we are honoured to be allowed to recite these words; God may grant to a sincere reciter what He granted the Imams (L01 ¶22, paraphrased).
4. **Prayers for ordinary nights** [3.3]
   - The Mafatih sets these prayers on nights most believers keep:
     - the Shaʿbaniyyah, the prayer Imam ʿAli and the Imams said in Shaʿban, "advisable to say… with presence of heart all the time" (pp. 243, 245)
     - Kumayl, on mid-Shaʿban and Thursday evenings (p. 129)
     - Abu Hamza, on the nights of Ramadan (p. 282; `journey` p. 111)
     - ʿArafah, on its day (p. 374).
   - Khomeini, to his students: "You are already familiar with the words used in Imams' supplications" (`light` p. 156).
   - **Side by side (D20):** `journey` p. 43 says the depth of these prayers is grasped by "a small number of believers", and `light` p. 157 speaks of "those who are fit to do so". The Sheikh recommends the Munajat to every listener (L01 ¶22). The chapter claims access, not attainment.
5. **Words already on our lips** [conclusion]
   - The conclusion is stated once. The full Munajat is in the Appendix.
   - Hand-off: "These prayers keep asking God to work on the heart and the self. What is this self?"
