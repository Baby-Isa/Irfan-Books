**Conclusion (one sentence the chapter proves):** Of those who reached annihilation, some stayed lost in it and others came back sober, and the sober went further; the theory of what they saw, Ibn ʿArabi's unity of being, is contested, but it is neither pantheism nor licence.

## Chapter points
- **14.1** Of those who reached annihilation, the drunk stayed lost in it and spoke of it, while the sober came back, kept its secret, and went further. [B4]
- **14.2** Ibn ʿArabi gave what they saw a theory, the unity of being: there is one existence, and creation is its real manifestation, neither God nor apart from Him, known by experience rather than argument. [B4]
- **14.3** The critics feared that the ʿurafaʾ were not sincere Muslims, that their God was merged with the world, and that the law was set aside; the masters' own words and lives answer each fear, and only Ibn ʿArabi's own loyalties stay open. [B4]

## Argument, in order
1. Some who reached annihilation stayed lost in it and spoke ecstatic words (Bayazid, Hallaj) (L13 ¶13–22; `light` pp. 25, 27; `kernel` p. 73).
2. Those words, read rightly, do not make a man God (Q 28:30; L13 ¶20–23); the sober kept the secret, because those who have not seen cannot bear it (L13 ¶30; `light` p. 23).
3. The sober came back more aware of themselves in a new life in God, and went further (L13 ¶28–29; `light` p. 27; `inner_secrets` p. 162).
   - **Interim conclusion A (14.1):** the sober went furthest.
4. What they saw, Ibn ʿArabi called the unity of being: one existence, creation its manifestation (L13 ¶55–62; Q 57:3; `nahj` 1.1.1).
5. Creation is neither God nor nothing (L13 ¶63–66; `light` p. 144), and the theory is grasped by experience, checked by those who have it (L13 ¶42–43, ¶66–67).
   - **Interim conclusion B (14.2):** the theory says one existence and real manifestations, not identity.
6. The critics' fears (insincerity, pantheism, licence) each have an answer from the masters' words and lives (`light` pp. 13–14, 27; `kernel` p. 84; `inner_secrets` pp. 162–163; `forty_hadith` pp. 345, 402); Ibn ʿArabi's own loyalties stay open, and the theory is taken without them (L13 ¶47–55; `light` p. 34; `inner_secrets` p. 99).
   - **Conclusion:** the sober went furthest, and the theory of what they saw, though contested, is neither pantheism nor licence.

## Where scholars differ (D20, shown side by side and gently)
- Hallaj: apostate to the jurists, martyr to the ʿurafaʾ (`light` p. 27); "very unacceptable" statements to the Sheikh (L13 ¶15).
- Bayazid's fourth day: the *Kernel*'s version is used (`kernel` p. 73); the Sheikh's voice (L13 ¶18) is in no held source and is not told.
- Bayazid and remaining: Mutahhari credits him with speaking of subsistence too (`light` p. 25); "stopped at the river" is the Sheikh's account (L13 ¶28–29), attributed.
- Whether creation is "a mere shadow" (Tabatabaʾi, `light` p. 103) or "more real than you think" (L13 ¶66); all deny that anything stands on its own beside God. No parenthesis in the body (Fable).
- Ibn ʿArabi's Seal of the Saints: himself, as the Sheikh reports others reading him (L13 ¶49), or Imam ʿAli (`inner_secrets` p. 99); his sect: the Sunni case "much much more powerful" (L13 ¶55), his chain via Gilani (`light` p. 34).
