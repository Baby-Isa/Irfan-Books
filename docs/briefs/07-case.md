## The approved outline for this chapter (`docs/outline/part-2.md`)
#### 7. Finding a guide
**Subheading:** A guide helps, and Shia teachers who walked the road exist within living memory; but authority stays with the Imam, so a guide is trusted, never blindly obeyed, and is tested by the shariʿa, not by wonders.
**Conclusion:** a guide helps, and must be tested. **Serves:** B2 **Old number:** 10 (+ short form of old 9.3)
**Opener:** On Fridays many of us read Surah al-Kahf. A prophet, Musa, asks a stranger: "May I follow you for the purpose that you teach me some of the probity you have been taught?" (Q 18:66). Even a prophet sought a teacher (L06 ¶37). No epigraph.
**Words you'll meet:** *shaykh* · *murid* · *silsila* · *wali* · *wusul*
**Sections:**
1. ## Why a companion [7.1, old 10.2] — the road has turns a traveller cannot read alone; scholars weigh the need differently.
   - The soul passes through strange states: "it comes time that you hate worship"; someone who "actually have a journey along the path" can say what it means (L14 ¶23–24).
   - Side by side (D20): the Sheikh: begin with the law; the teacher comes later: "First you have to comply completely with whatever Sharia tells" (L07 ¶14–15); orders' claim that you are lost without them is "something of an exaggeration" (L14 ¶25). Mutahhari: without a guide the traveller "is in danger of going astray" [@light, p. 10]; the verse "Leave not this stage without the company of Khidr" (Persian: n.a.).
2. ## A guide beside the Imam, not in his place [7.2, old 9.3 short] — for Shia, authority belongs to the Imam, so there is no chain of authority and no blind obedience.
   - "We don't have such a thing as tariqa in Shia": Imamah leaves no room for a silsila of authority (L14 ¶6–8). Shia "find an instructor for themselves", someone "you could entrust your soul" to (L14 ¶22).
   - Tabatabaʾi's two teachers: a general teacher, consulted as "the people of the Reminder" (Q 16:43), and the special teacher, "the Apostle of Allah and his true successors": "The wayfarer cannot dispense with the company of the special teacher in any circumstance whatsoever"; that company is "the inner company of the Imam with the wayfarer" [@kernel, p. 80].
   - Pointer to Ch 15 for orders, pledges and the full comparison. Do not use the ghusl image (L14 ¶21).
3. ## Teachers within living memory [7.3, old 10.1] — Shia teachers who walked the road form a line to our own time.
   - Akhund Mulla Husayn-Quli Hamadani; his pupils (Maliki Tabrizi, Karbalaʾi, Bahari); Qadi; Tabatabaʾi: "My preceptor was the late Haji Mirza Ali Agha Qazi who was a pupil of Agha Sayyid Ahmad Karbalai." [@light, p. 106]. Khomeini with Shahabadi (callback to Ch 4's box) [@forty_hadith, p. 8].
   - They were jurists outside the orders: "But we belong to none of these orders and follow the directions of the late Akhund" [@light, p. 105].
   - Hamadani's method was knowledge of the soul, "which is essential to the gnosis of the Lord" [@kernel, p. 87]: callback to Ch 4.
   - The Sheikh calls Tabatabaʾi a "symbol of Shiah mysticism" (L10 ¶44).
   - Prose names at most five (Hamadani, Qadi, Tabatabaʾi, Khomeini, Mutahhari); Karbalaʾi, Bahari, Maliki Tabrizi, Shahabadi and Shushtari go to the boxes or a list.
4. ## How to test a guide [7.4, old 10.3] — a true guide is known by the shariʿa and by not wanting followers; wonders prove nothing.
   - Feats are no proof: not "extraordinary feats, knowledge of hidden things, of private thoughts of persons" [@kernel, p. 81]; recall Ch 6's Kubra rule (L06 ¶17–22).
   - Long company is the only test [@kernel, p. 81]; "A thousand trap lies concealed in this wilderness" [@kernel, p. 83] (Persian: n.a.).
   - The charlatan: "They have distracted the attention of the creatures of God from Him, attracting them towards themselves" [@forty_hadith, p. 102].
   - Where no inquiry is possible, weigh what he says "in the light of the Book of God and the Sunnah of the Messenger of Allah and the practice of the Immaculate Imams" [@kernel, p. 83].
   - End here. ʿIsa: sit "With one the sight of whom reminds you of Allah, and whose speech increases you in knowledge, and whose actions make you desire the next world." [@lantern, p. 13].
**Boxes:** Story: the weaver who knocked on Sayyid ʿAli Shushtari's door, and the start of the chain [@light, pp. 105–106]. Who's Who: Mirza Jawad Maliki Tabrizi, fourteen years with Hamadani [@journey, p. 8].
**Try this:** This week, notice whose company reminds you of God, whose speech adds to what you know, and whose conduct makes you want the next world, and seek it out (`lantern` p. 13).
**Hand-off:** Once a guide is found, what does the road itself look like?
