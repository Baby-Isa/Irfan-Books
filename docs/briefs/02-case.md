# Chapter 2: Case brief

**Status:** approved 2026-10-12 (user; D39–D41). Moved from Ch 3 to Ch 2 at the user's request (D41).
**Title:** Knowing about God is not knowing Him
**Serves:** B1 (structure v3, `docs/outline/FRAMEWORK.md`)
**Subheading:** Most of what we know of God is knowledge *about* Him. The more the Qur'an invites is knowing Him, which is a different kind of knowing: it comes as light in a purified heart, so the path is purification, and study prepares for it.

**Conclusion:** knowing God differs in kind from knowing about God, and it is reached by purification, which study serves but cannot replace.

## Chapter points
- **2.1** Acquired knowledge (ʿilm) defines and limits; knowledge by presence (maʿrifah) arrives. (L02 ¶29–32, ¶36–37)
- **2.2** Reason analyses attributes and stops short of any essence, above all God's. (L03 ¶25–28, ¶33, ¶35, ¶38)
- **2.3** Knowledge of God is light that God places in a purified heart, passed heart to heart, not mind to mind. (L03 ¶10–11, ¶18–21; added: `forty_hadith` p. 331, `lantern` p. 10)
- **2.4** Therefore the path is purification (suluk), not thought alone; study prepares the ground but cannot replace it. (L03 ¶19–20, ¶47–49; added, attributed: `forty_hadith` p. 403; `kernel` p. 56; `light` p. 42)

_Fidelity: 2.1–2.3 and the first half of 2.4 are the Sheikh's. The second half of 2.4 ("study prepares the ground") is Khomeini's and Tabatabaʾi's, and the prose says so (D39)._

## Sections (each heading serves the point in brackets)
0. **Opener** (under 150 words, before "Words you'll meet")
   - A fast in Ramadan. No one has to be told they are hungry, or learn it from a book: the hunger is simply known. This is the Sheikh's own example (L02 ¶31–32, ¶37).
   - Then link to Ch 1: the minimum the believer knows (fiqh, belief, history) is knowledge *about* God; the "more" of Ch 1 is the other kind.
   - State the gap plainly and without presumption about the reader (TONE 4).
1. **Knowing about, and knowing** [2.1]
   - **Acquired knowledge** (*ʿilm al-husuli*) comes from outside, by the senses or by reasoning. It works through definitions, and to define is to limit (*hadd*). (L02 ¶29, ¶36)
   - **Knowledge by presence** (*ʿilm al-huduri*) is with the soul already, as hunger is; one does not understand it but arrives at it. (L02 ¶29–32, ¶37)
   - The Sheikh's caveat: religious texts use ʿilm and maʿrifah for one thing; the distinction is a working one. (L02 ¶36; L03 ¶21)
   - Khomeini: the ʿarif knows God "through direct witnessing", the ʿalim "through philosophical proofs" (`forty_hadith` p. 542). Mutahhari: the ʿarif's knowledge is "experienced and witnessed" (`light` p. 41).
2. **Where reason stops** [2.2]
   - Reason knows a thing by its attributes (colour, size, function) and never reaches what carries them. In the Sheikh's account, even Ibn Sina granted this. (L03 ¶25–27)
   - Quote: Imam ʿAli, "Soaring thoughts cannot reach him and deep minds cannot fathom him" (Nahj 1.1.1, `nahj` p. 122; `ar="[VERIFY]"`).
   - "We cannot know the essence of god… But we want to know him", through His attributes. This is a positive knowledge, not only "God is not this". (L03 ¶33, ¶35, ¶38; follow ¶38 exactly)
3. **Light in the heart** [2.3]
   - Quote: "Knowledge is not extensive learning. Rather, it is a light that God casts in the heart of whomever He wills." (`forty_hadith` p. 331; cf. `lantern` p. 10; L03 ¶18; `ar="[VERIFY]"`)
   - Quote: Q 24:35, "Allah is the Light of the heavens and the earth" (Arabic from the build). The light is everywhere; the heart is closed; nothing is withheld on God's side. (L03 ¶20–21)
   - Heart to heart: "not from mind to mouth but from heart"; for Shia it comes through the Imams. (L03 ¶10–11) The thousand-doors hadith is left out (D39).
4. **The path is purification** [2.4]
   - *fikr* against *suluk*: "You purify yourself and then wait for Allah to put the light in your heart." (L03 ¶20) First, polish the soul by what the Prophet asked; "without this there is no maarifah". (L03 ¶47–49)
   - Quote: Munajat §88, "One who follows Your path is enlightened" (`light` p. 165; Arabic: duas §88).
   - **Side by side on study (added, attributed):**
     - Khomeini: mere knowledge can be "the greatest of veils", yet "one has to approach a veil in order to tear it into shreds" (`forty_hadith` p. 403).
     - Tabatabaʾi: first the rules of the shariʿa from a jurist, then practice; "knowledge gives birth to action and action in turn brings knowledge" (`kernel` p. 56).
     - Mutahhari: the ʿarif's means are "the heart and the purification, disciplining and perfecting of the self" (`light` p. 42).
5. **Knowing by drawing near** [conclusion]
   - The conclusion is stated once.
   - Hand-off: "Purification sounds far off. Is there anywhere an ordinary believer already stands on this path?"

## Try this
In prostration, say "Glory belongs to my Lord, the Exalted, and Him do I praise" while believing what it says, and notice one's own lowliness and God's majesty (`kernel` p. 56). It turns a phrase known *about* into something known *by presence*, inside a prayer the reader already says.
