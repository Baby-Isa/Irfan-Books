# Chapter 2: Case brief

**Status:** draft for Gate A (2026-10-12)
**Title:** You already know these words
**Serves:** B1 (`docs/ARGUMENT.md` v2.1)

**Conclusion:** the goal of ʿirfan is already on the believer's lips.

## Chapter points (copied from ARGUMENT.md v2.1)
- **2.1** The Imams' supplications ask for exactly what the mystic seeks: severance from all else, illumined hearts, love, and reaching God. (L01 ¶18–23, ¶27–30)
- **2.2** They ask from the seeker's posture: need and humility, a pupil reaching above his level. (L01 ¶17, ¶22)
- **2.3** These are the prayers of the Mafatih, recited in Shaʿban, Ramadan and at ʿArafah, so the goal is neither foreign nor reserved for an elite. (L01 ¶17, ¶22; added: Mafatih pp. 129, 243, 282, 374; `light` pp. 138, 156)

## Sections (each heading serves the point in brackets)
0. **Opener** (under 150 words, before "Words you'll meet")
   - Thursday night, Duʿa Kumayl, a prayer most of us know. Epigraph: "And my heart enthralled by Your love!" (Kumayl §221, Arabic held). The first paragraph names the prayer, says the line is a request for love of God, and states the chapter's question: what are we asking for when we say words like these?
1. **Cut off from all else** [2.1]
   - The Munajat Shaʿbaniyyah opens its last petitions with severance: "grant me absolute devotion to You" (§104; Arabic held). The Sheikh reads it as being cut off in the heart from everything else, the first condition: "they have to cut off from everything first". (L01 ¶18, ¶20)
   - The same request in other prayers we recite: Abu Hamza, "expel love for the world out of my heart" (§384). ʿArafah says God has "removed love for others from the hearts of Your lovers" (§783–784).
2. **To see, to love, to arrive** [2.1]
   - **Sight:** the prologue's line about the eyes of the heart is recalled in one sentence, not re-quoted. ʿArafah: "It is You Who have lit the illuminations in the hearts of Your intimate servants" (§781). The Sheikh's point is that the prayer asks the heart to see what the eye cannot. (L01 ¶19)
   - **Love:** "place me among Your friends… one who hopes for an increase in Your love" (Munajat §92–93, Arabic held). The Sheikh reads it as two things that keep increasing: maʿrifah and love. (L01 ¶23)
   - **Arrival:** "to reach the Source of Grandeur" (Munajat §107). Abu Hamza: "make me love meeting You" (§599). The Lovers ask for "gazing upon Thy face" and nearness (Sahifa pp. 656–657). (L01 ¶27–29)
   - **Hence:** these are the two aims Chapter 1 gave ʿirfan, maʿrifah and love, asked for in our own prayers. Mutahhari says these supplications "all contain the most sublime spiritual ideas" (`light` p. 20).
3. **A pupil reading above his level** [2.2]
   - The Sheikh's question: why are we told to recite such words? "It's like if you tell a primary school boy to read books of university." (L01 ¶17; quoted, since it carries weight)
   - The posture of the prayers themselves: "standing before You, showing submission to You, imploring You" (Munajat §6–7); "Here I am begging You by my need for You" (ʿArafah §689).
   - His answer: we are honoured to be allowed to recite them; the words are above our level, and God may grant to a sincere reciter what He granted the Imams. (L01 ¶22, paraphrased)
4. **Prayers for ordinary nights** [2.3]
   - The Mafatih sets these prayers on nights most believers keep. The Shaʿbaniyyah is the prayer that Imam ʿAli and the Imams said in Shaʿban, and is "advisable to say… with presence of heart all the time" (Mafatih pp. 243, 245). Kumayl belongs to mid-Shaʿban and Thursday evenings (p. 129); Abu Hamza to the nights of Ramadan (p. 282; `journey` p. 111); ʿArafah to the Day of ʿArafah (p. 374).
   - Khomeini, to his students: "You are already familiar with the words used in Imams' supplications" (`light` p. 156).
   - **Side by side (D20):** Maliki Tabrizi says the depth of these prayers is grasped by "a small number of believers" (`journey` p. 43); Khomeini speaks of "those who are fit to do so" praying in this style (`light` p. 157). The Sheikh agrees that the words are above our level, and still recommends the Munajat to every listener (L01 ¶22). Both views are given, gently.
5. **Words already on our lips** [conclusion]
   - The conclusion is stated once.
   - Hand-off: "The prayers ask for knowledge of God. What kind of knowing is that?"

## Where scholars differ (D20)
- **Whether the depth of these prayers is for the few:** `journey` p. 43 and `light` p. 157 lean that way. The Sheikh (L01 ¶22) and the Mafatih's "all the time" (p. 245) set them before everyone. The chapter claims access, not attainment: everyone is given the words; how far each goes is the subject of Part Four.
