# Chapter 1: Case brief

**Status:** draft v1, awaiting approval

**Title:** Seeking, not powers

**Conclusion (the one sentence the chapter proves):** ʿIrfan is the training of the soul to perceive God's signs. It is open to every believer, and it is pursued for knowing and loving God alone, never for powers.

## Argument, in order (each step depends only on the steps before it)
1. **The question.** Islam sets a minimum, the wajib, and that minimum is enough for salvation. So why do more? *(L01 ¶5, ¶16. Conceded in full.)*
2. **The Quran points beyond the minimum.** It lists signs of God in ordinary life and says each is for a particular kind of person: those who reflect, know, listen, reason. *(Q 30:21–24)*
3. **Most people miss them.** *(Q 12:105)*
4. **The cause is inside us, not in the signs.** People have hearts, eyes and ears that do not understand, see or hear. *(Q 7:179; L02 ¶4–5)* The faculty exists; it is not working. This is the lecturer's "inner machine". *(L01 ¶10–12)*
5. **Some people's faculty does work.** The Quran names a class whose hearts tremble at God's mention and who are "brought near" *(Q 8:2; 3:18; 35:28; 10:62)*. Imam ʿAli describes the friends of God as those who see the inner side of the world *(Nahj 3.403)*.
6. **That class is open to ordinary believers.** Of the four ranks in Q 4:69, the righteous (salihin) is open to anyone *(L01 ¶15; L02 ¶10)*. The human being was made with the capacity to become a friend of God *(L01 ¶25)*.
   - **Conclusion A:** beyond the minimum there is a real, Quran-described possibility, which is to perceive what most people miss. ʿIrfan is the name for training the soul towards it.
7. **Soul-training can serve one of two aims:** acquiring powers, or reaching God. *(L01 ¶3–4, 13)*
8. **Islamic ʿirfan rejects the first aim.** The lecturer calls seeking powers shirk *(L01 ¶32)*. Tabatabaʾi *(kernel pp. 28–29)*, Khomeini *(forty_hadith p. 61)* and Mutahhari *(light p. 44)* concur, each in his own terms (D20, set side by side).
   - **Conclusion B:** ʿirfan is defined by its aim, maʿrifah and love of God, "seeking, and nothing else" *(L01 ¶32)*.
9. **Hence the title:** seeking, not powers.
