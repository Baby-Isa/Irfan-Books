## Sections
1. **The Qur'an, the companions and the Imams** [13.1]
   - **Point:** The road's material was in Islam from its first generation, in the Qur'an, among the companions, and most fully in Imam ʿAli (as) and the Imams after him.
   - **Paragraph points (the reader's questions):** 1. Some picture Islam as simple: a builder God, a piety of fear and hope, companions who were only pious men; Mutahhari rejects it (`light` pp. 16–17). 1a. Is God a builder outside His house? His essence "is everywhere and with everything" (`light` p. 17); Q 2:115. 1b. Was early piety only fear and hope? Harithah recalled (Ch 3); "burning spiritual love" (`light` p. 19). 1c. Were the companions only pious men? Abu Dharr, Salman, Uways, named by Sunni and Shia alike (L11 ¶35, ¶38; box). 2. Whose words hold it most fully? Imam ʿAli's, "a treasure of knowledge" (`light` pp. 4–5); the chains run to him (pointer to Ch 15). 3. And after him? Imam al-Sadiq, one of the greatest early teachers (Schimmel, as the Sheikh reports, L11 ¶45–46). 4. Where does an ordinary believer meet it? In Kumayl, Abu Hamza, the Sahifa and the Shaʿbaniyya (`light` p. 20; recalls Ch 3). 5. Therefore: 13.1.
2. **The first seekers and the Imams above them** [13.2]
   - **Point:** The first seekers after the companions learned from the Imams, and stood below them.
   - **Paragraph points:** 1. Who carried it into the next century? Second-century ascetics who "conceded that they were introduced to spiritualism by the first group and trained by it" (`light` p. 5); Ch 11's three temperaments recalled. 1a. Fear: Fudayl (Ch 9), whose lessons with Imam al-Sadiq are said to be the *Lantern of the Path* (`light` p. 25). 1b. Renunciation: Ibrahim ibn Adham, taught by Imam al-Sadiq (L12 ¶11); his three levels of *zuhd*, the highest being Imam ʿAli's saying, Nahj 3.221 (L12 ¶12–16). 1c. Love: Maʿruf al-Karkhi, a Muslim at Imam al-Rida's hands (`light` p. 25); love a "divine gift" (L12 ¶39); Rabiʿa recalled (Ch 11). 2. Why are the famous names Sunni? (`light` p. 5) 2a. The Imams' names were dangerous: Hasan al-Basri hid Imam ʿAli's name (L11 ¶67–68); Shaqiq did not name the Ahl al-Bayt (L12 ¶24). 2b. The Imams' followers spoke only of the Imams (L11 ¶56–57). 3. Were they the Imams' equals? No: "an echo" (L11 ¶58); the Lovers' prayer above Rabiʿa's verses; love "missing" in them but not in the Imams (L12 ¶56). 4. Told story: Shaqiq and the young man on the Mecca road, who was Imam al-Kazim (L12 ¶30–37; Q 49:12, Q 20:82). 5. "completely different in terms of their calibre" (L12 ¶37). 6. Therefore: 13.2.
3. **Not brought in from outside** [13.3]
   - **Point:** So the road was not brought in from outside; at most, some techniques and later theory were borrowed.
   - **Paragraph points:** 1. Why look elsewhere? They "cannot believe that Islam as a religion of law and jurisprudence can produce mystics" (L11 ¶3); three candidates. 1a. Monks: Q 57:27, monasticism invented and not prescribed; "this is not the way" (L11 ¶10–11); "Sufi" from wool (`light` p. 22). 1b. Neoplatonism: came late, with the theory (L11 ¶20–21; `light` p. 16). 1c. Buddhism: Khurasan and the Buddha-like prince; but the concepts differ, renunciation is in the Qur'an, and the prince learned from Imam al-Sadiq (§2); at most techniques (L11 ¶21–23, ¶74). 2. What is certain: "from Islam itself and from nowhere else" (`light` p. 16). 3. Western scholarship came round: Nicholson and Massignon; "Surely the seeds of mysticism are here." (`light` pp. 20–21). 4. Jurdaq's pitcher, back to Abu Dharr; "the great ocean of Islam" (`light` p. 20). 5. Therefore (section and chapter).

## Where scholars differ (D20, shown side by side and gently)
- Hasan al-Basri: Mutahhari begins the history with him and orders trace through him to Imam ʿAli (`light` pp. 5, 23); the Sheikh finds him ambivalent (L11 ¶64). §2 box.
- Dhu'l-Nun: Nicholson saw Neoplatonic influence; Schimmel saw the Imams' teaching surfacing in him (both as the Sheikh reports, L11 ¶58; L12 ¶63–64). §2 box.
- Outside influence: the Sheikh allows some techniques (L11 ¶22–23); Mutahhari records a view that philosophy shaped the later structure (`light` p. 16). Both are certain of the source. §3.
- Left for Ch 15: Tabatabaʾi's report that some masters hold the mystics' system was devised by them yet approved, as monasticism was (`light` p. 5).
