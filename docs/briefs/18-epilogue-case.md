# Epilogue: Case brief

**Status:** draft, pipeline Stage 1 (D55, 2026-11-01); for the Stage 2 Fable challenge. Ladder: `docs/ladders/18-epilogue.md`.
**Title:** First steps
**Serves:** B3 in practice; closes B5 (`docs/ARGUMENT.md`, `docs/ladders/00-book-chain.md`). Adds no line to the book chain.

**Conclusion (one sentence the epilogue proves):** The road begins with one small act the book has already named, kept inside the law, measured to the traveller and never rushed: minutes a day this week, one practice through a month, one practice for a year, and the keeping itself asked of God.

## Chapter points (E.1–E.4)
- **E.1** [B2, B3] Every step stands inside the law, suits the traveller, is small enough to keep, and grows gradually; a beginner need not wait for a guide, but tests any guide he follows.
- **E.2** [B3] This week: repentance once, then one act of minutes from a chapter's Try this, at a time of day already kept; the act the heart is present in is the one to keep.
- **E.3** [B3] This month: that one act daily for a month; Shaʿban, Ramadan and Dhu'l-Qaʿdah give it a longer form, within what the traveller can carry.
- **E.4** [B3, B5] This year: one practice for a year, not stopped when the year ends; the keeping itself is asked of God, in the Munajat's words.

## Sections
1. **How to begin** [E.1]
   - **Point:** Every step stands inside the law, suits the traveller, is small enough to keep, and grows gradually; a beginner need not wait for a guide, but tests any he follows.
   - **Paragraph points:** 1. obligations first; the law holds to the end (Ch 6, Ch 9 in words) → 2. one act that suits you: review your state, "select the proper program which is suitable for him" (`journey` p. 109); the measured dose (Ch 10 §4 in words) → 3. small and kept (Ch 10 in words); not dropped if it cannot be done in full, "he should perform it, as much as possible" (`journey` p. 161) → 4. not rushed: the young man (Ch 9, a clause); "a linear gradual improvement" (L08 ¶26) → 5. no need to wait for a guide; any guide tested as in Ch 7 (words) → 6. therefore.
2. **This week** [E.2]
   - **Point:** Begin with repentance, then one act of a few minutes from a chapter's Try this, at a time of day already kept, and notice which the heart is present in.
   - **Paragraph points:** 1. repentance first: the Dhu'l-Qaʿdah prayer, allowed any time, "the first step" (L08 ¶29–33; form in Ch 9) → 2. one act fixed to a time already kept (reader told "four times"): 2a in and after the prayer (Ch 2, 4, 6, 10, 12, 14); 2b with the Qur'an and the duʿas (Ch 1, 3, 11); 2c at night (Ch 5, 8, 13); 2d among people (Ch 7, 16) → 3. the week shows which act to keep (Ch 15, L14 ¶70–71) → 4. therefore.
3. **This month** [E.3]
   - **Point:** Keep that one act daily for a month; Shaʿban, Ramadan and Dhu'l-Qaʿdah give it a longer form, within what the traveller can carry.
   - **Paragraph points:** 1. a month is the middle rung, nothing yet added (book's arrangement of "gradually") → 2. three months (reader told): 2a Shaʿban, the whole Munajat (Ch 3, L01 ¶22); 2b Ramadan, verses as addressed to you (Ch 17), the disciplines already kept (Ch 10 opening); 2c Dhu'l-Qaʿdah, the repentance prayer at its best time (Ch 9) → 3. heavier programmes only if "spiritual condition and mood allow him" (`journey` pp. 109, 155): the ceiling, not the start → 4. therefore.
4. **This year** [E.4]
   - **Point:** Keep one practice for a year and do not stop when the year ends; the keeping itself is asked of God, in the Munajat's words.
   - **Paragraph points:** 1. a year: "for at least a year or so" (L10 ¶26); Qadi (Ch 7/8, a clause) → 2. which practice: the month's, or al-Muzzammil / half an hour of night prayer (Ch 10 in words) → 3. the opener answered: a year places one among those who plead for forgiveness at dawns (L10 ¶26–27; Q 3:17 recalled) → 4. not stopped: "doesn't mean that after one year you stop it"; "more power to ask for more" (L10 ¶27); none dropped (Ch 8) → 5. God's part: He "accepts deed, and not its being small or big" (`journey` p. 161) → 6. therefore → close: Part Five's petition (§128–131) in words; end on §124, "make me one of those who always remember You" (`light` p. 166; Arabic held). Stop.

## Argument, in order
1. **The law comes first and holds to the end** (Ch 6 §4; Ch 9 §3; L07 ¶15; L08 ¶39–40).
2. **Recommended acts are chosen to suit the person** (`journey` p. 109; Ch 10 §4; L14 ¶70–71).
3. **They are small and kept, and done as far as possible** (L10 ¶23–24; `lantern` p. 55; `journey` p. 161).
4. **They grow gradually, never rushed** (L08 ¶26; Ch 9 §2).
5. **A beginner starts without waiting for a guide, and tests any guide** (Ch 7 §1, §4; L07 ¶15–16).
   - **Interim conclusion A (E.1):** begin inside the law, with one suited act, small, gradual, beside a tested companion.
6. **The first act is repentance; then one act of minutes daily from the Try this boxes** (L08 ¶29–33; Ch 1–17 boxes).
7. **The week shows which act to keep** (L14 ¶70–71).
   - **Interim conclusion B (E.2).**
8. **That act is kept a month; three months give it a longer form, within one's means** (L01 ¶22; `journey` pp. 46, 109, 155; `mafatih` p. 356).
   - **Interim conclusion C (E.3).**
9. **A practice is kept a year and then continued** (L10 ¶26–27; L07 ¶16; Ch 8 §3).
10. **God accepts the act, small or big, and the keeping is asked of Him** (`journey` p. 161; Munajat §124, `light` p. 166).
   - **Conclusion:** the epilogue's point.
