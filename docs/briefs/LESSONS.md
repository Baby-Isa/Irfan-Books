# Lessons from the user's reviews of Chapters 1–3

_Read this before drafting or reviewing any chapter. Each line is something the user corrected, so a draft that repeats it will come back. The full rules, each with a before/after, are in `docs/TONE.md` (1–37)._

## The argument
1. **Each section is a chain.** Every paragraph adds one link between the section's opening and its point note, and the strongest point lands as a conclusion the reader has been led to. A paragraph that only adds another true, sourced remark goes. (TONE 25)
2. **The section's point is usually simple, so say it in few paragraphs.** By halfway through the first draft of Ch 2 the user said it felt bloated.
3. **Build before "therefore".** Lay out the premises before any conclusion. The worry a reader would have ("cut off from everything, so must I leave my family?") is raised and then answered. (TONE 27)
4. **An apparent contradiction is named, then resolved step by step.** For example, the hadith says God casts the light "into the heart of whomever He wills", yet His light is everywhere. (TONE 30, 37)
5. **A puzzling remark from the lecture is explained in full or cut.** No dangling "some take these words for mistakes." (TONE 29)
6. **Callbacks to earlier chapters re-explain the gist** for someone who read that chapter a week ago. (TONE 26)

## The voice
7. **Name the Sheikh sparingly.** His teaching is the book's own exposition. Name him when a point is distinctively his, when he is quoted, or when he is set beside another scholar. Never twice in a paragraph, and no "he says" filler. The cap is 5 mentions per 1,000 words. (TONE 23)
8. **Straight prose, not "he said, she said".** Bring in a scholar only where his words add something the argument needs; one or two voices per point. Don't build sections around fitting in quotations. (TONE 24)
9. **No mic-drop endings.** No short reveal sentence at the end of a paragraph; fold it into the sentence before. No tidy aphorisms or balanced closing lines. (TONE 32; LINGO)
10. **Fine points go in short, clean sentences**, one half each, with no clutter of commas and semicolons. (TONE 36)
11. **Contrasts take "whereas"**, not a bare semicolon. (TONE 35)
12. **Name the referent** whenever it is not in the reader's working memory: "the second kind" becomes "knowledge by presence"; "those eyes" becomes "our physical eyes". (TONE 13, 28)
13. **Vary neighbouring sentences** that share a structure and length. (TONE 31)
14. **Plain English for every technical idea.** Gloss an unfamiliar word in brackets on first use ("a quatrain (a poem of four lines)"). Explain a school or concept that needs more than a sentence in a box. (TONE 34)
15. **Theological care.** Nothing is said to act upon God, diminish Him, or limit Him. "Each statement removes something from Him" became "each statement says what God is not". (TONE 33)

## House style
16. Oxford comma in lists of three or more. Chapter numbers in words ("Chapter Four"). (as) for the Imams, ﷺ for the Prophet. "Qur'an". "Abu Hamza" in our own prose.
17. One callout box per section, plus Try this at the end. A box must make a point or tell a story with a point; it must not be a side history lesson. (Ch 2's Ibn Sina box was "boring… doesn't make a point" until it carried the chapter's point.)
18. Inline Arabic is used sparingly; displayed quotations carry their Arabic.

## Sourcing (unchanged, but repeated because drafts slip)
19. Every quotation is copied exactly from a held extract. Arabic comes only from `sources/quran/` or `sources/extracts/duas-*.md`; anything else is `[VERIFY]`.
20. Keep the Sheikh's words apart from the book's own explanation. Where the book draws out his point, it says so ("The point is worth drawing out…") and does not put the inference in his mouth.
21. No questions for the Sheikh (D39). Go around an unclear passage: leave it out, use a clear one, or word it so it is true on either reading.
