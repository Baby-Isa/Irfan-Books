## The approved outline for this chapter (`docs/outline/part-2.md`)
#### 4. Know yourself, know your Lord
**Subheading:** The knowing the prayers ask for happens in the soul, so the road to God runs through the self; and since God is already near, the journey lifts veils rather than covering distance.
**Conclusion:** the way to God runs through the self. **Serves:** B2 **Old number:** 4
**Opener:** In Shaʿban we say, "I have been unjust to my soul for I have not looked after it" [4-M]. We say it as a confession of sins. What would looking after it mean? The Sheikh begins all of ʿirfan's theory there. No epigraph.
**Words you'll meet:** *nafs* · *ruh* · *hijab* (pl. *hujub*) · *qurb* · *liqaʾ Allah* [add to GLOSSARY first]
**Sections:**
1. ## The instrument of all knowing [4.1] — the soul is what we know with, so it comes first.
   - The Sheikh opens theory with the soul, "the means to understand and to learn" (L04 ¶7). The soul knows itself by presence, the clearest case of Ch 2's second knowing (L04 ¶4–5, ¶49).
   - Q 91:7–10: the soul is fashioned with discernment; "the whole story of human being in this world is about human soul" (L04 ¶12). quote [4-Q91] "one who purifies it is felicitous, and one who betrays it fails." — Q 91:9–10
2. ## Forgetting God, forgetting ourselves [4.2] — the Qur'an ties the two losses together; the tradition ties the two knowings together.
   - quote [4-Q59] "Do not be like those who forget Allah, so He makes them forget their own souls." (Q 59:19). The Sheikh: most of us have forgotten our souls; remembering God turns attention back to the soul (L04 ¶13–14). Shomali: "forgetting Him causes us to forget ourselves in turn" [@self_knowledge, p. 8].
   - Q 6:20 (held), "Those who have ruined their souls will not have faith": to see the soul is to see its Maker (L04 ¶18–19).
   - The saying, "a two way relationship" (L04 ¶21). quote [4-H] "Whoever knows his own self knows his Lord" — `lantern` p. 9 (the Prophet) — Arabic: VERIFY (self_knowledge p. 9 Arabic garbled). Side by side (D20): Maliki Tabrizi gives it to Imam ʿAli: "Whoever has identified his self in reality has recognised his Creator" [@journey, p. 125]. Word it: "handed down from the Prophet, and from Imam ʿAli".
   - Tabatabaʾi: the seeker "must see Allah through the window of his own soul" [@light, p. 7].
3. ## Near already: meeting, not grasping [4.3] — meeting God means nearness, not comprehension; He is near, so the journey removes veils.
   - Q 6:31: "losers who deny the encounter with Allah". Meeting means "We remove the layers that cover our soul" and see it, a reflection of God, not God (L04 ¶41–44).
   - Limits: "You can get some understanding but knowing him completely … is impossible" (L04 ¶31, ¶33). Khomeini: liqaʾ Allah "is not that it is permissible to fathom the mystery of the Divine Essence" [@forty_hadith, p. 398].
   - Seed (D26, a few sentences): "We are actually in his presence at the moment. However, he is not in our presence." (L04 ¶54). The journey is inward, the lifting of veils (L07 ¶4; L08 ¶9 in a pointer only). Forward pointer to Ch 8. Do not quote Q 50:16 (Ch 8's).
**Boxes:** Word Study: "Whoever knows himself": two attributions, two readings (L04 ¶29–33). Who's Who: Mirza Muhammad ʿAli Shahabadi, Khomeini's teacher, "a murid with his murshid" [@forty_hadith, p. 8].
**Try this:** After the daily prayers, recite Q 59:18–24 and reflect on its meaning, as Shahabadi asked his pupils to: "Our Shaykh would ask us to recite these verses after daily prayers and to contemplate over their meaning" [@forty_hadith, p. 201].
