**Title:** Adorning: love, the engine

**Chapter point:** The third stage, adorning, is God's gift to the emptied heart: knowledge and light, and love, which carries the traveller until even love is left at the door.

**Section chain:**
1. Once the heart is emptied, God adorns it with gifts no effort makes, briefly at first.
2. Among those gifts is love, which the Imams asked for, and worship from love is the worship of the free.
3. Love too is a means, so at the Beloved even love is left behind.

## Opening
1. Epigraph [M59]: "If You haul me into the hell, I will tell its inmates that I love You". No trader would say such a sentence.
2. Part One's "awakened me to Your love" recalled in words: the prayers speak of love as something God awakens. The chapter sets out the third stage, where the work passes from the traveller to God, and the love it brings.

## §1 What God gives the emptied heart
**Point: Once the heart is emptied, God adorns it with gifts no effort makes, briefly at first.**
1. Adorning (*tahliya*) is God's act on the soul, not the traveller's: insight, inspiration and a deeper understanding of the Qur'an (L07 ¶36); the base qualities give way to their opposites, "gnosis, wisdom, forbearance, humility, generosity, chastity and courage" [IS109].
2. The gifts reach everyone, but only a purified soul catches them: the mystics see "not because… they are chosen ones but because they have furbished their souls" (L07 ¶37); "a light by which he walks among the people" (Q 6:122).
3. Story (prose): the Tehran preacher who stated a teaching and asked his listeners for the verse or hadith, sure there was one: knowledge given to a heart, as the Qur'an rewards those who do good (Q 12:22 cited) (L10 ¶11–15). The rider kept: formal study still matters, and the two support each other (¶15–16).
4. The gifts come briefly at first, "like flashes of lightning appearing and instantly vanishing" [LT48], and later stay (L15 ¶20–22, ¶41).
5. Story (prose): Uways, at that later stage, spent one night in rukuʿ and the next in sajda; asked if he tired, he said the night was short (L15 ¶41–43). The body follows the soul, so one at home with God does not tire.
6. These gifts are "not a great deal": they are not arrival (L07 ¶39).
7. Therefore: God adorns the emptied heart with gifts no effort makes, briefly at first.

## §2 Love, the gift the Imams asked for
**Point: Among those gifts is love, which the Imams asked for, and worship from love is the worship of the free.**
1. Love cannot be made by us; after purification it is placed in the heart by God (L07 ¶10): God "shows the face of himself", and the one shown cannot stop loving (L12 ¶39–42); "a people whom He loves and who love Him" (Q 5:54). *(Word Study box: *mahabba*.)*
2. Because it is given, the Imams always asked for it (L12 ¶41): "How can I endure separation from You?" (Kumayl [K123]); "who can have tasted the sweetness of Thy love, then wanted another in place of Thee?" [SAH656].
3. Love is what carries the traveller: early mystics walked with fear, renunciation and love, none excluding the others, and love became "the victorious side" (L11 ¶61–63), "the heart of mysticism" (L12 ¶56); the Lantern's order, fear, hope, then love, when "the breeze of love stirs" [LAN58]. Fear stays: "perfect expansion while possessing perfect fear" is the Imams' alone [kernel p. 70].
4. Worship from love is a different kind of worship: Imam ʿAli (as) names the worship of merchants, of slaves and of the free [NB3.222]; Imam al-Sadiq (as): "Their worship is that of freemen" [LT94]; the Sheikh: because He "deserves to be worshipped" (L12 ¶60).
5. Story (prose): Rabiʿa with the torch and the water, to burn paradise and put out hell, so that no one worships from fear or hope (L11 ¶72–73; L12 ¶54; "the story is told"). The Sheikh sets her beside the worship of the free (L12 ¶60).
6. "Love of Allah is a fire which does not pass by anything without burning it up" [LAN88].
7. Therefore: love is God's gift, the Imams asked for it, and it makes worship the worship of the free.

## §3 Even love is left at the door
**Point: Love too is a means, so at the Beloved even love is left behind.**
1. "In the beginning the spiritual traveler goes forward with the help of love, but after traversing a few stages he realizes that love is different from the beloved", so he gives up the love that was his means [LT95].
2. Saʿdi: "If you look to your friend for his favors, / You are tied to yourself not to your friend" [SADI45].
3. *(Reflection box: Imam Khomeini, "Ask your heart whether it is in search of God or whether it is in love with itself" [FH89].)*
4. Rabiʿa's prayer, as the Sheikh reads it: "you are more than enough for me" (L12 ¶59–60).
5. Therefore (section and chapter): God adorns the emptied heart, love carries the traveller, and at the door even love is left, so that only the Beloved remains.
