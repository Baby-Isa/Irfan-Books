# Ch 11. Love, the engine: proof ladder (proposed 2026-10-19; Gate A)

**Chapter point:** Discipline alone runs dry; love carries the traveller on, until even love is left at the door.

**Section chain:**
1. Fear and renunciation can start the journey, but love carries it.
2. Love is God's gift, so the Imams asked for it, and worship from love is the worship of the free.
3. Love too is a means, so at the Beloved even love is left behind.

## Opening
1. Epigraph [M59]: "If You haul me into the hell, I will tell its inmates that I love You". In the nights of Shaʿban, among requests for pardon, comes a sentence no trader would say. *(Hook; explains the epigraph.)*
2. Part One's divider line, "except when You awakened me to Your love", recalled in words: the prayers have spoken of love from the start. The chapter sets out why love, not fear or discipline alone, carries the traveller, and why even love is left at the door.

## §1 Fear starts the journey; love carries it
**Point: Fear and renunciation can start the journey, but love carries it.**
1. The early mystics walked with three motives, fear, renunciation and love, none excluding the others (L11 ¶61–63). *(Reader told: "taken one at a time".)*
   - a. Fear: of God, and of the reckoning (L11 ¶62).
   - b. Renunciation (*zuhd*): the ascetic gives up "the minimum, to gain the maximum", whereas the mystic gives up only what diverts him, so renunciation is a means (L15 ¶11–13).
   - c. Love: in the Sheikh's words, "the heart of mysticism", which the schools of fear and renunciation lacked (L12 ¶56); it became "the victorious side" (L11 ¶63).
2. The Lantern gives them in order: "Fear is the branch of knowledge; hope is the branch of certainty, and love is the branch of gnosis", and when "the breeze of love stirs, he settles in the shadow of the Beloved" [LAN58].
3. Chapter Ten left a question: what keeps a traveller going? Uways did not tire, because he was at home with God (Ch 10, recalled in a sentence); being at home with God is love, so love is what carries.
4. Fear is not left behind: perfection is "perfect expansion while possessing perfect fear", a state the Kernel gives to the Imams (as) alone [kernel p. 70].
5. Therefore: fear and renunciation can start the journey, but love carries it.

## §2 Love as the Imams pray it
**Point: Love is God's gift, so the Imams asked for it, and worship from love is the worship of the free.**
1. Love is not learned or earned: God "shows the face of himself", and the one shown cannot stop loving (L12 ¶39–42); "a people whom He loves and who love Him" (Q 5:54). *(Word Study box: *mahabba*.)*
2. Because it is given, the Imams always asked for it (L12 ¶41): "suppose… that I am able to endure Your chastisement, How can I endure separation from You?" (Kumayl [K123]); "who can have tasted the sweetness of Thy love, then wanted another in place of Thee?" (the Whispered Prayer of the Lovers [SAH656]).
3. Worship from love is a different kind of worship. Imam ʿAli (as) names three: of merchants, of slaves, and of the free, who worship "to give thanks for his favors" [NB3.222]; Imam al-Sadiq (as) names the third by love: "There are till others who worship Allah because they love Him. Their worship is that of freemen" [LT94]. The Sheikh: because He "deserves to be worshipped" (L12 ¶60).
4. Story (prose): Rabiʿa hurries through the street with fire in one hand and water in the other, to burn paradise and put out hell, so that no one worships from fear or hope (L11 ¶72–73; L12 ¶54; "the story is told"). The Sheikh sets her beside Imam ʿAli's worship of the free (L12 ¶60).
5. Such love consumes everything else: "Love of Allah is a fire which does not pass by anything without burning it up" (Imam ʿAli (as), in the Lantern [LAN88]).
6. Therefore: love is a gift the Imams asked for, and it makes worship the worship of the free.

## §3 Even love is left at the door
**Point: Love too is a means, so at the Beloved even love is left behind.**
1. Love is still a state of the traveller's own, not the Beloved: "In the beginning the spiritual traveler goes forward with the help of love, but after traversing a few stages he realizes that love is different from the beloved", so he gives up the love that was his means [LT95; same passage in `kernel` pp. 72–73].
2. Saʿdi shows why: "If you look to your friend for his favors, / You are tied to yourself not to your friend" [SADI45]; attention on one's own love still looks at oneself.
3. *(Reflection box: Imam Khomeini, "Ask your heart whether it is in search of God or whether it is in love with itself" [FH89].)*
4. Rabiʿa reaches this in prayer: give my share of this world to Your enemies and of the next to Your friends, since "you are more than enough for me" (L12 ¶59–60): nothing is wanted but Him.
5. Therefore (section and chapter): fear and renunciation start the journey, love carries it, and at the door even love is left, so that only the Beloved remains.
- Close: Try this (the Whispered Prayer of the Lovers, once this week, slowly); "Where does love carry the traveller in the end?"
