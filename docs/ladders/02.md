# Ch 2. Knowing about God is not knowing Him: proof ladder (proposed 2026-10-18)

**Chapter point:** That perceiving is not knowing *about* God; it is a light God gives to a purified heart, so it is reached by purification, which study serves but cannot replace.

**Section chain:**
1. The ʿarif knows God by presence, not by definition.
2. Reason cannot reach God Himself, so He must be known by presence, within the soul.
3. That knowing is a light God gives to a purified heart, not something gathered by study.
4. Since the light is held back from no heart (3), the path to knowing God is purification, which study serves but cannot replace.
5. Therefore knowing God is not knowing about Him: it is a light given to a purified heart, reached by purification. *(Closing section; no new point.)*

## Opening
1. A fasting person knows their hunger from the inside, not from a book or a clock; the Sheikh uses hunger for a knowing that study cannot give.
2. Most of what we know of God is knowing about Him, gained by study; the Qur'an's invitation is to know Him directly, as the fasting person knows hunger. The chapter sets out, in its title's words, why knowing about God is not knowing Him, and how He comes to be known. *(Words box.)*

## §1 Knowing about, and knowing
**Point: The ʿarif knows God by presence, not by definition.**
1. There are two kinds of knowing. *(Reader told: "Let's start with the two kinds of knowing.")*
   - a. Acquired knowledge reaches us from outside, through the senses or by reasoning; what we know about God is of this kind.
   - b. It works by defining, and to define a thing is to limit it.
   - c. Knowledge by presence is already with the soul, as our self and our hunger are.
   - d. It is complete: nothing of the hunger is missed, and even a false hunger is felt as real.
2. The mystics seek knowledge by presence: to arrive at God, not to define Him, because a definition draws a limit and God has none.
3. Imam Khomeini records the same line between two kinds of people: the ʿarif knows God by witnessing, which is knowledge by presence; the scholar by proofs, which is acquired knowledge.
4. Therefore: the ʿarif knows God by presence, not by definition. *(Word Study box.)*

## §2 Where reason stops
**Point: Reason cannot reach God Himself, so He must be known by presence, within the soul.**
1. Acquired knowledge is the work of reason, and reason knows a thing's attributes, never the thing itself (Ibn Sina).
2. If reason stops short of ordinary things, it stops further short of God, who has no colour or size for the mind to hold.
3. Imam ʿAli: neither the widest nor the deepest reason reaches God, who has no boundaries; there is no line to draw round Him.
4. Reason can still argue that God exists and say what He is not; this is knowledge about God, and apart from the proof, it is negative.
5. ʿIrfan seeks the positive side, His attributes known from within; only knowledge by presence can know God (Mulla Sadra).
6. Therefore: we know by presence only what is within us, where a faint sense of God already lives, so God must be known within the soul. *(Who's Who box.)*

## §3 Light in the heart
**Point: Knowledge of God is a light God gives to a purified heart, not something gathered by study.**
1. Knowledge that reason cannot produce must be given; a hadith says God gives it, as a light cast in the heart.
2. Learning is gathered from outside, whereas the light is placed in the heart by God.
3. "Whomever He wills" could mean God picks some hearts and passes over others; the Sheikh reads it through Surah al-Nur (Q 24:35).
4. God's light is everywhere and held back from no heart, so what keeps a heart dark is that it is closed.
5. Opening the heart is purifying it, and once the soul is purified, God's teaching follows as His gift (Imam Khomeini; Q 2:282).
6. The verse puts wariness of God first and His teaching second: the light comes to the hearts of the pure.
7. Therefore: knowledge of God is a light given to a purified heart, not gathered by study. *(Story box: A thousand doors of knowledge, D47, shows such knowledge passing heart to heart.)*

## §4 The path is purification
**Point: The path to knowing God is purification, which study serves but cannot replace.**
1. If the light is everywhere and the heart is closed, the path is opening the heart: wayfaring (*suluk*), not thought (*fikr*).
2. Wayfaring has two parts: purify yourself, then wait for God to give the light.
3. The Munajat Shaʿbaniyyah says who receives the light it asks for: whoever follows God's path (§88).
4. That path begins with the shariʿa, in doing what the Prophet ﷺ asked; without it there is no maʿrifah (Q 8:24).
5. Practice needs knowledge of what to practise, so study comes early, and then knowing and doing feed each other (Tabatabaʾi).
6. Therefore: without purification there is no maʿrifah, so study serves purification but cannot replace it. *(Story box: The philosopher and the ʿarif.)*

## §5 Knowing by drawing near
**Point: Knowing God is a different kind of knowing, reached by purification, which study serves but cannot replace.** *(No new point.)*
1. Therefore: what study cannot give comes through purification; the ʿurafaʾ are brought near and know God by presence, as a light given to a purified heart, and that differs in kind from knowing about Him.
- Close: "Where, then, does an ordinary believer already stand on this path?" *(Try this box.)*
