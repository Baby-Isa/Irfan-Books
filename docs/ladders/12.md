# Ch 12. Losing the self, finding God: proof ladder (v2, 2026-10-19; Gate A)

_v2: Stage 4 of 4 (passing away, then return). See the map in `docs/ladders/08.md`._

**Chapter point (proposed wording):** In the fourth stage, love ends in losing the false self before God's glory, and in coming back sober, seeing God in all things while remaining oneself.
*(The approved book chain says "seeing only God while remaining oneself". "Seeing only God" is the state of passing away itself (§1); the sober return of §3 sees God and creation together, which is what the closing line of Imam ʿAli (as) says. Proposed change for approval.)*

**Section chain:**
1. In passing away the traveller loses the sense of self before God's glory; he neither ceases to exist nor becomes God, so what is lost is a false self.
2. Passing away comes in three degrees, in action, in attributes and in essence, each the lived truth of words we already say.

## §1 What is lost is the false self
**Point: In passing away the traveller loses the sense of self before God's glory; he neither ceases to exist nor becomes God, so what is lost is a false self.**
1. At the height of adornment (Ch 11), God shows the traveller His glory, and no one can watch that glory and perceive himself at the same time, so the sense of self goes, as a person seized by fear or joy does not feel a cut hand (L07 ¶55–57).
2. So passing away is "the annihilation of self perception of the mystics in perceiving the unveiling [of] the reality of God" (L07 ¶59): a change in what is seen, "an epistemological issue" (L09 ¶4). Tabatabaʾi: the world does not lose its existence; "the devotee sees unity in plurality" [LT63].
3. The soul remains: "not that you have no soul" (L07 ¶56). Unlike nirvana, the individual goes on "more expanded, more capable" (L09 ¶8); otherwise there would be no one to taste what is shown (L07 ¶49–50).
4. Nor does the traveller become God: any merging of God and creature "is just a shirk or kufr" (L07 ¶60–61).
5. What goes, then, is the false picture of a self that stands on its own, the self Chapter Ten said is the last thing left behind: the move is "out of the imaginary you to the real you" (L09 ¶9). *(Word Study box: *fanaʾ* and *baqaʾ* in Q 55:26–27, "Everyone on it is ephemeral, yet lasting is the majestic and munificent Face of your Lord" [kernel pp. 14–15].)*
6. Therefore: what is lost is the false self; the soul remains, and God remains God.

## §2 Three degrees: in action, in attributes, in essence
**Point: Passing away comes in three degrees, each the lived truth of words we already say.**
1. Amuli quotes the gnostics: "The essence is veiled by the attributes and the attributes by the action" [IS115b], so the unveiling goes from the outermost veil inwards. *(Reader told: "three degrees, from the outermost".)*
   - a. In action: the traveller sees that every act comes from God's power, "it is really not you who is seeing" (L09 ¶52–54): the lived truth of *la hawla wa la quwwata illa billah*, tasted rather than thought, as a dish is tasted and not only read in a recipe (¶57–59); "There, all authority belongs to Allah, the Real" (Q 18:44).
   - b. In attributes: wherever he looks he sees God's attributes, the truth of *la ilaha illa Allah* (L09 ¶60–61); "Allah—there is no god except Him—is the Living One, the All-sustainer" (Q 2:255; ¶63).
   - c. In essence: not even attributes, "just his glory, which is overshadowing everything", and only *Huwa*, "He" (L09 ¶64–66); "Say, 'He is Allah, the One'" (Q 112:1).
2. Story (prose): Imam ʿAli (as) dreams of Khidr before Badr and is taught a prayer that calls on God as *Huwa*, He and none but He; on the day of Badr he is seen fighting on every side, reciting Surah al-Ikhlas and these words (L09 ¶65). Given as the Sheikh's report of a hadith; the prayer paraphrased.
3. Side by side (D20): Tabatabaʾi counts four degrees, adding the Names [`light` pp. 107–108]; one clause.
4. Summit: the prologue's prayer asked that the eyes of our hearts pierce the veils of light; the Munajat goes on, "and let our souls get suspended by the glory of Your sanctity" [M108].
5. Therefore: three degrees, each the lived truth of words we already say.

## §3 Coming back, sober
**Point: Passing away is a beginning, not an end: the healthy outcome is to come back to oneself, sober, seeing God and creation together.**
1. "That's the beginning, that's not the end": nearness is not safety, as Satan's fall from near the throne shows (L09 ¶20–23).
2. Not everyone comes back well. *(Reader told: "three ways, as principles, not names".)*
   - a. Some stay lost, held for ever by what they saw (L09 ¶30).
   - b. Some speak ecstatic words (*shath*) and say their duties are lifted, which is "not acceptable at all, especially to shiah position" (L09 ¶39–42).
   - c. Some fall into licence, "whatever sin is done is done by God" (L09 ¶43); Tabatabaʾi lists the "claim of incarnation and identification with God, denial of being obligated to abide by religious injunctions and regarding everything lawful" [LT102b].
3. The cause is rushing; the law, a measured pace and a guide prevent it (L09 ¶44, ¶47–48; L07 ¶64), as Chapters Six, Seven and Nine set out. Bayazid's ecstatic sayings and Junayd's sober way named in one line, with a pointer to Chapter Fourteen.
4. The healthy outcome is "separation after unification", "sobriety after obliteration" (L09 ¶45): grace comes "bringing him to himself and returning him to the domain of his own self, whereat he finds himself in the state of awakening (*sahw*)" [FH515].
5. In that state neither veils the other: for us God is veiled by creation; in passing away creation is veiled by God; now "neither of them is veiled by the other" (L09 ¶46); Amuli, "the one who joins them is the one of true affirmation" [IS116].
6. Therefore (section and chapter): love ends in losing the false self before God's glory and in coming back sober, seeing God in all things while remaining oneself. Last line: Imam ʿAli (as), "I did not see a thing without beholding God with it, in it, before it, and after it" [FH516] (Arabic [VERIFY]).
