# Ch 9. Setting out: proof ladder (v2, 2026-10-19; Gate A)

_v2: Stage 1 of 4 (polishing). See the map in `docs/ladders/08.md`._

**Title:** Polishing: setting out

**Chapter point:** The first stage, polishing, begins with repentance, open to anyone after any sin, and is kept by the law, whose first demand is putting things right with people.

**Section chain:**
1. The road begins with repentance, and repentance is open to anyone after any sin.
2. Repentance is kept up at a steady pace: not rushed, and not put off.
3. Polishing is keeping the law strictly, and its first demand is putting things right with people.

## Opening
1. Epigraph [M64]: "My Lord, I have wasted my life committing the crime of forgetting You…". After salah many of us say *astaghfirullah* by habit; the epigraph asks forgiveness for something else, a life spent forgetting God. *(Hook; explains the epigraph.)*
2. Chapter Eight's map put polishing first, and repentance opens it; this chapter sets out the first stage in full: repentance anyone can begin, kept up at a steady pace, and the law kept strictly, starting with people.

## §1 Anyone can begin
**Point: The road begins with repentance, and repentance is open to anyone after any sin.**
1. Setting out has two moves: waking from heedlessness, then repenting (L08 ¶10).
2. Repentance (*tawba*) here reaches past sins: it turns from casualness towards God, from taking everything for granted and from wasting our lives (L08 ¶10); the Lantern names both kinds: "the repentance of the elite is for being occupied with anything other than Allah, and the repentance of the common people is for wrong actions" [LAN48].
3. Since repentance covers every wrong attitude towards God, a sinner qualifies (L08 ¶11–12). *(Story box: Fudayl the highwayman hears Q 57:16 from the house he is breaking into, answers "yes, it is time, my Lord", and becomes a trusted reporter in both traditions (L12 ¶19–22); Bishr al-Hafi in a line (L08 ¶12).)*
4. What holds a sinner back is despair, "this path is not for me", not the sin (L08 ¶16–18); Q 39:53 speaks to exactly that: "do not despair of the mercy of Allah. Indeed Allah will forgive all sins."
5. Sincere repentance brings God's love [FH249] and polishes the heart "by the burnish of repentance" [LAN59], the polishing of Chapter Eight.
6. Therefore: anyone can begin, with the words the Munajat gives: "I am Your powerless sinning slave and Your repentant bondman" [M100].

## §2 Steady, not fast
**Point: Repentance is kept up at a steady pace: not rushed, and not put off.**
1. A repentant traveller may slip back; the answer is to repent again, because repentance is kept up, not done once (L08 ¶13–15). Side by side (D20): Tabatabaʾi asks that a repented sin "must not be committed again" (`light` p. 89); the two meet, since the resolve is never to return and a fall is met with fresh repentance.
2. The self fights back, "would come back and… take revenge" (L08 ¶15); this struggle with the self is *mujahada*.
3. Because the self fights back, rushing is dangerous: the Sheikh warns of "the dangers of rushing things forward" and asks for "the pace that our Aimmah have advised" (L08 ¶26–27).
4. Story (prose, unnamed): a young man in Tehran recited Ziyarat ʿAshura daily until light showed in his face; an elder told his father to stop him: "you need to have basis. You are rushing" (L08 ¶24–27).
5. Imam al-Sadiq (as) gives the same pace: faith has ten degrees, so "pull him up to you gently"; "Too much enthusiasm in the beginning leads to too little interest in the end" [LT89].
6. Not rushing is not putting off: age makes the road harder, "but it is not impossible" (L08 ¶19–22); Imam Khomeini presses harder, "do not postpone the task of reform to old age" [LT126], since "the springtime for tawbah is the time of youth" [FH250]; all agree sooner is easier.
7. Therefore: repentance is kept up at a steady pace, not rushed and not put off.

## §3 The law, starting with people
**Point: Polishing is keeping the law strictly, and its first demand is putting things right with people.**
1. Polishing is keeping the law "in the strictest sense", the obligatory and the recommended (L07 ¶12–13), as Chapter Six set out (one sentence).
2. After repentance, "the first and foremost and most important condition is to behave yourself with regards to people" (L08 ¶39).
3. The reason: a "system of justice" underlies every action, and a wrong done to anyone, even an animal, blocks the traveller's rise until it is put right (L08 ¶39–40).
4. So the inner road cannot go around people: "It is not possible to acquire the position of the virtuous and the pure without acquiring their qualities" [LT70]; the Prophet ﷺ: "I have been sent to perfect good behaviour" [IS108].
5. The Prophet ﷺ and the Imams (as), at the highest station, were people "everyone loved to sit with" (L08 ¶40–41); Imam ʿAli (as) describes the God-fearing: "They forgive those who oppress them, give to those who refuse them, and foster those who cut them off" [NB1.191].
6. *(Story box: Hatim "the Deaf" spares a woman her embarrassment by pretending not to hear, and carries the name for life (L12 ¶25).)* Good conduct reaches down to the smallest courtesy to an ordinary person.
7. Therefore (section and chapter): the first stage, polishing, begins with repentance, open to anyone and kept up steadily, and is kept by the law, starting with people.
