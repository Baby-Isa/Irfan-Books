# Ch 10. The five disciplines: proof ladder (v2, 2026-10-19; Gate A)

_v2: Stage 2 of 4 (emptying). The five disciplines are emptying's tools. See the map in `docs/ladders/08.md`._

**Chapter point:** The second stage, emptying, clears the heart of everything but God, and the five disciplines are its tools, each in a measured dose.

**Section chain:**
1. ʿIrfan proper begins with emptying the heart, and five disciplines are its tools.
2. Silence, hunger and the night prayer shut the doors through which the world fills the heart: the tongue, the stomach and sleep.
3. Seclusion separates the heart from what distracts it, and remembrance dusts off what remains.
4. Each discipline is taken in a dose measured to the person, small and kept up.

## Opening
1. Epigraph [M83]: "a tongue the truth of which may be submitted to You". In Ramadan we keep most of the disciplines without naming them: hunger by day, waking before dawn, fewer idle words, more Qur'an and remembrance.
2. ʿIrfan keeps a measure of them all year, because they are the tools of the road's second stage; the chapter sets out that stage, its five tools, and how much of each to take.

## §1 Where ʿirfan begins
**Point: ʿIrfan proper begins with emptying the heart, and five disciplines are its tools.**
1. Polishing (Ch 9) is what any believer can begin; the Sheikh places the beginning of ʿirfan proper at the next stage, emptying the heart (L07 ¶22).
2. Emptying clears the heart of every "occupation other than Allah". Keeping the practices is easy, whereas emptying the heart is hard, as anyone knows who has tried to keep other thoughts out of salah (L07 ¶22–24).
3. The test is what occupies the heart, not what it loves: many loves sit inside the love of God, and only what stops us giving ourselves wholly to Him is cleared (L07 ¶23).
4. Emptying goes in four steps, which Ibn Sina names: separating the heart from what diverts it, dusting off the traces, leaving, and rejecting; the self is the last thing left behind (L07 ¶22–29).
5. Mystics of every faith name the same five disciplines for this work: silence, hunger, the night vigil, seclusion and constant remembrance (L08 ¶55, ¶58; L10 ¶6); the Lantern names four of them as the way to rest [LAN56]. They are acts the law recommends, so keeping them is still polishing; what makes them emptying's tools is their target, the heart (L07 ¶22).
6. Therefore: emptying is the second stage, and the five disciplines are its tools. *(Reader told: "three shut doors; two clear the heart".)*

## §2 Silence, hunger and the night
**Point: Silence, hunger and the night prayer shut the doors through which the world fills the heart: the tongue, the stomach and sleep.**
1. Silence: speak only of what concerns you and benefits you or others (L08 ¶59); "Silence is a door from among the doors of wisdom" [KR76]; the epigraph asks for a tongue that answers to God; Maryam's fast of silence (Q 19:26).
2. Hunger: never eat to heaviness, or "you cannot remember God" (L08 ¶72–73); a clause recalls ʿUnwan's rules for eating (Ch 7). *(Story box: the guest of the mosque, for whom God found no better food than hunger [kernel p. 77].)*
3. Together they guard the heart from the chain of idle thoughts that drags us into sin, and make watchfulness of God (*muraqaba*) possible (L08 ¶50–51); "The result of silence is wisdom…" [JR16].
4. The night: the Qur'an asks the Prophet ﷺ to rise for part of it (Q 73:1–4, 73:6), because the day is "prolonged occupation" (L10 ¶19); the righteous "used to sleep a little during the night, and at dawns they would plead for forgiveness" (Q 51:17–18).
5. Each loosens the body's hold on the soul by choice, as sleep loosens it by itself (Ch 5, one sentence).
6. Therefore: the tongue, the stomach and sleep are the doors these three shut.

## §3 Seclusion and remembrance
**Point: Seclusion separates the heart from what distracts it, and remembrance dusts off what remains.**
1. Seclusion, in Shia teaching, is "seclusion of the heart rather than seclusion of the body" (L10 ¶42): Ibn Sina's first step, separating.
2. "Dedicate yourself to Him with total dedication" (Q 73:8), yet the Prophet ﷺ stayed among people, so the dedication is of the heart, which can live "as if… in a cave" in a busy city (L10 ¶49–51). *(Word Study box: *tabattul*.)*
3. The night prayer of §2 is the traveller's daily seclusion: alone with God for half an hour or more each night (L10 ¶44).
4. Remembrance dusts off the traces that remain, with a real, present effect (L07 ¶25–27): Ibn Sina's second step. "Remember Allah with frequent remembrance" (Q 33:41); the Tasbih of al-Zahraʾ is its minimum [MAF802].
5. Remembrance is answered: "Remember Me and I will remember you" (Q 2:152; L10 ¶60–63).
6. Its words come from the hadith; a teacher only chooses which suit the traveller, and none are made up (L10 ¶58–59).
7. Therefore: seclusion separates, remembrance dusts off; with §2, the five tools of emptying.

## §4 A medicine, measured to the patient
**Point: Each discipline is taken in a dose measured to the person, small and kept up.**
1. None is kept to the full: the Prophet ﷺ and Imam ʿAli (as) spoke a great deal, fasting two days without a break is not allowed, and the Qur'an eased the night, "recite as much of the Quran as is feasible" (Q 73:20; L08 ¶55–57); so the question is "where is the balance" (L08 ¶58).
2. To begin, "half an hour is enough" (L10 ¶23); too much and "we just quit" (¶24); keep one practice for a year (¶25–27). Fifteen minutes of sajda tires us because we are not yet used to it (L15 ¶42).
3. The held sources give the same measures: hunger short of "weakness" (`kernel` p. 76); the dawn vigil "to the extent permitted by the wayfarer's constitution" (`kernel` p. 78); devotions "are just like a medicine which may suit some and may not suit others" [LT102].
4. "The most consistent, be it ever so small" [LAN55].
5. Seclusion, side by side (D20): the Sheikh prefers the nightly hour, and says long retreats and feats can stir unusual powers but are "not desirable" (L10 ¶44–47); the Kernel calls a special seclusion "essential by the masters" [KR77]; the Lantern says every prophet and successor chose retreat [LAN50]. They differ on the long retreat and agree on the nightly one.
6. Therefore (section and chapter): emptying clears the heart, and its five tools are taken in measured doses.
