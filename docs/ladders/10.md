# Ch 10. The five disciplines: proof ladder (proposed 2026-10-19; Gate A)

**Chapter point:** Once the soul has turned, five disciplines loosen the body's hold on it, each in a measured dose.

**Section chain:**
1. Five disciplines train the soul by loosening the body's hold on it, and the question for each is how much.
2. Silence, hunger and the night prayer loosen that hold where it is strongest: at the tongue, the stomach and sleep.
3. Seclusion turns the heart away from distraction, and remembrance turns it towards God.
4. Each discipline is taken in a dose measured to the person, small and kept up.

## Opening
1. Epigraph [M83]: "a tongue the truth of which may be submitted to You". In Ramadan we keep most of the disciplines without naming them: hunger by day, waking before dawn, fewer idle words, more Qur'an and remembrance. *(Hook; the epigraph is spoken to in §2's silence step.)*
2. ʿIrfan keeps a measure of them all year; the chapter sets out the five disciplines, what each does, and how much of each to take.

## §1 Five disciplines, one aim
**Point: Five disciplines train the soul by loosening the body's hold on it, and the question for each is how much.**
1. Once the soul has turned (Ch 9), it is trained; mystics of every faith name the same five: silence, hunger, the night vigil, seclusion and constant remembrance (L08 ¶55, ¶58; L10 ¶6).
2. They are not borrowed: the Lantern, attributed to Imam al-Sadiq (as), names four of them as the way to rest, "silence… retreat… hunger… and wakefulness, which illuminates your heart" [LAN56], and each has a root in the Qur'an, as the sections below show.
3. Their aim: the body's hold on the soul loosens by itself in sleep (callback to Ch 5 in one sentence); the disciplines loosen it by choice, to "prepare the soul to receive… the knowledge which is everywhere" (L10 ¶20).
4. None is kept to the full: the Prophet ﷺ and Imam ʿAli (as) spoke a great deal, fasting two days without a break is not allowed, and the Qur'an eased the night (L08 ¶55–57); so for each the question is "where is the balance" (L08 ¶58).
5. Therefore: five disciplines, one aim, each with its measure. *(Reader told: "the first three train the body, at the tongue, the stomach and sleep; the last two turn the heart".)*

## §2 Silence, hunger and the night
**Point: Silence, hunger and the night prayer loosen the body's hold where it is strongest: at the tongue, the stomach and sleep.**
1. Silence: speak only of what concerns you and benefits you or others (L08 ¶59); "Silence is a door from among the doors of wisdom" [KR76]; the epigraph asks for a tongue that answers to God; Maryam's fast of silence (Q 19:26; L08 ¶74).
2. Hunger: never eat to heaviness, or "you cannot remember God" (L08 ¶72–73); a clause recalls ʿUnwan's rules for eating (Ch 7). *(Story box: "the guest of the mosque": God found no better food for His guest than hunger [kernel p. 77].)*
3. Silence and hunger together open the way to wisdom, "The result of silence is wisdom; the result of wisdom is enlightenment; the result of enlightenment is certainty" [JR16], and to watchfulness of God (*muraqaba*) (L08 ¶46–51, ¶75).
4. The night: the Qur'an asks the Prophet ﷺ to rise for part of it (Q 73:1–4, 73:6), because the day is "prolonged occupation" (L10 ¶19–21); the righteous "used to sleep a little during the night, and at dawns they would plead for forgiveness" (Q 51:17–18).
5. Story (prose): Uways spent one night in rukuʿ and the next in sajda; asked if he tired, he said the night was short, and he wished his whole life were one night passed in one prostration. The body follows the soul, so one at home with God does not tire (L15 ¶41–43).
6. Therefore: at the tongue, the stomach and sleep, the body's hold is loosened.

## §3 Seclusion and remembrance
**Point: Seclusion turns the heart away from distraction, and remembrance turns it towards God.**
1. Seclusion, in Shia teaching, is "seclusion of the heart rather than seclusion of the body" (L10 ¶42).
2. The Qur'an tells the Prophet ﷺ, "dedicate yourself to Him with total dedication" (Q 73:8), yet he stayed among people, so the dedication is of the heart, which can live "as if… in a cave" in a busy city (L10 ¶49–51). *(Word Study box: *tabattul*, total dedication.)*
3. The night prayer of §2 is the traveller's daily seclusion: alone with God for half an hour or more each night (L10 ¶44).
4. Remembrance: "Remember Allah with frequent remembrance" (Q 33:41); the Tasbih of al-Zahraʾ after each prayer is its minimum, and "whoever abides by it will have remembered Almighty Allah frequently" [MAF802] (L10 ¶55).
5. Remembrance is answered: "Remember Me and I will remember you" (Q 2:152; L10 ¶60–63).
6. Its words come from the hadith; a teacher only chooses which suit the traveller, and none are made up (L10 ¶58–59).
7. Therefore: seclusion turns the heart from distraction and remembrance turns it to God; with §2, all five disciplines serve one aim.

## §4 A medicine, measured to the patient
**Point: Each discipline is taken in a dose measured to the person, small and kept up.**
1. §1 said each discipline has a measure; the Qur'an sets one for the night: "recite as much of the Quran as is feasible" (Q 73:20; L10 ¶23).
2. To begin, "half an hour is enough" (L10 ¶23); too much and "we just quit" (¶24); keep one practice for a year (¶25–27). Uways never tired, whereas fifteen minutes of sajda tires us, because we are not yet at home in it (L15 ¶42).
3. The held sources set the same measures: hunger short of "weakness" (`kernel` p. 76); the dawn vigil "to the extent permitted by the wayfarer's constitution" (`kernel` p. 78); devotions "are just like a medicine which may suit some and may not suit others" [LT102].
4. The best worship is the steadiest: "the most consistent, be it ever so small" [LAN55].
5. Seclusion, side by side (D20): the Sheikh prefers the nightly hour and says long retreats and feats can stir unusual powers but are "not desirable" (L10 ¶44–47); the Kernel calls a special seclusion "essential by the masters (*mashayikh*) of the Way" [KR77], and the Lantern says every prophet and successor (*wasi*) chose retreat [LAN50]. They differ on the long retreat and agree on the nightly one.
6. Therefore (section and chapter): five disciplines loosen the body's hold on the soul, each in a measured dose, small and kept up.
