## §1 How to begin
**Point: Every step stands inside the law, suits the traveller, is small enough to keep, and grows gradually; a beginner need not wait for a guide, but tests any guide he follows.**
1. *Where does a beginner start, before any extra practice?* With the obligations (*wajibat*). The law is the first stage of the road and holds to the last breath (Ch 6 §4; Ch 9 §3, in words); the Sheikh's first steps are keeping whatever the shariʿa says (L07 ¶15, already quoted in Ch 7 §1: recall in words). Recommended acts stand on the obligations and never replace them, and the first demand of the law is putting things right with people (Ch 9 §3; L08 ¶39–40, in words).
2. *Of the many recommended acts, which one?* One that suits the person. **New here:** Maliki Tabrizi tells the wayfarer who wants to take on recommended acts to review his state first, "whether he is in low, moderate, and high mood", and then "select the proper program which is suitable for him" [J109] (`journey` p. 109: "in order to find out whether he is in low, moderate, and high mood. After reviewing his situation then he should select the proper program which is suitable for him." (checked)). Chapter Ten's medicine image in a clause: each discipline is taken in a dose measured to the person (Ch 10 §4; its `light` p. 102 quote not repeated).
3. *How much of it?* Little, and kept. Chapter Ten in words: half an hour is enough to begin the night prayer, and going past the limit ends in quitting (L10 ¶23–24, quoted in Ch 10: words); the Lantern asks for worship that is consistent however small (`lantern` p. 55, quoted in Ch 10: words). **New here:** an act that cannot be done in full is not dropped for that reason: "he should perform it, as much as possible" [J161] (`journey` p. 161: "Also, lest he renounces a good deed because he is not able to perform it, instead, he should perform it, as much as possible" (checked)).
4. *If the act is going well, why not more, and faster?* Because rushing breaks the traveller. One clause recalls the young man told to stop his daily Ziyarat ʿAshura (Ch 9 §2, told there in full; not retold). **New here:** the elder wanted "a linear gradual improvement" (L08 ¶26: "so you have to do it with some basis with a linear gradual improvement rather than say for example, fasting suddenly for three months" (checked); Ch 9 does not quote this phrase, grep).
5. *Must I find a guide before I begin?* No. The first steps are the law, and a beginner needs only a teacher who says which practices matter most (Ch 7 §1; L07 ¶15–16, in words). Any teacher is tested as Chapter Seven set out: one who breaks the law is ruled out, and a true one turns people towards God, not towards himself (Ch 7 §4, in words; no quote; the guide-or-charlatan checklist of the old outline stays in Ch 7).
6. Therefore: begin inside the law, with one act that suits you, small enough to keep and growing gradually, beside a tested companion if one is found.

## §2 This week
**Point: Begin with repentance, then take one act of a few minutes from a chapter's Try this, at a time of day already kept, and notice which act the heart is present in.**
1. *What is the very first act?* Repentance, which opens the first stage (Ch 9 §1, in words). The Sheikh's own suggestion for setting out is the prayer of repentance taught for a Sunday of Dhu'l-Qaʿdah, which the traditionists allow at any time (L08 ¶29–30 (checked): "you could do it at any time, but the high time is on a Sunday, in Zilqadah"); he calls it "the first step" (L08 ¶33: "And so this is the first step Sunday inshallah." (checked)). Its form is in Chapter Nine's Try this from the Mafatih [@mafatih, p. 356]: pointer only, not repeated.
2. *Then what, each day?* One act from the table, fixed to a time of day a believer already keeps. *(Reader told: "four such times: the prayer, the Qur'an and the duʿas, the night, and other people"; the grouping is the book's.)* One short paragraph each; acts named and pointed back by chapter, not re-explained; no quotation repeated.
   a. *In and after the prayer:* the prostration dhikr said slowly, believing it (Ch 2; `kernel` pp. 56–57); one prayer at the start of its time, with its time kept free (Ch 6; `forty_hadith` p. 375); Q 59:18–24 after one daily prayer (Ch 4; `forty_hadith` p. 201); the Tasbih of al-Zahraʾ after each obligatory prayer (Ch 10; `mafatih` pp. 802–803); a pause on *Huwa* in Surah al-Ikhlas (Ch 12; L09 ¶66–68); *la hawla wa la quwwata illa billah*, said meaning that every strength is held from Him (Ch 14, planned; L13 ¶64 (checked): "that's the meaning of 'la hawla wala quwwata illa billah' meaning that here is no power and no strength except with Allah").
   b. *With the Qur'an and the duʿas:* Q 30:21–24 slowly, once a day (Ch 1); the Munajat's petitions §82–111 once, slowly, with the English beside the Arabic (Ch 3; `mafatih` p. 245); the Whispered Prayer of the Lovers once (Ch 11; `sahifa` pp. 656–657).
   c. *At night:* the Lantern's intention for sleep (Ch 5; `lantern` pp. 24–25); an account of the day before sleep, asking forgiveness for faults and thanking God if there are none (Ch 13, planned; `kernel` p. 68 (checked): "should it appear to the wayfarer during muhasabah that he has violated his duties, he must seek forgiveness (istighfar) and, in case he hasn't done any, he should thank God"); one of the Musabbihat before sleep (Ch 8; L07 ¶15–16; `mafatih` p. 1036).
   d. *Among people:* ʿIsa's three questions asked of the company one keeps (Ch 7; `lantern` p. 13); in duʿa, others first, by name, then oneself (Ch 16, planned; `journey` p. 83 (checked): "that is firstly we pray for them and then should mention our own wants and desires").
3. *How do I know which act to keep?* The week shows it: notice in which act the heart is most present, and give that act a fixed daily time (Ch 15's Try this, planned; L14 ¶70–71 (checked): "Everyone should find his or her own suitable type of practice and worship", quoted in Ch 15, so words here). This makes §1.2's "suitable" concrete.
4. Therefore: this week, repentance once, then one act of minutes each day, and the act the heart was present in is the one to keep.

## §3 This month
**Point: Keep that one act daily for a month; Shaʿban, Ramadan and Dhu'l-Qaʿdah give it a longer form, within what the traveller can carry.**
1. *Why a month, and not a year at once?* Because the road is taken gradually (§1.4): a month is the middle rung, the week's one act kept at its fixed time, with nothing yet added (the book's arrangement, stated so; L10 ¶23, L08 ¶26 behind it).
2. *Do the months of the year offer anything?* Three months carry longer forms of acts the week began. *(Reader told: "three months"; the grouping is the book's.)*
   a. *Shaʿban:* the whole Munajat, recited as much as one can, the Sheikh's own advice (L01 ¶22 (checked): "I recommend that you recite this in the month of Shaban as much as you can", quoted in Ch 3's Try this, so words here), with the English beside it: the longer form of §2.2b's petitions.
   b. *Ramadan:* a few verses each day, taken as addressed to oneself (Ch 17's Try this, planned; `journey` p. 46 (checked): "must regards them as though all of them have been revealed especially for him", quoted there, so words here). Chapter Ten's opening recalled in a clause: in Ramadan we already keep most of the disciplines without naming them.
   c. *Dhu'l-Qaʿdah:* the prayer of repentance of §2.1 on a Sunday of this month, its best time (L08 ¶30 (checked); `mafatih` p. 356 via Ch 9).
3. *And if the month's programme asks more than I can carry?* **New here:** in Maliki Tabrizi's Ramadan programme even the thousand rakʿas of supererogatory prayer are for the wayfarer whose "spiritual condition and mood allow him" [J109b] (`journey` p. 109: "If the wayfarer's spiritual condition and mood allow him to perform this special prayer what other grace better than this can he ask for?" (checked)); the hundred rakʿas of the Night of Power (`journey` p. 155 (checked), named, not quoted) are of the same kind. Heavier programmes are the ceiling, not the start; §1.2–3 still govern.
4. Therefore: this month, keep one act daily, and let Shaʿban, Ramadan or Dhu'l-Qaʿdah give it its longer form, within what you can carry.

## §4 This year
**Point: Keep one practice for a year and do not stop when the year ends; the keeping itself is asked of God, in the Munajat's words.**
1. *How long before a practice takes root?* A year. The teachers of this road wanted a practice kept "for at least a year or so" (L10 ¶26 (checked): "they wanted them to do it for at least a year or so"; Ch 10 §4 paraphrases this sentence, so either quote these words here or keep Ch 10's paraphrase, not both). One clause recalls Qadi giving beginners the Musabbihat every night for a year before a second instruction (Ch 7 §1 and Ch 8's Try this; not retold).
2. *Which practice?* The one the month kept; or one of the Sheikh's examples recalled from Ch 10: Surah al-Muzzammil each night, about three minutes, or the night prayer begun at half an hour (L10 ¶23, ¶25, in words).
3. *What does the year give?* The opening's answer, in words: kept a year, the night prayer or the asking of forgiveness in the heart places the traveller under the title the verse gives, those who plead for forgiveness at dawns (L10 ¶26–27 (checked): "So if you do one year of Salaatul lail or if you do one year of this Istighfar in the heart then the title would actually is speaking for you"; paraphrase, garbled; Q 3:17 recalled, not re-quoted).
4. *And when the year ends?* The practice goes on: the year "doesn't mean that after one year you stop it", and the traveller gains "more power to ask for more" (L10 ¶27 (checked): "Now it doesn't mean that after one year you stop it… It means that after that, probably you get more power to ask for more or to do to want to do more"; quote only these two fragments, as whole phrases, no splice inside one sentence of ours). Each rung is kept as the next is added, as no stage of the road is dropped (Ch 8 §3, in words).
5. *What if I fail to keep it?* Both parts (TONE 47). Ours is to keep a small act; God's is to accept it. **New here:** "what is important and useful for the servant is that Allah (the Glorious, the Exalted), accepts deed, and not its being small or big" [J161b] (`journey` p. 161 (checked), verbatim including the comma after "Exalted)"; the writer may quote from "what is important" to "small or big").
6. Therefore (section and epilogue): begin inside the law with one small act that suits you; keep it a week, a month and a year, never rushing and never dropping what you have; and ask God for the keeping.
