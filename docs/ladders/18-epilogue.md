## §1 How to begin
**Point: Every step stands inside the law, suits the traveller, is small enough to keep, and grows gradually; a beginner need not wait for a guide, but tests any guide he follows.**
1. *Where does a beginner start, before any extra practice?* With the obligations (*wajibat*). The law kept strictly is the first stage of the road, polishing (Ch 8 §2, Ch 9 §3, in words), and it holds to the last breath, only the reason for keeping it changing (Ch 6 §4: "The law holds to the last breath; only the reason for keeping it changes", recall in words); the Sheikh's first steps are keeping whatever the shariʿa says (L07 ¶15, already quoted in Ch 7 §1: recall in words). Recommended acts stand on the obligations and never replace them, and the first demand of the law is putting things right with people (Ch 9 §3; L08 ¶39–40, in words).
2. *Of the many recommended acts, which one?* One that suits the person. **New here:** Maliki Tabrizi tells the wayfarer who wants to take on recommended acts to review his state first, "whether he is in low, moderate, and high mood", and then "select the proper program which is suitable for him" [J109] (`journey` p. 109: "in order to find out whether he is in low, moderate, and high mood. After reviewing his situation then he should select the proper program which is suitable for him." (checked)). Chapter Ten's medicine image in a clause: each discipline is taken in a dose measured to the person (Ch 10 §4; its `light` p. 102 quote not repeated).
3. *How much of it?* Little, and kept. Chapter Ten in words: half an hour is enough to begin the night prayer, and going past the limit ends in quitting (L10 ¶23–24, quoted in Ch 10: words); the Lantern asks for worship that is consistent however small (`lantern` p. 55, quoted in Ch 10: words); the measure is the person's, not the Prophet's, whose practice needed a personality like his (Ch 10 §4, L10 ¶24–25, in words). **Told story (D47), the Sheikh's account, two or three sentences, prose:** a listener once asked him about the bread of Imam ʿAli's (as) time, so hard that people likened it to rock, which the Imam told others not to copy. The Sheikh's answer: "he's in level 1000 we just want to start level one", and the Imam's words are "instruction for level one" (L08 ¶82–83 (checked); quote the two short phrases only; the questioner's garlic aside and "harder than sometimes" are out). **Held source beside it, new here:** the Imam's own letter to his governor of Basra says it in his voice: "Your leader is satisfied for his share of the world with his two shabby garments and his two pieces of bread", and "You will not be able to do what I do—but help me by practicing restraint and striving for good" [N2.45.2] (`nahj` 2.45.2, p. 626 (checked, both; the dash is the extract's); cite `[@nahj, 2.45.2, p. 626]`). *Work done:* the story answers "how much" with the Imam's own measure for followers: restraint and striving for good, not his bread. **New here:** and an act that cannot be done in full is not dropped for that reason: "he should perform it, as much as possible" [J161] (`journey` p. 161: "Also, lest he renounces a good deed because he is not able to perform it, instead, he should perform it, as much as possible" (checked)).
4. *If the act is going well, why not more, and faster?* Because rushing breaks the traveller. One clause recalls the young man told to stop his daily Ziyarat ʿAshura (Ch 9 §2, told there in full; not retold). **New here:** the elder wanted "a linear gradual improvement" (L08 ¶26: "so you have to do it with some basis with a linear gradual improvement rather than say for example, fasting suddenly for three months" (checked); Ch 9 does not quote this phrase, grep).
5. *Must I find a guide before I begin?* Not the guide who reads the soul's states. Chapter Seven's three accounts differ on how early that guide is needed, and the epilogue does not reopen them (D20, one clause); on all three a beginner can set out: the first steps are the law, and what a beginner needs is a teacher who says which practices to begin with, consulted as one consults anyone learned, as Qadi set the Musabbihat (Ch 7 §1 and §3: the Sheikh, L07 ¶15–16; Tabatabaʾi's general teacher, `kernel` pp. 80–81; all in words). Any teacher is tested as Chapter Seven §4 states it, in words and in this order: one who breaks the law is ruled out; wonders prove nothing; only long company shows how far he has travelled; where he turns people shows whether he is true (Ch 7 §4's point: "A guide who breaks the law is ruled out; a true one is shown by long company and recognised by where he turns people, never by wonders"; no quote; the guide-or-charlatan checklist of the old outline stays in Ch 7).
6. Therefore: begin inside the law, with one act that suits you, small enough to keep and growing gradually, beside a tested companion if one is found.

## §2 This week
**Point: Begin with repentance, then take one act of a few minutes from a chapter's Try this, fixed to the time of day already kept in which the heart is most present.**
1. *What is the very first act?* Repentance, which opens the first stage (Ch 9 §1, in words). The Sheikh's own suggestion for setting out is the prayer of repentance taught for a Sunday of Dhu'l-Qaʿdah, which the traditionists allow at any time (L08 ¶29–30 (checked): "you could do it at any time, but the high time is on a Sunday, in Zilqadah"); he calls it "the first step" (L08 ¶33: "And so this is the first step Sunday inshallah." (checked)). Its form is in Chapter Nine's Try this from the Mafatih [@mafatih, p. 356]: pointer only, not repeated (the count of *istighfar* and the surahs are settled there; do not restate them).
2. *Then one act: but which, and when?* Chapter Fifteen's test answers both at once. Among the acts a believer already keeps, the prayer, the Qur'an, a duʿa, notice in which the heart is most present, and fix the new act to that time, since everyone must find the practice that suits them (Ch 15's Try this; L14 ¶70–71 (checked): "Everyone should find his or her own suitable type of practice and worship", quoted in Ch 15, so words here). This makes §1.2's "suitable" concrete, and it keeps §1.3: one act, not a week of sampling. *(Corrected in the Fable review: the v1 step 3 had the reader try the table's acts and see which the heart was present in; Ch 15's test is of what one already keeps.)*
3. *What acts sit at each of those times?* The table's, grouped by the times of day a believer already keeps. *(Reader told: "four such times: the prayer, the Qur'an and the duʿas, the night, and other people"; the grouping is the book's.)* One short paragraph each; acts named and pointed back by chapter, not re-explained; no quotation repeated; the reader takes one.
   a. *In and after the prayer:* the prostration dhikr said slowly, believing it (Ch 2; `kernel` pp. 56–57); one prayer at the start of its time, with its time kept free (Ch 6; `forty_hadith` p. 375); Q 59:18–24 after one daily prayer (Ch 4; `forty_hadith` p. 201); the Tasbih of al-Zahraʾ after each obligatory prayer (Ch 10; `mafatih` pp. 802–803); a pause on *Huwa* in Surah al-Ikhlas (Ch 12; L09 ¶66–68).
   b. *With the Qur'an and the duʿas:* Q 30:21–24 slowly, once a day (Ch 1); the Munajat's petitions §82–111 once, slowly, with the English beside the Arabic (Ch 3; `mafatih` p. 245); the Whispered Prayer of the Lovers once (Ch 11; `sahifa` pp. 656–657); a few verses read as addressed to oneself (Ch 17's Try this; `journey` p. 46 (checked): "must regards them as though all of them have been revealed especially for him", quoted there, so words here).
   c. *At night:* the Lantern's intention for sleep (Ch 5; `lantern` pp. 24–25); an account of the day before sleep, asking forgiveness for faults and thanking God if there are none (Ch 13's Try this, *muhasaba*; `kernel` p. 68 (checked): "should it appear to the wayfarer during muhasabah that he has violated his duties, he must seek forgiveness (istighfar) and, in case he hasn't done any, he should thank God"; Ch 13 paraphrases it, so words here); one of the Musabbihat before sleep (Ch 8; L07 ¶15–16; `mafatih` p. 1036), and when al-Hadid is the one, a stop at its third verse, "He is the First and the Last, the Manifest and the Hidden", recalled once in the day when something in creation stops you: what you see is His manifestation, but it is not He (Ch 14's Try this; Q 57:3, Qaraʾi via `build/quran.py 57:3` (checked); L13 ¶64–65 (checked): "You see His manifestation but he's not him"; the Sheikh's second half must stay, as Ch 14's review ruled).
   d. *Among people:* ʿIsa's three questions asked of the company one keeps (Ch 7; `lantern` p. 13); in duʿa, others first, by name, then oneself (Ch 16's Try this; `journey` p. 83 (checked): "that is firstly we pray for them and then should mention our own wants and desires").
4. Therefore: this week, repentance once, then one act of minutes each day, at the time the heart is already most present.

## §3 This month
**Point: Keep that one act daily for a month; Shaʿban, Ramadan and Dhu'l-Qaʿdah give it a longer form, within what the traveller can carry.**
1. *Why a month, and not a year at once?* Because the road is taken gradually (§1.4): a month is the middle rung, the week's one act kept at its fixed time, with nothing yet added (the book's arrangement, stated so; L10 ¶23, L08 ¶26 behind it).
2. *Do the months of the year offer anything?* Three months, if one of them falls in the month being kept, carry longer forms of acts the week began. *(Reader told: "three months"; the grouping is the book's; nothing here is added to the one act, only lengthened.)*
   a. *Shaʿban:* the whole Munajat, recited as much as one can, the Sheikh's own advice (L01 ¶22 (checked): "I recommend that you recite this in the month of Shaban as much as you can", quoted in Ch 3's Try this, so words here), with the English beside it: the longer form of §2.3b's petitions.
   b. *Ramadan:* the verses read as addressed to oneself (§2.3b), kept through the month of the Qur'an; Maliki Tabrizi gives that rule in his etiquette of recitation for Ramadan (`journey` p. 46, in its section "Etiquette of Recital of the Holy Qur'an" of the Ramadan programme (checked); Ch 17 quotes it, so words here). Chapter Ten's opening recalled in a clause: in Ramadan we already keep most of the disciplines without naming them.
   c. *Dhu'l-Qaʿdah:* the prayer of repentance of §2.1 on a Sunday of this month, its best time (L08 ¶30 (checked); `mafatih` p. 356 via Ch 9).
3. *And if the month's programme asks more than I can carry?* **New here:** in Maliki Tabrizi's Ramadan programme even the thousand rakʿas of supererogatory prayer are for the wayfarer whose "spiritual condition and mood allow him" [J109b] (`journey` p. 109, in "Some Other Deeds of the Holy Month of Ramadhan": "If the wayfarer's spiritual condition and mood allow him to perform this special prayer what other grace better than this can he ask for?" (checked)); the hundred rakʿas of the Night of Power (`journey` p. 155 (checked): "he should offer 100 rak'ats special prayer for the Night of Power"; named, not quoted) are of the same kind. Heavier programmes are the ceiling, not the start; §1.2–3 still govern. One sentence for each, no more: the paragraph reassures, it does not prescribe.
4. Therefore: this month, keep one act daily, and let Shaʿban, Ramadan or Dhu'l-Qaʿdah give it its longer form, within what you can carry.

## §4 This year
**Point: Keep one practice for a year and do not stop when the year ends; the keeping itself is asked of God, in the Munajat's words.**
1. *How long before a practice takes root?* A year. Chapter Ten already tells, in words, that the teachers of this road asked a student to keep one practice for at least a year (its sentence: "The teachers of this road asked a student to keep one practice for at least a year, such as reciting Surah al-Muzzammil each night, which takes about three minutes", from L10 ¶25–26), so the epilogue recalls it in a clause and does not quote "for at least a year or so"; the opener's "one year is enough" is the epilogue's own line. One clause recalls Qadi giving beginners the Musabbihat every night for a year before a second instruction (Ch 7 §1 and Ch 8's Try this; not retold).
2. *Which practice?* The one the month kept; or one of the Sheikh's examples recalled from Ch 10: Surah al-Muzzammil each night, about three minutes, or the night prayer begun at half an hour (L10 ¶23, ¶25, in words; both sentences are Ch 10's, so a clause each, no figures repeated beyond the two).
3. *What does the year give?* The opening's answer, in words and as the Sheikh's account: kept a year, he says, the night prayer or the asking of forgiveness in the heart brings the traveller under the title the verse gives, those who plead for forgiveness at dawns (L10 ¶26–27 (checked): "So if you do one year of Salaatul lail or if you do one year of this Istighfar in the heart then the title would actually is speaking for you"; paraphrase, garbled; Q 3:17 recalled, not re-quoted; keep "in the hope that" or "he says", TONE 47: purity opens the way without buying the result).
4. *And when the year ends?* The practice goes on: the year "doesn't mean that after one year you stop it", and the traveller gains "more power to ask for more" (L10 ¶27 (checked): "Now it doesn't mean that after one year you stop it… It means that after that, probably you get more power to ask for more or to do to want to do more"; quote only these two fragments, as whole phrases, no splice inside one sentence of ours; "revert back to our lower position" in words if used). Each rung is kept as the next is added, as no stage of the road is dropped (Ch 8 §3, in words).
5. *Does so small an act, kept, count for anything?* Both parts (TONE 47). Ours is to keep a small act; God's is to accept it, and that is what counts. **New here:** "what is important and useful for the servant is that Allah (the Glorious, the Exalted), accepts deed, and not its being small or big" [J161b] (`journey` p. 161 (checked), verbatim including the comma after "Exalted)"; the writer may quote from "what is important" to "small or big"). *(The v1 question, "What if I fail to keep it?", was answered by §1.3's "as much as possible", not by this quotation; reworded so the quotation answers the question asked.)*
6. Therefore (section and epilogue): begin inside the law with one small act that suits you; keep it a week, a month and a year, never rushing and never dropping what you have; and ask God for the keeping.
