# Ch 6. No path outside the law: proof ladder (proposed 2026-10-18)

**Chapter point:** Work this inward can go astray, so it must stay inside the law: the path is the inside of the shariʿa, never a way around it, to the last breath.

**Section chain:**
1. The path and the truth are the inside of the law, so they are reached only through it.
2. Since the truth lies only through the law (1), no wonder or experience stands in for it: what is found without the law goes astray.
3. Since the path is the inside of the law's own acts (1), the inner work begins inside them, with attention in prayer.
4. The law holds to the last breath; only the reason for keeping it changes.
5. Therefore the path is the inside of the shariʿa, never a way around it (1–3), to the last breath (4).

## Opening
1. The Munajat asks for two things together: to remember God and to keep His commands.
2. In our prayers the two come apart: the mind wanders, yet the prayer is valid in law.
3. That gap tempts a conclusion: if the remembering is what matters, the law is an outer layer to leave behind; some traditions taught exactly this.
4. Most masters of the path taught the opposite. The chapter sets out, in its title's words, why there is no path outside the law. *(Words box follows.)*

## §1 Shell, core, and kernel
**Point: The path and the truth are the inside of the law, so they are reached only through it.**
1. The ʿurafaʾ name the depths of the religion with three words. *(Reader told: "the three words".)*
   - a. The law (*shariʿa*) tells us what to do and how to do it validly.
   - b. We keep the law to draw near to God: the way to that nearness is the path (*tariqa*), and what it reaches is the truth (*haqiqa*).
2. The path is not a second set of acts: a jurist would rule our copy of the Prophet's prayer as valid as his, because he judges only the outward form.
3. The two prayers differ in what the jurist cannot see, the inward way each is done: that is the path, and what is seen through it is the truth. So both lie inside the law's form.
4. The Prophet ﷺ put the three in one line: his words are the law, his actions the path, his states the truth.
5. If the path and the truth lie inside the law, the three are one reality at three depths: shell, core and kernel (Hamadani).
6. A single prayer holds all three depths: service, drawing near, arrival.
7. The order runs one way: the law can be kept without the path, but the path has nothing to work on without the law.
8. Therefore each depth is reached only through the one before: the path and the truth only through the law. *(Kubra's ship, sea and pearl.)*

## §2 The inner never cancels the outer
**Point: No wonder or experience stands in for the law.**
1. If the truth is reached only through the law, a wonder-worker who abandons one command of God is lying (Kubra).
2. The wonders may be real; the lie is the claim to know God, and the feat is magic, not *karama*. *(Word Study box: karamat, muʿjiza, sihr.)*
3. The claim fails without the law because of what the law does to the one who keeps it: the law makes good character, and good character is the way into knowing God (Bahari).
4. Each link needs the one before: God is not known in a classroom, only through a purified self, so without the law the chain never starts.
5. Knowledge of God comes to the heart as a light, and it does not descend on a heart that sets the law aside (Khomeini).
6. What comes without the law is easily mistaken for that light: forty days of hunger will show anyone something, and it is taken for God.
7. Even a real light can be mistaken for His. *(The Sheikh's story, told in a prose paragraph: the master and the light of his wudu, L06 ¶67; D47.)*
8. Even the experiences of the true path are only a preliminary: what counts is a lasting change in the self (*Kernel*).
9. Therefore the lasting change comes from keeping the law for a whole life, not from any wonder or experience: nothing stands in for the law.

## §3 The first inner work
**Point: The inner work begins inside the law, with attention in prayer.**
1. That lifelong keeping is where the inner work begins: the path deepens the way we practise the law, starting with knowing what we say at *Allahu akbar*.
2. Knowing what we say needs attention, and attention cannot be held by force.
3. Standing before God ought to hold the mind as a cliff edge would, yet no one can bring that sense of presence to prayer by deciding to.
4. The difficulty is a circle: that sense of presence needs a knowledge that only practice gives, and practice needs the attention that keeps slipping.
5. The circle does not mean the faculty is missing, since a book can hold it; what is missing is the habit of bringing it to prayer (Khomeini).
6. Therefore the inner work is done inside the law, by building that habit a prayer at a time.

## §4 To the last breath
**Point: The law holds to the last breath; only the reason for keeping it changes.**
1. No station on the road releases the traveller from the law; whoever claims the path while leaving it, even slightly, is a hypocrite (*Kernel*).
2. The Prophet ﷺ kept every precept to the last moments of his life, so no station is beyond the law.
3. Once the truth is unveiled, the law is kept more carefully, not less (Khomeini).
4. Worship has stages: for a growing soul it is the means of growth; for a soul that has arrived it is not. What changes on arrival is the reason, not the law.
5. The Prophet ﷺ gave the new reason himself: to be God's grateful servant. *(Hadith quote.)*
6. For a soul that has arrived, worship is thanks, which is what the Munajat at the head of the chapter asks.
7. The road runs past arrival, back among people, and the law binds there too.
8. Therefore there is no path outside the law: the three depths are reached in order, nothing inward stands in for the law, the inner work starts inside it, and after arrival it is kept as thanks.
- Close: "If the law marks out the road, who walks it beside the traveller?"
