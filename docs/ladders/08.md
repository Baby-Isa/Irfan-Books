## The road at a glance (Part Three's framework)
| Stage | What happens | Whose act | Chapter |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Polishing (*tajliya*) | Wake; repent; keep the law strictly, starting with the rights of people. Removes the dark veils our deeds make. | ours | 9 |
| 2. Emptying (*takhliya*) | Clear the heart of everything but God. Tools: the five disciplines (silence, hunger, night vigil, seclusion, remembrance), each in a measured dose. ʿIrfan proper begins here. | ours | 10 |
| 3. Adorning (*tahliya*) | God's gifts to the emptied heart: insight, understanding of the Qur'an, and love, which carries the traveller until even love is left at the door. | God's | 11 |

Rules: the order is fixed (each stage needs the one before); the stages are cumulative (no stage is dropped when the next begins); the first two are ours, the last two God's. Nothing is nested deeper than this: repentance opens Stage 1, and the five disciplines are Stage 2's tools.

**Title:** The stages of the journey (the map; no stage in the title)

**Chapter point:** Since God is already near, the journey covers no distance: it removes veils in four stages, two ours and two God's, each resting on the one before and none ever dropped.

**Section chain:**
1. God is already near, so the journey covers no distance; it removes veils inside us.
2. The veils come off in four stages: we polish the self and empty the heart, then God adorns it and the false self is annihilated.
3. Each stage rests on the one before, and none is ever dropped.

## Opening
1. Epigraph [M80]: "You Near One, Who is not far from one who is away from You." We save for years to travel to Makka, Najaf or Karbala; the Munajat speaks of another journey, to a God who is near even to the one who is away from Him.
2. Duʿa Abu Hamza says what makes us "away": God hides from no one unless their own deeds stand between them and Him [AH46].
3. The chapter sets out the stages of the journey: why it covers no distance, what its four stages are, and why they come in order.

## §1 Nearer than the jugular vein
**Point: God is already near, so the journey covers no distance; it removes veils inside us.**
1. God is "nearer to him than his jugular vein" (Q 50:16), so there is no distance to cross.
2. So the journey (*suluk*) is inward: "We are actually finding ourselves" (L08 ¶4).
3. It removes the veil that blocks the heart's vision, so that God's light can shine in it (L08 ¶9).
4. The veils are of two kinds. *(Reader told.)*
   - a. Dark veils are made by our own deeds, as Abu Hamza says (L09 ¶73).
   - b. Light veils are finer; some are never lifted, even for the Prophet ﷺ, because creation could not stand without them (L09 ¶73–74; L07 ¶59).
5. "There is no hindrance from the other side, the hindrance is inside us" (L10 ¶21).
6. Therefore: the journey covers no distance; it removes veils inside us.

## §2 Four stages: two ours, two God's
**Point: The veils come off in four stages: we polish the self and empty the heart, then God adorns it and the false self is annihilated.**
1. The mystics count four stages to the end of this first journey (L07 ¶11–12). *(Reader told: "one at a time, in order"; one paragraph each.)*
   - a. Polishing (*tajliya*): repentance opens it, and the law, kept strictly, obligatory and recommended, polishes the self (L07 ¶12–13; L08 ¶10). It removes the dark veils of our deeds. *(Ch 9.)*
   - b. Emptying (*takhliya*): the heart is cleared of every "occupation other than Allah" (L07 ¶22). *(Ch 10.)*
   - c. Adorning (*tahliya*): God gives the emptied heart what no effort makes: insight, inspiration, understanding of the Qur'an, and love (L07 ¶10, ¶36). *(Ch 11.)*
   - d. Annihilating (*fanaʾ*): before God's glory the last veil, the self, falls away (L07 ¶57–59), "the last of the levels and the highest of the stations of the path" [IS195]. *(Ch 12.)*
2. The first two stages are the traveller's work; the last two are God's act on the prepared heart (L07 ¶10, ¶36–37). *(Box: "The road at a glance", the table above, without the rules row.)*
3. Therefore: four stages, two ours and two God's.

## §3 In order, and none dropped
**Point: Each stage rests on the one before, and none is ever dropped.**
1. Each stage needs the one before. Without polishing, "you cannot move to the next step" (L07 ¶13); only a purified soul catches God's gifts (¶37); annihilation comes at the height of adorning (¶55).
2. None is dropped: polishing "should accompany a mystic until the end" (L07 ¶21), as Chapter Six showed, so each stage is added to the ones before.
3. Every believer who keeps the law already stands in the first stage; the Sheikh places the beginning of ʿirfan proper at the second (L07 ¶22).
4. Annihilation ends only the first of the four journeys the mystics describe (L09 ¶70–75); the return is Chapter Twelve's and Chapter Sixteen's. One sentence.
5. *(Box: "A philosopher's ladder": Ibn Sina counts the same road differently, will and training, then brief flashes "like flashes of lightning" [LT48], then a lasting presence, then arrival (L15; `light` pp. 45–50). Point: another master's count keeps the same order.)*
6. Therefore (section and chapter): four stages, in order, two ours and two God's, none dropped.
