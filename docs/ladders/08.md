# Ch 8. The stages of the journey: proof ladder (proposed 2026-10-19; Gate A)

**Chapter point:** Since God is already near, the journey covers no distance: it removes veils, in four stages, in order.

**Section chain:**
1. God is already near, so the journey covers no distance; it removes veils inside us.
2. The traveller's part comes first, in two stages: polishing the self with the law, then emptying the heart of everything but God.
3. Once the heart is emptied, God adorns it, and the journey's first stretch ends in passing away; so the four stages come in a fixed order.

## Opening
1. Epigraph [M80]: "You Near One, Who is not far from one who is away from You." We save for years to travel to Makka, Najaf or Karbala; the Munajat speaks of another journey, to a God who is near even to the one who is away from Him. *(Hook; explains the epigraph.)*
2. Duʿa Abu Hamza, said on Ramadan nights, says what makes us "away": the traveller to God arrives easily, and God hides from no one unless their own deeds stand between them and Him [AH46].
3. The chapter sets out, in its title's words, the stages of the journey: why it covers no distance, and why its four stages come in order.

## §1 Nearer than the jugular vein
**Point: God is already near, so the journey covers no distance; it removes veils inside us.**
1. A journey usually crosses a distance, but God is "nearer to him than his jugular vein" (Q 50:16), so there is no distance to cross (callback to Ch 4 in one clause: He is reflected in the soul).
2. So the mystics call the journey (*suluk*) an inward one: "We are not traveling from one place to another place. We are actually finding ourselves" (L08 ¶3–4).
3. What the journey does is remove "the veil that obstruct[s] the vision of the heart", so that God's light can shine in it (L08 ¶9).
4. The veils are of two kinds. *(Reader told: "dark and light, taken in turn".)*
   - a. Dark veils are made by our own deeds, as Abu Hamza's words in the opening say (L09 ¶73; L08 ¶10).
   - b. Light veils are finer and belong to the heart and spirit; some are never lifted, even for the Prophet ﷺ, because creation could not stand without them (L09 ¶73–74; L07 ¶59).
5. Since the veils are on our side, the hindrance is ours: "There is no hindrance from the other side, the hindrance is inside us" (L10 ¶21).
6. Therefore: God is already near; the journey covers no distance and removes veils inside us.

## §2 Our part: polishing and emptying
**Point: The traveller's part comes first, in two stages: polishing the self with the law, then emptying the heart of everything but God.**
1. The mystics count four stages to the end of this first stretch of the journey: polishing (*tajliya*), emptying (*takhliya*), adornment (*tahliya*) and passing away (*fanaʾ*). The first two are the traveller's work; the last two are God's (L07 ¶11–12, ¶36). *(Reader told: "the first two now, the last two in the next section".)*
2. Polishing: the body and the lower self are polished with everything the Prophet ﷺ taught, the obligatory and the recommended kept and the forbidden and disliked avoided, "in the strictest sense" (L07 ¶12–13). It is where repentance belongs (L07 ¶12), which the next chapter takes up.
3. Polishing comes first: "unless you have furbished your soul and your body with whatever sharia said you cannot move to the next step" (L07 ¶13). It never ends either; it "should accompany a mystic until the end" (L07 ¶21), as Chapter Six showed.
4. Emptying: the heart is cleared of every "occupation other than Allah" (L07 ¶22). Keeping the practices is easy, whereas emptying the heart is hard, as anyone knows who has tried to keep other thoughts out of salah (L07 ¶22–24).
5. The test is what occupies the heart, not what it loves: many loves sit inside the love of God, and only what stops us giving ourselves wholly to Him is cleared (L07 ¶23).
6. Emptying goes in steps, which Ibn Sina names: separating, dusting off the traces, leaving, and rejecting; remembrance (*dhikr*) dusts off the traces, and the self is the last thing left behind (L07 ¶22–29).
7. Therefore: our part comes first, in order; the law polishes the self, and then the heart is emptied.

## §3 God's part: adornment and passing away
**Point: Once the heart is emptied, God adorns it, and the journey's first stretch ends in passing away; so the four stages come in a fixed order.**
1. What is emptied is filled: the base qualities give way to their opposites (Amuli: vanity, arrogance, miserliness… exchanged for "gnosis, wisdom, forbearance, humility, generosity, chastity and courage" [IS109]).
2. Adornment is God's act on the soul: visions, inspirations and a deeper understanding of the Qur'an (L07 ¶36).
3. These gifts reach everyone, but only a purified soul catches them: the mystics see what others do not, "not because… they are chosen ones but because they have furbished their souls" (L07 ¶37); Q 6:122, "a light by which he walks among the people".
4. Story (prose): the Tehran preacher who stated a teaching and asked his listeners for the verse or hadith, sure there was one: knowledge given to a purified heart, as the Qur'an rewards those who do good (Q 12:22 cited) (L10 ¶11–15). The Sheikh's rider kept: formal study still matters, and the two support each other (¶15–16).
5. Adornment is not arrival: "this is not a great deal", and a long way remains (L07 ¶39). *(Box: "A philosopher's ladder": Ibn Sina's stations from will to arrival; the first gifts come "like flashes of lightning appearing and instantly vanishing" [LT48]; Tusi counts nine stages [light p. 50]. Point: the masters count differently but agree the gifts come after the training, and briefly at first.)*
6. The first stretch ends in passing away, "the last of the levels and the highest of the stations of the path" [IS195]; what it is, and what it is not, is Chapter Twelve's subject.
7. Therefore (section and chapter): the four stages come in a fixed order, polishing, emptying, adornment and passing away; each rests on the one before, and the first is never left behind.
