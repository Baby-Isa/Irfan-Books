**Question the book answers:** is there more to the religion than the obligations, and if so, how does a believer reach it safely?

## Part One: The Call. Why travel?
1. **Ch 1.** Keeping the obligations saves, but the Qur'an invites more: to perceive God in His signs and love Him for His own sake, and any believer can accept.
2. **Ch 2.** That perceiving is not knowing *about* God; it is a light God gives to a purified heart, so it is reached by purification, which study serves but cannot replace.
3. **Ch 3.** Our own prayers already ask for this sight, love and nearness, so a believer has already begun to ask for what ʿirfan seeks.
- **B1, therefore:** ʿirfan is worth pursuing, and it is already ours to ask for.

## Part Two: Getting ready. What do I need before I set out?
4. **Ch 4.** Since knowing God comes by purifying the heart (2), and God is reflected in the soul, the way to God runs through knowing and purifying our own self.
5. **Ch 5.** That self reaches into worlds deeper than this one, and the form it takes there is made by what we do now, so what it becomes is, by God's grace, in our hands.
6. **Ch 6.** Work this inward can go astray, so it must stay inside the law: the path is the inside of the shariʿa, never a way around it, to the last breath.
7. **Ch 7.** Inside the law, a traveller still needs a companion who has walked the road: a guide under the Imams, tested by the law and by where he turns people, never by wonders.
- **B2, therefore:** the journey runs through the self, inside the law, beside a tested guide.

## Part Three: The Road. How is it walked?
8. **Ch 8.** Since God is already near (4), the journey covers no distance: it removes veils in four stages, two ours and two God's, each resting on the one before and none ever dropped.
9. **Ch 9.** The first stage, polishing, begins with repentance, open to anyone after any sin, and is kept by the law, whose first demand is putting things right with people.
10. **Ch 10.** The second stage, emptying, clears the heart of everything but God, and the five disciplines are its tools, each in a measured dose.
11. **Ch 11.** The third stage, adorning, is God's gift to the emptied heart: knowledge and light, and love, which carries the traveller until even love is left at the door.
12. **Ch 12.** In the fourth stage, love ends in losing the false self before God's glory, and in coming back sober, seeing God in all things while remaining oneself.
- **B3, therefore:** the road is walked in a known order, four stages from repentance to the loss of the false self, two ours and two God's.

## Part Four: Fellow travellers. Who else is on this road, and where did they arrive?
13. **Ch 13.** This road began in the Qur'an, the Prophet and the Imams; its first seekers learned from the Imams and stood below them, so it was not brought in from outside, though some techniques may have been borrowed. _(2026-11-01, awaiting user approval)_ _Approved wording: "…the early seekers walked it in the Imams' orbit, and below them."_
14. **Ch 14.** Of those who reached annihilation, the drunk stayed lost in it and the sober came back and went further; the theory of what they saw is contested, but its defenders show that it neither makes the world God nor puts God inside a man. _(2026-11-01, awaiting user approval)_ _Approved wording: "Those who reached the end divided into the drunk and the sober; the sober went further, and the great theory of what they saw is neither pantheism nor licence."_
15. **Ch 15.** The road later became the Sufi orders; Shia share the orders' source and path, and may follow a shaykh who has walked it, but the authority an order gives to its chain of masters belongs, in Shia teaching, to the Imams. _(2026-11-01, awaiting user approval)_ _Approved wording: "…Shia share the path and its source, but not the orders' authority, which belongs to the Imam."_
- **B4, therefore:** others have walked this road, it is native to Islam, and its authority stays with the Imam.

## Part Five: The Destination. What is the road for?
16. **Ch 16.** The completed journey comes back to people, with God, as the Prophet and the Imams show and, in this book's reading, as far as Karbala; its fullest form belongs first to them. _(2026-11-01, awaiting user approval)_ _Approved wording: "…as the Prophet and the Imams show, to Karbala."_
17. **Ch 17.** Every soul returns to God by giving back what it was given, willingly or not; ʿirfan is making that return willingly and in this life. _(2026-11-01, awaiting user approval)_ _Approved wording: "…ʿirfan is making that return willingly, now."_
- **B5, therefore:** the road is for God, and it brings the traveller back to people and to Him.

## Thesis, therefore
ʿIrfan, the disciplined journey to know and love God, is worth every believer's pursuit (B1): it travels through the self and never leaves the shariʿa (B2); it is walked by a tested and ordered method (B3); it is rooted in the teachings of the Qur'an, the Prophet ﷺ, and the Imams from his Ahl al-Bayt (B4); and it ends not in withdrawal but in return, to people with God, and to God Himself (B5).
