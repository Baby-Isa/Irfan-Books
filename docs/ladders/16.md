**Title:** Back among people

**Subheading (outline):** The road does not end alone with God; it ends back among people, with God, as it did for the Prophet and the Imams.

**Chapter point (book chain line 16, sharpened):** The completed journey comes back to people, with God: losing the self ends only the first of four journeys, the last two return the traveller to creation to guide it, and the Prophet ﷺ and the Imams (as) walked them to the end, as far as Karbala.

**Section chain (each section uses only what the ones before it established):**
1. Losing the self ends only the first of four journeys; in the second, God takes the traveller through His names and gives him back to himself as His servant.
2. The road back has two more journeys: the third returns that servant to creation, seen now as God's names at work, and the fourth keeps him among people, with God, to guide them.
3. The whole return belongs to the Prophet ﷺ and the Imams (as), and the rest of us are called to follow them as far as God wills.
4. Imam al-Husayn (as) shows the return in one life: before God among his people at ʿArafah, and with them and for them at Karbala.

**The reader's chain (chapter level, TONE 47):** Chapter Twelve left the traveller coming back to himself, sober, and called annihilation a beginning. *A beginning of what? Does the friend of God end up alone with Him, lost to his family and his people?* (opening) → *Isn't losing the self the end?* (§1) → *Then where does he go once he has found God?* (§2) → *Who goes the whole way, and can I?* (§3) → *What does the whole way look like in a life we know?* (§4) → *The friends of God come back to people. Where does every soul go back to?* (hand-off to Ch 17).

## §1 A quarter of the way
**Point: Losing the self ends only the first of four journeys; in the second, God takes the traveller through His names and gives him back to himself as His servant.**
1. *Isn't losing the self the end of the road?* The mystics count four journeys to complete it, "the four divine journeys" (L09 ¶70 (checked)): from creation to God (*al-Haqq*, "he's the only reality and reality is haqq", L09 ¶78 (checked)); in God, by God; from God back to creation, by God; and in creation, by God (L09 ¶71–72). Mutahhari gives the same four in the same order [@light, p. 49] (checked: "the journey from creation to God; the journey with God in God; the journey with God from God to creation; and finally, the journey in creation with God"). Mulla Sadra (Ch 2's Who's Who, a clause) named his great book after them (L09 ¶70). *(Table box "The four journeys" here, see Boxes.)* *(Reader told, naturally: the first two go to God and into Him, the last two come back.)*
2. *Where does annihilation fall?* The first journey is the road this book has walked: the dark veils made by our deeds and the luminous veils removed, the self raised from the commanding soul to the heart to the spirit, ending in annihilation (L09 ¶72–74; Ch 8 and 12 recalled in a clause, not retold). Yet "this is just one quarter of the way" (L09 ¶75 (checked)). *(Breather-length; one short paragraph.)*
3. *What does "by God" mean?* The lectures name the second, third and fourth journeys "by haqq" (L09 ¶72) and explain the phrase at the second: from here the traveller "has nothing of himself and is under complete Wilayat of God. It is God who is actually taking them towards this tour" (L09 ¶79 (checked)). Chapter Six noted the phrase (one clause). Gloss *wilaya* in brackets: God's guardianship, His taking charge. The Sheikh's own gloss is a prayer the reader knows, the qunut of the witr prayer, which asks for exactly this (L09 ¶80: "most of you know this dua… take charge of my soul among those who take charge of their soul"): inline quote [SL162] "take care of my affair as those whose affairs You have taken care of" [@duas_salat_al_layl_tahajjud_prayer, §162] (checked; Arabic held: وَ تَوَلَّنِيْ فِيْمَنْ تَوَلَّيْتَ). **Work:** answers the question in words the reader says at night; the Sheikh reads the prayer as asking to be put among those God takes charge of (¶80, paraphrased). **Both sides (TONE 47):** up to annihilation the work was to get the self out of the way ("Up till now, it was just to get rid of him", L15 ¶60 (checked)); from here God carries him. *(Do not say the traveller's own work "ended" with the first journey: Ch 8 already gave adorning and annihilating to God.)*
4. *What does God show him in the second?* God takes him through His names and attributes, one after another (L09 ¶80), as He once taught Adam:
   - Inline verse [Q2:31]: "And He taught Adam the Names, all of them" (Qaraʾi via `build/quran.py 2:31` (checked); ayah block `words="1-4"` or inline). **Work:** answers the question; the verse is the Sheikh's own (¶80).
   - Mutahhari: in the second journey the ʿarif "becomes acquainted with His Qualities and Names and himself becomes adorned with the same" [@light, p. 49] (checked). The lectures call it "more pleasurable and more difficult than this first journey" (L15 ¶59 (checked)).
   - The lectures' own limit, kept in one sentence because it seeds §3: he learns the names one after another "except those names, the knowledge of which exclusively belongs to God or people of higher caliber", and above everyone stands the Prophet ﷺ, "there is no one above him" (L09 ¶80–81 (checked both)).
5. *And at its end?* Annihilation gives way to remaining (*baqaʾ*): the knower comes back from effacement to sobriety, "feels his existence again", and knows himself "a servant of Allah" (L09 ¶81 (checked both)). Chapter Twelve described this coming back; on the lectures' map it stands at the end of the second journey, after the tour of the names (¶81, "this is where the journey ends"). Word it so: Ch 12 followed L09 ¶45–50, which place sobriety "after fanaa" without naming a journey; both are the Sheikh's, and the map adds a place, not a contradiction. Recall in one clause, no re-quoting of Ch 12's Khomeini, Mutahhari or Tabatabaʾi lines.
6. Therefore: losing the self is a quarter of the way, and at the end of the second journey the traveller is himself again, God's servant, still facing God. *(Leaves the question for §2: he has not yet turned back toward people.)*

## §2 The way back
**Point: The road back has two more journeys: the third returns that servant to creation, seen now as God's names at work, and the fourth keeps him among people, with God, to guide them.** *(Worded as a description of the road, not of who walks it, so that it is true under the Sheikh's reservation of the fourth journey in §3.)*
1. *What does the third journey show him?* He comes "back from God to the world under the Wilayah of the Lord" (L09 ¶83 (checked)) and is shown how anything can exist beside God. He is taken through the three worlds Chapter Five named (one clause): the world of the names, the world of angels and spirits, and the material world (*jabarut*, *malakut*, *nasut*, L09 ¶83), and finds "there is no difference. It's all different manifestations of the names of Allah" (L09 ¶83 (checked)). *("Why come back" is step 3's question; do not raise it here.)*
2. *What does that sight give him?* The Sheikh's verse for this station (L09 ¶84–85):
   - Ayah block [Q6:75]: "Thus did We show Abraham the dominions of the heavens and the earth, that he might be of those who possess certitude." (Qaraʾi via `build/quran.py 6:75` (checked); Arabic by build). **Work:** answers; "the outcome of this journey is reassurance about everything" (L09 ¶85 (checked)). Gloss *malakut* = "dominions".
3. *What is the certitude for?* For telling others. He now knows God's attributes and acts and "could inform others about it" (L09 ¶85 (checked)); one small particular: we know God is the Merciful because the Prophet ﷺ told us, "otherwise we couldn't know" (L09 ¶85 (checked)). From here the Sheikh calls him the knower rather than the mystic (L09 ¶86, "I don't call this mystical Aarif anymore" (checked)); his reason is garbled ("mystics usually cannot accomplish something or they do"), so none is given (D39). Mutahhari: "In the third journey, he returns towards the creation, without becoming separated from God, in order to guide the people" [@light, pp. 49–50] (checked; the sentence runs across the page break).
4. *What does the fourth journey add?* In creation, by God, he is shown the reality of things, their effects in this world and the next, and "the way everything returns to Allah" (L09 ¶87–88 (checked)), so he can tell us "the harms and benefits of everything and the causes of felicity or misery" (L09 ¶88 (checked)). Mutahhari: "The fourth journey is amongst the people while still united with God. In this journey the 'arif is with and amongst the people and seeks to guide their affairs so as to lead them towards God" [@light, p. 50] (checked). *(Forward seed for Ch 17: how everything returns to God; no pointer sentence, just the words.)*
5. *Can a man who has seen only God live among people again, and will they not pull him away from Him?* The reader's real worry, answered from both ends of the road.
   - a. At the end of the first journey the trouble runs the other way: he cannot really see them. Tabatabaʾi quotes a gnostic who lived "among the people for 30 years" and "did not see them and did not know anyone but Allah" [@light, p. 63] (checked: "I was among the people for 30 years They were under the impression that I was taking part in all their activities but actually throughout this period I did not see them and did not know anyone but Allah."; Tabatabaʾi gives it for the state of annihilation, seeing only God, which is what the chapter says it shows). The Sheikh says the same of those who finish only the first journey: it is "very difficult for them to mix with the people" (L09 ¶91 (checked)). Ch 14's drunk travellers recalled in a clause only if Ch 14 is written first (there is no ladder 14 yet); otherwise drop the clause.
   - b. After the fourth journey, no: it "would bring him down to the level of ordinary people and they can live with ordinary people and that's why they could guide them" (L09 ¶91 (checked)), and for the Prophet ﷺ mixing with people is the easiest thing, because everything is now seen connected to God (L09 ¶91, paraphrased). So people do not pull him away: he sees God in them. Ch 10's dedication of the heart that can live "as if… in a cave" in a busy city (Ch 10, "Seclusion and remembrance", step 2) recalled in a clause: this is where it leads.
6. *What does that look like from outside?* Outwardly among people, inwardly with God; the epigraph in words: God whispers to them in secret and they work for Him in the open (M111, recalled, not re-quoted). *(Story box: Imam al-Sadiq's robe, below. The Sheikh tells it for hidden renunciation; tying it to the return is the book's reading and is said so.)*
7. Therefore: the third journey brings the traveller back to creation with certitude, and the fourth keeps him among people, with God, guiding them.

## §3 Who completes it
**Point: The whole return belongs to the Prophet ﷺ and the Imams (as), and the rest of us are called to follow them as far as God wills.**
1. *Who goes the whole way?* In the lectures the third journey is open to "some very high caliber mystics" (L09 ¶87 (checked: "some very high caliber mystics could come")); the fourth "belongs to the Prophets only and of course successor" (L09 ¶89 (checked)). ¶87 first says "only reserved for the Prophet"; ¶89 adds the successors, and the chapter follows ¶89 (true on either reading: the Prophet ﷺ first, the Imams (as) after him).
2. *What does it look like in them?* When people asked the Imams (as) about anything, they knew, from an inner knowledge reaching even what "the Sharia" and "the book" do not mention; yet "of course, they follow the Sharia", and they gave their answers "by referring to the book by sharia" (L09 ¶89 (checked all three)). Ch 6 recalled in a clause: the law binds to the last stage. Ch 12's "combines the two realms of Unity and plurality" (`kernel` p. 60) recalled in words only, not re-quoted.
3. *Is holding both worlds, God's and ours, really reserved for the Prophet ﷺ and the Imams (as)?* Side by side (D20, gently). ʿAllama Tabatabaʾi calls the strong wayfarer the one who, while witnessing these states, "also attends to the world of pluralities and who takes care of both the worlds"; the rank is "very sublime and elevated, and access to it very difficult. Perhaps it is exclusive to the prophets and the awliya' and whoever God wishes" [@kernel, p. 20] (checked). He shows both worlds in the Prophet ﷺ himself: inwardly, in a saying of the Prophet that the *Kernel* quotes (name the speaker, TONE 47), "I have with God certain states which are beyond the capacity of any archangel" [@kernel, p. 20] (checked; Arabic [VERIFY: the Kernel's Arabic of this saying is garbled in the extract]), and outwardly:
   - Ayah block [Q18:110, first clause]: "Say, 'I am just a human being like you.'" (Qaraʾi via `build/quran.py 18:110` (checked); `words="1-5"`, قُلۡ إِنَّمَآ أَنَا۠ بَشَرٞ مِّثۡلُكُمۡ). **Work:** answers the question: both worlds at once, in the Prophet's own case; the *Kernel* cites this verse at this point. *(Block count: with N3.133, Q6:75, Q18:110, Q33:21 and A182 the chapter has five blocks; keep Q2:31 inline.)*
4. *Is the road then closed to the rest of us?* Tabatabaʾi separates the office from the station: "the office of prophethood and Imamate is something exclusive, but attainment to the station of absolute tawhid and fana' in the Essence of the One‑which is called wilayah‑is not at all exclusive"; the Prophet's summons "implies the possibility of travelling towards that purpose" [@kernel, p. 20] (checked both), and he cites:
   - Ayah block [Q33:21]: "There is certainly a good exemplar for you in the Apostle of Allah—for those who look forward to Allah and the Last Day, and remember Allah much." (Qaraʾi via `build/quran.py 33:21` (checked)). **Work:** answers the worry; an exemplar is someone to follow. One citation only (`kernel`; `light` p. 64 is the parallel and is not cited, outline "cite one").
5. *So what does it mean for me now?* The shape of the return is asked of every traveller from the start: at all these stages, the lectures say, the traveller stays involved in society, and Islam asks him not to retreat but to do both at the same time (L15 ¶67, paraphrased; garbled middle gone around, D39). *(¶69's Musa and Yusuf cut: it answers a question about place that the chapter does not raise.)*
6. Therefore: both views place the whole return in the Prophet ﷺ and the Imams (as), and both leave the way towards it open as far as God gives (the Sheikh: the third journey to high-calibre mystics; Tabatabaʾi: "whoever God wishes"); the rest of us are called to follow them as far as God wills.

## §4 From ʿArafah to Karbala
**Point: Imam al-Husayn (as) shows the return in one life: before God among his people at ʿArafah, and with them and for them at Karbala.**
*(Karbala is offered as the book's reflection (D16), not as the Sheikh's teaching; say so once, plainly.)*
1. *What does the whole way look like in a life we know?* Ayatullah Khomeini points to one: "The Prophets prayed and said liturgy etc. and it were they who established justice in the world and rose against the wrongdoers. Imam Husayn also did the same thing. Just see his Supplication of the Day of Arafah and ponder over it." [@light, p. 138] (checked). **Work:** raises the next step, and carries the section's "for them" (justice, against the wrongdoers); the chapter does as he says and looks at ʿArafah. *(His "Those who say that spiritual sciences make a man idler, are mistaken" (same page, checked) is cut: §2 step 5 and §3 step 5 have answered that worry twice, and a third time is drag.)*
2. *What does ʿArafah show?* The Mafatih's scene: he left his tent "with submission and reverence", walked slowly, "accompanied by a group of his household, sons, and servants", stopped by Mount ʿArafat facing the Kaʿba, and raised his hands "just like a poor man begging food" [@mafatih, p. 374] (checked all three; the Mafatih says "at the 'Arafat Night", so name no time of day). The book's reading: among his people, and before God as a beggar, at the same moment. Ch 3 already names the duʿa as his; recall in a clause, do not re-introduce.
3. *What did he ask, standing among them?* A main-body line (FRAMEWORK §8.2):
   - Quote block [A182]: "O Allah, (please) make me fear You as if I can see You." [@duas_dua_arafah_imam_husain, §182] (checked; Arabic held: اَللَّهُمَّ ٱجْعَلْنِي أَخْشَاكَ كَأَنِّي أَرَاكَ). **Work:** answers; standing among his household he asks to live as if he saw God (the line asks for fear of God as if seeing Him, *khashya*; do not turn it into a request to see God). (Replaces the outline's §742–743 and §790–791, which sit in the closing section whose attribution is debated; see Changes.)
4. *Where did that lead?* The book's reflection (D16): the Imam who prayed at ʿArafah among his household was martyred on ʿAshura [@mafatih, p. 401] (checked: "On this day, Imam al-Husayn (a.s.) was martyred."), with his companions at Karbala (in words; the same page names "the companions of Imam al-Husayn (a.s.) at Karbala'" (checked)): with his people to the last, and for them, on Khomeini's reading in step 1 ("established justice… rose against the wrongdoers"). *(Mafatih p. 400's "those who were martyred with him" is dropped: its sentence is about a pilgrim's reward, not the martyrdom.)* The ʿArafah prayer's year is not tied to Karbala (true on either reading). Khomeini's line on the same page carries the bridge if needed: through such prayers a man "becomes more active and ever ready to render service to his fellow human beings" [@light, p. 138] (checked; use at most one of this and step 1's second quote).
5. Therefore (section and chapter), stated once: the road does not end alone with God; the completed journey comes back to people, with God, as the Prophet ﷺ and the Imams (as) show, as far as Karbala. Stop on the concrete: on ʿAshura the Mafatih asks those who mourn him to "express sympathy to each other" [@mafatih, p. 401] (checked). No closing aphorism.
- Hand-off (plain): "The friends of God come back to people. Where does every soul go back to?"
