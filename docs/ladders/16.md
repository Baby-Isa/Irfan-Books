**Title:** Back among people

**Subheading (outline):** The road does not end alone with God; it ends back among people, with God, as it did for the Prophet and the Imams.

**Chapter point (book chain line 16, sharpened):** The completed journey comes back to people, with God: losing the self ends only the first of four journeys, the last two return the traveller to creation to guide it, and the Prophet ﷺ and the Imams (as) walked them to the end, as far as Karbala.

**Section chain (each section uses only what the ones before it established):**
1. Losing the self ends only the first of four journeys; in the second, God takes the traveller through His names and gives him back to himself as His servant.
2. The third and fourth journeys bring that servant back to people, with God, to guide them.
3. The whole return belongs to the Prophet ﷺ and the Imams (as), and the rest of us are called to follow them as far as God wills.
4. Imam al-Husayn (as) shows the return in one life: before God among his people at ʿArafah, and with them and for them at Karbala.

**The reader's chain (chapter level, TONE 47):** Chapter Twelve left the traveller coming back to himself, sober, and called annihilation a beginning. *A beginning of what? Does the friend of God end up alone with Him, lost to his family and his people?* (opening) → *Isn't losing the self the end?* (§1) → *Then where does he go once he has found God?* (§2) → *Who goes the whole way, and can I?* (§3) → *What does the whole way look like in a life we know?* (§4) → *The friends of God come back to people. Where does every soul go back to?* (hand-off to Ch 17).

**Epigraph:** [M111] "You whisper to them secretly and they work for You openly." (`light` p. 165 (checked); Arabic held, `duas-munajat-shabaniyah.md` §111 (checked: فَنَاجَيْتَهُ سِرّاً وَعَمِلَ لَكَ جَهْراً)). The chapter recalls it in words at §2 step 5.

## Opening (≤150 words)
1. *Hook, a face (D41: "the first section stays with the Nahj 3.133 scene").* Kumayl ibn Ziyad, whose duʿa many of us hear on Thursday nights (Ch 3 and 11, a clause), tells how Imam ʿAli (as) "took my hand and led me toward the cemetery. When he reached the desert, he sighed deeply" (`nahj` p. 736 (checked); the editor's note: the word "frequently refers in the sources to the cemetery outside Kufa", n. 4, p. 736 (checked)). Of the few who uphold God's proof he says:
   - Quote block [N3.133]: "In this world they live in bodies whose spirits are linked to the highest abode. They are God's vicegerents on his earth, who call to his religion. Ah! Would that I could see them!" (`nahj` p. 738 (checked)). Arabic: [VERIFY: Arabic of Nahj 3.133, "wa sahibu al-dunya bi-abdan…"; `nahj` p. 737 holds it but the extract's Arabic is not clean]. **Work:** raises the chapter's question. These people are with God (spirits linked to the highest abode) and in the world (bodies, calling people) at once.
2. *How does a traveller who lost himself before God (Ch 12) become one of these?* Chapter Twelve left him come back to himself, sober; Chapter Fifteen ended on the Imam, whose company the traveller still needs after arrival (recall in words; Ch 15 ends on `kernel` p. 81). This chapter follows the traveller the rest of the way, back among people, with God, as the Prophet ﷺ and the Imams (as) show, as far as Karbala. *(Title words early, TONE 39.)*

## §1 A quarter of the way
**Point: Losing the self ends only the first of four journeys; in the second, God takes the traveller through His names and gives him back to himself as His servant.**
1. *Isn't losing the self the end of the road?* The mystics count four journeys to complete it, "the four divine journeys" (L09 ¶70 (checked)): from creation to God (*al-Haqq*, "he's the only reality and reality is haqq", L09 ¶78 (checked)); in God, by God; from God back to creation, by God; and in creation, by God (L09 ¶71–72). Mutahhari gives the same four in the same order [@light, p. 49] (checked: "the journey from creation to God; the journey with God in God; the journey with God from God to creation; and finally, the journey in creation with God"). Mulla Sadra (Ch 2's Who's Who, a clause) named his great book after them (L09 ¶70). *(Table box "The four journeys" here, see Boxes.)* *(Reader told, naturally: the first two go to God and into Him, the last two come back.)*
2. *Where does annihilation fall?* The first journey is the road this book has walked: the dark veils made by our deeds and the luminous veils removed, the self raised from the commanding soul to the heart to the spirit, ending in annihilation (L09 ¶72–74; Ch 8 and 12 recalled in a clause, not retold). Yet "this is just one quarter of the way" (L09 ¶75 (checked)). *(Breather-length; one short paragraph.)*
3. *What does "by God" mean in the last three?* Chapter Six noted the phrase (recall); the lectures explain it: from here the traveller "has nothing of himself and is under complete Wilayat of God. It is God who is actually taking them towards this tour" (L09 ¶79 (checked)). Gloss *wilaya* in brackets: God's guardianship. **Both sides (TONE 47):** the traveller's own work ended with the first journey; what follows is God's.
4. *What does God show him in the second?* God takes him through His names and attributes, one after another (L09 ¶80), as He once taught Adam:
   - Inline verse [Q2:31]: "And He taught Adam the Names, all of them" (Qaraʾi via `build/quran.py 2:31` (checked); ayah block `words="1-4"` or inline). **Work:** answers the question; the verse is the Sheikh's own (¶80).
   - Mutahhari: in the second journey the ʿarif "becomes acquainted with His Qualities and Names and himself becomes adorned with the same" [@light, p. 49] (checked). The lectures call it "more pleasurable and more difficult than this first journey" (L15 ¶59 (checked)).
5. *And at its end?* Annihilation gives way to remaining (*baqaʾ*): the knower comes back from effacement to sobriety, "feels his existence again", and knows himself "a servant of Allah" (L09 ¶81 (checked both)). This is the sober return of Chapter Twelve, now placed on the map: it closes the second journey (recall in one clause, no re-quoting of Ch 12's Khomeini, Mutahhari or Tabatabaʾi lines).
6. Therefore: losing the self is a quarter of the way, and at the end of the second journey the traveller is himself again, God's servant, still facing God. *(Leaves the question for §2: he has not yet turned back toward people.)*

## §2 The way back
**Point: The third and fourth journeys bring that servant back to people, with God, to guide them.**
1. *Having found God, why come back, and what does he see on the way?* In the third journey he comes "back from God to the world under the Wilayah of the Lord" (L09 ¶83 (checked)) and is shown how anything can exist beside God. The heavens, the unseen and the material world turn out to be one thing seen at different depths: "there is no difference. It's all different manifestations of the names of Allah" (L09 ¶83 (checked)).
2. *What does that sight give him?* The Sheikh's verse for this station (L09 ¶84–85):
   - Ayah block [Q6:75]: "Thus did We show Abraham the dominions of the heavens and the earth, that he might be of those who possess certitude." (Qaraʾi via `build/quran.py 6:75` (checked); Arabic by build). **Work:** answers; "the outcome of this journey is reassurance about everything" (L09 ¶85 (checked)). Gloss *malakut* = "dominions".
3. *What is the certitude for?* For telling others. He now knows God's attributes and acts and "could inform others about it" (L09 ¶85 (checked)); one small particular: we know God is the Merciful because the Prophet ﷺ told us, "otherwise we couldn't know" (L09 ¶85 (checked)). The Sheikh no longer calls him a mystic, since mystics often cannot do this (L09 ¶86, paraphrased; "I don't call this mystical Aarif anymore" (checked) if quoted). Mutahhari: "In the third journey, he returns towards the creation, without becoming separated from God, in order to guide the people" [@light, pp. 49–50] (checked; the sentence runs across the page break).
4. *What does the fourth journey add?* In creation, by God, he is shown the reality of things, their effects in this world and the next, and "the way everything returns to Allah" (L09 ¶87–88 (checked)), so he can tell us "the harms and benefits of everything and the causes of felicity or misery" (L09 ¶88 (checked)). Mutahhari: "The fourth journey is amongst the people while still united with God. In this journey the 'arif is with and amongst the people and seeks to guide their affairs so as to lead them towards God" [@light, p. 50] (checked). *(Forward seed for Ch 17: how everything returns to God; no pointer sentence, just the words.)*
5. *Doesn't living among people pull him away from God?* The reader's real worry, answered from both ends of the road.
   - a. At the end of the first journey, yes: a gnostic Tabatabaʾi quotes lived "among the people for 30 years" and "did not see them and did not know anyone but Allah" [@light, p. 63] (checked: "I was among the people for 30 years They were under the impression that I was taking part in all their activities but actually throughout this period I did not see them and did not know anyone but Allah."). Those who finish only the first journey find it "very difficult for them to mix with the people" (L09 ¶91 (checked)). Ch 14's drunk travellers recalled in a clause (check Ch 14's wording at prose stage).
   - b. In the fourth, no: it "would bring him down to the level of ordinary people and they can live with ordinary people and that's why they could guide them" (L09 ¶91 (checked)), and for the Prophet ﷺ mixing with people is easy, because everything is seen connected to God (L09 ¶91, paraphrased). Ch 10's seclusion of the heart in a busy city (L10 ¶49–51) recalled in a clause: this is where it leads.
6. *What does that look like from outside?* Outwardly among people, inwardly with God; the epigraph in words: God whispers to them in secret and they work for Him in the open (M111, recalled, not re-quoted). *(Story box: Imam al-Sadiq's robe, below. The Sheikh tells it for hidden renunciation; tying it to the return is the book's reading and is said so.)*
7. Therefore: the third journey brings the traveller back to creation with certitude, and the fourth keeps him among people, with God, guiding them.

## §3 Who completes it
**Point: The whole return belongs to the Prophet ﷺ and the Imams (as), and the rest of us are called to follow them as far as God wills.**
1. *Who goes the whole way?* In the lectures the third journey is open to "some very high caliber mystics" (L09 ¶87 (checked: "some very high caliber mystics could come")); the fourth "belongs to the Prophets only and of course successor" (L09 ¶89 (checked)). ¶87 first says "only reserved for the Prophet"; ¶89 adds the successors, and the chapter follows ¶89 (true on either reading: the Prophet ﷺ first, the Imams (as) after him).
2. *What does it look like in them?* When people asked the Imams (as) about anything, they knew, from an inner knowledge reaching even what "the Sharia" and "the book" do not mention; yet "of course, they follow the Sharia", and they gave their answers "by referring to the book by sharia" (L09 ¶89 (checked all three)). Ch 6 recalled in a clause: the law binds to the last stage. Ch 12's "combines the two realms of Unity and plurality" (`kernel` p. 60) recalled in words only, not re-quoted.
3. *Side by side (D20, gently): how does ʿAllama Tabatabaʾi see it?* He calls the strong wayfarer the one who, while witnessing these states, "also attends to the world of pluralities and who takes care of both the worlds"; the rank is "very sublime and elevated, and access to it very difficult. Perhaps it is exclusive to the prophets and the awliya' and whoever God wishes" [@kernel, p. 20] (checked). He shows it in the Prophet ﷺ himself, inwardly "I have with God certain states which are beyond the capacity of any archangel" [@kernel, p. 20] (checked; Arabic [VERIFY: the Kernel's Arabic of this saying is garbled in the extract]) and outwardly:
   - Ayah block [Q18:110, first clause]: "Say, 'I am just a human being like you.'" (Qaraʾi via `build/quran.py 18:110` (checked); `words="1-5"`, قُلۡ إِنَّمَآ أَنَا۠ بَشَرٞ مِّثۡلُكُمۡ). **Work:** answers step 2's question in the Prophet's own case; the Kernel cites this verse at this point.
4. *Is the road then closed to the rest of us?* Tabatabaʾi separates the office from the station: "the office of prophethood and Imamate is something exclusive, but attainment to the station of absolute tawhid and fana' in the Essence of the One‑which is called wilayah‑is not at all exclusive"; the Prophet's summons "implies the possibility of travelling towards that purpose" [@kernel, p. 20] (checked both), and he cites:
   - Ayah block [Q33:21]: "There is certainly a good exemplar for you in the Apostle of Allah—for those who look forward to Allah and the Last Day, and remember Allah much." (Qaraʾi via `build/quran.py 33:21` (checked)). **Work:** answers the worry; an exemplar is someone to follow. One citation only (`kernel`; `light` p. 64 is the parallel and is not cited, outline "cite one").
5. *So what do the two views share, and what does it mean for me now?* Both place the full return in the Prophet ﷺ and the Imams (as), and both leave the way towards it open as far as God gives (the Sheikh: the third journey to high-calibre mystics; Tabatabaʾi: "whoever God wishes"). Its shape is asked of every traveller from the start: at all these stages, the lectures say, the traveller stays involved in society, and Islam asks him not to retreat but to do both at the same time (L15 ¶67, paraphrased; garbled middle gone around, D39). *(Optional, one clause: nor does the place decide it, as Musa in Pharaoh's house and Yusuf in the house of the ʿAziz show, L15 ¶69; keep only if the paragraph stays under ~110 words.)*
6. Therefore: the whole return is the Prophet's ﷺ and the Imams' (as), and the rest of us are called to follow them as far as God wills.

## §4 From ʿArafah to Karbala
**Point: Imam al-Husayn (as) shows the return in one life: before God among his people at ʿArafah, and with them and for them at Karbala.**
*(Karbala is offered as the book's reflection (D16), not as the Sheikh's teaching; say so once, plainly.)*
1. *Doesn't all this inner work make a person withdraw from the world?* Ayatullah Khomeini answers: "Those who say that spiritual sciences make a man idler, are mistaken"; "The Prophets prayed and said liturgy etc. and it were they who established justice in the world and rose against the wrongdoers. Imam Husayn also did the same thing. Just see his Supplication of the Day of Arafah and ponder over it." [@light, p. 138] (checked both). **Work:** raises the next step; the chapter does as he says and looks at ʿArafah.
2. *What does ʿArafah show?* The Mafatih's scene: he left his tent "with submission and reverence", walked slowly, "accompanied by a group of his household, sons, and servants", stopped by Mount ʿArafat facing the Kaʿba, and raised his hands "just like a poor man begging food" [@mafatih, p. 374] (checked all three). The book's reading: among his people, and before God as a beggar, at the same moment. Ch 3 already names the duʿa as his; recall in a clause, do not re-introduce.
3. *What did he ask, standing among them?* A main-body line (FRAMEWORK §8.2):
   - Quote block [A182]: "O Allah, (please) make me fear You as if I can see You." [@duas_dua_arafah_imam_husain, §182] (checked; Arabic held: اَللَّهُمَّ ٱجْعَلْنِي أَخْشَاكَ كَأَنِّي أَرَاكَ). **Work:** answers; his heart, among his family, is on seeing God. (Replaces the outline's §742–743 and §790–791, which sit in the closing section whose attribution is debated; see Changes.)
4. *Where did that lead?* The book's reflection (D16): the Imam who prayed at ʿArafah among his household was martyred on ʿAshura [@mafatih, p. 401] (checked: "On this day, Imam al-Husayn (a.s.) was martyred."), together with "those who were martyred with him" [@mafatih, p. 400] (checked): among his people and for them, with God, to the last. The ʿArafah prayer's year is not tied to Karbala (true on either reading). Khomeini's line on the same page carries the bridge if needed: through such prayers a man "becomes more active and ever ready to render service to his fellow human beings" [@light, p. 138] (checked; use at most one of this and step 1's second quote).
5. Therefore (section and chapter), stated once: the road does not end alone with God; the completed journey comes back to people, with God, as the Prophet ﷺ and the Imams (as) show, as far as Karbala. Stop on the concrete: on ʿAshura the Mafatih asks those who mourn him to "express sympathy to each other" [@mafatih, p. 401] (checked). No closing aphorism.
- Hand-off (plain): "The friends of God come back to people. Where does every soul go back to?"

## Boxes (≤3)
