## Book thesis
ʿIrfan, the disciplined journey to know and love God, is worth every believer's pursuit: it travels through the self and never leaves the shariʿa; it is walked by a tested and ordered method; it is rooted in the Quran and the Imams; and it ends not in withdrawal but in return, to people with God, and to God Himself.

## Book points (each answered by one Part)
The Parts follow the reader's journey: why travel → getting ready → travelling → who else you meet on the road → the destination.

| # | Book point | Question it answers | Part |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B1** | ʿIrfan is worth pursuing. The Quran invites more than the minimum, which is knowing God and not only knowing about Him; that knowing is reached by purifying the heart; and our own prayers already ask for it. | Why travel? | One: The Call (Ch 1–3) |
| **B2** | The journey runs through the self, which is deeper than it looks; the shariʿa is its boundary; and a tested guide is its companion. | What do I need before I set out? | Two: Getting ready (Ch 4–7) |
| **B3** | It is walked in a known order: veils removed in stages, beginning with repentance and character, trained by measured disciplines, carried by love, and reaching the loss of the false self. | How is it walked? | Three: The Road (Ch 8–12) |
| **B4** | Others have walked it. It is rooted in the Quran and the Imams; some travellers lost their footing in ecstasy while the sober reached the end; and Shia masters keep the path without a Sufi institution of authority, because authority belongs to the Imam. | Who else is on this road, and where did they arrive? | Four: Fellow travellers (Ch 13–15) |
| **B5** | Not the road but God: the friends of God return to people, with God; every soul returns to God; ʿirfan is making that return willingly, now. | What is the road for? | Five: The Destination (Ch 16–17) |

**MECE check:**
- B1 covers motive (why go).
- B2 covers preparation: the terrain (the self), the boundary (the shariʿa) and the companion (the guide).
- B3 covers method (how the road is walked).
- B4 covers company: where the road came from, who walked it and where they arrived, and how Shia relate to the Sufi orders.
- B5 covers outcome.

Why → what to bring → how → who else → to what end. The guide moves into B2 because a traveller needs one before setting out, not after. Lineage and legitimacy (old B3) follow the Road because the reader is by then invested and wants to know who walked it before. Safety stays in B2 (the boundary) and is not repeated. The thesis names all five.

**Tone arc (user, D41):**
- **Part Three** builds to the crescendo in Ch 12, where the reader feels it.
- **Part Four** is the analytical step back: calm, clear, curious.
- **Part Five** brings the emotion home: faces, Karbala, the willing return, the Munajat's last petitions.

## Chapter points, in sequence
_Each chapter states one conclusion, proved by its numbered points, in an order where each depends only on earlier ones. **[Bn]** marks the book point served; every point in a Part serves that Part's B. Divider lines and chapter epigraphs: see Threads._

### Part One: The Call (B1). Why travel?
_Divider (Munajat §73–74): "My Lord, I was not strong enough to move away from Your disobedience, except when You awakened me to Your love." [@light, p. 164]_

**Ch 1. More than the minimum.** (old 1; approved text; retitled D41)
- **Conclusion:** ʿirfan is worth pursuing: it is the training of the soul to perceive and love God, open to every believer, and sought for God alone.
- 1.1 The minimum saves, but the Quran openly invites more: its signs are "for a people who reflect", and most people pass them by. [B1] L01 ¶5–12, ¶16; Q 30:21–24, 12:105
- 1.2 The failure is in us, not in the signs: our inner faculties are not working, and what is broken can be repaired. [B1] L01 ¶9–12; L02 ¶3–5; Q 7:179
- 1.3 The repaired state is real and worth having: the Quran describes those who perceive God's signs and promises them nearness, "a good life" and freedom from fear and grief, and that rank is open to ordinary believers. [B1] L01 ¶15–16, ¶25–27; L02 ¶7–19; Q 4:69, 10:62, 16:97
- 1.4 Its only legitimate aim is God Himself: seeking powers is not ʿirfan. [B1] L01 ¶3–4, ¶13, ¶32 (side by side: Tabatabaʾi, `light` p. 101)
- → If the aim is to know and love God, what kind of knowing is that?

**Ch 2. Knowing about God is not knowing Him.** (old 3, "Two kinds of knowing"; moved up by the Ch 2/3 swap, D41)
- **Conclusion:** knowing God differs in kind from knowing about God, and it is reached by purification, which study serves but cannot replace.
- 2.1 Acquired knowledge (ʿilm) defines and limits; knowledge by presence (maʿrifah) arrives. [B1] L02 ¶29–32, ¶36–37 (old 3.1)
- 2.2 Reason analyses attributes and stops short of any essence, above all God's. [B1] L03 ¶25–28, ¶33, ¶35, ¶38 (old 3.2)
- 2.3 Knowledge of God is light that God places in a purified heart, passed heart to heart, not mind to mind. [B1] L03 ¶10–11, ¶18–21 (added: `forty_hadith` p. 331; `lantern` p. 10) (old 3.3)
- 2.4 Therefore the path is purification (suluk), not thought alone; study prepares the ground but cannot replace it. [B1] L03 ¶19–20, ¶47–49 (added, side by side: `forty_hadith` p. 403; `kernel` p. 56; `light` p. 42) (old 3.4)
- → Purification sounds far off. Is there anywhere an ordinary believer already stands on this path?

**Ch 3. You already know these words.** (old 2; moved down to close Part One on hope, D41)
- **Conclusion:** the goal of ʿirfan is already on the believer's lips.
- 3.1 The Imams' supplications ask for exactly what the mystic seeks: severance from all else, illumined hearts, love, and nearness to God. [B1] L01 ¶18–23, ¶27–30 (Munajat Shaʿbaniyyah, Abu Hamza, ʿArafah, the Lovers) (old 2.1)
- 3.2 They ask from the seeker's posture: need and humility, a pupil reaching above his level. [B1] L01 ¶17, ¶22 (old 2.2)
- 3.3 These are the prayers of the Mafatih, recited on nights ordinary believers keep, so the goal is neither foreign nor reserved for an elite. [B1] L01 ¶17, ¶22 (added: Mafatih pp. 129, 243, 245, 282, 374; `light` p. 156; side by side: `journey` p. 43, `light` p. 157 on who is fit) (old 2.3; no L06 or L10 anchors, D38)
- → These prayers keep asking God to work on the heart and the self. What is this self?

### Part Two: Getting ready (B2). What do I need before I set out?
_Divider (Munajat §5–7): "I have come running to You and am standing before You imploring You in humility." [@light, p. 163]_

**Ch 4. Know yourself, know your Lord.** (old 4)
- **Conclusion:** the way to God runs through the self.
- 4.1 The soul is the instrument of all knowing, so it is the first thing to understand. [B2] L04 ¶3–9, ¶12 (Q 91:7–10)
- 4.2 The Quran ties forgetting God to forgetting oneself (Q 59:19), and the tradition ties knowing oneself to knowing one's Lord. [B2] L04 ¶12–23 (attribution per `lantern` p. 9; side by side: Imam ʿAli, `journey` p. 125; D15; added: `self_knowledge` p. 8)
- 4.3 "Meeting" God through the self means presence and nearness, never comprehension of His essence; since He is already near, the journey removes veils rather than covering distance (seeded here, developed in 8.1; D26). [B2] L04 ¶24–60; L07 ¶4; L08 ¶3–9 (added: `forty_hadith` p. 398)
- → If the self is the door, how deep does it go?

**Ch 5. The soul and its worlds.** (old 5)
- **Conclusion:** the self is deeper than it appears, and its final shape is in our hands.
- 5.1 Reality has depths, not distances: the material, the imaginal and the intellectual worlds are one reality at three levels. [B2] L04 ¶61–96; L05 ¶3–8 (kept to about a page)
- 5.2 The soul mirrors them and moves among them, in sleep, in death and in contemplation. [B2] L04 ¶61–96; L05 ¶16–31 (added: `lantern` p. 24; `self_knowledge` p. 76)
- 5.3 Deeds shape an inner form, deformed or luminous, that we will meet. [B2] L05 ¶10–15 (added: `forty_hadith` p. 41; `self_knowledge` pp. 21, 77)
- 5.4 The self has states (commanding, reproaching, at peace), and it can move between them. [B2] L05 ¶47–57 (side by side: `inner_secrets` p. 192 counts four)
- → What keeps so inward a work from going astray?

**Ch 6. No path outside the law.** (old 6, "The ship, the sea and the pearl")
- **Conclusion:** there is no inner path outside the shariʿa.
- 6.1 Law, path and truth are one reality at three depths: shell, core and kernel. [B2] L06 ¶7–15, ¶23–25, ¶43–48 (added: `inner_secrets` pp. 32, 48; `light` p. 15)
- 6.2 The inner never cancels the outer: wonders without the shariʿa are magic, not karamat, and attention in prayer is the first inner work. [B2] L06 ¶17–22, ¶27–33, ¶65–70 (added: `forty_hadith` p. 35; `light` p. 135; `inner_secrets` p. 42). L06 ¶20 ("probably") is left out; the rule is given as Kubra's, with Khomeini beside it (D39).
- 6.3 Even the end of the road is lived under the law: it finishes among people, not above them. [B2] L09 ¶70–72 (a sentence or two of preview; the four journeys are walked in Ch 16, FRAMEWORK §8.1) (added: `kernel` p. 30)
- → The law is the boundary. Who walks beside the traveller?

**Ch 7. Finding a guide.** (old 10, plus a short form of old 9.3)
- **Conclusion:** a guide helps, and must be tested.
- 7.1 The road has turns a traveller cannot read alone, but scholars weigh the need for a guide differently, from "none at the start" to "never without one". [B2] L14 ¶23–25; L07 ¶14–15 (old 10.2; side by side: `light` pp. 10, 100–102; `inner_secrets` p. 192)
- 7.2 For Shia, authority belongs to the Imam, so there is no chain of authority beside him and no blind obedience; a trusted teacher, yes. [B2] L14 ¶6–8, ¶22 (short form of old 9.3; added: `kernel` p. 80; the full comparison with the orders is Ch 15)
- 7.3 There are guides to be found: a Shia chain of teachers who have walked the path runs within living memory. [B2] L14 ¶22–25; L06 ¶21–25; L10 ¶44 (old 10.1; added: the chain as given in `kernel` pp. 87–89 and `light` pp. 105–106; `forty_hadith` p. 8; `journey` p. 8). The v2.1 `[QUERY: which Hamadani]` is resolved: Akhund Mulla Husayn-Quli Hamadani (this chapter) and Mulla ʿAbd al-Samad Hamadani (Ch 6) are kept apart.
- 7.4 A true guide is known by his adherence to the shariʿa and his indifference to power and followers; feats prove nothing. [B2] L06 ¶17–22; L14 ¶22–25 (old 10.3; added: `kernel` pp. 81, 83; `light` pp. 100–102; `forty_hadith` pp. 102–103, 465; `journey` pp. 131–133; `lantern` p. 13). The chapter ends here, on the test, not on the scholars' disagreement.
- → Once a guide is found, what does the road itself look like?

### Part Three: The Road (B3). How is it walked?
_Divider (Munajat §94): "My Lord, inspire me with a passionate love of remembering You so that I may keep on remembering You." [@light, p. 165]_

**Ch 8. The stages of the journey.** (old 11, "The stages of the first journey")
- **Conclusion:** the road is inward, and it has an order.
- 8.1 Travel means removing veils, not covering distance: God is already nearer than the jugular vein. [B3] L07 ¶4; L08 ¶3–10; L09 ¶73; L10 ¶21 (full treatment; seeded in 4.3, D26; Q 50:16)
- 8.2 The first journey has four stages in order: polishing by the law, emptying the heart, adornment by God, and annihilation; none is skipped. [B3] L07 ¶11–13, ¶21–29, ¶36–39; L09 ¶2–3 (the triad's names are the lecturer's framing; substance in `inner_secrets` pp. 108–110, 195)
- _Box, not a point (old 11.3):_ Ibn Sina's ladder of stations maps the same ascent, step by step, to arrival. L15 ¶3–59 (analogies are the lecturer's; `light` pp. 45–50)
- → Where on it does one set foot first?

**Ch 9. Setting out.** (old 12)
- **Conclusion:** the first steps are repentance and character.
- 9.1 The road begins with repentance, and anyone can begin, after any sin. [B3] L08 ¶10–12, ¶16–18 (added: `lantern` pp. 48, 59; `forty_hadith` p. 249)
- 9.2 Repentance is paced: no vows one cannot keep, no rush, and sooner is easier. [B3] L08 ¶13–15, ¶19–22, ¶26–27 (side by side: `light` pp. 89, 126–127; `forty_hadith` pp. 118, 250 press harder)
- 9.3 Good conduct toward people is the first condition of the inner work. [B3] L08 ¶39–42 (added: `inner_secrets` p. 108; `light` p. 70; Nahj 1.191)
- → What trains the soul once it has turned?

**Ch 10. The five disciplines.** (old 13)
- **Conclusion:** five disciplines train the soul, in measured doses.
- 10.1 Five disciplines loosen the soul's bondage to the body, each with a Quranic root: silence, hunger, night prayer, seclusion and remembrance. [B3] L08 ¶43–88; L10 ¶6–65, ¶11–12, ¶40 (added: `lantern` p. 56; `kernel` p. 76; `journey` p. 16; Mafatih p. 802)
- 10.2 Each is a medicine, dosed and never forced; on seclusion the lecturer prefers the private hour where the masters prescribe retreat. [B3] L08 ¶55–58; L10 ¶23–27, ¶41–54 (side by side: `kernel` pp. 77–78; `lantern` pp. 50, 55; `light` p. 102)
- → What keeps a traveller going when discipline alone runs dry?

**Ch 11. Love, the engine.** (old 14)
- **Conclusion:** love drives the path.
- 11.1 Fear and renunciation start the journey; love carries it, and the early schools lacked it. [B3] L11 ¶61–63; L12 ¶38–61; L15 ¶11–13 (added: `lantern` p. 58; `kernel` p. 70). The early schools' history is Ch 13's, which calls back here.
- 11.2 The Imams voice this love: the worship of the free, not of merchants or slaves; the Lovers' prayer. [B3] L12 ¶38–42, ¶60; L01 ¶27–31 (Nahj 3.222 set beside al-Sadiq's love version, `light` p. 94, because the Nahj saying speaks of thanks, FRAMEWORK §8.7; Sahifa pp. 656–657; Kumayl §123–124; `lantern` p. 88)
- 11.3 Even love is finally a means, left behind at the Beloved. [B3] L12 ¶59–60 (Rabiʿa in a line) (added: `light` pp. 45, 95)
- → Where does love carry the traveller in the end?

**Ch 12. Losing the self, finding God.** (old 15) **The crescendo of the book.**
- **Conclusion:** the first journey ends in seeing only God while remaining oneself.
- 12.1 Fana is the loss of false self-perception before God's glory, not becoming God and not ceasing to exist. [B3] L07 ¶41–63; L09 ¶3–9; L15 ¶55–57, ¶60 (added: `inner_secrets` p. 115; `light` pp. 49, 63)
- 12.2 It has degrees: in action, in attributes, in essence. [B3] L09 ¶52–69 (added: `inner_secrets` pp. 113–117; `light` pp. 107–108). The climax of the chapter and of Part Three.
- 12.3 It has dangers (captivation, ecstatic utterance, licence), stated as principles; the healthy outcome is baqa, separation after union, which ends the first journey and begins the second. [B3] L09 ¶20–50; L15 ¶59 (added: `forty_hadith` pp. 515–516; `inner_secrets` p. 116; `light` p. 102). The chapter ends on Imam ʿAli's "I did not see a thing without beholding God…" (`forty_hadith` p. 516). Bayazid and Junayd are named in a line, with a pointer to Ch 14.
- → Others have walked this far. Where did the road come from, and who walked it first?

### Part Four: Fellow travellers (B4). Who else is on this road, and where did they arrive?
_Divider (Munajat §109–110, in the duas.org wording, FRAMEWORK §8.3): "My Lord, appoint me of those who responded to You when You called them, and at whom You glanced so he swoons before Your Majesty." [duas §109–110; cf. @light, p. 165]_
_Tone: the analytical step back after the crescendo. Calm, clear, curious._

**Ch 13. Where it comes from.** (old 7, "Roots and the first seekers")
- **Conclusion:** ʿirfan is native to Islam, and the Imams stand above those who first walked it.
- 13.1 Its roots are the Quran, the Prophet's companions and the Imams. [B4] L11 ¶34–46 (Schimmel is the lecturer's reference; `light` pp. 4–5, 19, 24–25 carry the evidence) (old 7.2)
- 13.2 The early seekers developed fear, renunciation and love, in that order (the order the reader met in Ch 11), often in the Imams' orbit, and the Imams stand above them, not among them. [B4] L11 ¶58–63, ¶78–90; L12 ¶3–37, ¶53–56 (old 7.3; added: `light` pp. 5, 24–25)
- 13.3 So it was not brought in from outside: it did not come from monks, Neoplatonists or yogis; at most, techniques were borrowed. [B4] L11 ¶3–24 (added: `light` pp. 16, 20, 21) (old 7.1). Placed last and kept short, so the chapter opens on people (FRAMEWORK §9; FRAMEWORK §8.4: opens on a companion, not Kumayl).
- → Where did those travellers arrive?

**Ch 14. Drunk and sober.** (old 8)
- **Conclusion:** of those who reached fana, the sober went furthest, and the tradition's great theory, though contested, is neither pantheism nor licence.
- 14.1 The knowers divided over the state beyond the self, Bayazid's intoxication or Junayd's sobriety, and the tradition ranked the return higher. [B4] L13 ¶3–34 (added: `light` pp. 25, 27, 57; `kernel` p. 73)
- 14.2 Ibn ʿArabi gave the tradition its theory, the unity of being, which the lecturer holds is grasped by experience, not argument. [B4] L13 ¶35–67 (added: `light` pp. 34–35, 144; Nahj 1.1.1)
- 14.3 That theory drew Shia critics; the held defenders answer that it is neither pantheism nor licence, and the lecturer himself declines to judge. [B4] L13 ¶43, ¶47–55; L01 ¶6 (side by side: `light` p. 34 against `inner_secrets` p. 99 on Ibn ʿArabi's Seal of the Saints; added: `forty_hadith` pp. 345, 402; `light` pp. 13–14, 144; `inner_secrets` pp. 98–99). The critics' concerns are reported as the defenders give them (FRAMEWORK §9); no critical source is held.
- → How did the road become orders, and where do Shia stand?

**Ch 15. Sufis and Shia.** (old 9)
- **Conclusion:** Shia ʿirfan shares the path and its source with Sufism, but not its institution of authority.
- 15.1 A Sufi order is an institution: a founder's method, a chain back to the Prophet, and total submission to a shaykh. [B4] L14 ¶3–21, ¶27–30, ¶36, ¶56–59, ¶62–78 (old 9.1; added: `light` pp. 5–6, 22–23, 31, 34, 40)
- 15.2 Nearly every chain runs back to ʿAli, so the Sufi and the Shia seeker share a source. [B4] L14 ¶8–9, ¶36–37, ¶79 (old 9.2; added: `kernel` p. 87; `light` pp. 6, 25; `inner_secrets` p. 13)
- 15.3 Set techniques are secondary, because each soul receives God's grace in its own shape; what a seeker needs is a trustworthy guide (Ch 7), not a lineage. [B4] L14 ¶59–71 (old 9.4, now before authority; added: `inner_secrets` p. 195; `kernel` p. 87)
- 15.4 For Shia, authority belongs to the Imam, so there is no chain of authority beside him and no blind obedience, though Shia masters have used teacher and pledge. [B4] L14 ¶6–8, ¶22–25 (old 9.3; side by side: `inner_secrets` pp. 8, 14, 192; `kernel` pp. 60, 64, 81, 88; `forty_hadith` p. 8; `light` pp. 40–41). The chapter ends here, on the Imam (FRAMEWORK §8.10).
- → If authority belongs to the Imam, what does the completed journey look like in him?

### Part Five: The Destination (B5). What is the road for?
_Divider (Munajat §128–131): "My Lord, let me be attached to the Light of Your Majestic Glory, so that I may know You alone, be away from others, and have a heart fearful of You and an eye watchful of You." [@light, p. 166]_
_Tone: the emotion comes back and is brought home._

**Ch 16. Back among people.** (old 16, "The four journeys and the perfect human"; ends on Karbala)
- **Conclusion:** the path's completion is a return to people, with God, shown fully in the Prophet and the Imams.
- 16.1 After fana the road goes on and the traveller returns to creation: the four journeys, with their Quranic anchors. [B5] L09 ¶70–88, ¶91 (old 16.1; the Nahj 3.133 scene of a face comes first, FRAMEWORK §9; added: `light` pp. 49–50; `inner_secrets` p. 140)
- 16.2 How far ordinary believers share in that return is left open: the lecturer reserves the completed fourth journey for the Prophet and the Imams; ʿAllama Tabatabaʾi, gently set beside him, holds that wilaya is not exclusive and others reach it in their measure; both place the full return in the Prophet and the Imams. [B5] L09 ¶87–89 (old 16.3; side by side: `kernel` p. 20; `light` p. 64; D25). The `[QUERY]` of v2.1 is resolved: L09 ¶89 is followed over ¶87 (D39).
- 16.3 The Prophet and the Imams embody this return; Karbala is offered to the reader as a reflection on it, through Duʿa ʿArafah. [B5] L09 ¶87–89 (old 16.2; added, D16: `light` p. 138; `duas_dua_arafah_imam_husain` §742–743, §790–791; Mafatih pp. 374, 401). Where a line is given as Imam Husayn's, the main body of the prayer is used (FRAMEWORK §8.2). The chapter ends on this reflection.
- → The friends of God return to people. Where does every soul return?

**Ch 17. The return to God.** (old 17, "Reading the Quran with the heart, and the return to God")
- **Conclusion:** every soul returns to God, and ʿirfan is making that return willingly, now.
- 17.1 The Quran may be read from the inside, on condition that the inner meaning agrees with the outer and never cancels it. [B5] L16 ¶3–17 (the opener scene; added: `light` p. 110; `journey` p. 54)
- 17.2 The return reverses creation: existence, knowledge, power and will were given, and are surrendered in reverse order to be re-given by God (Tusi). [B5] L16 ¶17–32 (`forty_hadith` p. 517 holds the Tusi text)
- 17.3 Hell is the same return, refused: the same surrender undergone against one's will. [B5] L16 ¶39–55; L08 ¶5–8 (Kubra's involuntary and voluntary journeys, FRAMEWORK §8.6) (side by side, as agreement: `forty_hadith` pp. 137, 365 keep the fire literal as well)
- _Closing section [conclusion], no new point:_ the willing return, now. Hell comes before it.
- → So how does one begin, this week?

**Prologue** (approved, edited for v3, D41): states the thesis and B1–B5 as a map of the book; says what ʿirfan is not (magic, a cult, a replacement for fiqh or taqlid); answers "isn't this Sufism?" in a line (Ch 15 answers it fully); opens with the Munajat's §105–107 petition.
**Epilogue: First steps.** Turns B3 into first steps (this week, this month, this year), gathering every "Try this" with `journey` for the graded plan, and closes the B5 circle with Munajat §124 ("make me one of those who always remember You").
