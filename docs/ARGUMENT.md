# The book's argument (pyramid). Canonical: D23

_Status: draft v2 (Fable-amended), awaiting user approval._

**Hierarchy:**
- **Book thesis**, supported by
- **Book points B1–B5** (one per Part), each supported by
- **Chapter points N.1, N.2…** (each tagged with the B it serves), each supported by
- **Section headings** (each tagged with the chapter point(s) it serves; set in `docs/briefs/NN-case.md`).

Every level answers the one above. Nothing at a lower level is without a parent.

**Marks used below:**
- `L03 ¶12–15` after a point: the lecture passage that carries it (see `notes/IrfanNN.md`).
- **(added: key)**: the point, or the clause it follows, comes from the wider literature, not the lectures (D20). It stays visible so the lecture is seen to carry the majority.
- **(side by side)**: the lecturer and a held source differ; both are shown, gently (D20).
- **→**: the one question the next chapter answers. The last chapter of a Part hands off to the next Part.

## Book thesis
ʿIrfan, the disciplined journey to know and love God, is worth every believer's pursuit: it travels through the self and never leaves the shariʿa; it is rooted in the Quran and the Imams; it is walked by a tested and ordered method; and it ends not in withdrawal but in return, to people with God, and to God Himself.

## Book points (each answered by one Part)
| # | Book point | Question it answers | Part |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B1** | ʿIrfan is worth pursuing. It answers the Quran's open invitation to know God, its goal is already on the believer's lips, and it is sought for God alone. | Why bother? | One: The Call (Ch 1–3) |
| **B2** | It travels through the self, which is deeper than it appears, and it never leaves the shariʿa. | What is travelled through, and what keeps it safe? | Two: The Terrain (Ch 4–6) |
| **B3** | It is ours. It is rooted in the Quran and the Imams, it was carried (and sometimes bent) by Sufism, and Shia masters hold its sober core without a Sufi institution of authority. | Is it ours? | Three: The Travellers (Ch 7–9) |
| **B4** | It is walked by a known method: a tested guide, ordered stages, repentance and character, measured disciplines, love, and the loss of the false self. | How is it walked? | Four: The Road (Ch 10–15) |
| **B5** | It ends in return: the saints to people, with God; and every soul to God, willingly or not. | Where does it end? | Five: The Return (Ch 16–17) |

**MECE check:**
- B1 covers motive.
- B2 covers the terrain: the model of the self and the boundary (shariʿa) around the work.
- B3 covers lineage and legitimacy.
- B4 covers method.
- B5 covers outcome.

Why → what → whose → how → to what end. Safety belongs to B2 (the boundary) and is not repeated in B3. The thesis names all five.

## Chapter points, in sequence
_Each chapter states one conclusion, proved by its numbered points, in an order where each depends only on earlier ones. **[Bn]** marks the book point served; every point in a Part serves that Part's B._

### Part One: The Call (B1)
_Epigraph: "My Lord, I was not strong enough to move away from Your disobedience, except when You awakened me to Your love." [@light, p. 164; duas §73–74] [VERIFY against Mafatih p. 244]_

**Ch 1. Seeking, not powers.**
- **Conclusion:** ʿirfan is worth pursuing: it is the training of the soul to perceive and love God, open to every believer, and sought for God alone.
- 1.1 The minimum saves, but the Quran openly invites more: its signs are "for a people who reflect", and most people pass them by. [B1] L01 ¶5–12, ¶16; Q 30:21–24, 12:105
- 1.2 The failure is in us, not in the signs: our inner faculties are not working, and what is broken can be repaired. [B1] L01 ¶9–12; L02 ¶3–5; Q 7:179
- 1.3 The repaired state is real and worth having: the Quran describes those who perceive God's signs and promises them nearness, "a good life" and freedom from fear and grief, and that rank is open to ordinary believers. [B1] L01 ¶15–16, ¶25–27; L02 ¶7–19; Q 4:69, 10:62, 16:97
- 1.4 Its only legitimate aim is God Himself: seeking powers is not ʿirfan. [B1] L01 ¶3–4, ¶13, ¶32 (side by side: Tabatabaʾi, `light` p. 101)
- → If the aim is to know and love God, what does that longing sound like?

**Ch 2. You already know these words.**
- **Conclusion:** the goal of ʿirfan is already on the believer's lips.
- 2.1 The Imams' supplications ask for exactly what the mystic seeks: severance from all else, illumined hearts, love, and reaching God. [B1] L01 ¶17–31 (Munajat Shaʿbaniyyah, the Lovers)
- 2.2 They ask from the seeker's posture: need and humility, a pupil reaching above his level. [B1] L01 ¶17–31 ("a primary-school boy reading university books")
- 2.3 These are the prayers of the Mafatih, recited in Shaʿban, Ramadan and at ʿArafah, so the goal is neither foreign nor reserved for an elite. [B1] L01 ¶17; L06; L10 (added: `light` pp. 20, 138; `journey` pp. 42–43)
- → The prayers ask for knowledge of God. What kind of knowing is that?

**Ch 3. Two kinds of knowing.**
- **Conclusion:** knowing God differs in kind from knowing about God, and it is reached by purification, which study serves but cannot replace.
- 3.1 Acquired knowledge (ʿilm) defines and limits; knowledge by presence (maʿrifah) arrives. [B1] L02 ¶29–38; L03 ¶3–10
- 3.2 Reason analyses attributes and stops short of any essence, above all God's. [B1] L03 ¶23–28, ¶35
- 3.3 Knowledge of God is light that God places in a purified heart, passed heart to heart, not mind to mind. [B1] L03 ¶10, ¶18–21 (added: `lantern` Ch 6 p. 10)
- 3.4 Therefore the path is purification (suluk), not thought alone; study prepares the ground but cannot replace it. [B1] L03 ¶18–21 (added: `forty_hadith` p. 403; `light` p. 146; `kernel` pp. 56–57, side by side on the role of study)
- → If that knowing happens in the soul, what is the soul?

### Part Two: The Terrain (B2)
_Epigraph: "Enlighten the eyes of our hearts with the light of their looking at You, to the extent that they penetrate the veils of light and reach the Source of Grandeur." [@light, p. 165; duas §105–107] [VERIFY against Mafatih p. 244]_

**Ch 4. Know yourself, know your Lord.**
- **Conclusion:** the way to God runs through the self.
- 4.1 The soul is the instrument of all knowing, so it is the first thing to understand. [B2] L04 ¶3–9
- 4.2 The Quran ties forgetting God to forgetting oneself (Q 59:19), and the tradition ties knowing oneself to knowing one's Lord. [B2] L04 ¶12–23 (attribution per `lantern` Ch 5; D15)
- 4.3 "Meeting" God through the self means presence and nearness, never comprehension of His essence. [B2] L04 ¶24–60
- → If the self is the door, how deep does it go?

**Ch 5. The soul and its worlds.**
- **Conclusion:** the self is deeper than it appears, and its final shape is in our hands.
- 5.1 Reality has depths, not distances: the material, the imaginal and the intellectual worlds are one reality at three levels. [B2] L04 ¶61–96; L05 ¶3–8
- 5.2 The soul mirrors them and moves among them, in sleep, in death and in contemplation. [B2] L04 ¶61–96; L05 ¶16–31
- 5.3 Deeds shape an inner form, deformed or luminous, that we will meet. [B2] L05 ¶10–15
- 5.4 The self has states (commanding, reproaching, at peace), and it can move between them. [B2] L05 ¶47–57 (states, per `inner_secrets` p. 192)
- → What keeps so inward a work from going astray?

**Ch 6. The ship, the sea and the pearl.**
- **Conclusion:** there is no inner path outside the shariʿa.
- 6.1 Law, path and truth are one reality at three depths: shell, core and kernel. [B2] L06 ¶7–15, ¶23–25, ¶43–48
- 6.2 The inner never cancels the outer: wonders without the shariʿa are magic, not karamat, and attention in prayer is the first inner work. [B2] L06 ¶17–33, ¶65–70 (added: `forty_hadith` p. 35; `[QUERY]` on L06 ¶20 "probably")
- 6.3 Even the end of the road is lived under the law: the four journeys, in outline, finish among people, not above them. [B2] L09 ¶70–93 (preview; full treatment in Ch 16)
- → Who has actually walked this route?

### Part Three: The Travellers (B3)
_Epigraph: "My Lord, appoint me of those who responded to You when You called them, and at whom You glanced so he swoons before Your Majesty." [duas §109–110; cf. @light, p. 165] [VERIFY against Mafatih p. 244]_

**Ch 7. Roots and the first seekers.**
- **Conclusion:** ʿirfan is native to Islam.
- 7.1 It did not come from monks, Neoplatonists or yogis; at most, techniques were borrowed. [B3] L11 ¶3–24 (added: `light` p. 16)
- 7.2 Its roots are the Quran, the Prophet's companions and the Imams. [B3] L11 ¶34–61 (Schimmel is the lecturer's reference; `light` pp. 4–5, 19 carry the evidence)
- 7.3 The early seekers developed fear, renunciation and love, often in the Imams' orbit, and the Imams stand above them, not among them. [B3] L11 ¶61–92; L12 ¶3–37
- → Where did the tradition divide as it matured?

**Ch 8. Drunk and sober.**
- **Conclusion:** the tradition has a sober, defensible core.
- 8.1 The knowers divided over the state beyond the self, Bayazid's intoxication or Junayd's sobriety, and the tradition ranked sobriety higher. [B3] L13 ¶3–34 (added: `light` p. 57)
- 8.2 Ibn ʿArabi gave the tradition its theory, the unity of being, which the lecturer holds is grasped by experience, not argument. [B3] L13 ¶35–67
- 8.3 That theory drew Shia critics; its defenders answer that it is neither pantheism nor licence, and the lecturer himself declines to judge. [B3] L13 ¶55; L01 ¶6 (added: `forty_hadith` pp. 345, 402; `light` pp. 13–14, 144; `inner_secrets` pp. 98–99)
- → How did the path become orders, and do Shia belong to them?

**Ch 9. Sufis and Shia.**
- **Conclusion:** Shia ʿirfan shares the path with Sufism but not its institution of authority.
- 9.1 A Sufi order is an institution: a founder, a chain back to the Prophet, and total submission to a shaykh. [B3] L14 ¶3–21
- 9.2 Nearly every chain runs back to ʿAli, so the Sufi and the Shia seeker share a source. [B3] L14 ¶36–37 (added: `kernel` pp. 87–88)
- 9.3 For Shia, authority belongs to the Imam, so there is no chain of authority beside him and no blind obedience, though Shia masters have used teacher and pledge. [B3] L14 ¶6–8, ¶22–25 (side by side: `inner_secrets` pp. 8, 14, 192; `kernel` pp. 60, 64, 88)
- 9.4 Set techniques are secondary, because each soul receives God's grace in its own shape; what a seeker needs is a trustworthy guide, not a lineage. [B3] L14 ¶65–71
- → If not an order, then with whom does a Shia seeker begin?

### Part Four: The Road (B4)
_Epigraph: "My Lord, inspire me with a passionate love of remembering You, so that I may keep on remembering You." [@light, p. 165; duas §94] [VERIFY against Mafatih p. 244]_

**Ch 10. Finding a guide.**
- **Conclusion:** a guide helps, and must be tested.
- 10.1 There are guides to be found: a Shia chain of teachers who have walked the path runs within living memory. [B4] L14 ¶22–25; L06 ¶21–25; L10 ¶41–54 (added: the chain as given in `kernel` pp. 87–89 and `light` pp. 105–106; `[QUERY: which Hamadani]`)
- 10.2 Scholars weigh the need for a guide differently, from "none at the start" to "never without one", and all judge him by the Prophet's and the Imams' example. [B4] L07 ¶14 (side by side: `kernel` p. 80; `inner_secrets` p. 192; `light` pp. 100–102)
- 10.3 A true guide is known by his adherence to the shariʿa and his indifference to power and followers; feats prove nothing. [B4] L06 ¶17–22; L14 ¶22–25 (added: `light` pp. 100–102; `forty_hadith` pp. 102–103, 465; `journey` pp. 131–133)
- → Once a guide is found, what does the road itself look like?

**Ch 11. The stages of the first journey.**
- **Conclusion:** the road is inward, and it has an order.
- 11.1 Travel means removing veils, not covering distance: God is already nearer than the jugular vein. [B4] L07 ¶4; L08 ¶3–9
- 11.2 The first journey has four stages in order: polishing by the law, emptying the heart, adornment by God, and annihilation; none is skipped. [B4] L07 ¶11–12; L09 ¶2–3 (the triad's names are the lecturer's framing; substance in `inner_secrets` pp. 108–110)
- 11.3 Ibn Sina's ladder of stations maps the same ascent, step by step, to arrival. [B4] L15 ¶3–59 (analogies are the lecturer's)
- → Where on it does one set foot first?

**Ch 12. Setting out.**
- **Conclusion:** the first steps are repentance and character.
- 12.1 The road begins with repentance, and anyone can begin, after any sin. [B4] L08 ¶10–28
- 12.2 Repentance is paced: no vows one cannot keep, no rush, and sooner is easier. [B4] L08 ¶13–28 (side by side: `light` pp. 89, 126–127; `forty_hadith` pp. 118, 250 press harder)
- 12.3 Good conduct toward people is the first condition of the inner work. [B4] L08 ¶39–42
- → What trains the soul once it has turned?

**Ch 13. The five disciplines.**
- **Conclusion:** five disciplines train the soul, in measured doses.
- 13.1 Five disciplines loosen the soul's bondage to the body, each with a Quranic root: silence, hunger, night prayer, seclusion and remembrance. [B4] L08 ¶43–88; L10 ¶6–65, ¶11–12, ¶40 (added: `lantern` Ch 54 p. 56)
- 13.2 Each is a medicine, dosed and never forced; on seclusion the lecturer prefers the private hour where the masters prescribe retreat. [B4] L08 ¶80–88; L10 ¶41–54 (side by side: `kernel` p. 77; `lantern` Ch 45)
- → What keeps a traveller going when discipline alone runs dry?

**Ch 14. Love, the engine.**
- **Conclusion:** love drives the path.
- 14.1 Fear and renunciation start the journey; love carries it, and the early schools lacked it. [B4] L12 ¶38–61; L11 ¶61–63
- 14.2 The Imams voice this love: the worship of the free, not of merchants or slaves; the Lovers' prayer; ʿArafah. [B4] L12 ¶60; L01 ¶27–31; L08 ¶43–51 (Nahj 3.222; Sahifa pp. 656–657)
- 14.3 Even love is finally a means, left behind at the Beloved. [B4] (added: `kernel` pp. 72–73; `light` p. 95)
- → Where does love carry the traveller in the end?

**Ch 15. Losing the self, finding God.**
- **Conclusion:** the first journey ends in seeing only God while remaining oneself.
- 15.1 Fana is the loss of false self-perception before God's glory, not becoming God and not ceasing to exist. [B4] L07 ¶41–63; L09 ¶3–8; L15 ¶55–57 (added: `inner_secrets` p. 115)
- 15.2 It has degrees: in action, in attributes, in essence. [B4] L09 ¶52–69 (added: `inner_secrets` pp. 113–117)
- 15.3 It has dangers (captivation, ecstatic utterance, licence), and the healthy outcome is baqa, separation after union, which ends the first journey and begins the second. [B4] L09 ¶20–50; L15 ¶59
- → And after that?

### Part Five: The Return (B5)
_Epigraph: "You whisper to them secretly and they work for You openly." [@light, p. 165; duas §111] [VERIFY against Mafatih p. 244]_

**Ch 16. The four journeys and the perfect human.**
- **Conclusion:** the path's completion is a return to people, with God, shown fully in the Prophet and the Imams.
- 16.1 After fana the traveller returns to creation: the four journeys, with their Quranic anchors. [B5] L09 ¶70–93 (added: `light` p. 50; `inner_secrets` p. 140)
- 16.2 The Prophet and the Imams embody this return; Karbala is offered to the reader as a reflection on it, through Duʿa ʿArafah. [B5] L09 ¶87–89 (added, D16: `light` p. 138; `duas_dua_arafah_imam_husain` §736–741)
- 16.3 How far others follow them is debated: the lecturer reserves the fourth journey for the Prophet and the Imams; Tabatabaʾi holds that wilaya is not exclusive. [B5] L09 ¶87–89 (side by side: `kernel` p. 20; `light` p. 64; `[QUERY]`)
- → The saints return to people. Where does every soul return?

**Ch 17. Reading the Quran with the heart, and the return to God.**
- **Conclusion:** every soul returns to God, and ʿirfan is making that return willingly, now.
- 17.1 The Quran may be read from the inside, on condition that the inner meaning agrees with the outer and never cancels it. [B5] L16 ¶3–17
- 17.2 The return reverses creation: existence, knowledge, power and will were given, and are surrendered in reverse order to be re-given by God (Tusi). [B5] L16 ¶17–32 (`forty_hadith` p. 517 holds the Tusi text)
- 17.3 Hell is the same return, refused: the same surrender undergone against one's will. [B5] L16 ¶39–55 (side by side: `forty_hadith` pp. 137, 365 keep the fire literal as well)
- → So how does one begin, this week?

**Prologue:** states the thesis and B1–B5 as a map of the book; says what ʿirfan is not (magic, a cult, a replacement for fiqh or taqlid); opens with the Munajat's first petition.
**Epilogue:** turns B4 into first steps (this week, this month, this year) and closes the B5 circle with the Munajat's final petition.
