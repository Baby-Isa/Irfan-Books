## Book thesis
ʿIrfan, the disciplined journey to know and love God, is the inner life of Islam itself. It is worth pursuing, it is rooted in the Quran and the Ahl al-Bayt, it is safe within the shariʿa, and it is open to every believer.

## Book points (each answered by one Part)
| # | Book point | Question it answers | Part |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B1** | ʿIrfan is worth pursuing. It is the fullest answer to the Quran's invitation to know God, it is open to all, and it is sought for God alone. | Why bother? | One: The Call (Ch 1–3) |
| **B2** | It rests on a coherent picture of the human being. The soul is the door to God, reality and the self have depths, and the shariʿa is the vehicle that is never left behind. | What is there to travel through? | Two: The Terrain (Ch 4–6) |
| **B3** | It is authentically Islamic and Shia. It is rooted in the Quran and the Imams, it was carried (and sometimes bent) by Sufism, and it is held by a living Shia tradition. | Is it ours, and is it sound? | Three: The Travellers (Ch 7–9) |
| **B4** | It is walked by a known and safe method: a tested guide, ordered stages, repentance, measured disciplines, love, and the loss of the false self. | How is it walked? | Four: The Road (Ch 10–15) |
| **B5** | It ends not in withdrawal but in return: to people, with God, and finally to God Himself. | Where does it end? | Five: The Return (Ch 16–17) |

**MECE check:**
- B1 covers motive.
- B2 covers the model, the theory.
- B3 covers legitimacy and lineage.
- B4 covers method.
- B5 covers outcome.

Why → what → whose → how → to what end. There is no overlap, and nothing a newcomer needs is missing.

## Chapter points, in sequence
_Each chapter states one conclusion, proved by its numbered points. **[Bn]** marks the book point served. **→** shows what the next chapter picks up._

### Part One: The Call (B1)

**Ch 1. Seeking, not powers.**
- **Conclusion:** ʿirfan is worth pursuing. It is the training of the soul to perceive and love God, open to every believer, and sought for God alone.
- 1.1 The minimum saves, but the Quran openly invites more: its signs are "for a people who reflect", and most people pass them by. [B1]
- 1.2 The failure is in us, not in the signs. Our inner faculties are not working, and what is broken can be repaired. [B1]
- 1.3 The repaired state is real, and it is worth having. The Quran describes those who perceive God's signs, and promises them nearness, "a good life" and freedom from fear and grief. That rank is open to ordinary believers. [B1]
- 1.4 Its only legitimate aim is God Himself. Seeking powers is not ʿirfan. [B1]
- → If the aim is to know and love God, what does that longing actually sound like?

**Ch 2. You already know these words.**
- **Conclusion:** the goal of ʿirfan is already on the believer's lips.
- 2.1 The Imams' supplications state the mystic's aim exactly: severance from all else, illumined hearts, love, and reaching God (Munajat Shaʿbaniyyah, Lovers, ʿArafah). [B1]
- 2.2 They also model the posture of the seeker: humility and need, a pupil reaching above his level. [B1]
- 2.3 So ʿirfan is not foreign. Its first textbook is already in the Mafatih. [B1, B3]
- → The prayers ask for *knowledge* of God. What kind of knowing is that?

**Ch 3. Two kinds of knowing.**
- **Conclusion:** knowing God is a different kind of knowledge from knowing about God, and it is reached by purification, which study serves but cannot replace.
- 3.1 Acquired knowledge (ʿilm) differs from knowledge by presence (maʿrifah). [B1]
- 3.2 Reason reaches its limit before God's essence. [B1]
- 3.3 Knowledge of God is light given to a purified heart. [B1]
- 3.4 Therefore the path is purification. Study serves it but does not replace it. [B1]
- → Purification of what? The heart, the soul. What is that?

### Part Two: The Terrain (B2)

**Ch 4. Know yourself, know your Lord.**
- **Conclusion:** the way to God runs through the self.
- 4.1 The soul is the instrument of that knowing. [B2]
- 4.2 The Quran ties forgetting God to forgetting oneself (Q 59:19). The tradition ties knowing oneself to knowing one's Lord. [B2]
- 4.3 This knowledge has limits. It is presence and nearness, never grasping God's essence. [B2]
- → If the self is the door, how deep does it go?

**Ch 5. The soul and its worlds.**
- **Conclusion:** the self is deeper than it appears, and its final shape is in our hands.
- 5.1 Reality has layers: the material, the imaginal and the intellectual worlds. [B2]
- 5.2 The soul mirrors them and moves among them, in sleep, in death and in contemplation. [B2]
- 5.3 Deeds shape an inner form that we will meet. [B2]
- 5.4 The self has states (commanding, reproaching, at peace), and it can move between them. [B2]
- → What keeps this inner work sound?

**Ch 6. The ship, the sea and the pearl.**
- **Conclusion:** there is no inner path outside the shariʿa.
- 6.1 Law, path and truth are one reality at three depths. [B2]
- 6.2 The inner never cancels the outer. Wonders without the shariʿa are not karamat. [B2]
- 6.3 The whole route, in outline: the four journeys. [B2]
- → Who has actually walked this route?

### Part Three: The Travellers (B3)

**Ch 7. Roots and the first seekers.**
- **Conclusion:** ʿirfan is native to Islam.
- 7.1 It did not come from monks, Neoplatonists or yogis. At most, techniques were borrowed. [B3]
- 7.2 Its roots are the Quran, the Prophet's companions and the Imams. [B3]
- 7.3 The early seekers developed fear, renunciation and love, often in the Imams' orbit. [B3]
- → How did the tradition mature, and where did it divide?

**Ch 8. Drunk and sober.**
- **Conclusion:** the tradition has a sober, defensible core.
- 8.1 The mystics divided over the state beyond the self: intoxication or sobriety. [B3]
- 8.2 Ibn ʿArabi systematised the theory, including the unity of being. [B3]
- 8.3 The critics' objections, and how Mutahhari, Khomeini and Amuli answer them. [B3]
- → How did all this become institutions, and how do Shia relate to them?

**Ch 9. Sufis and Shia.**
- **Conclusion:** Shia ʿirfan shares the path with Sufism but not its institution of authority.
- 9.1 What the orders are: chain, shaykh, practice. [B3]
- 9.2 The shared ground: ʿAli as the gateway. [B3]
- 9.3 The difference: authority belongs to the Imam, and there is no blind obedience, though Shia masters have used teacher and pledge. [B3]
- → So how does a Shia seeker actually begin, and with whom?

### Part Four: The Road (B4)

**Ch 10. Finding a guide.**
- **Conclusion:** a guide helps, and must be tested.
- 10.1 The Shia chain of teachers is real and recent. [B3, B4]
- 10.2 Scholars weigh the need for a guide differently (shown side by side). [B4]
- 10.3 The marks of a true guide, and of a charlatan. [B4]

**Ch 11. The stages.**
- **Conclusion:** the road has an order.
- 11.1 Polish, empty, adorn, lose oneself in God. [B4]
- 11.2 Ibn Sina's ladder maps the same ascent. [B4]

**Ch 12. Setting out.**
- **Conclusion:** the first steps are repentance and character.
- 12.1 Anyone can begin, after any sin. [B4]
- 12.2 Pace matters, and sooner is easier. [B4]
- 12.3 Good character is the first condition. [B4]

**Ch 13. The five disciplines.**
- **Conclusion:** five disciplines train the soul, in measured doses.
- 13.1 Silence, hunger, night prayer, seclusion and remembrance. [B4]
- 13.2 Dosage and balance, including where views on seclusion differ. [B4]

**Ch 14. Love, the engine.**
- **Conclusion:** love drives the path.
- 14.1 Fear and renunciation start the journey; love carries it. [B4]
- 14.2 Love as the Imams voice it. [B4]
- 14.3 Even love is finally a means to God. [B4]

**Ch 15. Losing the self, finding God.**
- **Conclusion:** the first journey ends in seeing only God while remaining oneself.
- 15.1 Fana is the loss of false self-perception, not becoming God. [B4]
- 15.2 Its degrees: in action, attributes and essence. [B4]
- 15.3 Its dangers, and the healthy return (baqa, sobriety). [B4]
- → And after that?

### Part Five: The Return (B5)

**Ch 16. The four journeys and the perfect human.**
- **Conclusion:** the path ends in return to people, with God.
- 16.1 After fana, the traveller returns to creation. [B5]
- 16.2 The Prophet and the Imams embody this. Karbala is offered as a reflection. [B5]
- 16.3 Whether others complete it: the views side by side. [B5]

**Ch 17. Reading the Quran with the heart, and the return to God.**
- **Conclusion:** every soul returns to God. ʿIrfan is choosing to return willingly, now.
- 17.1 The inner reading of the Quran never cancels the outer. [B5]
- 17.2 The return reverses creation: the surrender of will, power, knowledge and existence (Tusi). [B5]
- 17.3 Hell is the same return, refused. [B5]

**Prologue:** states the thesis and B1–B5 as a map of the book.
**Epilogue:** turns B4 into first steps, and closes the B5 circle.
