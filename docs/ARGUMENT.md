## Book thesis
ʿIrfan, the disciplined journey to know and love God, is worth every believer's pursuit: it travels through the self and never leaves the shariʿa; it is walked by a tested and ordered method; it is rooted in the teachings of the Qur'an, the Prophet ﷺ, and the Imams from his Ahl al-Bayt; and it ends not in withdrawal but in return, to people with God, and to God Himself.

## Book points (each answered by one Part)
The Parts follow the reader's journey: why travel → getting ready → travelling → who else you meet on the road → the destination.

| # | Book point | Question it answers | Part |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B1** | ʿIrfan is worth pursuing. The Quran invites more than the minimum, which is knowing God and not only knowing about Him; that knowing is reached by purifying the heart; and our own prayers already ask for it. | Why travel? | One: The Call (Ch 1–3) |
| **B2** | The journey runs through the self, which is deeper than it looks; the shariʿa is its boundary; and a tested guide is its companion. | What do I need before I set out? | Two: Getting ready (Ch 4–7) |
| **B3** | It is walked in a known order: four stages from repentance to the loss of the false self, two ours (polishing, emptying) and two God's (adornment, passing away), each resting on the one before and none dropped. | How is it walked? | Three: The Road (Ch 8–12) |
| **B4** | Others have walked it. It is rooted in the teachings of the Qur'an, the Prophet ﷺ, and the Imams from his Ahl al-Bayt; some travellers lost their footing in ecstasy while the sober reached the end; and Shia masters keep the path without a Sufi institution of authority, because authority belongs to the Imam. | Who else is on this road, and where did they arrive? | Four: Fellow travellers (Ch 13–15) |
| **B5** | Not the road but God: the friends of God return to people, with God; every soul returns to God; ʿirfan is making that return willingly, now. | What is the road for? | Five: The Destination (Ch 16–17) |

**MECE check:**
- B1 covers motive (why go).
- B2 covers preparation: the terrain (the self), the boundary (the shariʿa) and the companion (the guide).
- B3 covers method (how the road is walked).
- B4 covers company: where the road came from, who walked it and where they arrived, and how Shia relate to the Sufi orders.
- B5 covers outcome.

Why → what to bring → how → who else → to what end. The guide moves into B2 because a traveller needs one before setting out, not after. Lineage and legitimacy (old B3) follow the Road because the reader is by then invested and wants to know who walked it before. Safety stays in B2 (the boundary) and is not repeated. The thesis names all five.

**Tone arc (user, D41):**
- **Part Three** builds to the crescendo in Ch 12, where the reader feels it.
- **Part Four** is the analytical step back: calm, clear, curious.
- **Part Five** brings the emotion home: faces, Karbala, the willing return, the Munajat's last petitions.

## Chapter points, in sequence
_Each chapter states one conclusion, proved by its numbered points, in an order where each depends only on earlier ones. **[Bn]** marks the book point served; every point in a Part serves that Part's B. Divider lines and chapter epigraphs: see Threads._

### Part One: The Call (B1). Why travel?
_Divider (Munajat §73–74): "My Lord, I was not strong enough to move away from Your disobedience, except when You awakened me to Your love." [@light, p. 164]_

**Ch 1. More than the minimum.** (old 1; approved text; retitled D41)
- **Conclusion:** Keeping the obligations saves, but the Qur'an invites more: to perceive God in His signs and love Him for His own sake, and any believer can accept.
- 1.1 The minimum saves, but the Quran openly invites more: its signs are "for a people who reflect", and most people pass them by. [B1] L01 ¶5–12, ¶16; Q 30:21–24, 12:105
- 1.2 The failure is in us, not in the signs: our inner faculties are not working, and what is broken can be repaired. [B1] L01 ¶9–12; L02 ¶3–5; Q 7:179
- 1.3 The repaired state is real and worth having: the Quran describes those who perceive God's signs and promises them nearness, "a good life" and freedom from fear and grief, and that rank is open to ordinary believers. [B1] L01 ¶15–16, ¶25–27; L02 ¶7–19; Q 4:69, 10:62, 16:97
- 1.4 Its only legitimate aim is God Himself: seeking powers is not ʿirfan. [B1] L01 ¶3–4, ¶13, ¶32 (side by side: Tabatabaʾi, `light` p. 101)
- → If the aim is to know and love God, what kind of knowing is that?

**Ch 2. Knowing about God is not knowing Him.** (old 3, "Two kinds of knowing"; moved up by the Ch 2/3 swap, D41)
- **Conclusion:** That perceiving is not knowing *about* God; it is a light God gives to a purified heart, so it is reached by purification, which study serves but cannot replace.
- 2.1 The ʿarif knows God by presence, not by definition: acquired knowledge (ʿilm) defines and limits; knowledge by presence (maʿrifah) arrives. [B1] L02 ¶29–32, ¶36–37 (old 3.1)
- 2.2 Reason cannot reach God Himself, so He must be known within the soul. [B1] L03 ¶25–28, ¶33, ¶35, ¶38 (old 3.2)
- 2.3 Knowledge of God is a light God gives to a purified heart, not something gathered by study. [B1] L03 ¶10–11, ¶18–21 (added: `forty_hadith` p. 331; `lantern` p. 10) (old 3.3)
- 2.4 The path to knowing God is purification (suluk), which study serves but cannot replace. [B1] L03 ¶19–20, ¶47–49 (added, side by side: `forty_hadith` p. 403; `kernel` p. 56; `light` p. 42) (old 3.4)
- _Closing section [conclusion], no new point:_ knowing God is a different kind of knowing, reached by purification.
- → Where, then, does an ordinary believer already stand on this path?

**Ch 3. You already know these words.** (old 2; moved down to close Part One on hope, D41)
- **Conclusion:** Our own prayers already ask for this sight, love and nearness, so a believer has already begun to ask for what ʿirfan seeks.
- 3.1 The prayers' first request is a severance from everything but God, made in the heart. [B1] L01 ¶18, ¶20, ¶25–26 (Munajat Shaʿbaniyyah, Abu Hamza, ʿArafah) (old 2.1, first half)
- 3.2 The prayers then ask for what ʿirfan seeks: to see God with the heart, to love Him, and to come near Him. [B1] L01 ¶19, ¶23, ¶27–29 (the Lovers) (old 2.1, second half)
- 3.3 Words above our level are still ours to say, as an honour and as a plea from need. [B1] L01 ¶17, ¶22 (old 2.2)
- 3.4 These prayers are set on nights we already keep, so anyone may begin them. [B1] L01 ¶17, ¶22 (added: Mafatih pp. 129, 243, 245, 282, 374; `light` p. 156; side by side: `journey` p. 43, `light` p. 157 on who is fit; the chapter claims access, not attainment) (old 2.3; no L06 or L10 anchors, D38)
- _Closing section [conclusion], no new point:_ the goal of ʿirfan is already on the believer's lips.
- → These prayers keep asking God to work on the heart and the self. What is this self?

### Part Two: Getting ready (B2). What do I need before I set out?
_Divider (Munajat §5–7): "I have come running to You and am standing before You imploring You in humility." [@light, p. 163]_

**Ch 4. Know yourself, know your Lord.** (old 4)
- **Conclusion:** Since knowing God comes by purifying the heart (2), and God is reflected in the soul, the way to God runs through knowing and purifying our own self.
- _Opener, not a point (old 4.1):_ the soul is the instrument of all knowing, so it is the first thing to understand. L04 ¶3–9
- 4.1 The Qur'an places the human story in the soul: whoever purifies it succeeds, and whoever betrays it fails. [B2] L04 ¶12 (Q 91:1–10)
- 4.2 Knowing God begins with knowing the soul, because the two are forgotten together and known together: the Quran ties forgetting God to forgetting oneself (Q 59:19), and the tradition ties knowing oneself to knowing one's Lord. [B2] L04 ¶12–23 (attribution per `lantern` p. 9; side by side: Imam ʿAli, `journey` p. 125; D15; added: `self_knowledge` p. 8)
- 4.3 God is already near, so reaching Him means lifting, in this life, the veils over our own soul: "meeting" God means presence and nearness, never comprehension of His essence (seeded here, developed in 8.1; D26). [B2] L04 ¶24–60; L07 ¶4; L08 ¶3–9 (added: `forty_hadith` p. 398)
- → If the self is the door, how deep does it go?

**Ch 5. The soul and its worlds.** (old 5)
- **Conclusion:** That self reaches into worlds deeper than this one, and the form it takes there is made by what we do now, so what it becomes is, by God's grace, in our hands.
- 5.1 The world has three depths, so the dead have gone deeper into it, not away: the material, the imaginal and the intellectual worlds are one reality at three levels. [B2] L04 ¶61–96; L05 ¶3–8 (kept to about a page)
- 5.2 The soul moves between the worlds, as far as the life it lives takes it: in sleep, in death and in contemplation. [B2] L04 ¶61–96; L05 ¶16–31 (added: `lantern` p. 24; `self_knowledge` p. 76)
- 5.3 What we do now shapes the image body we will meet after death, deformed or luminous. [B2] L05 ¶10–15 (added: `forty_hadith` p. 41; `self_knowledge` pp. 21, 77)
- 5.4 The one self can rise from its lowest state to peace, so what it becomes is, by God's grace, in our hands: it has states (commanding, reproaching, at peace). [B2] L05 ¶47–57 (side by side: `inner_secrets` p. 192 counts four)
- → What keeps so inward a work from going astray?

**Ch 6. No path outside the law.** (old 6, "The ship, the sea and the pearl")
- **Conclusion:** Work this inward can go astray, so it must stay inside the law: the path is the inside of the shariʿa, never a way around it, to the last breath.
- 6.1 The path and the truth are the inside of the law, so they are reached only through it: law, path and truth are one reality at three depths, shell, core and kernel. [B2] L06 ¶7–15, ¶23–25, ¶43–48 (added: `inner_secrets` pp. 32, 48; `light` p. 15)
- 6.2 No wonder or experience stands in for the law: wonders without the shariʿa are magic, not karamat. [B2] L06 ¶17–22, ¶27–33 (added: `forty_hadith` p. 35; `light` p. 135; `inner_secrets` p. 42). L06 ¶20 ("probably") is left out; the rule is given as Kubra's, with Khomeini beside it (D39).
- 6.3 The inner work begins inside the law, with attention in prayer. [B2] L06 ¶65–70 (new section; split from old 6.2)
- 6.4 The law holds to the last breath; only the reason for keeping it changes: even the end of the road is lived under the law, and it finishes among people, not above them. [B2] L09 ¶70–72 (a sentence or two of preview; the four journeys are walked in Ch 16, FRAMEWORK §8.1) (added: `kernel` p. 30) (old 6.3)
- → The law is the boundary. Who walks beside the traveller?

**Ch 7. Finding a guide.** (old 10, plus a short form of old 9.3)
- **Conclusion:** Inside the law, a traveller still needs a companion who has walked the road: a guide under the Imams, tested by the law and by where he turns people, never by wonders.
- 7.1 A traveller needs a companion who has walked the road, wherever scholars place the need: they weigh it differently, from after the law's first steps to every stage. [B2] L14 ¶23–25; L07 ¶14–15 (old 10.2; side by side: `kernel` p. 80; `light` pp. 10, 100–102; `inner_secrets` p. 192)
- 7.2 A guide stands under the Imams, never in their place, and a traveller submits to him only if he meets a high bar. [B2] L14 ¶6–8, ¶22 (short form of old 9.3; added: `kernel` pp. 80–83, 87–88; `light` pp. 100–102; the full comparison with the orders is Ch 15)
- 7.3 Shia can name teachers of this road in recent generations, and Tabatabaʾi's own line belonged to no Sufi order. [B2] L14 ¶22–25; L06 ¶21–25; L10 ¶44 (old 10.1; added: the chain as given in `kernel` pp. 87–89 and `light` pp. 105–107; `forty_hadith` p. 8; `journey` p. 8). The v2.1 `[QUERY: which Hamadani]` is resolved: Akhund Mulla Husayn-Quli Hamadani (this chapter) and Mulla ʿAbd al-Samad Hamadani (Ch 6) are kept apart.
- 7.4 A true guide is ruled out by breaking the law, shown by long company, and recognised by where he turns people, never by wonders. [B2] L06 ¶17–22; L14 ¶22–25 (old 10.3; added: `kernel` pp. 81, 83; `light` pp. 101–102; `forty_hadith` p. 102; `lantern` p. 13). The chapter ends here, on the test, not on the scholars' disagreement.
- → Once a guide is found, what does the road itself look like?

### Part Three: The Road (B3). How is it walked?
_Divider (Munajat §94): "My Lord, inspire me with a passionate love of remembering You so that I may keep on remembering You." [@light, p. 165]_

_Part Three was restructured per D50 (2026-10-19): one stage per chapter. Ch 8 is the map, Ch 9–12 take the four stages in order; wordings follow `docs/ladders/00-book-chain.md` and `docs/ladders/08.md`–`12.md`, which are authoritative._

**Ch 8. The stages of the journey.** (old 11, "The stages of the first journey"; the map, no stage in the title)
- **Conclusion:** Since God is already near (4), the journey covers no distance: it removes veils in four stages, two ours and two God's, each resting on the one before and none ever dropped.
- 8.1 God is already near, so the journey covers no distance; it removes veils inside us. [B3] L07 ¶4, ¶59; L08 ¶3–10; L09 ¶73–74; L10 ¶21 (full treatment; seeded in 4.3, D26; Q 50:16)
- 8.2 The veils come off in four stages: we polish the self and empty the heart, then God adorns it and the self passes away. [B3] L07 ¶10–13, ¶22, ¶36–37, ¶57–59; L09 ¶2–3 (the stages' names are the lecturer's framing; substance in `inner_secrets` pp. 108–110, 195). The table "The road at a glance" shows the stages, whose act each is, and the chapter that takes each.
- 8.3 Each stage rests on the one before, and none is ever dropped. [B3] L07 ¶13, ¶21–22, ¶37, ¶55; L09 ¶70–75 (box: Ibn Sina's ladder of stations keeps the same order, L15 ¶3–59; analogies are the lecturer's; `light` pp. 45–50)
- → The first stage begins with a step any believer can take. What is it?

**Ch 9. Polishing: setting out.** (old 12, "Setting out"; Stage 1 of 4)
- **Conclusion:** The first stage, polishing, begins with repentance, open to anyone after any sin, and is kept by the law, whose first demand is putting things right with people.
- 9.1 The road begins with repentance, and repentance is open to anyone after any sin. [B3] L08 ¶10–12, ¶16–18; L12 ¶19–22 (added: `lantern` pp. 48, 59; `forty_hadith` p. 249; Q 39:53)
- 9.2 Repentance is kept up at a steady pace: not rushed, and not put off. [B3] L08 ¶13–15, ¶19–22, ¶24–27 (side by side: `light` pp. 89, 126–127; `forty_hadith` pp. 118, 250 press harder)
- 9.3 Polishing is keeping the law strictly, and its first demand is putting things right with people. [B3] L07 ¶12–13; L08 ¶39–42; L12 ¶25 (added: `inner_secrets` p. 108; `light` p. 70; Nahj 1.191)
- → Polishing is open to every believer. Where does ʿirfan proper begin?

**Ch 10. Emptying: the five disciplines.** (old 13, "The five disciplines"; Stage 2 of 4)
- **Conclusion:** The second stage, emptying, clears the heart of everything but God, and the five disciplines are its tools, each in a measured dose.
- 10.1 ʿIrfan proper begins with emptying the heart, and five disciplines are its tools. [B3] L07 ¶22–29; L08 ¶55, ¶58; L10 ¶6 (added: `lantern` p. 56). The five disciplines placed in emptying are the book's synthesis.
- 10.2 Silence, hunger and the night prayer shut the doors through which the world fills the heart: the tongue, the stomach and sleep. [B3] L08 ¶50–51, ¶59, ¶72–73; L10 ¶9, ¶19–21 (added: `kernel` pp. 76–77; `journey` p. 16)
- 10.3 Seclusion separates the heart from what distracts it, and remembrance dusts off what remains. [B3] L07 ¶25–27; L10 ¶42–63 (added: Mafatih p. 802)
- 10.4 Each discipline is taken in a dose measured to the person, small and kept up. [B3] L08 ¶55–58; L10 ¶23–27, ¶44–47; L15 ¶42 (side by side: `kernel` pp. 76–78; `lantern` pp. 50, 55; `light` p. 102)
- → Emptying ends at the self, the last thing left behind. What follows is no longer the traveller's work. What does God give the emptied heart?

**Ch 11. Adorning: love as the engine.** (old 14, "Love as the engine"; Stage 3 of 4)
- **Conclusion:** The third stage, adornment, is God's gift to the emptied heart: knowledge and light, and love, which carries the traveller until even love is left at the door.
- 11.1 Once the heart is emptied, God adorns it with gifts no effort makes, briefly at first. [B3] L07 ¶10, ¶36–39; L10 ¶11–16; L15 ¶20–22, ¶41–43 (added: `inner_secrets` p. 109; `light` p. 48)
- 11.2 Among those gifts is love, which the Imams asked for, and worship from love is the worship of the free. [B3] L07 ¶10; L11 ¶61–63; L12 ¶38–42, ¶54–56, ¶59–60; L01 ¶27–31 (Nahj 3.222 set beside al-Sadiq's love version, `light` p. 94, FRAMEWORK §8.7; Sahifa p. 656; Kumayl §123–124; `lantern` pp. 58, 88; `kernel` p. 70). Love is among the gifts, not the whole of adornment.
- 11.3 Love too is a means, so at the Beloved even love is left behind. [B3] L12 ¶59–60 (added: `light` pp. 45, 95; `forty_hadith` p. 89)
- → With even love left behind, what remains of the traveller before God?

**Ch 12. Passing away: losing the self, finding God.** (old 15, "Losing the self, finding God"; Stage 4 of 4) **The crescendo of the book.**
- **Conclusion:** In the fourth stage, love ends in losing the false self before God's glory, and in coming back sober, seeing God in all things while remaining oneself.
- 12.1 In passing away the traveller loses the sense of self before God's glory; he neither ceases to exist nor becomes God, so what is lost is a false self. [B3] L07 ¶41–63; L09 ¶3–9 (added: `kernel` pp. 14–15; `light` p. 63)
- 12.2 Passing away comes in three degrees, in action, in attributes and in essence, each the lived truth of words we already say. [B3] L09 ¶52–69 (added: `inner_secrets` pp. 113–117; `light` pp. 107–108). The climax of the chapter and of Part Three.
- 12.3 Passing away is a beginning, not an end: the healthy outcome is to come back to oneself, sober, seeing God and creation together. [B3] L09 ¶20–50; L07 ¶64 (added: `forty_hadith` pp. 515–516; `inner_secrets` p. 116; `light` pp. 57, 102; `kernel` p. 60). Dangers are stated as principles. The chapter ends on Imam ʿAli's "I did not see a thing without beholding God…" (`forty_hadith` p. 516). Bayazid and Junayd are named in a line, with a pointer to Ch 14.
- → Others have walked this far. Where did the road come from, and who walked it first?

### Part Four: Fellow travellers (B4). Who else is on this road, and where did they arrive?
_Divider (Munajat §109–110, in the duas.org wording, FRAMEWORK §8.3): "My Lord, (please) appoint me of those who responded to You when You called them, and at whom You glanced so he swoons before Your Majesty." [duas §109–110; cf. @light, p. 165]_
_Tone: the analytical step back after the crescendo. Calm, clear, curious._

**Ch 13. Where it comes from.** (old 7, "Roots and the first seekers")
- **Conclusion:** This road began in the Qur'an, the Prophet and the Imams; the early seekers walked it in the Imams' orbit, and below them.
- 13.1 Its roots are the Quran, the Prophet's companions and the Imams. [B4] L11 ¶34–46 (Schimmel is the lecturer's reference; `light` pp. 4–5, 19, 24–25 carry the evidence) (old 7.2)
- 13.2 The early seekers developed fear, renunciation and love, in that order (the order the reader met in Ch 11), often in the Imams' orbit, and the Imams stand above them, not among them. [B4] L11 ¶58–63, ¶78–90; L12 ¶3–37, ¶53–56 (old 7.3; added: `light` pp. 5, 24–25)
- 13.3 So it was not brought in from outside: it did not come from monks, Neoplatonists or yogis; at most, techniques were borrowed. [B4] L11 ¶3–24 (added: `light` pp. 16, 20, 21) (old 7.1). Placed last and kept short, so the chapter opens on people (FRAMEWORK §9; FRAMEWORK §8.4: opens on a companion, not Kumayl).
- → Where did those travellers arrive?

**Ch 14. Drunk and sober.** (old 8)
- **Conclusion:** Those who reached the end divided into the drunk and the sober; the sober went further, and the great theory of what they saw is neither pantheism nor licence.
- 14.1 The knowers divided over the state beyond the self, Bayazid's intoxication or Junayd's sobriety, and the tradition ranked the return higher. [B4] L13 ¶3–34 (added: `light` pp. 25, 27, 57; `kernel` p. 73, Bistami line only)
- 14.2 Ibn ʿArabi gave the tradition its theory, the unity of being, which the lecturer holds is grasped by experience, not argument. [B4] L13 ¶35–67 (added: `light` pp. 34–35, 144; Nahj 1.1.1)
- 14.3 That theory drew Shia critics; the held defenders answer that it is neither pantheism nor licence, and the lecturer himself declines to judge. [B4] L13 ¶43, ¶47–55; L01 ¶6 (side by side: `light` p. 34 against `inner_secrets` p. 99 on Ibn ʿArabi's Seal of the Saints; added: `forty_hadith` pp. 345, 402; `light` pp. 13–14, 144; `inner_secrets` pp. 98–99). The critics' concerns are reported as the defenders give them (FRAMEWORK §9); no critical source is held.
- → How did the road become orders, and where do Shia stand?

**Ch 15. Sufis and Shia.** (old 9)
- **Conclusion:** The road later became the Sufi orders; Shia share the path and its source, but not the orders' authority, which belongs to the Imam.
- 15.1 A Sufi order is an institution: a founder's method, a chain back to the Prophet, and total submission to a shaykh. [B4] L14 ¶3–21, ¶27–30, ¶36, ¶56–59, ¶62–78 (old 9.1; added: `light` pp. 5–6, 22–23, 31, 34, 40)
- 15.2 Nearly every chain runs back to ʿAli, so the Sufi and the Shia seeker share a source. [B4] L14 ¶8–9, ¶36–37, ¶79 (old 9.2; added: `kernel` p. 87; `light` pp. 6, 25; `inner_secrets` p. 13)
- 15.3 Set techniques are secondary, because each soul receives God's grace in its own shape; what a seeker needs is a trustworthy guide (Ch 7), not a lineage. [B4] L14 ¶59–71 (old 9.4, now before authority; added: `inner_secrets` p. 195; `kernel` p. 87)
- 15.4 For Shia, authority belongs to the Imam, so there is no chain of authority beside him and no blind obedience, though Shia masters have used teacher and pledge. [B4] L14 ¶6–8, ¶22–25 (old 9.3; side by side: `inner_secrets` pp. 8, 14, 192; `kernel` pp. 60, 64, 81, 88; `forty_hadith` p. 8; `light` pp. 40–41). The chapter ends here, on the Imam (FRAMEWORK §8.10).
- → If authority belongs to the Imam, what does the completed journey look like in him?

### Part Five: The Destination (B5). What is the road for?
_Divider (Munajat §128–131): "My Lord, let me be attached to the Light of Your Majestic Glory, so that I may know You alone, be away from others, and have a heart fearful of You and an eye watchful of You." [@light, p. 166]_
_Tone: the emotion comes back and is brought home._

**Ch 16. Back among people.** (old 16, "The four journeys and the perfect human"; ends on Karbala)
- **Conclusion:** The completed journey comes back to people, with God, as the Prophet and the Imams show, to Karbala.
- 16.1 After fana the road goes on and the traveller returns to creation: the four journeys, with their Quranic anchors. [B5] L09 ¶70–88, ¶91 (old 16.1; the Nahj 3.133 scene of a face comes first, FRAMEWORK §9; added: `light` pp. 49–50; `inner_secrets` p. 140)
- 16.2 How far ordinary believers share in that return is left open: the lecturer reserves the completed fourth journey for the Prophet and the Imams; ʿAllama Tabatabaʾi, gently set beside him, holds that wilaya is not exclusive and others reach it in their measure; both place the full return in the Prophet and the Imams. [B5] L09 ¶87–89 (old 16.3; side by side: `kernel` p. 20; `light` p. 64; D25). The `[QUERY]` of v2.1 is resolved: L09 ¶89 is followed over ¶87 (D39).
- 16.3 The Prophet and the Imams embody this return; Karbala is offered to the reader as a reflection on it, through Duʿa ʿArafah.
