# Storyline: the book as a slide deck

_One "slide" per chapter. The heading is the chapter title. The subheading is the point of the chapter in one plain sentence. Reading the subheadings alone, from top to bottom, should tell the whole argument. The bullets are the steps that prove each subheading, in order. Source of truth for the logic: `docs/ARGUMENT.md` (v2.1); this file is its plain-English reading (D39)._

**The whole book:** ʿIrfan, the journey to know and love God, is worth every believer's pursuit. It runs through the self and stays inside the shariʿa; it comes from the Qur'an and the Imams; it follows a tested method; and it ends in a return to people and to God.

---

### Prologue: An invitation
**The prayers we already say ask to know God and come near Him. This book argues that this less-practised side of our religion, called ʿirfan, is worth every believer's pursuit.**

---

## Part One: The Call. Why bother?
**The Qur'an invites us to know God, our own prayers already ask for it, and that knowing comes through a purified heart.**

### 1. Seeking, not powers
**The Qur'an invites us beyond the minimum to see God's signs. We miss them because something inside us needs repair. The repaired state is open to every believer, and its only aim is God, never powers.**
- The obligations are enough to be saved, but the Qur'an asks for more: to reflect on its signs.
- Most of us miss the signs because an inner faculty is not working, and it can be repaired.
- Those in whom it works are promised nearness, a good life and freedom from fear and grief, and that rank is open to ordinary believers.
- The aim is God Himself. Seeking powers is not ʿirfan.

### 2. You already know these words
**The prayers we recite in Shaʿban, in Ramadan and at ʿArafah already ask for what the mystics seek. So the goal of ʿirfan is not foreign or reserved for an elite: it is already on our lips.**
- Our prayers ask to be cut off from everything but God, to see Him with the heart, to love Him and to reach Him.
- They ask humbly, like a pupil reading above his level, and we are honoured to be allowed to say them.
- They are the familiar prayers of the Mafatih, said by ordinary believers on familiar nights.

### 3. Two kinds of knowing
**Those prayers ask to know God, which is different from knowing about Him. It comes as light in a purified heart, so the path is purification, and study prepares for it.**
- Knowing *about* something comes from outside, through ideas and definitions. Knowing it directly is like knowing your own hunger.
- Reason can describe God's attributes but can never grasp what He is.
- Direct knowledge of God is a light He places in a heart that has been cleaned.
- So the path is purifying the soul. Study prepares the ground (Khomeini, Tabatabaʾi) but cannot do the work.

---

## Part Two: The Terrain. What do we travel through, and what keeps us safe?
**The journey to God runs through our own self, which is deeper than it looks, and the shariʿa is its boundary.**

### 4. Know yourself, know your Lord
**That knowing happens in the soul, so the road to God runs through the self. Because God is already near, the journey removes veils rather than covering distance.**
- The soul is what we know everything with, so it is the first thing to understand.
- The Qur'an links forgetting God with forgetting ourselves; the tradition links knowing ourselves with knowing our Lord.
- "Meeting" God means nearness, never grasping His essence, and the journey removes veils.

### 5. The soul and its worlds
**The self is deeper than it appears. It lives in more than one world and is shaped by what we do, so what it finally becomes is in our hands.**
- Reality has depths: the physical, the imaginal and the intellectual are one reality at three levels.
- The soul moves among them in sleep, in death and in contemplation.
- Our deeds shape an inner form, ugly or radiant, that we will meet.
- The self has states (commanding, blaming, at peace), and it can move between them.

### 6. The ship, the sea and the pearl
**An inward journey like this is kept safe by the shariʿa. Law, path and truth are one thing at three depths, so there is no inner path outside the law.**
- Law, path and truth are the shell, the core and the kernel of one reality.
- The inner never cancels the outer. Wonders without the shariʿa are magic, and attention in prayer is the first inner work.
- Even the end of the road is lived under the law, among people.

---

## Part Three: The Travellers. Is it ours?
**ʿIrfan grew from the Qur'an and the Imams. Sufism carried it, and Shia masters keep its sober core without a Sufi chain of authority.**

### 7. Roots and the first seekers
**ʿIrfan did not come from monks or philosophers outside Islam. Its roots are the Qur'an, the Prophet's companions and the Imams, who stand above the early seekers rather than among them.**
- It did not come from outside; at most, some techniques were borrowed.
- Its roots are the Qur'an, the companions and the Imams.
- The early seekers developed fear, renunciation and love, often close to the Imams, who remain above them.

### 8. Drunk and sober
**As the tradition matured, it split between ecstatic and sober mystics, and it ranked the sober higher. Its great theory drew critics, and those objections have answers.**
- The mystics divided between ecstatic intoxication and sober clarity, and the tradition preferred sobriety.
- Ibn ʿArabi gave the tradition its theory, the unity of being, which is grasped by experience rather than argument.
- Some Shia scholars objected; its defenders answer that it is neither pantheism nor licence.

### 9. Sufis and Shia
**Shia ʿirfan shares its path and its source with Sufism, but not its institution. Authority belongs to the Imam, so a seeker needs a trustworthy guide, not a lineage.**
- A Sufi order is an institution: a founder, a chain back to the Prophet, and total obedience to a shaykh.
- Nearly every chain runs back to Imam ʿAli, so Sufi and Shia seekers share a source.
- For Shia, authority belongs to the Imam, so there is no rival chain and no blind obedience.
- Set techniques matter less than a trustworthy guide, because each soul receives God's grace in its own way.

---

## Part Four: The Road. How is it walked?
**With a tested guide, through ordered stages: repentance and good character first, then measured disciplines, carried by love, ending in the loss of the false self.**

### 10. Finding a guide
**A guide helps, and real Shia teachers exist within living memory. Scholars differ on how essential one is, but all agree a guide must be tested by faithfulness to the shariʿa, not by wonders.**
- There are guides to be found: a chain of Shia teachers runs to our own time.
- Scholars weigh the need for a guide differently.
- A true guide is known by keeping the shariʿa and not caring for power or followers; wonders prove nothing.

### 11. The stages of the first journey
**The road is inward, a matter of removing veils since God is already near, and it has a fixed order of stages that cannot be skipped.**
- Travel means removing veils, not covering distance.
- The first journey has four stages in order: cleaning by the law, emptying the heart, adornment by God, and losing the self. None is skipped.
- Ibn Sina's ladder of stations maps the same climb.

### 12. Setting out
**The first steps are repentance, open to anyone after any sin and taken at a steady pace, and good conduct towards people.**
- The road begins with repentance, and anyone can begin.
- Repentance is paced: no vows you cannot keep, and sooner is easier.
- Good conduct towards people is the first condition of the inner work.

### 13. The five disciplines
**Five disciplines train the soul: silence, hunger, night prayer, seclusion and remembrance. Each is rooted in the Qur'an and taken in measured doses, never forced.**
- The five disciplines loosen the soul's grip on the body, and each has a Qur'anic root.
- Each is a medicine, dosed to the person; on seclusion, the Sheikh prefers a private hour to a long retreat.

### 14. Love, the engine
**Fear and renunciation start the journey, but love carries it, the love the Imams voice in their prayers. In the end even love is left behind at the Beloved.**
- Fear and renunciation start the journey; love keeps it going.
- The Imams voice that love: worship as free people, not as traders or slaves.
- Love too is finally a means, left behind on arrival.

### 15. Losing the self, finding God
**The first journey ends in fana: losing the false sense of self before God's glory, not becoming God. The traveller then comes back to themselves, sober, seeing only God.**
- Fana is losing a false picture of the self, not ceasing to exist or becoming God.
- It has degrees: in action, in attributes, in essence.
- It has dangers. The healthy outcome is coming back to oneself (baqa), which ends the first journey and begins the second.

---

## Part Five: The Return. Where does it end?
**Not in withdrawal. The saints return to people, with God, and every soul returns to God, willingly or not.**

### 16. The four journeys and the perfect human
**The path is completed by returning to people with God, as the Prophet and the Imams did. How far others share in that return is left open, because scholars differ.**
- After losing the self, the traveller returns to creation: the four journeys.
- The Prophet and the Imams embody this return, and Karbala is offered as a reflection on it.
- The Sheikh reserves the completed return for the Prophet and the Imams; Tabatabaʾi holds that others reach it in their measure.

### 17. Reading the Qur'an with the heart, and the return to God
**Every soul returns to God, giving back what it was given. ʿIrfan is making that return willingly, now; hell is the same return, refused.**
- The Qur'an can be read from the inside, as long as the inner meaning agrees with the outer.
- The return reverses creation: existence, knowledge, power and will are given back to God, to be given again.
- Hell is the same return, undergone against one's will.
