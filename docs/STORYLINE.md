# Storyline: the book as a slide deck

_One "slide" per chapter. The heading is the chapter title. The subheading is the point of the chapter in one plain sentence. Reading the subheadings alone, from top to bottom, should tell the whole argument. The bullets are the steps that prove each subheading, in order. Source of truth for the logic: `docs/ARGUMENT.md` (v3, D40–D41); this file is its plain-English reading (D39). The book follows the reader's journey: why travel, getting ready, travelling, who else is on the road, the destination._

**The whole book:** ʿIrfan, the journey to know and love God, is worth every believer's pursuit. It runs through the self and stays inside the shariʿa; it is walked in a tested, ordered way; it comes from the teachings of the Qur'an, the Prophet ﷺ, and the Imams from his Ahl al-Bayt; and it ends in a return to people and to God.

---

### Prologue: An invitation
**The prayers we already say ask to know God and come near Him. This book argues that this less-practised side of our religion, called ʿirfan, is worth every believer's pursuit.**

---

## Part One: The Call. Why travel?
**The Qur'an invites us to know God, not only to know about Him. That knowing comes through a purified heart, and our own prayers already ask for it.**

### 1. More than the minimum
**Keeping the obligations saves, but the Qur'an invites more: to perceive God in His signs and love Him for His own sake, and any believer can accept.**
- The obligations save, but the Qur'an asks for more: to reflect on, know, listen to and reason about its signs.
- Most of us, the devout included, do not perceive those signs.
- Since the signs are plain and we miss them, the fault is a faculty in us; it has rusted, so it can be repaired.
- If the faculty can be repaired, some have repaired it: the Qur'an describes believers in whom it works, the ʿurafaʾ.
- What they are given is worth having, and their rank is open to every believer.
- The repair is training the soul, and that training must aim at God alone, never at powers.
- ʿIrfan is seeking the knowledge and love of God and nothing else, and it is worth pursuing.

### 2. Knowing about God is not knowing Him
**That perceiving is not knowing *about* God; it is a light God gives to a purified heart, so it is reached by purification, which study serves but cannot replace.**
- The ʿarif knows God by presence, not by definition.
- Reason cannot reach God Himself, so He must be known by presence, within the soul.
- Knowledge of God is a light God gives to a purified heart, not something gathered by study.
- The path to knowing God is purification, which study serves but cannot replace.
- Knowing God is a different kind of knowing, reached by purification, which study serves but cannot replace.

### 3. You already know these words
**Our own prayers already ask for this sight, love and nearness, so a believer has already begun to ask for what ʿirfan seeks.**
- The prayers' first request is a severance from everything but God, made in the heart.
- The prayers then ask for what ʿirfan seeks: to see God with the heart, to love Him, and to come near Him.
- Words above our level are still ours to say, as an honour and as a plea from need.
- These prayers are set on nights we already keep, so anyone may begin them.
- The goal of ʿirfan is already on our lips: in these prayers, we have already begun to ask for it.

## Part Two: Getting ready. What do I need before I set out?
**The road to God runs through your own self, which is deeper than it looks. The shariʿa is its boundary from the first step to the last, and a tested guide walks beside you, under the Imam and never in his place.**

### 4. Know yourself, know your Lord
**Since knowing God comes by purifying the heart (2), and God is reflected in the soul, the way to God runs through knowing and purifying our own self.**
- The human story is the soul's story: we succeed by purifying it and fail by betraying it.
- Knowing God begins with knowing the soul, because the two are forgotten together and known together.
- God is already near and reflected in the soul, so reaching Him means lifting the veils over that soul, and doing it in this life.

### 5. The soul and its worlds
**That self reaches into worlds deeper than this one, and the form it takes there is made by what we do now, so what it becomes is, by God's grace, in our hands.**
- Creation has three worlds, one inside another, and none of them is far away.
- When the body's hold on the soul loosens, the soul passes into the middle world; at death it stays there.
- Because the soul is not fixed like an angel, what we do shapes it, and it meets that shape in the middle world.
- The self that does the deeds can change its state, so what the soul becomes is, by God's grace, in our hands.

### 6. No path outside the law
**Work this inward can go astray, so it must stay inside the law: the path is the inside of the shariʿa, never a way around it, to the last breath.**
- The path and the truth are the inside of the law, so they are reached only through it.
- No wonder or experience stands in for the law.
- The inner work begins inside the law, with attention in prayer.
- The law holds to the last breath; only the reason for keeping it changes.

### 7. Finding a guide
**Inside the law, a traveller still needs a companion who has walked the road: a guide under the Imams, tested by the law and by where he turns people, never by wonders.**
- A traveller needs a companion who has walked the road, wherever scholars place the need.
- A guide stands under the Imams, never in their place, and a traveller submits to him only if he meets a high bar.
- Shia can name guides of this kind in recent generations, in a line that belonged to no Sufi order.
- A guide who breaks the law is ruled out; a true one is shown by long company and recognised by where he turns people, never by wonders.

## Part Three: The Road. How is it walked?
**In a known order: four stages from repentance to the loss of the false self, two ours and two God's.** _(Restructured per D50: one stage per chapter; wordings from `docs/ladders/00-book-chain.md` and `08.md`–`12.md`.)_

### 8. The stages of the journey
**Since God is already near, the journey covers no distance: it removes veils in four stages, two ours and two God's, each resting on the one before and none ever dropped.**
- God is already near, so the journey covers no distance; it removes veils inside us.
- The veils come off in four stages: we polish the self and empty the heart, then God adorns it and the self passes away.
- Each stage rests on the one before, and none is ever dropped. A box shows Ibn Sina's ladder keeping the same order.

### 9. Polishing: setting out
**The first stage, polishing, begins with repentance, open to anyone after any sin, and is kept by the law, whose first demand is putting things right with people.**
- The road begins with repentance, and repentance is open to anyone after any sin.
- Repentance is kept up at a steady pace: not rushed, and not put off.
- Polishing is keeping the law strictly, and its first demand is putting things right with people.

### 10. Emptying: the five disciplines
**The second stage, emptying, clears the heart of everything but God, and the five disciplines are its tools, each in a measured dose.**
- ʿIrfan proper begins with emptying the heart, and five disciplines are its tools.
- Silence, hunger and the night prayer shut the doors through which the world fills the heart: the tongue, the stomach and sleep.
- Seclusion separates the heart from what distracts it, and remembrance dusts off what remains.
- Each discipline is taken in a dose measured to the person, small and kept up.

### 11. Adorning: love, the engine
**The third stage, adornment, is God's gift to the emptied heart: knowledge and light, and love, which carries the traveller until even love is left at the door.**
- Once the heart is emptied, God adorns it with gifts no effort makes, briefly at first.
- Among those gifts is love, which the Imams asked for, and worship from love is the worship of the free.
- Love too is a means, so at the Beloved even love is left behind.

### 12. Passing away: losing the self, finding God (the crescendo)
**In the fourth stage, love ends in losing the false self before God's glory, and in coming back sober, seeing God in all things while remaining oneself.**
- In passing away the traveller loses the sense of self before God's glory; he neither ceases to exist nor becomes God, so what is lost is a false self.
- Passing away comes in three degrees, in action, in attributes and in essence, each the lived truth of words we already say.
- Passing away is a beginning, not an end: the healthy outcome is to come back to oneself, sober, seeing God and creation together.

## Part Four: Fellow travellers. Who else is on this road, and where did they arrive?
**Others have walked it. The road runs from the teachings of the Qur'an, the Prophet ﷺ, and the Imams from his Ahl al-Bayt; some travellers lost their footing in ecstasy while the sober went furthest; and Shia masters keep the road without a Sufi chain of authority, because authority belongs to the Imam.**

_Tone: the analytical step back after the crescendo. Calm, clear and curious._

### 13. Where it comes from
**This road began in the Qur'an, the Prophet and the Imams; the early seekers walked it in the Imams' orbit, and below them.**
- Its roots are the Qur'an, the Prophet's companions and the Imams.
- The early seekers leaned to fear, renunciation or love, often close to the Imams, who stand above them.
- It did not come from monks or philosophers outside Islam; at most, some techniques were borrowed.

### 14. Drunk and sober
**Those who reached the end divided into the drunk and the sober; the sober went further, and the great theory of what they saw is neither pantheism nor licence.**
- Travellers divided between ecstatic intoxication and sober clarity, and the tradition ranked the return higher.
- Ibn ʿArabi gave the experience a theory, the unity of being, which is grasped by experience rather than argument.
- Some Shia scholars objected; its defenders answer that it is neither pantheism nor licence.

### 15. Sufis and Shia
**The road later became the Sufi orders; Shia share the path and its source, but not the orders' authority, which belongs to the Imam.**
- A Sufi order is an institution: a founder's method, a chain back to the Prophet, and total obedience to a shaykh.
- Nearly every chain runs back to Imam ʿAli, so Sufi and Shia seekers share a source.
- Set techniques matter less than a trustworthy guide, because each soul receives God's grace in its own way.
- For Shia, authority belongs to the Imam, so there is no rival chain and no blind obedience.

## Part Five: The Destination. What is the road for?
**Not the road but God. Those who reach Him come back to people, with Him. Every soul goes back to Him, and ʿirfan is going back willingly, now.**

_Tone: the emotion comes back and is brought home._

### 16. Back among people
**The completed journey comes back to people, with God, as the Prophet and the Imams show, to Karbala.**
- After losing the self, the traveller returns to creation: the four journeys.
- How far ordinary believers share in that return is left open: the Sheikh reserves the completed return for the Prophet and the Imams, and Tabatabaʾi holds that others reach it in their measure.
- The Prophet and the Imams embody the return, and Karbala is offered as a reflection on it.

### 17. The return to God
**Every soul returns to God, willingly or not; ʿirfan is making that return willingly, now.**
- The Qur'an can be read from the inside, as long as the inner meaning agrees with the outer.
- The return reverses creation: existence, knowledge, power and will are given back to God, to be given again.
- Hell is the same return, undergone against one's will.
