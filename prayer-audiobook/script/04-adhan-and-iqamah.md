# Track 4: The Adhan and the Iqamah

We have washed. We know the time, and the place, and which way to face. We are standing at the edge of the prayer mat. But the prayer has not begun yet.

Before the prayer, there is a call. The *adhan*, the call to prayer. And then, just before we begin, a second and shorter call, the *iqamah*. The word *iqamah* means the setting up, the establishing. The *adhan* announces that the prayer is coming. The *iqamah* says that it is here.

In this track we will walk through the words of both calls, one phrase at a time. And we will listen to what Imam Khomeini says about them, in his book the Disciplines of the Prayer.

---

First, the outward form.

In the school of the Ahl al-Bayt, the *adhan* and the *iqamah* are recommended before each of the five daily prayers. Strongly recommended. Even when you pray alone, in your own room, with nobody to call, you are encouraged to say them. Of the two, the *iqamah* is the more emphasised.

The *adhan* begins with *Allahu akbar*, four times. God is greater.

Then, twice, *ash-hadu an la ilaha illallah*. I bear witness that there is no god but God.

Then, twice, *ash-hadu anna Muhammadan rasulullah*. I bear witness that Muhammad is the Messenger of God.

Then come three calls, each said twice. *Hayya 'alas-salah*. Come to the prayer. *Hayya 'alal-falah*. Come to success. And *hayya 'ala khayril-'amal*. Come to the best of deeds. That third call is part of the *adhan* as the school of the Ahl al-Bayt recites it.

Then *Allahu akbar*, twice more. And the *adhan* closes with *la ilaha illallah*, twice. There is no god but God.

The *iqamah* has the same shape, only shorter. *Allahu akbar* twice, not four times. The two testimonies, twice each. The three calls, twice each. Then comes a phrase that is only found in the *iqamah*. *Qad qamatis-salah*. The prayer has begun. That too is said twice. Then *Allahu akbar* twice, and *la ilaha illallah*, just once.

---

There is one more phrase you will often hear. After the testimony to the Prophet, many people add, *ash-hadu anna 'Aliyyan waliyyullah*. I bear witness that Ali is the friend of God.

Imam Khomeini handles this with care, and so will we. He says that the narrations which put this phrase inside the *adhan* are not counted as reliable, and that Shaykh al-Saduq rejected them. But he also says that to bear witness to the guardianship of Ali after bearing witness to the Prophet is a good thing, and recommended in general. So his advice is this. Say it, with the intention of drawing near to God. But say it as a remembrance in its own right, not as a part of the *adhan* itself.

And he passes on a lovely saying of his own teacher, Shaykh Shahabadi. The testimony to the Prophet already holds within it the testimony to the guardianship, because the guardianship is the inside of the messengership.

---

Now, the inner meaning. What is the *adhan* actually for?

Imam Khomeini gives a picture. He says the one who prays has a kingdom inside him. The heart is its king. And the senses, the limbs, the thoughts and the feelings are its soldiers, scattered all over the place. The *adhan*, he says, is an announcement to that whole kingdom. The time of the meeting has drawn near. Get ready.

Why do they need warning? He gives two reasons. If you are one of the eager lovers, you need to be ready, so that you do not lose control when the Beloved suddenly shows Himself. And if you are one of the veiled, like most of us, you need to be ready so that you do not walk into the presence of the King without any preparation at all.

And the *iqamah*? The *adhan* calls the soldiers. The *iqamah* stands them in their places. It sets up the whole of you, inside and out, in the presence.

He says the right feeling for the *adhan* is an awareness of how great this meeting is, and how small we are, and how much we depend on God's mercy to make up the difference. And the right feeling for the *iqamah* is a mixture. Awe, and shyness, and a firm hope in a mercy that has no limit.

He reminds us that the Prophet, peace be upon him and his family, used to wait for the time of the prayer. His longing would grow and grow. And at last he would say to Bilal, who gave the call, Relieve us, O Bilal. In other words, give us rest. For the Prophet, the *adhan* was not the start of a duty. It was the end of a wait.

---

Now, Imam Khomeini is honest. He knows that most of us do not feel this. He calls us the veiled, and he includes himself. So what is the discipline for people like us?

The first thing, he says, before anything else, is to drive despair out of the heart. Do not think these states were cut to fit a few special people, and that your hand is too short to reach them. That thought, he says, is one of Satan's soldiers.

And then he gives very practical advice. At the beginning, he says, we only understand greatness the way the world understands it. So use that. Think of how you would stand at the court of a great king, a king whose greatness you really felt. Then tell your heart that the whole world is God's court, because He is present in every place. And that the prayer is a special permission to enter, a special appointment to meet Him.

At first, he says, this will not come easily. But the heart gradually gets used to it. And then, in his words, the imagery becomes real.

That is a gentle and hopeful teaching. You do not have to feel it first. You can start by imagining it. And the imagining, done faithfully, slowly turns into something true.

---

Now let us go back to the words, one at a time.

The *adhan* begins with *Allahu akbar*, four times. Why four?

Imam Khomeini offers two answers. The first is that the one who prays is announcing God's greatness to four audiences, one after another. First, to all the powers inside himself. Second, to the angels who are in charge of those powers. Third, to every being in the seen and unseen worlds. And fourth, to the angels in charge of the heavens and the earth. So the *takbir* ripples outward, from the centre of your own heart to the edge of creation.

His second answer is about what the word greater really means. Greater than what? He says the four *takbirs* may point to four steps. God is greater than any description of His essence. Greater than any description of His attributes. Greater than any of His names, as we understand them. And greater than any of His acts, as we can grasp them. Whatever you say about Him, He is greater than that.

He quotes Imam Ali, peace be upon him, to the same effect. God is far above being understood by those who describe Him, because they describe Him by their own measure, not by the measure of His greatness.

And in the *iqamah* there are only two *takbirs*, not four. Imam Khomeini suggests why. By the time of the *iqamah*, you have gathered yourself. You have moved a little, from the many towards the one. You need fewer words.

But he adds a warning about the *takbir*, which is worth hearing. Its discipline, he says, is to keep greatness for God alone. If, in your heart, anyone else seems great, in their own right, and not as a light borrowed from God, then the heart is sick. And he gives a very simple test for that sickness. It is when you would rather please people than please God. When, to win the approval of a creature, you are willing to lose the approval of the Creator.

Any of us can apply that test. Whose approval am I really chasing today?

---

Next come the testimonies. *Ash-hadu an la ilaha illallah*. I bear witness that there is no god but God.

Here Imam Khomeini, in the Disciplines of the Prayer, brings a story about the night of the ascension. He gives two narrations of it from Imam al-Sadiq, peace be upon him, which tell it in slightly different ways. I will put them together, in my own words.

The Prophet was carried up, in a carriage of light, to the gates of the first heaven. When the angels saw his light, they were overwhelmed, and they fled from the gates. They were afraid that here was a second god. A god on earth, and a god in heaven. Then God commanded Gabriel to call out, *Allahu akbar, Allahu akbar*. And the angels came back to the gates, and the gate was opened.

At the second heaven, it happened again. The angels fled. And this time the words were, I bear witness that there is no god but God. I bear witness that there is no god but God. And the angels returned, because they understood that he was a creature, a servant. And that gate opened too.

At the third heaven, Gabriel said, I bear witness that Muhammad is the Messenger of God. Twice. And the angels gathered and greeted the Prophet. And so it went on, heaven after heaven, until Gabriel had finished the *iqamah*.

Imam Khomeini draws the lesson. The words of the *adhan* open the gates of heaven. They pierce the veils. And there is a veil inside each of us that these words are meant to pierce. It is the veil of thinking that other things act by themselves. That this person, or that job, or that bank account, is what really holds up my life.

---

He describes the testimony as having several levels.

The first is the testimony of the tongue. That one we know.

The second is the testimony of action. If you really believe there is no power but God, then you live like it. You do not stretch out the hand of need to anybody but Him. He quotes a saying from the Ahl al-Bayt. A believer's dignity is to manage without the help of people.

The third is the testimony of the heart. It is when this truth has actually settled inside you. He quotes Imam Ali ibn al-Husayn, peace be upon him, who said: I found that all goodness is gathered in cutting off one's longing for what is in the hands of others.

And he says that the testimony of the tongue, without at least a little of the testimony of the heart, is not really a testimony at all.

So the testimony in the *adhan* is not only a statement. It is a little act of house clearing. Imam Khomeini says the one who prays should smash the idols, big and small, that have been set up in the Ka'bah of the heart. As long as the love of the world sits there like an idol, he says, the traveller will find no way to his goal.

And the testimony to the Prophet? Imam Khomeini says this is choosing your companion before you set off. He quotes the old saying, the companion first, then the journey. The prayer is an ascension, and you do not climb alone. You hold on to the one who has already made the climb, and who brought the prayer back as a gift for us.

---

Then come the three calls. *Hayya 'ala*. Come to.

Why is each one said twice? Imam Khomeini suggests that one is for your inner powers, and the other for your outer powers.

*Hayya 'alas-salah*. Come to the prayer. With this, he says, you tell your heart and every part of you that the time of presence is near. Get ready.

And then *hayya 'alal-falah*, come to success, and *hayya 'ala khayril-'amal*, come to the best of deeds. Here Imam Khomeini says something I find very beautiful. He says these two calls are meant to wake up our *fitrah*, our deepest nature. Because every human being, without exception, loves happiness. We all want perfection, and we all want rest. And the prayer is where both are found. He quotes the Qur'an. Surely by the remembrance of God are hearts set at rest.

These calls are a reminder to your own nature of what it has wanted all along. Come to success. Come to the best of deeds. You were looking for this.

---

And then, in the *iqamah*, the new phrase. *Qad qamatis-salah*. The prayer has begun.

Imam Khomeini says that at this moment you should see yourself standing before the King of all kings. You should feel shy of how little you have done, and come forward with steps of fear and hope. Do not think you have brought any provisions for this journey, he says. Do not think your deeds are good. Think of them, in his words, as worth not a penny. And then, he says, if that feeling settles in the heart, there is reason to hope for God's care. And he quotes the Qur'an. Who answers the distressed one, when he calls on Him, and removes the evil?

In the Spiritual Journey of the Mystics there is a story about a scholar called Haj Agha Malaki. On the last day of his life, lying in bed, washed and perfumed and ready for the midday prayer, he recited the *adhan* and the *iqamah*. The friend who tells the story says that the traditions describe *qad qamatis-salah* as the time of meeting with the Beloved. And he says that, for Haj Agha Malaki, this became real that day. What happened next belongs to a later track. But hold on to that description. *Qad qamatis-salah*. The time of meeting with the Beloved.

---

One last teaching.

Imam Khomeini relates from Imam al-Sadiq that when you say the *adhan* and the *iqamah*, two rows of angels pray behind you. And if you say only the *iqamah*, one row. In another narration, Imam al-Sadiq is asked how long these rows are. He says the shortest is as long as the distance between the east and the west, and the longest is as long as the distance between the heaven and the earth.

So when you pray alone in your room, you are not quite alone. You are leading a congregation.

Imam Khomeini turns this inward too. Inside you, he says, the heart is the leader of the prayer, and all your powers stand behind it as its congregation. So look after its presence. It is praying for everyone.

---

To carry into your next prayer.

Say the *adhan* and the *iqamah*, even when you are alone. Even quietly. And as you say them, picture the court of a great king, and tell your heart, this is an appointment. Remember Imam Khomeini's promise, that the picture, held faithfully, slowly becomes real.

And when you reach *qad qamatis-salah*, let that phrase mean what the friend of Haj Agha Malaki said it means. The time of meeting with the Beloved.

In the next track, the call is over. We stand, and we make our intention.
