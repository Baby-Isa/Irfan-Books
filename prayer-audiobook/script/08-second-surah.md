# Track 8: The Second Surah: al-Ikhlas and al-Qadr

We are still standing. *Al-Fatiha* is complete. You have asked to be guided on the straight path, and the prayer has not yet let you bow. In the first and second *rak'ahs*, there is one more thing to recite. A second surah.

---

The outward form is simple. In the first two *rak'ahs* of every prayer, after *al-Fatiha*, we recite one complete surah of the Qur'an. A whole surah, from its beginning to its end. Not a few verses taken from the middle of one. In the third and fourth *rak'ahs* there is no second surah. We will come to what is said there in a later track.

In the school of the Ahl al-Bayt, the *bismillah* at the head of a surah belongs to that surah. Imam Khomeini says so in the Disciplines of the Prayer. So when you say *bismillah*, in the name of God, after *al-Fatiha*, the second surah has already begun.

Many surahs may be recited here. But two short ones are among the most loved. The first is *al-Ikhlas*, Sincerity, which is also called *al-Tawhid*, the Oneness of God. The second is *al-Qadr*, the surah of the Night of Majesty. This track is about those two.

---

There is a hadith about them which Imam Khomeini places at the end of his long commentary on these surahs, and it is worth hearing first.

It concerns the prayer that the Prophet, peace be upon him and his family, performed in the heavens, on the night of the ascension. In the first *rak'ah*, God revealed to him: Read, O Muhammad, the lineage of your Lord. He is Allah, the One. He is besought by all. He begets not, nor is He begotten. And none is comparable to Him. Then, in the second *rak'ah*, after *al-Fatiha*, God revealed to him: Read, We sent it down. And God said that this is your lineage, and the lineage of your offspring, until the Day of Resurrection.

From this, Imam Khomeini draws a beautiful conclusion. If the surah of *al-Tawhid* belongs to the sacred Essence of God, he says, then the surah of *al-Qadr* belongs to the Ahl al-Bayt.

So the two surahs make a pair. The first tells you who God is. The second tells you how His word comes down to us, and through whom.

---

Before we look at the words, one warning.

These two surahs are so short, and so familiar, that the tongue can recite them while the mind is somewhere else entirely. They can slip by in a few seconds.

In his Forty Hadith, Imam Khomeini quotes the Commander of the Faithful, Imam Ali, on how the Qur'an should be recited. Read it, he says, in a way that will arouse and startle your hardened hearts. And let your aim not be to reach the end of the surah.

Let your aim not be to reach the end of the surah. That is the whole discipline of this moment, in one line.

Imam Khomeini also quotes Imam al-Baqir, who said that there are three kinds of people who recite the Qur'an. One uses it as merchandise, to win favour with the powerful. One memorises its letters perfectly, and neglects what it asks of him. And the third recites it as a remedy for the sickness of his own heart. That third kind, said the Imam, is rarer than the philosopher's stone.

So the second surah is not a formality between *al-Fatiha* and the bow. It is medicine. Let us see what is in it.

---

We begin with *al-Ikhlas*.

Imam Khomeini, in the Disciplines of the Prayer, relates from Imam al-Sadiq how it was revealed. Some people came to the Prophet and said, tell us the lineage of your Lord. Who is His family? Where does He come from? The Prophet gave no reply for three days. Then this surah was revealed.

So this short surah is, in the words of the hadith, the lineage of God. And it is a lineage with no ancestors and no descendants. It describes Him by saying what He is, and then by saying everything He is not.

Imam Khomeini also relates from Imam Zayn al-Abidin that God knew there would be, at the end of time, people of deep thought. So He sent down this surah, and the opening verses of the surah of Iron, for them. The surah is short enough for a child. And deep enough for the deepest thinker.

---

The first verse. *Qul huwa Allahu ahad*. Say: He is Allah, the One.

The first word is *qul*. Say. Imam al-Baqir explains it as, make known what We have revealed to you. And Imam Khomeini makes a quiet point here, which I'll put simply. We cannot describe God from our own selves, in our own language. We can only repeat what He has told us to say. So the surah begins with a command. These are not your words about God. They are His words about Himself, given to you to say.

The next word is *huwa*. He.

Imam al-Baqir, as Imam Khomeini relates, gives a remarkable explanation of this little word. The idol worshippers, he says, used to point at their gods and say, these are our gods. You can see them. You can touch them. Now you, Muhammad, point to yours, so that we can see him too. And God answered with the word *huwa*. He. Not this, which points to something in front of you. But He, which points to someone beyond the reach of the eyes and every other sense.

So the very first thing this surah says about God is that you cannot point to Him. Imam Khomeini says that the hands of our hopes are too short to reach the hem of His majesty. And he reminds us of the words of the Prophet: We have not known You as You should be known.

Then comes the name *Allah*. Imam al-Baqir says that this name carries the sense of the One about whom all creatures are bewildered, unable to grasp His reality. And also the One in whom people take refuge, when something frightens them.

And then *ahad*. One. Imam al-Baqir says that *ahad* means the One who is unique, and has no match. And he adds a lovely detail. Numbers are made of ones. But one itself, he says, is not counted as a number. Two is a number. So God is One, but not in the way that one apple is one. He is not the first in a series.

---

The second verse. *Allahu as-samad*.

This word, *samad*, is hard to put into English, and the Imams gave it many meanings. Imam al-Baqir relates, from his father and his grandfather, Imam al-Husayn, that *as-samad* is the one who has no hollow inside him. The one whose mastery has reached its utmost. The one who neither eats nor drinks, and who does not sleep. The one who has always been, and always will be.

Imam al-Baqir also said that *as-samad* is the chief above whom there is no other chief. And in its most common meaning, as Imam Khomeini explains, it is the great master to whom all people turn for their needs.

Everything else is hollow. Everything else needs. He alone is full, and all needs turn to Him.

---

The third verse. *Lam yalid wa lam yulad*. He begets not, nor is He begotten.

Imam Khomeini, in his Forty Hadith, explains this simply. Nothing breaks away from Him, and He has not broken away from anything. He is the source of everything, and the end to which everything returns, and yet nothing is ever cut off from Him.

And the last verse. *Wa lam yakun lahu kufuwan ahad*. And none is comparable to Him.

Imam Khomeini points out that this short surah walks a narrow path between two mistakes. One mistake is to empty God of every quality, so that He becomes a distant blank. The first verse rules that out. He is Allah. He is real, and full, and present. The other mistake is to picture God like one of His creatures. The rest of the surah rules that out. He has no hollow, no parent, no child, no equal.

---

There is one more thing Imam Khomeini says about this surah, in his Forty Hadith, that I find very moving.

He says that the human heart, by its nature, is drawn towards absolute perfection. And it is repelled by every defect. Everything limited eventually disappoints it. So the heart is always looking for something that is one, and whole, and without any flaw. And he says that this surah describes exactly that. It is, in his words, about the laws of human nature.

So when you recite *al-Ikhlas*, you are not only describing God. You are telling your own heart what it has been looking for all along.

Imam Khomeini also relates a story about the virtue of this surah. When a companion of the Prophet named Sa'd ibn Mu'adh died, the Prophet prayed over his body. And he said that seventy thousand angels, Gabriel among them, had come down to pray over Sa'd. The Prophet asked Gabriel why Sa'd deserved this. And Gabriel said: because he used to recite, Say, He is Allah, the One. Standing and sitting. Riding and walking. Coming and going.

---

Now we turn to the second surah, *al-Qadr*.

*Inna anzalnahu fi laylat al-qadr*. We sent it down on the Night of *Qadr*.

Imam Khomeini notices first that God says, We sent it down. Not, Gabriel brought it. Here the sending is ascribed to God Himself, with the majestic We. Imam Khomeini says this We may point to God with all His names and all His qualities together. The whole of the Divine is behind this Book.

---

Why is it called the Night of *Qadr*? The scholars, Imam Khomeini tells us, gave several answers. Some said it means the Night of Majesty, because a great Book was sent down in it, by a great angel, to a great Messenger, for a great community. Some said it means the Night of Measure, because the affairs of the coming year, the lifespans and the provision of people, are measured out on that night. And some said the angels come down in such numbers that the earth itself becomes narrow for them.

But Imam Khomeini adds his own reflection, and it is the heart of this surah for him. That night was honoured, he says, because of what happened in it. It was the night on which the true lover reached his Beloved.

And then he goes further. He says that, in its inner reality, the Night of *Qadr* is the pure heart of the Prophet himself. The Qur'an coming down into that heart is the descent on the Night of *Qadr*. The light of God was hidden in him, the way the sun is hidden in the night, and from him it rose.

---

*Wa ma adraka ma laylat al-qadr*. And what will make you know what the Night of *Qadr* is?

Imam Khomeini stops at this. These words are addressed to the Prophet himself. Who could know more than he? And yet God asks, what will make you know? He says it is because some truths are too great to be held by arranged words and letters. So God does not describe the night. He only tells us its effects.

*Laylat al-qadri khayrun min alfi shahr*. The Night of *Qadr* is better than a thousand months.

On the outward level, Imam Khomeini says, the worship of that one night is better than a thousand months without it. And on the inner level, he reports, some of the gnostics have said that the thousand months stand for all the kinds of created things. The Perfect Man, the Prophet, is better than all of them together.

---

*Tanazzalul-mala'ikatu war-ruhu fiha*. The angels and the Spirit descend in it, by the permission of their Lord, for every affair.

Who is the Spirit? Imam Khomeini relates that Imam al-Sadiq was asked about it. He said it is a creature greater than Gabriel and Michael. It was with the Messenger of God, and it is with the Imams.

And where do the angels descend? Imam Khomeini says they come down to the guardian of the affair, the Imam of each age. In our own time, he says, that is the Imam of the Age, the son of Imam al-Hasan al-'Askari. On that night, the affairs of the year are laid open before him.

Then the last verse. *Salamun hiya hatta matla'il-fajr*. Peace it is, until the rising of the dawn.

Imam Khomeini gives several meanings. The night is safe from every evil of Satan until morning. Or the angels go about greeting the friends of God with peace, from God, all night long. And in another place he suggests that this peace is a hint at the journey of the soul. The life of this world is like a dark night. For the perfect friends of God, he says, the whole journey through that night is kept safe from Satan, until the dawn, when they see the beauty of the One.

Imam Khomeini even says that, seen with the eye of unity, the whole world is a single Night of *Qadr*. And whoever is certain of this is always in it.

---

To carry into your next prayer.

When you reach *al-Ikhlas*, slow down at the word *huwa*. He. Remember Imam al-Baqir. Not this, which you can point to. He, who is beyond every sense. And let the heart turn, for one moment, towards the One it has been looking for all along.

When you recite *al-Qadr*, remember that you are reciting about a descent. The Qur'an came down into the heart of the Prophet on a night better than a thousand months. And every time you recite it, something of that descent is still reaching you. So do not hurry. Let your aim not be to reach the end of the surah.

When the second surah is complete, we raise our hands, say *Allahu akbar*, and bow. In the next track, we go down into the *ruku'*.
