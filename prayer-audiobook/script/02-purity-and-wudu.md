# Track 2: Purity and Wudu

We ended the last track with water. So that is where we begin.

Before the first *Allahu akbar*, before you even face the qiblah, there is the washing. Wudu. It takes a minute or two. Most of us do it quickly, sometimes while thinking about something else entirely. In this track, we are going to slow it right down. We will ask what purity actually means. Then we will go through wudu, one washing at a time, and ask what each one is for. And at the end, we will look briefly at what happens when there is no water at all.

---

Let us start with the word itself. In Arabic, purity is *taharah*. Cleanness. Being fit to come near.

Imam Khomeini, in his book on the disciplines of the prayer, *Adab al-Salat*, begins his section on purity with a simple observation. The prayer, he says, has an outward form and an inward reality. And so purity has an outward form and an inward reality too. The outward rules have been set out by the jurists of the Ja'fari school, and he does not repeat them. What he wants to talk about is the inner purity.

He reminds us what the prayer is for. Its reality, he says, is ascension, and arriving in the presence of God. And on that road there are obstacles. He calls them impurities. Some are outward, and some are hidden.

Then he gives three stages, which I will put simply.

The first stage of purity is keeping the law. Doing what God has asked, and leaving what He has forbidden. Imam Khomeini is firm about this. Nobody, he says, should imagine that they can purify the heart while ignoring the outside. He calls that idea one of the great tricks of Satan.

The second stage is purity of character. Replacing bad habits with good ones. Pride with humility. Envy with generosity.

And the third stage is the purity of the heart. He describes it as handing the heart over to God. When that happens, he says, the heart becomes full of light, and the light flows out from the heart into everything else. Light upon light.

So purity is not only a matter of the body. But it starts with the body. That is the order. The outside first, and through the outside, the inside.

And there is one more thing he says, which I find very hopeful. All the powers God gave us, our eyes, our hands, our hearts, came to us pure. They were trusts, he says, given from the unseen world, clean and full of light. Whatever has stained them since, the task is not to become something new. It is to give the trusts back the way they were given.

---

Now, before we come to the washings, let us hear how Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq, peace be upon him, speaks about water itself. This is from the Lantern of the Path, in the chapter on purification.

He says this. If you seek purification and wudu, then go to water as you would go to the mercy of God. For He has made water the key to being near to Him, and a guide to the domain of His service.

Go to the water as you would go to the mercy of God.

That changes the walk to the sink, does it not? The Imam goes on. Just as God's mercy washes away the wrongdoing of His servants, so outward impurities are washed away by water. And he quotes the Qur'an. We have made every living thing from water.

Then he gives four short instructions. Imam Khomeini, in the Disciplines of the Prayer, takes them one by one, so let us do the same.

The first is about the limbs. Reflect, says the Imam, on the purity of water, its fineness, its blessing, and how gently it mixes with everything. And use it to purify the limbs God has told you to purify.

The second is about people. Deal with God's creation, he says, the way water deals with things. Water mixes with everything and gives everything its due, but it never changes in its own nature. And he quotes the Prophet, peace be upon him and his family. The sincere believer is like water. Imam Khomeini explains this beautifully. You can be among people, kind to all of them, giving each person what is right for them, and still never lose your own centre, which is your attention to God.

The third is about God Himself. Let your purity with God, says the Imam, be like the purity of water when He sent it down from the sky, and called it *tahur*. Purifying. Rain, before anything has touched it.

And the fourth gathers everything together. Purify your heart with *taqwa* and *yaqin*, as you purify your limbs with water.

*Taqwa* is mindfulness of God, the guarding of the self. *Yaqin* is certainty. So the Imam is saying: as the water passes over your face and arms, let something else pass over your heart.

Purify your heart with *taqwa* and *yaqin*, as you purify your limbs with water. That is perhaps the single most useful sentence in this whole track. We will come back to it at the end.

---

Why these limbs, though? Why the face, the arms, the head and the feet, and not the whole body?

Imam Khomeini quotes an answer from Imam al-Rida, peace be upon him. The servant was commanded to make wudu, said the Imam, so that he would be pure when he stands before the All-Powerful. And these are the parts that are exposed when we stand before Him. With the face, we prostrate. With the hands, we ask, we hope, we fear, and we plead. With the head, we bow. And with the feet, we stand and we sit.

So each part we wash is a part that is about to worship. You are getting the instruments ready.

And Imam Khomeini relates another narration, from the Prophet, which takes us right back to the beginning of the human story. When Satan whispered to Adam, Adam looked at the forbidden tree. That was the face. He walked towards it. Those were the feet. He took from it with his hand, and ate. Then, when he realised what he had done, he put his hand on the top of his head, and wept. God accepted his repentance. But He asked Adam and his children to purify those four places. The face, because it looked. The hands and arms, because they took. The head is wiped, because Adam laid his hand on it in grief. And the feet are wiped, because they walked towards the sin.

So every wudu is a small return. It is the children of Adam, retracing his steps, and asking to be taken back.

---

Now, let us go through the wudu itself, slowly. For each step, I will say what is done, in the Ja'fari way. Then I will give you what Sayyid Haydar Amuli says about it.

You will remember Amuli's three levels from the first track. The outward law. The path. And the reality. He goes through wudu on all three. On the level of the law, he describes it exactly as the school of the Prophet's household performs it. Then he goes deeper.

It begins with the intention. You intend to make wudu, for God, to draw near to Him. On the level of the path, Amuli says, the intention is that you resolve, in your heart, to do nothing that goes against what pleases God. And to make every act of worship for Him alone.

Before the obligatory part, it is recommended to wash the hands, and to rinse the mouth and the nose. And many like to clean their teeth first, with a *siwak*, a small twig from a tree, or a brush.

The Lantern of the Path has a short chapter on the *siwak*, and it is lovely. The teeth, says Imam al-Sadiq, are pure jewels. They become stained because they are there when we eat. When you clean them, they go back to how they were. Then he says this. In the same way, God created the heart pure and clean. And He made its food remembrance, reflection, awe and respect. When the heart turns grey, from being fed on heedlessness, it is polished by repentance, and washed with the water of regret. And it goes back to its first state.

---

Then the face. You wash it from the hairline down to the chin. Top to bottom.

Imam al-Rida said, the face is what prostrates. And in the story of Adam, the face is what looked at the tree.

On the level of the path, Amuli says, you wash the face of your heart clean of the world. Of everything in it that pulls at you.

And on the level of the reality, he goes further. You wash the face of the heart clean of seeing anything other than God. So that wherever you look, you see only His face. He quotes the Qur'an. Wherever you turn, there is the face of God.

---

Then the arms. First the right, from the elbow down to the fingertips. Then the left, the same way.

The hands, said Imam al-Rida, are what ask, what hope, what fear and what plead. In the story of Adam, they are what reached out and took.

On the level of the path, Amuli says, you wash your hands of whatever is in their grasp. Money, possessions, all the things we hold on to.

And on the level of the reality, he says something surprising. You wash your hands even of your good deeds. Even of your hopes for paradise. Because, he says, being pleased with your own worship is itself a kind of disobedience, in the eyes of the people of God. And he quotes a saying. A bad deed that makes you regret is better than a good deed that makes you proud.

So the hands come away empty. Ready to ask.

---

Then the head. With the moisture still on your right hand, you wipe the front of the head. No new water is taken.

In the story of Adam, the head is where he put his hand, and wept.

On the level of the path, Amuli says, you are wiping your true head, which is your mind. You are checking it, he says, to see whether any trace of love for the world is still there.

And on the level of the reality, the wiping of the head means raising your innermost self above the ego. Above the sense of a separate "I" that stands between you and the One you love. Amuli quotes a line from one of the mystics, which is really a prayer. I will put it simply.

Between me and You there is a self that stands against me. So by Your grace, take this self from between us.

---

And then the feet. With what moisture is left, you wipe the right foot, from the tips of the toes to the ankle. Then the left.

Imam al-Rida said, the feet are what stand and sit in the prayer. In the story of Adam, they are what walked towards the tree.

On the level of the path, Amuli says, wiping the feet means stopping them from walking anywhere that is not for God's pleasure.

And on the level of the reality, he recalls what God said to Moses at the burning bush. Take off your shoes. You are in the sacred valley. When you arrive, says Amuli, you no longer need your shoes. All the effort and striving that carried you here can be laid down.

---

Amuli then quotes a saying of the Prophet. Wudu upon wudu is light upon light. He explains it like this. When the inner wudu is added to the outer one, the light of the inward is added to the light of the outward. That is the whole point. Not to replace the washing with a meaning. But to let the meaning ride on the washing.

And a word, here, about doing too much.

You will remember from the first track Imam Khomeini's warning about *waswas*, obsessive doubt. He says that wudu is one of the places where it strikes hardest. In the Disciplines of the Prayer, he relates that someone once described to Imam al-Sadiq a man who kept repeating his wudu and his prayer, and called him a man of good sense. The Imam replied, what good sense is there in obeying Satan?

Imam Khomeini also points out that the Prophet washed his face with a single handful of water, his right arm with another, and his left with a third. A second washing does no harm. But a third, he says, is an innovation, and actually spoils the wudu. So the person who washes ten or twenty times, thinking they are being extra careful, may not be making wudu at all.

The water is mercy. It is not something to be anxious over. One careful washing, done with the heart present, is worth more than twenty done in fear.

---

Here is how some of the Imams approached this moment. Imam Khomeini relates that Imam al-Sajjad, the fourth Imam, peace be upon him, would turn pale whenever the time came to make wudu. Someone asked him what came over him. And he said: Do you not know before whose presence I am about to stand?

That is what wudu is. Not the prayer itself, but the doorway to it. The moment when you remember who you are about to meet.

And in the Forty Hadith, Imam Khomeini mentions a teacher of his, whom he calls simply our Shaykh. This teacher would advise his students to stay in a state of wudu all the time. Because, he would say, it is like a warrior's armour.

Imam Khomeini also says a word about the other great washing, the *ghusl*, the bathing of the whole body. Wudu, he says, is a limited light. The *ghusl* is an unlimited light. But the principle is the same.

---

Finally, what if there is no water? Or you are ill, and water would harm you?

Then there is *tayammum*. Purification with earth. You strike the palms of both hands on clean earth. You wipe your forehead with them, from the hairline down. And then you wipe the back of each hand with the palm of the other.

It sounds like a lesser thing. A stand-in. But listen to what the sources make of it.

Imam Khomeini, in the Disciplines of the Prayer, says this. The main road to God is through His mercy, and water is the sign of that mercy. But suppose the traveller finds his hand has fallen short of the water of mercy. Then he has one thing left. His own poverty. His own need. When he looks honestly at how helpless he is, and lets go of his pride, a door of mercy opens. And the earth of his own nature, he says, is turned into the white earth of mercy. Earth that purifies.

He gives a picture of this which I love. A small child, he says, sets out to walk on its own, proud of its own steps. And the father lets it go. But when the child realises it cannot manage, and turns back and reaches for its father, the father picks it up, holds it, and teaches it to walk, one step at a time.

Sayyid Haydar Amuli says something very similar, drawing on an earlier master. Earth, he says, is the lowest of the elements. It is also what we were made from. So when the servant puts his hands on the dust, he is reminded of where he came from, and of his poverty before God. And that humility, says Amuli, is the very aim of worship. He quotes a saying of the Arabs. When a man is in need, his hands become dusty.

So *tayammum* teaches something water does not. Water says: here is God's mercy, coming down to you. Earth says: here is your need, reaching up to Him. Both purify.

---

To carry into your next prayer.

When the water touches your face, remember the words of the Lantern of the Path. Purify your heart with *taqwa* and *yaqin*, as you purify your limbs with water. Let the outer washing and the inner washing happen at the same moment.

And when you wipe your feet, ask yourself, just once, where they have walked today. Then remember Adam, who walked towards the tree, and was forgiven.

In the next track, we move from the body to everything around it. The time of the prayer, the place, the clothes we stand in, and the direction we face.
