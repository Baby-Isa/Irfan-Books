# Track 6: Allahu Akbar: The Opening Takbir and Raising the Hands

We are standing on the prayer mat. We are facing the qiblah. The intention has been made, quietly, in the heart. Everything up to this point has been preparation.

Now comes the door.

It is only two words. *Allahu akbar*. God is greater. You have said them more times than you could count. In this track, we slow them right down. And at the end, there is a story of a man for whom this first *Allahu akbar* was, quite literally, the door out of this world.

---

First, the outward form.

The prayer begins with one *takbir*. One saying of *Allahu akbar*. This first one has its own name. The *takbirat al-ihram*. The *takbir* of entering the sacred state. It is a pillar of the prayer. Without it, the prayer has not begun. You say it standing, still, facing the qiblah, in Arabic, just as it is. *Allahu akbar*.

As you say it, it is recommended to raise your hands to the level of your ears, with the palms facing the qiblah. And in the school of the Ahl al-Bayt, it is also recommended to say seven *takbirs* at the opening of the prayer, with the hands raised each time. One of those seven is the *takbirat al-ihram*. The others are like an entrance hall leading up to it.

One more thing, and it is a gentle one. Say it once, and let it be. Imam Khomeini, in his Forty Hadith, speaks of people who repeat the intention and the *takbirat al-ihram* over and over, sometimes for an hour, and think it is piety. He says it is the work of Satan. So say it once, calmly, and walk through the door.

---

Now, the words.

*Allah*. The name of God. Not a description of Him, but His name.

And then *akbar*. This is where it gets interesting.

In Arabic, the word for great is *kabir*. *Akbar* is a different form of the same word. It means greater. It is sometimes translated as the greatest, but its plainest meaning is greater.

And notice what is missing. Greater than what? The sentence does not say. It just stops. *Allahu akbar*. God is greater. And the space after it is left open.

That open space is the whole point. God is great would be a description. You would be measuring Him with your own idea of greatness. *Allahu akbar* refuses to do that. Whatever you think of, He is greater than that. Whatever you are about to say about Him in this prayer, He is greater than that too.

Imam Khomeini, in the Disciplines of the Prayer, which in Arabic is called *Adab al-Salat*, puts it simply. The *takbir* means that God is greater than any description.

He also quotes a saying of Imam Ali, peace be upon him. When the caller to prayer says *Allahu akbar*, Imam Ali says, it is as if he were saying that God's attribute is far above being grasped by those who describe Him. For the describers describe Him according to their own measure, and not according to the measure of His greatness and majesty.

According to their own measure. That is what we always do. We describe God in our size. And *Allahu akbar* is the moment we admit it.

Imam Khomeini adds something lovely. Every *takbir*, he says, is an announcement of our own inability. We are about to praise God for the whole length of the prayer. And before we even start, we confess that our praise will fall short. That is why the *takbir* comes back at every change from one stage of the prayer to the next. So that two things are made firm in the heart. Our own inability. And the greatness of God.

---

Now, back to the name. The *takbirat al-ihram*.

You will know the word *ihram* from the pilgrimage. The pilgrim puts on the two simple white cloths, makes his intention, and enters the state of *ihram*. From that moment, things that were ordinary a minute ago become forbidden. The word comes from the same root as *haram*. Forbidden, and also sacred. A sanctuary.

The opening *takbir* does exactly the same thing to you.

A moment before it, you could speak, laugh, eat, drink, or turn around. The moment you say *Allahu akbar*, all of that becomes forbidden. You have stepped into a sanctuary, and you stay inside it until the final *salam* releases you.

Sayyid Haydar Amuli, in Inner Secrets of the Path, makes this connection himself. He says the opening *takbir* shows that you have entered the sanctity of the prayer, and that everything else is now excluded, just as for the pilgrims making for the House of God.

You may remember from the first track that Amuli looks at everything on three levels. The law, the path, and the reality. On the level of the law, the *takbir* is what we have just described.

On the level of the path, Amuli says, with this *takbir* you forbid your self from doing anything that might conflict with the command of God, or go beyond the bounds of His pleasure, whether in speech or in action. So it is not only the body that enters *ihram*. The self does too.

And on the level of the reality, he says something very beautiful. The *takbirat al-ihram*, he says, means that you are forbidding yourself from approaching any door but His door.

Any door but His door. Hold on to that. When you say *Allahu akbar*, every other door is closed for a while. There is only one door left open.

---

Now, Imam Khomeini is very honest about what actually happens to most of us at this moment.

In his Forty Hadith he says this. Our misfortune is that we store up all our stray thoughts and cares for the time of worship. As soon as we say the *takbirat al-ihram*, it is as if we have opened a shop, or a ledger of accounts, or an album. Our heart wanders off to other matters. And when we come back to ourselves, we have reached the *salam*.

In the Disciplines of the Prayer, he says it is as if the *takbirat al-ihram* were the key to the shop.

The *takbir* was meant to close every door but one. And instead, for so many of us, it is the very key that opens all the others.

That is not a reason for despair. It is simply where most of us begin.

---

Now the passage from the Lantern of the Path at the heart of this track. It is in the chapter called Opening the Prayer. Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq, peace be upon him, says this.

Stand at the foot of fear and hope. When you recite the *takbir*, you should belittle what is between the high heavens and the moist earth, which are all below His glory. For when God looks into the heart of His servant while he is saying the *takbir*, and sees in his heart something obstructing the truth of his declaring that God is great, He says, O liar! Do you try to deceive Me? By My might and My majesty, I will deny you the sweetness of My remembrance, and I will veil you from My nearness, and from joy in My intimate communion.

O liar. Do you try to deceive Me?

Those are hard words. But they are not there to frighten us. They are there to make us honest.

When you say *Allahu akbar*, you are making a claim. You are saying, nothing in my heart is bigger than God. Now suppose that, at that very moment, what is really biggest in your heart is a worry about money. Or what someone thinks of you. Then the tongue is saying one thing, and the heart is saying another. That gap between them is what the Imam calls a lie.

Sayyid Haydar Amuli says it in one plain sentence. The one who prays should not say *Allahu akbar* while there still remains something greater than God in his heart.

And Imam Khomeini, in the Disciplines of the Prayer, gives a very practical sign that something else has grown big in the heart. It is when we prefer the pleasure of people to the pleasure of God. When, to keep someone else happy, we are willing to displease our Creator.

---

Now look closely at what God says He will do to the one whose *takbir* is a lie. He does not say, I will strike you down. He says, I will deny you the sweetness of My remembrance. I will veil you from My nearness, and from joy in talking with Me.

Imam Khomeini picks this up, and speaks very tenderly. My dear, he says, the reason our hearts are deprived of the sweetness of remembering God, and the reason our spirit does not taste the joy of talking with Him, is that our hearts are sick, attracted to the world, and stuck to it.

So the punishment in this hadith is not something far away. Most of us already know it. It is the dryness. The prayer that feels like a chore. The *salam* that comes as a relief. That is what it feels like when the *takbir* is said by the tongue, and not by the heart.

And the other side is just as true. Imam Khomeini quotes another hadith from the Lantern of the Path. As you say the *takbir*, it says, regard all creatures as small beside the greatness of God. Then examine your heart during the prayer. If you taste the sweetness of the prayer, and your heart enjoys talking with God, then know that God has accepted your *takbirs*.

So the sweetness is a sign that the heart and the tongue were saying the same thing.

How do we get there? Imam Khomeini says not to stop at the outward form of the *takbir*. First we explain to our own heart, by reflection, that all greatness belongs to God alone, and every created thing is poor and in need. Then, by remembering it again and again, we make the heart familiar with this truth. When that happens, he says, the heart, the tongue, the outside and the inside all come into harmony. All the powers of the person, seen and unseen, say *Allahu akbar* together. And one of the thick curtains is drawn away.

---

Which brings us to the hands.

As you say the opening *takbir*, it is recommended to lift your hands to your ears, palms facing the qiblah. Imam Khomeini, in his Forty Hadith, comments on a tradition that says, make it your practice to raise your hands during prayer, and to turn them. He thinks this most likely means raising them at the *takbirs*, with the palms turned towards the qiblah. Some scholars even held it to be obligatory, so, he says, do not leave it out if you can help it.

Then he speaks of its beauty. Raising the hands at each *takbir*, he says, gives the prayer a kind of elegance. And he mentions a narration from Imam Ali that this is how Gabriel and the angels of the seven heavens pray.

Then he gives the reason, in the words of Imam Ali al-Rida, peace be upon him, which I will read as Imam Khomeini gives them.

The reason for raising the hands at the *takbir* is that it carries a kind of absorption, sincere dedication, and humility. God loves His servant to be totally attentive towards Him at the time of remembering Him, humble and sincere. And it is so that one's attention is gathered by the raising of the hands, and one becomes alert in one's intent, with a receptive heart.

Three words there are worth keeping. The first is *inqita'*. Being cut off from everything else. The second is *takhlis*. Making yourself pure and sincere for Him. And the third is *tadarru'*. Humility. A lowering of the self.

And notice that last line. The body helps the heart. Lifting your hands is a way of saying, I am here now.

---

Imam Khomeini then adds what some of the mystics have said, and this is the secret behind the raising of the hands.

As you raise your hands, they say, you are throwing everything other than God behind your back. Every thorn on the path to Him, they say, is cleared away. You are cut off from everything else, and only then does the real ascent begin. The *mi'raj* of the prayer.

He goes on. With the seven opening *takbirs*, he says, seven curtains are drawn back. The friends of God remove a curtain with every *takbir*. Behind each curtain, a new light appears to them. But they do not stop there, however beautiful it is. They do not let it hold them. They raise their hands again, and say *Allahu akbar* again. God is greater than this too.

And he quotes a tradition about the Prophet himself, peace be upon him and his family. On the night of the ascension, the Prophet passed through seven veils. At every veil he said *Allahu akbar*. And through that, God brought him to the furthest point of honour.

So when you raise your hands seven times at the start of the prayer, you are, in a small way, walking the road of the Prophet's ascension. Veil after veil. Greater. And greater. And greater.

At the last *takbir*, he says, the traveller declares with the Prophet Abraham, I have turned my face to Him who originated the heavens and the earth.

He admits that people like us can hardly understand these things. But, to put his point simply, do not deny them just because you have not reached them. Believing in them keeps the fire of longing alive.

---

And now a story, from the book called the Spiritual Journey of the Mystics.

It is about Mirza Javad Malaki Tabrizi, known as Haj Agha Malaki. He taught law and ethics in the city of Qum, about a hundred years ago, and he was a mystic of great depth. His friends remembered that at night he would go out into the yard, look up at the sky, and recite the verse, Lo! In the creation of the earth and the sky there are signs. And in prayer he wept a great deal, especially in the *qunut*.

The story of his death was told by another close friend, Haj Agha Hussein Fatimi.

The friend had just come back from the mosque at Jamkaran, and was told that Haj Agha Malaki had been asking for him. He knew that Haj Agha Malaki was ill, so he hurried to him. He remembered it as a Friday afternoon. He found him lying in bed, neat and clean. He had bathed. He had put on perfume. His hair was dyed. He was ready for the noon and afternoon prayers.

There, on his bed, Haj Agha Malaki recited the *adhan* and the *iqamah*. He recited some supplications. Then he raised his hands for the *takbirat al-ihram*, and said, *Allahu akbar*.

And as he said it, his spirit left this world.

His friend said that at that moment, the meaning of the saying, the prayer is the believer's ascension, became plain to see. And he said that the words of the *iqamah*, *qad qamat as-salat*, the prayer is ready, which the traditions describe as the time of meeting with the Beloved, came true for him. Because he hurried, through the prayer, to meet his Beloved.

Think of it. He spoke the word that opens the door, and the door opened all the way.

A story like this shows us what the opening *takbir* really is. It is a leaving. A small rehearsal, five times a day, for the great leaving. He had made that journey so many times in prayer that, when the real one came, he took it the same way. Hands raised, saying God is greater.

---

To carry into your next prayer.

Before the opening *takbir*, ask yourself one honest question. What is big in my heart right now? Just name it. A worry, a plan, a person, a hurt. You do not need to fight it. Simply notice it. And then, as you say *Allahu akbar*, let the words do what they mean. God is greater than that.

And as your hands come up beside your ears, remember the words of the mystics that Imam Khomeini passes on. You are putting everything else behind your back. For the length of this prayer, you have closed every door but His door.

In the next track, we walk through that door, and hear the first words spoken inside. *Al-Fatiha*, word by word.
