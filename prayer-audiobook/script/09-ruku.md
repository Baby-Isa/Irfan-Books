# Track 9: Ruku': The Bow

The recitation is complete. In the first two *rak'ahs*, that means *al-Fatiha* and the second surah. In the second *rak'ah*, there is also the *qunut*, which has a track of its own later on. And now the body leaves the upright position. In the first *rak'ah*, this is the first time it has done so since the prayer began. We bow.

---

Here is the outward form, kept short.

Before bowing, it is recommended to say *Allahu akbar*, God is greater, raising the hands as at the opening of the prayer. Then you bend forward from the waist, until your hands can rest on your knees. The back is level. You remain still in the bow for a moment. And you say the words of the bow.

The words are *subhana rabbiyal-'azimi wa bihamdih*. Glory be to my Lord, the Great, and with His praise. Saying it once is enough. Instead of it, you may say *subhan Allah*, glory be to God, three times.

Then you rise, and stand fully upright again, and are still for a moment. As you rise, it is recommended to say *sami' Allahu liman hamidah*. God hears the one who praises Him.

That is the whole of the outward form. A bow, a few words, and a return to standing. Let us see what is inside it.

---

We start with the *takbir* before the bow. Imam Khomeini, in the Disciplines of the Prayer, has something lovely to say about it.

He says that while we were standing, we were describing God. We praised Him in *al-Fatiha*. We declared His oneness in the second surah. And now, before we bow, we say *Allahu akbar*. God is greater. Greater, even, than all those descriptions we have just been making.

He says that a servant should not feel proud of his describing and his praising. He is weak, and he has nothing of his own. And Imam Khomeini reminds us of the words of Imam Zayn al-Abidin, in one of his supplications. How can I thank You, with this feeble tongue of mine?

So as you raise your hands for this *takbir*, Imam Khomeini says, it is as if you throw behind you, with your own hand, all your describing and all your praising. You raise your hands with the palms empty, facing the *qiblah*. And empty-handed, with a heart full of fear and hope, you enter the bow.

Fear that you have fallen short. And hope in the One who has honoured you with a station that belongs, in his words, to the bosom friends of God and to the perfect lovers.

---

Now, why bow at all?

Imam Khomeini says that the prayer has three principal positions. Standing, bowing and prostrating. Everything else prepares for them. And he explains them in terms that he says are suited to ordinary people, which I'll put more simply still.

The prayer, he says, stands on two foundations. The first is letting go of self-importance. The second is seeking God. And these two take shape gradually through the three positions.

In the standing, you let go of the idea that you are the one who really acts. In the bow, you let go of something deeper. You let go of the idea that your qualities are your own. Your knowledge, your power, your life, your will. All of it comes from Him. And in the prostration, you let go of yourself altogether.

Then Imam Khomeini says something very honest. In the bow, he says, we are claiming that in the whole house of existence there is no knowledge, no power, no life and no will except from God. That is a great claim. A delicate state. And he says plainly that people like us are not qualified to make it.

So what do we do? We turn inwardly to God with humility. We admit our shortcomings. And we hope that He will show us kindness, and reach out a hand to help us when we need it.

That is the bow. Not a claim to have arrived. An admission that we have not.

---

Sayyid Haydar Amuli, in Inner Secrets of the Path, offers a picture of the three positions that you may find helpful.

He says that human beings move upright. Animals move horizontally, along the ground. And plants grow with their roots downward, into the earth. So the standing in the prayer is the posture of the human being. The bow is the posture of the animals. And the prostration is the posture of the plants.

When you bow, he says, you are returning backwards, to your origin. You are remembering your humble beginnings, your need, and your dependence. You came into being by stages, from the plant to the animal to the human. And in the bow and the prostration, you go back down the ladder you came up. Not as a humiliation. As a return to the truth about yourself.

Amuli also says that there are angels whose whole duty is to stand, angels whose whole duty is to bow, and angels whose whole duty is to prostrate. When you bow, you join them.

And in another place he goes further. He says that when the human being bows before God, the earthly world and the unseen world bow with him. Because the human being has been made God's deputy in both of them, and everything in creation follows him.

So your bow is not only yours. When you bend, the world bends with you.

---

Now to the words of the bow.

Imam Khomeini relates a narration in the Disciplines of the Prayer. When the verse was revealed, Glorify the name of your Lord, the Great, the Prophet, peace be upon him and his family, said: Put this in your bow.

So the words of the bow come straight from the Qur'an. Let us take them one at a time.

*Subhana*. Glory be. This is a word of purifying. It declares that God is far above every flaw, every limit, every description. Imam Khomeini says that to glorify God is to declare Him free of every description and definition. Whatever picture of Him has formed in your mind, *subhana* says: He is beyond that.

*Rabbi*. My Lord. The word *Rabb* means the Lord who owns, and cares for, and brings His creature up, stage by stage, to what it is meant to become. And notice the small ending. Not the Lord. My Lord. In this tremendous moment of glorification, the word is personal. He is the Lord of the worlds, and He is also yours.

*Al-'azim*. The Great. The Tremendous. Imam Khomeini says that to call God great is to deny that He has any limit.

And there is a beautiful detail here. Imam Khomeini quotes a hadith from al-Kafi saying that the first name God took for Himself was *al-'Aliyy al-'Azim*. The Most High, the Great. And he suggests that *al-'Aliyy*, the Most High, may be the first of the names of His Essence. And *al-'Azim*, the Great, the first of the names of His qualities.

Remember what he said about the bow. It is the station where we let go of our own qualities, and see that every quality belongs to Him. So the name we say in the bow is the first of the names of His qualities. *Al-'azim*. And in the prostration, as we will hear in the next track, the name changes.

*Wa bihamdih*. And with His praise.

Imam Khomeini says that glorifying, magnifying and praising belong together in the bow. Glorifying says He is beyond every description. Magnifying says He has no limit. And praising says that He appears, in all His beauty, in the mirrors of creation. So with these few words, you say two things at once. He is beyond everything. And everything beautiful is His. He is not confined in any created form. And yet nothing in the world is more evident than Him.

*Subhana rabbiyal-'azimi wa bihamdih*. Glory be to my Lord, the Great, and with His praise.

---

Imam Khomeini also relates a hadith about how these words were first said.

On the night of the ascension, the Prophet was praying in the heavens. When he bowed, God said to him, look at My Throne. The Prophet said: I looked at a greatness which astonished my soul, and I fell into a swoon. Then I was inspired to say, Glory be to my Lord, the Great, and with His praise, because of the greatness that I saw. When I said it, I came back to myself. And he said it seven times, until he had fully come back to himself.

So these words were first spoken by someone overwhelmed. Someone who had seen a greatness too great to bear, and was given these words by God to steady him.

And here, once again, Imam Khomeini is very honest about us. We have not been admitted into that privacy, he says. We have not seen what the Prophet saw. So what can we do? He says it is good to use our incapability and our humility as our capital. Our helplessness is the one thing we can bring. And he says we should keep clinging to the hem of the One we are seeking, because God's habit is to be generous.

---

Now we come to the chapter on the bow in the Lantern of the Path. It is short. Here is most of it, as Imam al-Sadiq spoke it.

The servant of God does not truly bow, but that God adorns him with the light of His radiance, shades him in the shade of His greatness, and clothes him in the garment of His purity.

Bowing is first and prostration is second. There is courtesy in bowing, and in prostration nearness to God. Whoever is not good in the courtesy is not fit for nearness.

So bow, says the Imam, with the bow of one who is humble to God, abased in his heart and fearful under His power. Submitting his limbs to God, like one who is fearful and sorrowful at what he might miss of the blessings of those who bow.

Then the Imam tells a story. A man named Rabi' ibn Khuthaym used to stay awake all night, until dawn, in a single bow. In the morning he would sigh and say: Oh! The sincere have gone ahead, and we are cut off.

A whole night in one bow. And in the morning, he did not feel that he had achieved something. He felt that he had been left behind.

And the Imam continues. Make your bow properly by keeping your back straight. Let your heart flee from the whisperings, tricks and deceits of Satan. God will elevate His servants according to their humility to Him.

Imam Khomeini, commenting on this, draws out the point about order. The nearness of the prostration, he says, cannot be reached except through a true bow. Whoever wants to be fit for the second must first learn the courtesy of the first.

So the bow is where we learn our manners before God. We do not rush into His presence. We bow first.

And the promise is in the last line. God elevates His servants according to their humility. The lower you bow in your heart, the higher He lifts you.

---

Now we rise from the bow.

As you stand up straight, the recommended words are *sami' Allahu liman hamidah*. God hears the one who praises Him.

Imam Khomeini, in the Disciplines of the Prayer, quotes a sacred hadith in which God says that He has divided the prayer between Himself and His servant. When the servant says, All praise belongs to God, God says, My servant has praised Me. And Imam Khomeini adds: this is the meaning of, God hears the one who praises Him.

So these words, as you rise, are a kind of answer. You praised Him in *al-Fatiha*. You praised Him again in the bow, *wa bihamdih*. And now you stand and say: He heard.

Sayyid Haydar Amuli says something similar. He says that the fruit of glorifying God in the bow, after you have seen your own humility and need, is that you can stand upright again. And then the words, God hears the one who praises Him, mean that you witness God hearing everything. Above all, the words addressed to Him. He hears the one who prays, Amuli says, just as the one who prays hears Him. And he quotes one of the Imams, who said: I would repeat the verse until I heard it from the One who said it.

Imam Khomeini adds one more thing about the rising, which is very fine. He says that the important discipline here is to turn the heart completely towards God, and to stop paying attention to oneself. Even, he says, to one's own humility.

That is the last lesson of the bow. You bowed in humility. And now, as you stand, you let go even of that. You do not admire how humble you were. You simply turn to Him.

---

To carry into your next prayer.

When you bow, say *subhana rabbiyal-'azimi wa bihamdih* once, slowly, and lean on the word *rabbi*. My Lord. The Great One, beyond every description, is yours.

And as you rise, and say *sami' Allahu liman hamidah*, let it land in the heart as news. You praised Him, and He heard you.

Then we say *Allahu akbar* once more, and go down to the earth. In the next track, the prostration, where the courtesy of the bow becomes nearness.
