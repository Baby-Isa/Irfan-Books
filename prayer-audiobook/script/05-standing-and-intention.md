# Track 5: Standing and Intention

The call is over. The last words of the *iqamah* have been said. The prayer has begun.

And now you are standing. You are facing the qiblah. The first *Allahu akbar* has not yet been said. There is a short moment here, before the door of the prayer closes behind you. In this track we stay inside that moment.

Two things happen in it. You stand, and you intend. In Arabic, the standing is called *qiyam*, and the intention is called *niyyah*. We will take them one at a time. And along the way we will meet a third word, which is the soul of the intention. *Ikhlas*. Sincerity.

---

First, the outward form. It is very simple.

In the obligatory prayer, you stand, if you are able to. You stand upright and still. If you cannot stand, you pray sitting. And if you cannot sit, you pray lying down. Sayyid Haydar Amuli lists the standing first among the obligatory parts of the prayer, and he adds those words, when one is able. Nobody is asked for more than they can give.

While you stand, it is recommended to keep your eyes lowered, on the place where your forehead will rest in prostration.

And the intention? In the school of the Ahl al-Bayt, the intention is simply this. You know which prayer you are about to pray. And you are praying it for God, to draw near to Him. Many people hold that in the words *qurbatan ilallah*, seeking nearness to God. The intention is made just before the opening *takbir*, and it stays with you to the end. It does not need to be spoken aloud. It lives in the heart.

That is all. And as we will hear, Imam Khomeini is very firm that it should stay that simple.

---

Let us start with the standing.

Imam Khomeini, in the Disciplines of the Prayer, says that the people of knowledge see the three great postures of the prayer as three signs. The standing is a sign of one truth. The bow is a sign of a deeper one. And the prostration of the deepest. We will come to the bow and the prostration in their own tracks.

The standing, he says, is a sign that every action in the world is really God's action. That everything stands because He holds it up. One of the names of God is *al-Qayyum*. The Self-Subsisting. The One who stands by Himself, and by whom everything else stands.

So when you stand upright in the prayer, there is a quiet lesson in your own body. You are standing. But you are not standing by yourself. The strength in your legs, the breath in your chest, the life that keeps you upright, all of it is held up, moment by moment, by Him. Imam Khomeini quotes the Qur'an here. O people, it is you who are in need of God, and God is the Self-Sufficient, the Praised.

And then he points to two small details. The eyes are lowered to the place of prostration, which is dust, the place we came from. And the head is slightly bowed. Both, he says, speak of our humility and our need.

Sayyid Haydar Amuli adds a lovely thought. He says that standing upright is the posture that belongs to human beings. And he says that, in this posture, you are in harmony with those angels whose whole duty is to stand before God, always. For a few minutes, you join them.

---

Now, what should the heart be doing while the body stands?

Imam Khomeini says this. Consider yourself in the presence of God. See the whole world as His presence, and yourself as one of the company gathered there, standing before Him. And before the prayer starts, take a moment to let the heart understand how important this is.

He also gives some advice I did not expect. Look at how the Imams of the Ahl al-Bayt stood before God. Not just their names, he says, or the dates of their births and deaths. Look at how they prayed. And he gives us some narrations, which I will share with you.

Imam al-Sadiq, peace be upon him, said that whenever his grandfather, Imam Ali ibn al-Husayn, Zayn al-Abidin, stood for the prayer, his face would turn pale.

And Imam al-Sadiq also related, from his father, that when Imam Zayn al-Abidin stood for the prayer, he was like the trunk of a tree. Nothing of him moved, unless the wind moved it.

Someone once said to Imam al-Sadiq, I have noticed that when Ali ibn al-Husayn stood for the prayer, his colour changed. And the Imam said, by God, Ali ibn al-Husayn knew before whom he was standing.

He knew before whom he was standing. That one sentence could be the whole of this track.

---

Imam Zayn al-Abidin himself left us a description of the right of the prayer. Imam Khomeini quotes it. The right of the prayer, he says, is that you know it is a visit to God. And that in it, you are standing before Him.

A visit. Not a test, not a chore. A visit.

And Imam Khomeini also quotes the Prophet, peace be upon him and his family. Worship your Lord as if you see Him. And if you do not see Him, He sees you.

There is one more piece of advice he passes on, from a book of narrations, which is very practical. When you want to pray, it says, do not come to it lazily, or sleepily, or hurriedly, or absent-mindedly. Come to it calmly, with dignity, and slowly.

Calmly, with dignity, and slowly. That is a good description of how to stand.

---

Now we come to the Lantern of the Path. Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq has a short chapter on opening the prayer. Here is how it begins.

When you face the qiblah, despair of this world, and of what it contains, and of what other people are busy with. Empty your heart of every preoccupation that might distract you from God. See the immensity of God with your innermost being. And remember that you will stand before Him, on the day when every soul will know what it sent ahead, and they will be brought back to God, their true Patron. Stand at the foot of fear and hope.

Despair of this world. That sounds hard at first. But it does not mean despair in the ordinary sense. It means, for these few minutes, stop expecting anything from it. The shopping, the meeting, the message you are waiting for. None of it can give you what you have come here for. So let go of it, at the edge of the prayer mat.

Imam Khomeini, commenting on this passage, says these instructions are for people like us. The veiled. The ones who cannot hold God and the world in the heart at the same time.

And then he explains the last line. Stand at the foot of fear and hope. When we look at our own weakness and laziness and need, and at God's greatness, we feel fear and awe. And when we look at His mercy, His kindness and His boundless generosity, we feel hope.

I like to think of it this way. You stand on two feet. Let one be fear, and the other be hope. Lean too far on either one, and you fall.

---

Now, the intention.

Imam Khomeini begins with a definition. The intention, he says, is simply the decision to do something. And it is present in every voluntary act. You cannot do anything on purpose without it.

That leads him to one of his sharpest passages. He says that the whisperings of Satan about the intention may be the most ridiculous and the strangest of all. Because it is impossible to pray without an intention, even if you tried. And yet some people spend a long time, at every prayer, trying to make their intention firm.

Such a person, he says, is like someone who stands thinking for a long time about whether they have decided to go shopping, or to go for lunch.

We touched on this sickness, *waswas*, in the first track. Here Imam Khomeini adds a narration. A man was mentioned to Imam al-Sadiq, someone who kept repeating his wudu and his prayer, and he was described as a man of good sense. The Imam said, what sense does he have, if he obeys Satan? The companion asked how he was obeying Satan. And the Imam said, ask him where his doubts come from. He will tell you they come from Satan.

And Imam Khomeini gives a cure that I find almost funny, and very wise. When Satan whispers, your prayer is not valid, reply: if the acts of all the jurists of the community are invalid, then mine is invalid too.

So the intention is not something to wrestle with. You already have it. You are standing on the prayer mat, to pray this prayer, for God. That is your intention.

---

The Lantern of the Path has a chapter on intention too. Imam al-Sadiq says that the one with a sincere intention is the one with a sound heart. And he quotes the Prophet. The intention of the believer is better than his action. And, actions are by intentions, and everyone will have what he intended.

The intention comes from the heart, according to how pure its knowledge is. And it changes as faith changes. Sometimes it is strong. Sometimes it is weak. So the question is not whether you have an intention. You do. The question is how pure it is.

And he ends with a portrait of the person of sincere intention. He is often uncomfortable with himself, he says, struggling with his own desires. And yet people find ease at his hand.

---

This brings us to sincerity. *Ikhlas*.

Imam Khomeini defines it like this. Sincerity is cleaning the act of worship of anything done for other than God. And cleaning the heart of seeing anything but God.

He quotes the saying of the Prophet that everyone receives according to what he intended. Whoever migrates to God and His Messenger, his migration is to God and His Messenger. And whoever migrates for some gain of this world, his migration is to what he migrated for.

And Imam Khomeini says there are two migrations. One is of the body, from one town to another. The other is a migration of the soul. It starts from the dark house of the self, and its goal is God.

Then he says something that stays with me. If the traveller is really seeking something for himself, even spiritual rank, even nearness to God for his own sake, then he has not left home. He is just wandering from room to room, from corner to corner, inside his own house. It is a journey, he says, from self to self. And he adds, gently, that almost every traveller has to pass through this stage at the start.

---

Imam Khomeini then describes the stages of sincerity, like steps on a stair. I will put them simply.

The first step is to pray without wanting people's praise. Praying to be seen is the lowest kind of hypocrisy.

The second is to pray without wanting worldly gain from it. More income, say, or protection from illness.

The third is to pray without the aim of paradise and its pleasures. He calls that the worship of traders.

The fourth is to pray without the motive of fear of punishment. He calls that the worship of slaves.

And then the stair keeps climbing. Beyond spiritual delights. Beyond even the joy of meeting God. Beyond even the fear of being separated from Him.

Now, it is important to hear what he says about the lower steps. A prayer done for paradise, or out of fear of the Fire, is still a valid prayer. The law accepts it. And the reward is real. He is not telling us to stop praying until we are perfect. He is showing us how high the stair goes. And with great honesty, of one of the higher steps, he says it is beyond someone like himself.

So do not be discouraged by this. Be drawn by it.

---

There is one more stage of sincerity he mentions which is within reach of all of us. It is to stop thinking that we deserve a reward.

The Lantern of the Path says it beautifully, and Imam Khomeini quotes it. The lowest level of sincerity is that the servant does his very best, and then does not consider his action to be worth anything before God.

Imam Khomeini quotes Imam Musa ibn Ja'far, peace be upon him, who said to one of his sons: My son, be diligent, and never think yourself free of falling short in worshipping and obeying God. For He can never be worshipped as He deserves.

There is a great freedom in that. You do your best. And then you let it go. You do not stand there with your arms folded, waiting to be paid.

And for those who keep at it, there is a promise. Imam Khomeini quotes the Prophet. Whoever is sincere to God for forty mornings, springs of wisdom will flow from his heart to his tongue.

---

To carry into your next prayer.

When you stand, before the first *Allahu akbar*, remember the words about Imam Zayn al-Abidin. He knew before whom he was standing. Stand calmly, and slowly. Lower your eyes to the place of prostration. And let your feet be fear and hope.

And for the intention, ask yourself just one question, once, and let the heart answer. Who is this for? Then let it go, and begin.

In the next track, the door of the prayer closes behind us, with the opening *takbir*. *Allahu akbar*. And the raising of the hands.
