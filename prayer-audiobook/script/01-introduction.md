# Track 1: The Prayer Is a Journey

Peace be upon you, and welcome.

This is a companion for the road. Over the next three hours or so, we are going to walk slowly through the daily prayer. We will go from the water of wudu at the very beginning, to the final greeting of peace at the very end. At each step, we will ask two simple questions. What am I actually saying, or doing? And what does it mean?

You have probably prayed thousands of times. Most of us have. And most of us know the feeling of reaching the final salam and realising that our mind was somewhere else the whole time. On the shopping. On a conversation from yesterday. On the road ahead. This series is not here to make you feel guilty about that. It is here to give your heart something to hold on to, at each moment of the prayer, so that next time it has a reason to stay.

So you do not need to do anything right now except listen. You are driving. Keep your eyes on the road. The prayer will be waiting for you when you arrive.

---

Let us begin with a few words of the Prophet, peace be upon him and his family, that the scholars return to again and again.

The first is this. The prayer is the ascension of the believer. In Arabic, the *mi'raj* of the believer. The Prophet was taken up, on the night of the ascension, into the presence of his Lord. The prayer, five times a day, is your share of that journey. When you stand on the prayer mat, you are not just performing a duty. You are being invited up.

The second is this. The Prophet said, I love three things of your world: perfume, women, and the coolness of my eye in prayer. The coolness of the eye. In Arabic, that is an expression for deep contentment. The eye that has found what it was looking for, and stops searching. The prayer was not a burden for him. It was where he rested.

And the third is a warning, gentle but serious. Prayer is not accepted except with the presence of the heart.

That phrase, the presence of the heart, is what this whole series is about. In Arabic, *hudur al-qalb*. Being there, inside, while the body is going through the movements.

---

Now, the sources we will be drawing on.

Most of what you will hear comes from a handful of books. There is the Lantern of the Path, a collection of teachings of Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq, peace be upon him, which has short and very beautiful chapters on the opening of the prayer, the bow, the prostration, the testimony and the final greeting. There is Inner Secrets of the Path, by the great scholar Sayyid Haydar Amuli, who looks at every act of worship on three levels. There is Imam Khomeini's Forty Hadith, and his book on the disciplines of the prayer, called *Adab al-Salat*. And there are a few stories from the lives of the scholars, from a book called the Spiritual Journey of the Mystics.

The prayer we will follow is the prayer as it is performed in the school of the Ahl al-Bayt. The Ja'fari prayer. So you will hear about the *qunut*, the four glorifications, prostration on the earth, and the rest. But the inner meanings run deeper than any one school, and much of what you will hear belongs to every Muslim who prays.

---

Sayyid Haydar Amuli gives us a map that is worth holding on to from the very start.

He says that every act of worship can be understood on three levels. The first is the level of the *shari'ah*, the outward law. The second is the level of the *tariqah*, the path. And the third is the level of the *haqiqah*, the reality.

On the level of the law, the prayer is a set of actions done correctly. Standing, reciting, bowing, prostrating, at the right time, facing the right direction, in a state of purity. This level is not small. Without it there is no prayer at all.

On the level of the path, Amuli says, the prayer is drawing near. He quotes the Prophet: Prayer is the sacrifice by which every believer comes close to God. And he adds that this nearness is not nearness of place. It is nearness of the soul.

On the level of the reality, the prayer is arrival, and witnessing.

Amuli puts all three in one short line, which I would like you to remember. The *shari'ah* is that you worship Him. The *tariqah* is that you come into His presence. And the *haqiqah* is that you witness Him.

Worship Him. Come into His presence. Witness Him.

Most of us live on the first level most of the time. That is all right. The point of this series is to show you, at each moment of the prayer, that there is a door to the second level standing open. And to let you know that the third exists.

---

Amuli also gives a picture of the prayer that I find very striking.

He says that the prayer has a body and a spirit, like a living creature. The spirit of the prayer is the intention, the sincerity, and the awareness of the heart. The standing and the sitting are its body. The bow and the prostration are like its head and its limbs. And the words of remembrance and glorification are like its senses, its eyes and ears.

Then he tells a parable. Imagine, he says, that someone wants to come close to a great king, and so he brings the king a gift. He brings him a young servant. Now, if that servant has no breath of life in him, the gift is not a gift. It is an insult. If the servant has no hands or feet, it is a poor gift. And if he has eyes and ears, but cannot see or hear, it is still a broken gift.

A prayer without intention and sincerity, says Amuli, is like a gift with no life in it. A prayer that is missing the bow and the prostration is like a gift with no limbs. And a prayer without awareness of the heart, without attention to the meaning of the words, is like a servant whose eyes are open, but who cannot see.

The king in that parable, of course, is God. And the point is not that He needs our gift. It is that we should not bring Him something lifeless when we are capable of bringing Him something alive.

---

That brings us to something the Lantern of the Path says, at the very opening of the prayer. Imam al-Sadiq says this.

Know that God does not need your service. He is independent of you, your worship and your supplication. He summons you by His favour, to show you mercy, to put you far from His punishment, to spread over you some of the blessings of His kindness, to guide you to the path of His pleasure, and to open to you the door of His forgiveness.

So the prayer is not God's need. It is God's invitation. Five times a day, He calls you in, for your own sake.

And the Imam goes on. Make modesty your cloak, he says, and your helplessness your shawl.

---

Here is another surprising thing Amuli points out. He says that the prayer contains, within it, all the other great acts of worship.

While you are praying, you are fasting, because you cannot eat or drink. You are giving *zakat*, the purifying alms, because you are giving your body, your strength and your time to God. The Prophet said that everything has its *zakat*, and the *zakat* of the body is obedience.

You are on *hajj*, the pilgrimage, because you are facing the Ka'bah. And more than that. Amuli says that the one who prays is circling around his own heart, the way the pilgrim circles the Ka'bah, making sure that nothing but God enters it. The outward pilgrim travels to the House of God in Makkah. The one who prays travels to the House of God within, which is the heart.

And you are in *jihad*, the struggle, because the prayer is a struggle with the self. Every time your attention wanders and you bring it back, that is the greater struggle the Prophet spoke of. The struggle against the self.

So when you stand to pray, you are not doing one small thing. You are doing everything at once.

---

Now, Imam Khomeini, in his Forty Hadith, asks a very practical question. How does the presence of the heart actually come about?

He says it depends on two things.

The first is unoccupied time and an unoccupied heart. He means that the prayer should be given its own time. Not squeezed in between other things, not rushed through as an obstacle to what we imagine is the real business of the day. He says that just as we set aside time for earning a living, or for study, we should set aside time for prayer, and keep it free of other concerns.

And the second is understanding the importance of the prayer. Making the heart understand that this is not one task among many, but the most important thing we do.

And then, with real honesty, Imam Khomeini says of himself: if, like this author, one prays out of compulsion, and treats the worship of the Lord as a burden, one will delay it as long as possible, and then perform it carelessly.

That is a great scholar, saying that about himself. So if you recognise yourself in that, you are in good company. The point is to start from where you are.

He quotes Imam al-Baqir, peace be upon him, who told his companion Zurarah: Do not be careless about your prayer. For the Prophet said on his deathbed, the one who makes light of his prayer does not belong to me.

---

But there is a balance here, and Imam Khomeini is very clear about it.

He warns about a sickness he calls *waswas*. Obsessive doubt. The person who repeats their intention over and over. The person who strains so hard over the pronunciation of every letter that their face twists, and the words come out stranger than if they had just spoken them naturally. He says that all the spiritual secrets of the prayer get neglected, and all the attention goes to these niceties. And he says plainly that this does not come from God. It comes from Satan, who cannot lead a religious person into obvious sin, so he comes at them through their worship instead.

So the presence of the heart is not tension. It is not straining. It is attention, and it is love. A relaxed, wakeful attention. The way you listen to someone you love when they are speaking to you.

---

Here is how the rest of the series will go.

In the next track, we start with purity and wudu, and what each washing means. Then the time and the place of the prayer, and the qiblah. Then the call to prayer, the *adhan* and *iqamah*. Then we stand, and make our intention. Then the opening *takbir*, *Allahu akbar*, and the raising of the hands.

Then we will spend a long time with *al-Fatiha*, word by word, because it is the heart of the prayer. Then the second surah. Then the bow, the *ruku'*. Then the prostration, the *sujud*. Then the *qunut* and the four glorifications. Then the testimony, the *tashahhud*, and the final greeting, the *salam*. And last, what we do after the prayer is over.

Each track will end with one or two things to carry into your next prayer. Do not try to carry all of them at once. That would just be another kind of strain. Pick one. Hold it for a day, or a week. Then pick another.

---

To carry into your next prayer.

Before you say the first *Allahu akbar*, pause for one breath. Just one. And remember those three words of Sayyid Haydar Amuli. Worship Him. Come into His presence. Witness Him.

And remember the words of the Lantern of the Path. He does not need this prayer. He is inviting you, for your own sake.

In the next track, we begin where the prayer begins. With water.
