# Lecture 01: What Islamic Mysticism Is: Fixing the Inner Machine to Catch God's Signs

<!-- Detailed notes from transcripts/text/Irfan01.md. Every point cites ¶. -->

## 1. Thesis
This opening lecture is a short introduction before "the real lesson from next week" (¶3). It argues that Islamic mysticism (ʿirfan) is not a hunt for powers or an invention of mystics, but a Quran- and Imam-grounded programme of repairing the soul's "inner machine" so that it can hear, see and reflect on the signs of God that the Quran says most people pass by (¶9–12). It separates this from non-Islamic and power-seeking mysticism (¶3–4, 13, 32), places it at the level of the muqarrabun, the "brought near", above the ordinary believer who is content with the shariah (¶15–16), and uses Munajat Shaʿbaniyyah and a munajat from Sahifa Sajjadiyya to show what the mystics ask for: severance from everything else, vision of God, increasing love and knowledge (¶17–31). Its closing definition is "seeking and nothing else": marifah and love of Allah (¶32).

## 2. Detailed content (in lecture order)

### 2.1 Framing: mysticism is mysterious, and Islamic mysticism must be distinguished (¶3–4)
- This is a "very short introduction"; the real lessons start next week (¶3).
- "Mysticism is very mysterious. No one knows what mysticism is." Even mystics are in doubt, "except the real mystics" (¶3).
- Mysticism is not limited to Islam. Even non-religious people have an inclination towards it, which makes it harder to distinguish Islamic mysticism from other types, "which is very important" (¶3).
- Many people come to mysticism for power: for example, to be in two places at the same time, or to explore the mysteries of the unknown world of the human soul "so that they have some power" (¶3).
- That kind is found in other religions and must be told apart from the real mystic. The first questions are what we are discussing, who the mystics are and what they want on a journey that is "exhausting, time consuming, life consuming" (¶4).
- The sayings of the Imams (as) and some Quran verses, especially in their supplications, show a type of mysticism "very different from other type of mysticism that we find in other religion" (¶4).

### 2.2 The challenge: why strive if the wajib duties suffice? (¶5–6)
- Anecdote: someone told the Prophet he lacked eagerness for salatul-layl and mustahab fasting. If he prays the five daily prayers, fasts Ramadan, pays zakat and goes to hajj once, will he enter jannah? The Prophet said yes (¶5).
- The lecturer's question: "why should we work so much if it's so easy to go to jannah just by performing our wajib duties?" (¶5).
- Some Muslim jurists and activists are "completely against mysticism". They say the shariah is the only way and that mysticism is "made up by some mystics according to their vibes and wishes" (¶6).
- The test: "What the quran says about mysticism? Let's see." (¶6).

### 2.3 Surah Rum signs: they are for those who reflect, know, hear and use reason (¶6–10)
- He chose verses from Surah Rum, "many other verses in the same line" exist (¶6).
- Q 30:21 (mates, tranquillity, love and mercy; signs "for those who reflect"). Lecturer: do we see God's sign in a man–woman relationship? "Usually will see the sign of shaitan, isn't it?" The signs are there "not for everyone but for the people who reflect" (¶6–7).
- Q 30:22 (creation of heavens and earth, diversity of tongues and colours; signs "for those who know"). We see ethnic differences and racism, not a sign of God; the signs are "for the knowledgeable", "for the learned. Not for everyone" (¶7–8).
- He adds: "not even most pious people and not even most people who are very stipulant about shariah find signs in this" (¶8).
- Q 30:23 (sleep by night and day, seeking livelihood; signs "for those who hear"). "Do we hear? If we hear why... we are not broken down as the prophets or the awliyaallah break down when we see the signs of Allah" (¶8–9).
- Q 30:24 (lightning with fear and hope, rain reviving dead earth; signs "for those who are wise"/use reason) (¶9).
- Striking claim: "Actually Quran is insulting us." It says we do not understand, hear, reflect or know. "I don't know about you. I feel that Quran is insulting" (¶10).
- This resembles the Quranic remark that some have ears and eyes by which they do not hear and see (¶10).

### 2.4 The "machine" analogy and the definition of mysticism (¶10–12)
- The ear is only an instrument; it catches sound. But "there is something inside which doesn't catch it. There is a machine inside which is rust and doesn't see these signs" (¶10–11).
- We have often heard these verses and some of us have felt there is something in them we do not see. We accept "just out of obedience", but the question is how to catch the sign (¶11).
- Definition: "Mysticism is all about making ones soul to hear, to see and to reflect. I think this is the summary of mysticism... the summary of Islamic Mysticism" (¶11).
- The coming lectures will show how to do it "as it's very difficult" (¶11).
- Q 12:105: many signs in heavens and earth are passed by; people turn away. "We don't see. Something is wrong." The soul is the "inner machine", "broken. It doesn't work", and must be fixed to catch the signals of God. "This is not of course a joke and not an easy path. It needs lots of training" (¶12).

### 2.5 Islamic self-training differs from power-seeking (¶13–14)
- Self-training is done for many purposes; many mystics seek powers, or to see the future or past. "Islamic mysticism is not about this course" but about catching and becoming sensitive to God's signs (¶13).
- Q 28:71 (if God made the night perpetual, who else could bring light?; "Are you not hearing?") is cited as a "very moving" verse about catching the signals (¶13–14).
- Once the machine is started, God alone knows what signals the person can see and hear: "It is not possible for us to reckon what mystics can see and hear" (¶14).
- The peak of such people is the Imams ("the imam sees"); the lecturer also says "the kaafir see" in the same sentence, which looks garbled (see section 7) (¶14).

### 2.6 Muqarrabun: four categories and their different "terms and conditions" (¶15–16)
- Earlier teaching (previous course, not in this series' transcript): the muqarrabun comprise four categories: nabiyeen, siddiqeen, shohada, saleheen. He had explained the first three but "Saleheen I have never explained because it was not possible" (¶15).
- Saleheen are "very good", "at the bottom of the category of muqarrabun" (¶15).
- Prophets, Imams and siddiqeen are muqarrabun, but "ordinary people also can enter into the realm of muqarrabun", which makes them "completely different from other people"; the Quran distinguishes them from other mominoon. It is "very difficult to get into that last category" (¶15).
- "That is what Islamic Mysticism is about" (¶16).
- Ordinary mominoon: follow the shariah, succeed, go to jannah. "That is something for ordinary mominoon. For muqarrabun, of course the whole terms and conditions are different." They "are given jannah in this world itself", seek something else in the next world, and become awliyaAllah and "the proof of Allah" (¶16).
- These are "the true urafah or mystics" (¶16).
- Terminology: "mystic" is very general (Christian, Hindu, Buddhist mystics), whereas "aarif" is "a very nice term which we use in Islam when someone becomes true aarif" (¶16–17).

### 2.7 Munajat Shaʿbaniyyah: "like a primary school boy reading university books" (¶17–23)
- All the Imams recited this munajat; it is reported from Imam Ali (as) and Imam Ali Zayn al-Abidin (as) (¶17).
- "Sometimes I question why we are told to recite such words... like if you tell a primary school boy to read books of university." The words are at the peak of the muqarrabun's understanding and eagerness (¶17–18).
- Line 1 (as transcribed: "hab li kamala alinqita`i ilayka"): complete severance from everything else. Is total cutting off good? "Well, in the heart yes" (¶18). Linked with Q 73:8, cut off from everything and devote yourself wholeheartedly (¶18).
- Line 2 ("wa anir absara qulubina bidiya'i nazariha ilayka"): enlighten the eyes of our heart. Argument: if the physical eyes work, why do we not see the signs? Because "there is another mechanism" (the heart) that must comply with what it receives, and "it's blind" (¶19).
- Objection: the Quran says you can never see God, yet the dua speaks of looking at Him. He says people unfamiliar with mystical wording "say that these are made by mistake" (¶19).
- Line 3 ("hatta takhriqa absaru alqulubi hujuba alnnuri"): until the eyes of the hearts penetrate the veils of light. Mystics speak of veils of light and darkness that must be pierced to the very origin of God's azamah and kibriya (¶19–20).
- Line 4 ("fatasila ila ma`dini al`azamati"): reaching the source of grandeur. "This is of course something which we cannot understand", yet they want to know and look at Him and "be attracted towards him"; first they must cut off from everything (¶20).
- Line 5 ("wa tasira arwahuna mu`allaqatan bi`izzi qudsika"): our souls "suspended by the glory of your sanctity". "If we don't know what they are talking about it doesn't mean that it is senseless and meaningless" (¶20–21).
- Line 6 ("Ilahi waj`alni mimman nadaytahu..."): make me one of those whom You call and who respond, as prophets do. When You look at them they are "struck by your majesty", "thunder struck" (¶21).
- "This is munajat-e-shabaniyah. We cannot say that this is not good." (¶21).
- Practical advice: recite it in Shaban "as much as you can". We do not understand much of it, "but we have been honoured" to be allowed to recite what is "not of our level". "What immah wanted, Allah granted them" (¶22).
- Line 7 ("Ilahi aqimni fi ahli wilayatika"): two meanings of wilayat, marifah (knowing, looking at God) and love. These are the two things being asked, "incessant love for Allah and marifah which will ever be increasing", after the machine is fixed (¶23).
- Continuation (as transcribed, "...muqama man raja alzziyadata min mahabbatika"): place me among Your friends in the position of one who hopes for increase in Your love; the passionate love of remembering You so that he remembers only You (¶23).
- Line 8 ("Ilahi wa alhimni walahan bidhikrika..."): quoted, and "I need to explain", but the explanation is not given in this transcript (¶24).

### 2.8 Can a human reach this? Human beings and animals (¶24–25)
- Quran's separation of mominun and muqarrabun in Surah Waqiah (¶24).
- Question: is it possible for a human being to reach such knowledge and love? "We wrongly call ourselves human being. We are not yet human beings. We are animals who have potential to become human beings... created as animals but we have that capacity" (¶24).
- The prophets are "real human beings" (¶24).
- "A human being has the capacity to become a friend of god. Allah has created us at this strength and he wants us to reach that strength." But "we have become his enemy" instead (¶25).
- What makes the awliyaAllah different, and how can they fix the machine even in ordinary life? (¶25).

### 2.9 Nahjul Balagha: the awliyaAllah look at the inward side of the world (¶25–27)
- Lecturer uses awliyaAllah as a synonym of urafaa (¶25).
- Imam Ali (as): the friends of Allah look at the inward of the world while others look at the outward, "what is on the surface" (¶25–26).
- "That's why they please Allah." Why can't we? "Because they busy themselves with 29:49 while the other people busy themselves in the imaging" (¶26; text unclear, see section 7).
- Wealth example: wealth is not wrong in shariah; the problem is being preoccupied with it. "You can never reach that stage of awliyaAllah" that way (¶26).
- They "never think about themselves. They always think about god." They have family, friends and social position, "but in the heart we are completely cut off from him" (¶26). The sense is that they are cut off from all else, so the pronoun looks garbled (see section 7).
- They are "completely away and aloof from what goes on with other people" (¶27).
- He cites the "short statement of sermon 432" and advises students to read the rest themselves: "a very nice prescription of awliyaAllah" (¶27).

### 2.10 Munajat Khamsa Ashar (Sahifa Sajjadiyya): this is not a cave-dweller (¶27–31)
- It is from Imam Zayn al-Abidin (as). One might think it came from someone who left society and lived in a cave; yet look at what it says (¶27).
- "Who can have tasted the sweetness of Your love and is after anything else": if you are after other things "you have not tasted the sweetness of his love" (¶27).
- "Who is the one who used to be near him when they want to go somewhere else": familiarity with God's nearness leaves no desire to go elsewhere (¶28).
- Place us with those You chose for Your nearness and wilayat; purified them for Your love; made them eager to meet You and content with Your decree; gave them the gift of "looking at your face" (¶28).
- "Inshallah when we go to the lesson we will know what is looking at the face of Allah. Now it seems very meaningless" (¶29).
- Refuge from "the pain of separation from you". Contrast: for some, God seems "completely absent" and "we want to cry into the sky because we are very far away"; for others "they feel the presence and actually respond in a way which makes them break down and faint like the immah" (¶29).
- Chosen for His marifah, made worthy of worship, "whose heart you have captivated with the will" (¶30).
- Hayyaman: "worthy of love which never ever is quenched". Link with "malaikatil mohayyemeen", angels "burnt in the love of Allah", who look at Him and cannot turn away. Other angels in rows are "dumb"; when others ask them something "they cannot hear, they cannot see" (¶30).
- Some mystics who found this state "couldn't speak until the end of their life". "I don't say it is good because if they have enough capacity they can come back like the prophet goes to meraj and comes back" (¶31).
- Another line: "you have made his heart burn with the love of seeking you" (¶31).

### 2.11 Conclusion: seeking, not powers (¶31–32)
- "This is how mystics works": "It is actually seeking and nothing else."
- Not about power or "empowering the soul in a way to do karamat and mojezat. These are shirk." What Islamic mysticism wants is "Marefah and the love of Allah". How and what they find is the subject of the course (¶32).
- Closing salawat (¶33).

## 5. Stories, anecdotes and analogies
- (¶5) The man who asks the Prophet whether the obligatory acts alone get him jannah; the Prophet says yes. Told briefly.
- (¶10–12) Ears and eyes as mere instruments, with a "machine inside which is rust": the soul as a broken machine to be fixed. Recurring analogy, developed fully.
- (¶14) The machine, once started, "can now work"; what it picks up is known only to God.
- (¶17) Giving the Munajat Shaʿbaniyyah to ordinary people is like giving a primary school boy university books.
- (¶24) Humans as animals with potential to become human.
- (¶26) A rich person: wealth itself is not the problem, preoccupation is.
- (¶27) The listener might imagine Imam Zayn al-Abidin as a cave-dwelling hermit; he was not. Alluded to only.
- (¶29) Callers to God: for some He is absent and they cry into the sky; for others the Imams faint at His response.
- (¶30) Angels burning in love, dumb and unhearing (muhayyamin). Alluded to.
- (¶31) Mystics who lost speech for life after hayyaman; the Prophet's meraj and return as the contrast of capacity.
