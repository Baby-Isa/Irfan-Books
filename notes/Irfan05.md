# Lecture 05: The Soul Across the Worlds: Imaginal Body, Layers of the Ruh, and the Stations of the Nafs

<!-- Detailed notes from transcripts/text/Irfan05.md. Every point cites ¶. -->

## 1. Thesis
Continuing the series on the human soul, the lecturer recaps the "worlds" (the world of matter, the world of image/barzakh, the world of intellect) and argues that these are not places we travel to but deeper dimensions of one reality, and that the human soul, unlike the angels, has unlimited capacity to expand through all of them (¶3–8). He then draws out the consequences. The image body is deformed or made luminous by deeds (¶10–15). Knowing one's soul is the key to knowing creation (¶16). Many hadith about the Prophet, the Imams, the Quran and the Day of Judgement become intelligible only if the soul has depths (¶24–31). He then lists the "layers" of the one ruh (Serr, Mukhtafi, Ruh, Qalb, Kalimah, Fuʾad, and others) and the three conditions of the one nafs (¶31–49), with Ramadan, night prayer and dhikr as practices that free the soul from bondage to the body (¶50–57). It closes the unit on the soul that began earlier in the series (¶53).

## 2. Detailed content (in lecture order)

### 2.1 Recap: the three worlds (¶3–5)
- Last week's recap: the world of matter (`aalamul-mulk`) is surrounded by a larger world corresponding to it, `aalamul-masaal`/`aalamul-khayaal`, "the world of image", also called barzakh (¶3).
- Above that is `aalamul-oqul`, the world of intellect, whose inhabitants (angels) are "purely reason" and attached to nothing material; this is why the angelic world is "completely different" from the human (¶3).
- Key correction of imagination: these worlds are not somewhere we "move to" after death. Each is "another dimension of the same world in which we are living" (¶4).
- Why barzakh? A barzakh is a barrier between two things; the image world is a middle world between matter and intellect. It is detached from matter yet keeps some qualities of it: dimension, colour, and pleasures (¶4).
- Some Quranic pleasures belong to this middle world (¶5).
- Our souls are now wholly attached to the body, hence to `aalamul-shahadah`, the witnessed world. The higher worlds are `aalamul-ghayb`, unseen because the ordinary eye cannot perceive them without training. God as "knower of the unseen and the witnessed" knows both and the other worlds are "like an envelope around this world", "a much deeper dimension" (¶5).

### 2.2 Death, resurrection and the image body (¶6–9)
- At death "we don't go anywhere"; we pass into another dimension. Even at resurrection we just go "deeper and deeper", closer to the Jalal and Jamaal of God (¶6).
- At death we leave the physical body but keep a body with dimension and colour but no matter, the `badan-ul-misaali` (image body). It is not made of elements but resembles the earthly shape (¶6–7).
- A soul needs a body to progress. Angels cannot progress: "no evolution. No change, no movement." The human soul was placed so it can move and change, so its expansion is "infinite" (¶7).
- Angels each have "a known position" (Q 37:164, as cited): a fixed station they cannot exceed. Mystics say humans lack a "known station"; no one knows how far we can go. This is why the angels prostrated to Adam, and the capacity grows through learning the names of God (¶8).
- The image body is not the soul (¶8–9). People who claim to "bring the soul out of the body" mostly lie; at best they separate the two bodies (material and image) without either dying. The image body is always with us but unseen, being in another dimension (¶9).

### 2.3 Deformation and illumination of the image body (¶10–15)
- In this world thinking and faith show on the face to the shrewd or faithful; a person in great pain may show light. This effect is slow, small and limited; it does not deform the material body (¶10).
- The image body is "very sensitive" and is deformed by sins. Some people see others in other forms: donkeys, monkeys, other animals. The people seen cannot recognise this, but when the image body separates it shows (¶10–11).
- Hadith explaining Q 78:18 ("the day the trumpet is blown and you come in groups") says people will come in the shapes of animals because their image bodies are deformed (¶11).
- The pious have a "shiny" image body. On the Day of Judgement, believers' light runs before them (cited as 56:12) while the faces of the disbelievers are covered with night-darkness (Q 10:27). This is on the Day of Judgement and involves a body "different from this misaali body" (¶12).
- Those who "see people in different forms" have entered the dimension of the middle world, which is difficult; even there not all can recognise the deformation (¶13).
- Imagine the horror of seeing one's self-inflicted deformation at the resurrection (¶14).
- Our existence in the image world is "much bigger" than here; after death "we will see that this world was a film" that restricted our actions, pleasures and understanding (¶14).
- The image world is the world of imagination: "Whatever you imagine is you actually." So in paradise: "whatever they wish" (Q 39:34; 21:102 cited) and the wish "is materialisation of itself" (¶15).
- Qiyamah cannot simply be called `aalamul-oqul`; it is "a bit different". The soul is not divided among worlds; it is one thing with the capacity to connect with all (¶15–16).

### 2.4 Knowing the soul; seclusion (¶16–19)
- Mystics: to know God's creation, know your soul, but this is the hardest knowledge (¶16).
- Quoted from Ayatullah Hasanzadeh Amoli, "a contemporary aarif … still alive in Qum": knowledge of the soul is not attained "except through inner veils and patience"; it needs visions, self-training in private and "strong seclusion from people" (¶16).
- This seclusion is of the heart, not physical. Proof: Q 73:8 ("cut off from everyone through Him") was addressed to the Prophet, who after his mission never physically cut off from people (¶17).
- The practical difficulty: detaching from worldly worries; "talking about it is simple" but practice is hard (¶17–18).
- Objection: if we go through the middle world and higher anyway after death, and the prophets promised their followers will come "by your side", what is the point of the effort? (¶18–19). The answer is Q 56:10, "those who are ahead of others in this world" are ahead in the next. The drive must enter the heart; it comes once we have proven to God that we deserve it; Q 62:4 ("this is the grace of God") is for everyone but "does not want evil people to travel along this path" (¶19).

### 2.5 The human as threefold: "misaali, ʿaqli, ilahi" (¶20–23)
- The mystics' phrase (garbled in transcript, see section 7): the human is of this world (via body and its pleasures), is misaali (has khayal corresponding to the image world), is intellectual and is divine (¶20, 31).
- Two kinds of imagination: the "connected" one (my imagination differs from yours; "Your image in my mind is very real") and the question of a dimension of images independent of any mind. That exists, and "this world that you have is just a sample of that world", which the dead enter (¶20–22).
- Intellect: we grasp concepts like "human being" with no particular features, which exists nowhere in the outside world (each person has special features). The mind can therefore rise to what lacks particulars and corresponds to unity; this is the intellect world (¶22–23).
- Beyond the two there is the divine aspect, by which we can connect directly with God. We are weak but have the potential and some have realised it (¶23).
- Hadith: the Prophet has "hours with God" that no close angel nor messenger can share (¶23–24). Once this divine aspect is realised no creature reaches that level (¶24).

### 2.6 Hadith of the Friday night and arsh (¶24–28)
- Lecturer admits he "really [doesn't] understand" this hadith fully, from Imam Jaʿfar al-Sadiq via ʿAbdullah ibn ʿAbd al-Rahman: every Friday night has "pleasure of presence of God"; the Prophet and Imams visit the arsh and the narrator "does not return but with learnt knowledge". Other hadith say the Imams' knowledge increases every Friday night; otherwise it would be exhausted (¶24–25).
- Interpretation in the soul framework: nobody's body travels. One travels inward into the soul; "the heart of a momin is a place of Allah", so it is "as if you go to the arsh of Allah", "walking inside deeper and deeper". It is a conjunction between the Prophet's soul and the arsh (¶26).
- Why only on Friday night? "If it is all the time connected it will burn." The soul needs to breathe; times of divine exposure are like attacks of attraction followed by release (¶27).
- Miʿraj: mystics say the physical journey was Mecca to al-Aqsa; the ascent beyond was movement of the soul, not the body (¶28).
- The Quran's zahir and batin, seven depths and seventy each: the depths are in us, not the text. Different people at different depths of soul find deeper meanings in the same verse (¶28).

### 2.7 Hadith: "recite and ascend" (¶29–31)
- Reported by Shia and Sunni: in paradise one is told to recite and go up in proportion to the verses one knows. Picture thousands of stairs: know two verses, go up two steps (¶29).
- But the Quran has "above 6000 verses" and it is not 6000 stairs: it is the depth with which a verse has been internalised, not memorisation. "This book is your soul." (¶30).
- This is "a forceful argument for all mystics": hadith like this are otherwise explained only superficially or mechanically (¶30–31).

### 2.8 The great Ruh and its layers (¶31–46)
- There is a "great ruh", `ruhul aazam`, which God attributes to himself (¶31).
- An Imam was asked whether God has a ruh. Answer: God has none (not composed), but God dignifies, as with the Kaʿba "his house" and "my messenger". Ruhul aazam is God's greatest creation, reserved to be blown into human beings, and descends to the nafs (¶31–32).
- Even an evil nafs (nafsul ammara) carries a diluted aspect of it, by which it can recognise evil by a mechanism we do not understand (¶32–33).
- Named layers (names as transcribed):
  - Serr (secret): known only to God, though experienced in the knowledgeable, in whom the soul glows and illuminates mind and heart (¶33).
  - Mukhtafi (hidden): its reality is unknown even to angels; Q 17:85, "they ask you about the ruh" (¶33–34). Even by unveiling the reality of how it was created cannot be seen. This ruh can raise a human beyond the arsh, or the arsh can be inside the heart (¶34).
  - Q 31:28 (your creation and resurrection is as a single soul) leads to a question: souls are not reproduced like bodies but created by God through "that very soul which is our nafs" (¶35, garbled).
  - Ruh, lower layer, sub-branch of the great ruh; it gives spirit to the body and manages it. "It's like god in our body" (¶35–36).
  - Analogy: God, "the one whom hearing one does not prevent hearing another" (Jawshan-e-Kabir), and the falling leaf (Q 6:59). The soul likewise runs the body and does many things at once (¶36).
  - Self-observation: as he speaks, the soul simultaneously finds concepts, finds words and commands the tongue, eyes and so on (¶37).
  - Qalb: called so because it changes constantly; it receives from God and turns to the body, benefiting from both sides. Cited with Q 21:46 (their hearts did not understand) (¶43–44).
  - Kalimah: Q 4:171 (Jesus as God's word); the lowest aspect, because it "spells out" God's intentions; every creation is a kalimah (¶44).
  - Fuʾad / afʾidah: Q 46:26; the aspect that is "impressed" by looking into God's world (¶44–45).
  - "As-Sabr", explained as "the issuing": the place from which the light of God comes to the body (¶45; see section 7).
  - Another name for the whole is "Ar-Ruh" "because it fears Allah" (¶45; see section 7). Hadith from the Prophet: the Holy Spirit blew into his inner soul that no one dies until he has completed his decreed provision (¶45; see section 7).
  - Ruhul quddus: when the ruh is very close to God. High-level prophets' ruh becomes "identified" with it; it is "one deeper level of our soul" (¶58).
- Nafs: the general term for the totality, a manifestation of the ruh blown into human existence. The ruh remains after the body vanishes (¶46).
- Summary: "we don't have different types of nufus" (¶47). It is one nafs whose name changes with attitude.

### 2.9 Three conditions of the nafs (¶46–50)
- Ammara: the physical aspect overcomes the spiritual, and the soul seeks pleasure (¶46).
- Lawwama: the soul remembers and blames itself; this is "a very important sign" because it shows we hold higher values (¶47–48).
- Mutmainnah: continued self-blame leads to a stage where one no longer relies on intellect or the physical world but on what belongs to the other world; the nafs becomes tranquil and free of sin, greed and diversion toward the physical (¶48).
- "Physical aspect" includes subtle things: worrying about children's future, next year's job. The soul is "habituated very quickly" to bodily worry; if trained, it worries about higher things and sees the light (¶48–50).

### 2.10 Ramadan, night prayer (¶50–52)
- Previewing the next programme: Ramadan has many philosophies, but what matters is laylatul qadr, when angels descend. The whole month is "like a big meadow" of flowers; the soul is "exposed to this ziyafatullah", God's hospitality (¶50–51).
- If we don't fast, the soul stays occupied with the body, even digestion; those who fast, even thinking of iftar, are less affected by the body and unknowingly absorb the light (¶51–52).
- Salatul layl likewise helps the soul release itself from bodily discomfort (¶53).

### 2.11 Concluding on the soul (¶53–59)
- The soul "can be as big as arsh", and is easily lost by wasting life. Q 6:20, those who have "lost their own souls" (¶53). To forget God is to lose one's soul (¶54).
- Image: the soul is "like a very fine elastic material", which can be held in the palm yet expanded to the size of the universe; "you might imagine if you want to make a film on this" (¶54–55). Q 91:7 "by the soul and what proportioned it" (¶55).
- It is frightening that this is entrusted to us for a short time, and what we do with it "would remain with us forever" (¶55–56).
- Meditation: many speak of meditation without knowing the level or depth; true meditation is reflection inside the soul, not "not thinking about anything or sitting still". Dhikr is essential (Q 13:28); only remembrance gives peace to nafsul mutmainnah and without remembering God you cannot know your soul (¶56–57). Empty-mind meditation is "very shallow", just a distraction (¶57–58).
- Final remark: a human is connected to one ruh, but there may be other ruh not connected to the human ruh (¶59).
