The mind-to-mind account in ¶5–8 is represented fairly.

## Story 3: The young man who saw paradise (Ch 3; L07 ¶38, L11 ¶42–43, light p. 19)

Sources:
- L07 ¶38: "hadith of Zayd ibne haris, which the Prophet asked him how is your attitude? And he said my attitude is that I see the place of people in paradise. Of course Prophets approved of his attitude… The reason is not that Zayd was different from other companions… because he had gone through this purification of the soul and anyone… who goes through this process of purification… can see things that other people may not be able to see."
- L11 ¶43 (unnamed companion): "I see the hell and I see the paradise, I can tell you which of the companions are in the hell and which… in the paradise. And Prophet hushed him and said you never speak of such things, but I know that you are truthful."
- light p. 19 (Mutahhari, via al-Kafi): a lean, pale youth, "Harithah ibn Malik ibn Nu'man al-'Ansari". He says he sees the Throne, the people of Paradise and the people of hell. The Prophet tells the Companions "This is a man whose heart has been illuminated with the light of faith by God" and says "Preserve this condition". The youth asks for martyrdom. There is no hushing and no naming of companions in this version.

The quotation from Light is word for word.

## Story 8: ʿUnwan al-Basri at the door of Imam al-Sadiq (Ch 7; L07 ¶18–19, L08 ¶77–79)

Checked against the sources:
- Old man near the end of his life (L07 ¶18).
- Rejected at first, with the Imam "busy" and having azkar "every hour of the day" (¶19).
- Sent to Malik ibn Anas and the other scholars in Medina, because he was not at the level to be taught (L08 ¶77).
- He prayed that God would soften the Imam's heart, and the Imam received him warmly on his return and started to advise him (¶77–78).
- Eat only when hungry; eat only lawful food and begin in God's name (¶78–79).

No invented detail. The L08 ¶78 first counsel (do not eat what you have no appetite for) is omitted and the text says "Among the first counsels", which is accurate. The Sheikh's note that ʿUnwan was Sunni is also omitted, which is acceptable.
