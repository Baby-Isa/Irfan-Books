## Support for the chapter point ("seeing God in all things while remaining oneself")

- **`kernel` p.60: supports it.** The Imams "reached the station of subsistence after annihilation", a state "which combines the two realms of Unity and plurality", and "perpetually observe Divine Unity (ahadiyyat) in the manifestations of the worlds of contingency and plurality". They keep "the most minor of the laws". Both halves are there. It speaks of the Imams only, which the chapter respects.
- **`light` p.57: supports "remaining oneself", not "seeing God".** Return to the senses, "subsistence in God", "not ... a retrogression", "loftier even than mahw and mahq". Exact.
- **`light` p.63: supports only the first half, and only of the state of annihilation itself.** "The devotee sees unity in plurality" and the world keeps its existence. The same page describes the traveller who "no longer pays heed to himself" and the gnostic who "did not know anyone but Allah". It cannot be cited for "remaining oneself". The chapter cites it only in §1 for the world, which is correct. Keep it out of §3.
- **`forty_hadith` pp.515-516: supports both halves.** Grace comes "bringing him to himself and returning him to the domain of his own self, whereat he finds himself in the state of awakening (sahw)" (p.515). Plurality is "a veil for us" but "for them it is a mirror of epiphany", and the saying of Imam ʿAli (p.516). Quotations exact.
- **`inner_secrets` p.116: supports balance only.** "The one who joins them is the one of true affirmation". See the High item on "merging".
- **L09 ¶45-46, ¶50** also carry it: "seeing both unity and diversity, both God and the creation without being veiled by one from the other".

## Forty Hadith pp.515-516: "his being becomes divine"

Not quoted. The chapter takes only (1) p.515 "the eternal grace comes to his aid, bringing him to himself ... (sahw)" and (2) p.516 "I did not see a thing without beholding God with it, in it, before it, and after it". The sentence "his being becomes divine and God, the Exalted, observes other existents in the mirror of his beauty" (end of p.515) and the following "God sees through him, hears through him" are not used or paraphrased. "Mirror of epiphany" is rendered as "a place where He is seen" (see Medium item above). No phrase makes the traveller God, merges him, or limits or diminishes God. A reader who follows the p.516 citation will meet the omitted passage on the same page, so the Imam ʿAli line is better introduced as Khomeini's illustration of *sahw*, which it is.
