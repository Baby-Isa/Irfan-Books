# Stories audit: how well does Book 2 carry the Sheikh's stories?

_Written 2026-10-16 for the uncle's point that the stories "are the best bit". Scope: the sixteen lecture notes (`notes/Irfan01.md` to `Irfan16.md`, section 5), the transcripts, Book 2 Chapters 0 to 7 (`book2/chapters/00-prologue.md` to `07-finding-a-guide.md`), the Part Three to Five outlines (`docs/outline/part-3.md`, `part-4.md`, `part-5.md`), the Part One and Two outlines for earlier cuts, and `docs/OMISSIONS.md`. Nothing else was edited._

## Summary

**Counts.** A "story" here is a narrative with people and events that the Sheikh tells or retells, short ones included. Named events dropped in a clause are listed separately and not counted.

Check: 2 + 3 + 20 + 36 = 61.

**What the counts say**

- Chapters 0 to 7 hold almost none of the Sheikh's stories. Only two are told, and neither is his own telling: the Ibn Sina quatrains are Mutahhari's (`light` p. 29) and the Musa and Khidr scene is the Qur'an's. Of the three Story boxes, the other two ("Qadi meets himself", "The weaver at the judge's door") come from the held books (`kernel`, `light`), not the lectures.
- That is partly the lectures' fault. Lectures 1 to 7 are argument. They hold only 18 of the 61 stories, and 8 of those 18 were cut on purpose, mostly for lack of a held source (D15). The story-rich lectures are 8 to 16 (43 stories), which feed Parts Three to Five.
- The outlines for Parts Three to Five already give 20 of the remaining stories a place, but only 8 are planned as told stories. Five of those 8 sit in Ch 13 and two in Ch 14; Ch 9 has the eighth. **Chapters 8, 10, 11, 12, 15, 16, 17 and the Epilogue have no told story from the Sheikh.**
- 23 stories are cut. Six of them are the Sheikh's own experiences or eyewitness accounts (the Sikh meetings, the woman near madness, the Qadiri stone, the Cambridge anecdote, the desert camp and the warfront). The cuts rest on "no held source", but the Ibn Sina box already shows that a story can be told as "the Sheikh's account". That is a decision to revisit for the stories listed under "Worth reconsidering" below.
- Four loose ends between outlines and drafts need fixing: (1) Ch 13's cut line lists L11 ¶43 and ¶73 (and the range ¶24 to 36), although 13.1 uses ¶42 to 43 and 13.2 uses ¶72 to 73, and the range includes Abu Dharr and Salman; (2) the Ch 6 outline plans the Paris and Umra image (L06 ¶43 to 44) but the draft lacks it; (3) the Ch 5 outline leaves "recite and ascend" (L05 ¶29) for Ch 17, but Ch 17's outline has no slot for it; (4) the Ch 7 outline cuts Hasanzadeh consulting Tabatabaʾi (L14 ¶23 to 24) as "not held", while Ch 15.3 "recalls it in words".

**Flags**

- **Jokes as the point or the hook (3 stories).** Bukhari's forgotten verse (#17, "very funny"); the desert camp (#38, "a funny jurisprudential discussion"); the Cambridge scholar (#31, wry). In none of them is the joke the whole point, so each can be told for its point without the joke, but none is worth telling. Jokes that are not stories: the quip at L02 ¶12 (already handled in Ch 1: the argument is kept, the quip dropped), L08 ¶41 to 42 (husbands and wives), L11 ¶3 ("big Josh"). Hatim the Deaf (#42) is not a joke but is earthy and needs discreet telling.
- **Garbled or unclear in the transcript (D39).** Two cannot be told as transcribed: Hasan al-Basri and Imam Sajjad (#35, L11 ¶69 to 70, broken sentences) and the trees (#4, thin and garbled; go to the Nahj text). One needs its dates left out: Jilani (#57, L14 ¶28 to 29). About fifteen more have a garbled name or detail but a clear story: the wudu master's name (#12), Zayd/Haritha (#18), the Ayatollah's names (#19), the Khidr and Badr date (#28), Dehlvi (#29), Salman's lifespan (#33), Fudayl's ¶20 sentence (#41), the city in #59, and others, each noted in the table.

**Five strongest unused stories and their best home**

1. **Shaqiq and the young man who was Imam Kazim** (#43, L12 ¶30 to 37). Best home: **Ch 13 Story box** (already planned). It has a full arc, a clear transcript, the Sheikh calls it "a very nice story", and its point (the Imams stand above the early mystics) is the chapter's own. Trim the well miracle as the outline says.
2. **The Ayatollah's son and Ziyarat ʿAshura** (#19, L08 ¶24 to 27). Best home: **Ch 9 Story box** (already planned, no names). The only modern story, clear, and it makes the chapter's point (steady, not fast) without any source needed.
3. **Fudayl the highwayman** (#41, L12 ¶19 to 22). Best home: **Ch 13.2** (already planned). Option: tell it in **Ch 9.1** ("anyone can begin"), which has no story, and recall it in Ch 13, which already carries five. Skip the ¶20 sentence that contradicts the story and use Q 57:16 itself; `light` p. 25 gives a variant (he heard his victim reciting).
4. **Rabiʿa with the torch and the bowl** (#36, L11 ¶72 to 73; L12 ¶54). Best home: **Ch 13.2** (planned), or at the close of **Ch 11.3** ("even love is left at the door"), where it is now only a one-line pointer. It is the strongest single image in the lectures.
5. **Uways and the single night of prostration** (#60, L15 ¶41 to 42). Best home: **Ch 12 opener**, which is already built on sujud: "I would like the whole of life to be one night, passed in one prostration." **Unplanned.** The report is unattributed and not held, so give it as the Sheikh's account.

**Worth reconsidering (cut or unplanned, strong, on-message)**

- Hatim the Deaf (#42), **Ch 9.3**, good conduct towards people comes first.
- Jesus the physician (#30), **Ch 9.1**, sinners qualify (hadith needs a source).
- The master and the light of his wudu (#12), **Ch 12.3**, the danger of mistaking a first light for God (tell him unnamed).
- The Prophet's prayer at the Miʿraj (#26), **Ch 12.2**, with the two readings worded as true on either.
- Imam Ali's dream of Khidr before Badr (#28), **Ch 12 "Try this"**, which is the *Huwa* of Ikhlas.
- Nokhodaki and Reza Shah (#8, #9), a **Ch 6 box**, if Ch 6 is reopened: both fit "not powers".
- Musa and Yusuf in the tyrants' houses (#11), **Ch 10.1**, seclusion of the heart.
- Imam Ali's hard bread (#24), **Ch 10.2**, doses.
- Ibn ʿArabi and Surah Yasin (#51), **Ch 5** or an addition to the **Ch 17** opener.
- Sufyan and the robe (#40) and Rabiʿa freed by the light (#44), both **Ch 13.2**.

## Method and reliability

- I read section 5 and section 7 (problems) of all sixteen notes, searched section 2 for stories that section 5 omits, and read the transcript passage for every row below (the only exceptions are trivial one-clause mentions).
- **Spot checks against the transcripts.** Eight were checked in full, and the notes were reliable on who, what and where in every case: L01 ¶5 (the man asks the Prophet); L03 ¶23 to 25 (Ibn Sina); L04 ¶62 (the Cardinal); L05 ¶38 to 42 (Nokhodaki and Reza Shah); L06 ¶66 to 69 (the ʿAziz story and the well); L08 ¶24 to 27 (the Ayatollah's son); L12 ¶30 to 37 (Shaqiq); L13 ¶35 to 36 (Ibn ʿArabi and Yasin). Section 7's garble warnings also held up when read against the text (L11 ¶69 to 70; L12 ¶20; L06 ¶67; L05 ¶39 and ¶42).
- **Where section 5 was weak.** It omits five stories that are in section 2 or the transcript: the thousand doors (L03 ¶10), the Prophet's Sunday call to repent (L08 ¶29 to 30), the Miʿraj talk on hunger and silence (L08 ¶65, ¶74 to 76), Imam Ali's hard bread (L08 ¶82 to 83), and Hallaj's execution (L13 ¶15, ¶31). It also calls some items "alluded to" that the Sheikh tells in a few sentences (Satan's cry, L03 ¶57; Satan's fall, L09 ¶20 to 23). The same story is sometimes told in two lectures and is merged here: Haritha (L07 ¶38 and L11 ¶42 to 43), Rabiʿa's torch (L11 ¶72 to 73 and L12 ¶54), ʿUnwan al-Basri (L07 ¶18 to 19 and L08 ¶77 to 78), Ibrahim ibn Adham (L11 ¶78 and L12 ¶9 to 11), the Sikh meetings (L06 ¶28 and L07 ¶26), Musa and Yusuf in the tyrants' houses (L06 ¶47 to 48 and L15 ¶69).
- **Status keys.** "Cut" means the outline or OMISSIONS names it and removes it. "Planned" means an outline gives it a slot, even as a clause. "Unplanned" means no outline mentions it. A story that Book 2 uses and an outline plans again (Qadi's Musabbihat) is counted once, under the Book 2 use.
