# Ch 1 drag review (fresh editor, 2026-10-18)

Read: TONE.md rules 25, 44, 44a; CLAUDE.md "How the user works"; `book2/chapters/01-seeking-not-powers.md` in full, start to finish, as a reader. Reader assumed: a Khoja Shia believer new to ʿirfan, who may worry that this path goes around the law or means leaving the world. No edits made to the chapter.

## 1. "For the avoidance of doubt, this invitation from the Qur'an is not an invitation to circumvent the law…" (end of "What the minimum leaves out")

**What it does.** The section has just argued that the Qur'an asks for more than the wajibat. The reader this book is for will hear "more than the minimum" and ask, at once, "so is the minimum optional?" This paragraph answers before the question hardens, and it answers with Imam Khomeini, the name that settles it for this community. It is reassurance exactly where it is needed; cutting it would leave the worry alive through the next five sections.

**What is idle.** Only the third sentence. "Within those bounds, the Qur'an is just as insistent, in these verses and others, that it invites us to more than the obligatory acts" restates the previous paragraph's last line, and "in these verses and others" is filler. The turn back to "more" is worth keeping, so the section does not end on the brake; it just needs fewer words.

**Verdict: TRIM.**

Current:
> For the avoidance of doubt, this invitation from the Qur'an is not an invitation to circumvent the law or to abandon the prescribed wajibat. Imam Khomeini insists that a genuine seeker's devotion "is within the bounds of the Shari'ah" [@forty_hadith, p. 61]. Within those bounds, the Qur'an is just as insistent, in these verses and others, that it invites us to more than the obligatory acts.

Trimmed:
> For the avoidance of doubt, this invitation from the Qur'an is not an invitation to circumvent the law or to abandon the prescribed wajibat. Imam Khomeini insists that a genuine seeker's devotion "is within the bounds of the Shari'ah" [@forty_hadith, p. 61]. Within those bounds, the Qur'an is just as insistent: it invites us to more than the obligatory acts.

## 2. "Other people look at the surface of the world; the friends of God look at what lies inside it…" ("Some do see")

**What it does.** It speaks to the Nahj quotation, which has to be done (the quote is the one place in the chapter where a reader could take "occupy themselves with what is coming" as a call to withdraw). Then it closes the second worry the reader brings: that ʿirfan means leaving family, work and society. "There is nothing wrong with pursuing material wealth" is the sentence a Khoja reader will most want to hear, and "families, friends, and a place in society, as we do" is concrete. The last sentence puts the severance where it belongs, in the heart.

**What would be lost.** Cut it and the Nahj quote stands unglossed and the withdrawal worry stands unanswered until "live in the world without being soiled by it" in the next section, which is a half-line and arrives after three more verses. Trimming gains perhaps ten words and loses either the gloss of the quote or the wealth sentence, neither of which is spare. The brief echo at "live in the world without being soiled by it" is fine; it is a half-line, not a second paragraph.

**Verdict: KEEP.**

## 3. "That doorway is open to people like us…" ("What they are given, and why it is open to all")

**What it does.** Very little the reader has not just been told. The previous paragraph ends "the fourth is a doorway … The way in is righteous action." This paragraph opens by saying the doorway is open to us, says it again ("ordinary people can also enter the realm of the muqarrabun"), then "because we are made for it", which the chapter already argued in "The fault is in the receiver" (the Kernel quote: "part of how we are made"). The next paragraph is the section recap and says, for the third time, "That rank is open to any believer." Three consecutive statements of openness; two idle paragraphs side by side. This is the one place in the chapter where I felt it slow down.

**What earns its place.** The Sheikh's line "we are not yet human beings" is poignant, new, and the best sentence in the section. It is the only thing here worth saving, and it should be saved.

**Verdict: TRIM.**

Current:
> That doorway is open to people like us: ordinary people can also enter the realm of the muqarrabun. It is difficult, but it is open, because we are made for it. We call ourselves human beings, yet, in the Sheikh's words, "we are not yet human beings". We begin with the potential to become human in the full sense that the prophets are. A human being has the capacity to become a friend of God; God created us with that capacity, and wants us to reach it.

Trimmed:
> That doorway is open to people like us. It is difficult, but we are made for it. We call ourselves human beings, yet, in the Sheikh's words, "we are not yet human beings": we begin with the capacity to become human in the full sense that the prophets are, and God wants us to reach it.

(95 words to 55. The recap that follows then carries "open to any believer" once more, which is enough.)

## 4. "ʿAllama Tabatabaʾi's definition agrees: "The real gnostic is he who worships Allah not…" (last section)

**What it does.** It adds a second authority to the Sheikh's definition. But by this point the reader has had three definitions in a row within a page: the Sheikh's ("seeking and nothing else … maʿrifah and the love of God"), Mutahhari's in the Word Study box ("reach the very kernel and reality of existence, God"), and now Tabatabaʾi's. And its content, worship not from hope of reward or fear of punishment, is the same point Mutahhari made two paragraphs earlier through Imam ʿAli (as), which was the stronger version because it was the Imam's. "Agrees" also points back past the box to a definition the reader has to retrieve (rule 28).

**What would be lost.** A good quotation, and a named Shia authority on the final page. Nothing in the argument: "ʿIrfan is therefore worth pursuing" rests on the whole chain, not on this line, and reads cleaner following straight from the Sheikh's definition and the box. The Who's Who box's remark that several quotations come from *Light Within Me* stays true (pp. 6–7, 12, 44).

**Verdict: CUT.** If the author wants Tabatabaʾi's voice at the close, the place for it is instead of the Imam ʿAli recollection in the Mutahhari paragraph, not in addition to it; but the chapter does not need it.

## Pacing overall

Right for an opening chapter, with one sag. The first section is the best-paced in the chapter: a verse everyone knows, a story, two claims, three more verses, point made. The middle sections are short and move. "Some do see" is a ladder of five verses with one-sentence glosses and reads brisk, almost too brisk (the fear "that is not about punishment at all" is left as a question the chapter never answers). The sag is the end of "What they are given": three gifts, each with verse and gloss, then the doorway, then two paragraphs saying the door is open, then a recap. Trim paragraph 3 and the sag goes. The close piles up definitions; cut paragraph 4 and it ends in four clean steps: definition, therefore, question, exercise. Fourteen verse blocks in 3,400 words is a lot of stopping and starting, but that is the chapter's design and the glosses are short, so it does not feel padded; it feels like being walked through the Qur'an by someone who knows where he is going.
