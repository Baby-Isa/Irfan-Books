## What the choices teach (proposed reading, to be confirmed)
- He keeps a semicolon where the second sentence explains or illustrates the first (8, 9), not only where the two mirror.
- He keeps a colon where it delivers a reveal or a definition the sentence builds to (12, 27; 15 and 39 if earned), and drops it where it is a routine joint (16, 18, 21, 25, 31).
- He keeps an appositive or echo that adds a new shade, image or rhythm (20, 34, 36, 37, 38); only pure repetition goes ("one most of us know").
- He prefers a flowing connector to a split into two short sentences when the second half continues the first (33 "instead praying"), and adds words that sharpen the logic ("solely", "rather than").
- He values a comma for pause and emphasis, not only for grammar (41).
- He judges plain against rich by the neighbouring sentences, for variety (23).
