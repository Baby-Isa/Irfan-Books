## Section 1: Opening

**Point:** Hook the reader and say, in the words of the chapter title, what the chapter will establish; recall earlier ground in one or two sentences at most.

[verse]

¶1. In a single verse, Surah Al ʿImran gives five marks of the people it praises. They are patient, truthful, obedient and charitable. The fifth mark, in the words above, is that they plead for God's forgiveness at dawns. Sheikh Bahmanpour reports that someone once asked Imam al-Sadiq (as) how long a believer has to keep at it to reach that height. As he tells it, the answer was "one year is enough".

¶2. That answer measures the road in time rather than in feats. It leaves open what is kept for the year and, as Chapter Seventeen asked in closing, how a believer begins this week. Each chapter has offered one small step in its Try this box and the epilogue sets those steps on three rungs: a week, a month and a year. The rungs are the book's own arrangement and they rest on the lectures' advice to grow gradually. Let's begin with the rules that keep every rung safe.

## Section 2: How to begin

**Point:** Every step stands inside the law, suits the traveller, is small enough to keep, and grows gradually; a beginner need not wait for a guide, but tests any guide he follows.

¶3. Every step on this road stands on the obligations (wajibat) that we already keep. Chapter Nine called the law kept strictly the first stage of the road and Chapter Six showed that it holds to the last breath. The lectures put the first step as complying completely with whatever the shariʿa says and recommended acts are built on the obligations rather than standing in for them. Within the law, the first demand after repentance is putting things right with the people around us.

¶4. Among the many recommended acts, a beginner has to choose. Mirza Jawad Maliki Tabrizi tells the wayfarer to review his affairs and his workload first, to find out whether his mood is low, moderate or high. Only then, he writes, "he should select the proper program which is suitable for him". Chapter Ten made the same point through medicine, where each discipline is taken in a dose measured to the person.

¶5. Chapter Ten also answered how much to take on: a little, kept up. Half an hour is enough to begin the night prayer and going past the limit ends in giving up altogether. The measure is our own rather than the Prophet's ﷺ, whose practice called for a personality like his.

¶6. A listener once put the same measure to the Sheikh in the case of Imam ʿAli (as). People of the Imam's time, he had heard, likened his bread to rock, yet the Imam told them not to follow him in it. The Sheikh answered that "he's in level 1000 we just want to start level one" and that the Imam's words to others are "instruction for level one".

¶7. In a letter to his governor of Basra, Imam ʿAli (as) says the same. Content for his share of the world with "his two shabby garments and his two pieces of bread", he writes, "You will not be able to do what I do—but help me by practicing restraint and striving for good". Maliki Tabrizi adds that an act which cannot be done in full is still not dropped: "he should perform it, as much as possible".

¶8. An act that goes well invites the opposite mistake, which is taking on more and taking it faster. Chapter Nine told of the young man in Tehran whose daily Ziyarat ʿAshura put a light in his face, until an elder of the path told his father to make him stop. He was rushing and the elder wanted "a linear gradual improvement" in place of a burst of effort that leaves a person abandoned by his own soul.

¶9. A beginner who looks for a guide and finds none may conclude that the road is closed. Chapter Seven set the scholars' accounts side by side. In the Sheikh's, the first steps are the law and the guide who reads the soul's states comes after them. Before then a beginner needs a teacher who says which practices to begin with, as Mirza ʿAli Aqa Qadi set his beginners the Musabbihat and ʿAllama Tabatabaʾi's general teacher is consulted as one consults anyone learned.

¶10. Any teacher who is found is tested as Chapter Seven tested a guide. One who breaks the law is ruled out and wonders prove nothing about him. Only long company shows how far he has travelled and where he turns people shows whether he is true.

¶11. A beginner therefore starts inside the law with one act that suits them, small enough to keep and growing gradually and walks beside a tested companion if one is found.

## Section 3: This week

**Point:** Begin with repentance, then take one act of a few minutes, fixed to the time of day in which the heart is already most present.

¶12. The week opens with repentance (tawba), which Chapter Nine showed is open to anyone and begins the first stage. The Sheikh suggests the prayer of repentance that the Prophet ﷺ taught for a Sunday in Dhu'l-Qaʿdah; the traditionists allow it at any time and the lectures call it "the first step". Chapter Nine's Try this sets out its form from the Mafatih and it is prayed once, as the road begins.

¶13. After repentance comes one daily act and Chapter Fifteen's test chooses its time: of the acts we already keep each day (the salah, the Qur'an or a duʿa), the one in which the heart is most present. The new act is fixed to that time, on the lectures' advice that every traveller should find a practice of their own, since no two travellers are alike. One act is enough for the first week.

¶14. The chapters' steps fall around four times in our day: the prayer, the Qur'an and the duʿas, the night and the company of other people. The box below gives every step with its source and the first week needs only one of them.

¶15. Most of the steps belong to the prayer, which we already keep five times a day: Chapter Two's dhikr of prostration, said slowly in one prayer a day; Chapter Six's one prayer at the start of its time, kept free of other plans; Chapter Four's closing verses of Surah al-Hashr after one daily prayer; Chapter Ten's Tasbih of al-Zahraʾ after each obligatory one; and Chapter Twelve's pause on Huwa in Surah al-Ikhlas.

¶16. Where the heart is most present with the Qur'an and the duʿas, the steps are readings. Chapter One's four verses of Surah al-Rum are read once a day, Chapter Three's petitions from the Munajat Shaʿbaniyyah with the English beside the Arabic and Chapter Eleven's Whispered Prayer of the Lovers once and slowly. Chapter Seventeen asks for a few verses a day, read as though revealed to the reader alone, with one turned over from several sides.

¶17. Other steps belong to the night. Chapter Five gives the intention Lantern of the Path sets for sleep and Chapter Thirteen the account of the day (muhasaba), with forgiveness asked for any fault and thanks given if there is none. Chapter Eight's step is one of the Musabbihat before sleep and when that surah is al-Hadid, Chapter Fourteen adds a stop at its third verse, "He is the First and the Last, the Manifest and the Hidden" and a daily recollection that what we see in creation is His manifestation, but it is not He.

¶18. The last of the four times is spent among other people. Chapter Seven asks ʿIsa's three questions of the company we keep and Chapter Sixteen asks that our duʿa name other believers first and only then turn to our own needs.

¶19. The first week therefore holds repentance once and then one act of a few minutes each day at the time when the heart is already most present.

## Section 4: This month

**Point:** Keep that one act daily for a month; Shaʿban, Ramadan, and Dhu'l-Qaʿdah give it a longer form, within what the traveller can carry.

¶20. The month is the middle rung. Once the first week has shown that the act can be kept, the month keeps it each day at its fixed time and because the road is taken gradually nothing is yet added.

¶21. Three months of the year, when one of them falls within the month being kept, lengthen the act rather than add to it. In Shaʿban, Chapter Three's petitions grow into the whole Munajat Shaʿbaniyyah, which the lectures advise reciting through that month as much as one can, with the English beside the Arabic.

¶22. In Ramadan, the month of the Qur'an, Chapter Seventeen's step of reading a few verses as though revealed to oneself can be kept every day, as Maliki Tabrizi's etiquette for the month asks. Chapter Ten observed that in Ramadan we already keep most of the disciplines without naming them.

¶23. Dhu'l-Qaʿdah brings back the prayer of repentance from the first week, on a Sunday of the month, which is its best time.

¶24. Some of what Maliki Tabrizi sets for these months asks far more. His thousand rakʿas of supererogatory prayer in Ramadan are for the wayfarer whose "spiritual condition and mood allow him" and the hundred rakʿas he sets for the Night of Power are of the same kind. They are the month's ceiling rather than its starting point and the rules this epilogue began with still govern them.

¶25. The month therefore keeps the one act daily and Shaʿban, Ramadan or Dhu'l-Qaʿdah lengthens it within what the traveller can carry.

## Section 5: This year

**Point:** Keep one practice for a year and do not stop when the year ends; the keeping itself is asked of God, in the Munajat's words.

¶26. The last rung is a year, the time the teachers of this road asked a student to keep one practice, as Chapter Ten recalled. Chapter Eight showed Mirza ʿAli Aqa Qadi giving his beginners the Musabbihat every night for a year before any second instruction.

¶27. The practice for the year can be the one the month has kept. Chapter Ten offered two others from the lectures: Surah al-Muzzammil each night, which takes about three minutes and the night prayer begun at half an hour.

¶28. In the Sheikh's account, a year of the night prayer or of asking forgiveness in the heart brings the traveller under the opening verse's title of those who plead for forgiveness at dawns. The title is God's to give, so the year is kept in that hope.

¶29. The year is no finish line. Keeping a practice for a year, the lectures say, "doesn't mean that after one year you stop it", since stopping would let the traveller slip back to a lower place; what it may bring instead is "more power to ask for more". Each rung is kept as the next is added, as Chapter Eight found of the four stages.

¶30. A single act of a few minutes, kept for a year, can still seem too slight to count for much. Maliki Tabrizi turns the question from the size of the deed to the One who receives it: "what is important and useful for the servant is that Allah (the Glorious, the Exalted), accepts deed and not its being small or big". He adds that Allah accepts even insignificant deeds and rewards them handsomely.

¶31. The first steps therefore begin inside the law with one small act that suits the traveller. It is kept for a week, a month and a year without being rushed or dropped and the keeping itself is asked of God.
