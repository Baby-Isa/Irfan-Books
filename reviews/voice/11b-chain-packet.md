# Chain packet 11b

## Section 1: Opening

**Point:** Hook the reader and say, in the words of the chapter title, what the chapter will establish; recall earlier ground in one or two sentences at most.

[quotation]

¶1. These words come from the Munajat Shaʿbaniyyah. The Mafatih reports that Imam ʿAli (as) and the other Imams said this prayer in the month of Shaʿban and many of us recite it in that month too. The lines just before it ask for pardon, promising to cling to God's forgiveness whatever sins are held against the one praying. Then comes a sentence no trader would say. A trader weighs what he gives against what he hopes to get, whereas this reciter pictures the fire itself and still declares love.

¶2. Another line of the same Munajat, the one at the head of Part One, says where such love comes from. There the one praying admits to having had no strength to move away from disobedience except when God awakened them to His love.

¶3. That awakening has its place on the road to God, which has four stages. The first two, polishing the self and emptying the heart, are the traveller's work. With the third, adorning, the work passes from the traveller to God. This chapter sets out what God gives the emptied heart in that stage. Love is among His gifts and it carries the traveller until even love is left at the door.

## Section 2: What God gives the emptied heart

**Point:** Once the heart is emptied, God adorns it with gifts no effort makes, which come briefly at first and later stay.

¶4. Adorning (tahliya) is, in Sheikh Bahmanpour's words, "Allah's act on the soul", not the traveller's. Once the self is polished and the heart emptied, the soul begins to take its adornment from the spiritual world and God feeds it with what others cannot take in. The mystics name what comes as visions, inspirations (ilham) and a deeper understanding of the Qur'an. God gives these through the angels, the lecture adds and the angels bring them to everyone. <!-- src: L07 ¶10, ¶36-37 -->

¶5. What the traveller's own work makes is a soul able to catch them. Sayyid Haydar Amuli describes the change that comes when the self gives its energy to true spiritual exercises. It loses its vices (vanity, arrogance and envy among them) and takes on "gnosis, wisdom, forbearance, humility, generosity, chastity and courage". A soul purified in this way sees and hears what others cannot, "not because … they are chosen ones but because they have furbished their souls". The soul that was blind begins to see. Surah al-Anʿam describes such a person. <!-- src: L07 ¶37, ¶39 -->

[verse]

¶6. The verse joins the new life to a light and the one given it walks with that light among the people. Many verses of the Qur'an speak of this new life, which true faith brings. <!-- src: L07 ¶39 -->

¶7. Knowledge received in this way is a reward the Qur'an promises to those who do good. Surah Yusuf says so of the Prophet Yusuf (as), before he was given his mission.

[verse]

¶8. The verse's last words state a general rule. The virtuous (muhsinin) are those with depth of faith (ihsan) and whoever has it is rewarded with knowledge that does not come from reading books or analysing concepts. God puts it in the heart. Giving to the deprived and relying on God are among the marks of ihsan. <!-- src: L10 ¶11-13 -->

¶9. Sheikh Bahmanpour saw such knowledge in a preacher in Tehran whose gatherings many people attended every week and he went to them himself. The preacher had almost no formal learning and had not studied the hadith. He would state a teaching and then ask his listeners for the hadith or the verse behind it, sure that one existed. He knew the ideas of the hadith without having read them. Study still matters and the mystics, Imam Khomeini among them, all studied formally. Study alone repeats a teacher's concepts, whereas study matched with the knowledge God gives the heart resonates with it and the two reinforce each other. <!-- src: L10 ¶14-16 -->

¶10. At first the gifts come briefly and are gone. Ibn Sina's account of the first lights of the path fits this stage. As Murtada Mutahhari quotes him, flashes of the light of the Real (God) are at first "momentary like flashes of lightning appearing and instantly vanishing". With practice they come more often, until "the flashes become a prolonged blaze" and the traveller reaches "an enduring gnostic state which permanently accompanies him". The order has a reason. A light seen without its cloud would burn the eyes. In God's mercy it shines at first from behind the clouds, while the heart widens to receive more. <!-- src: L15 ¶20-22, ¶40 -->

¶11. Later the light stays and becomes the traveller's delight. If it leaves him, Ibn Sina says, "he becomes sad and bewildered". Uways al-Qarani shows what this stage looks like. He was a companion of Imam ʿAli (as) and the mystics trace their chains through him. It is reported that he waited all day for the night. When it came, he said that this night was for bowing (rukuʿ) and he stayed bowed until morning. The next night he spent in prostration (sajda). <!-- src: L15 ¶41-42 -->

¶12. Someone asked whether the long nights tired him. He answered that the night was not long to him but short. He is also reported to have wished that his whole life were a single night, passed in one prostration to God. The light had come to stay and the body follows the soul. The body tires in prayer when the soul is after other pleasures and it does not tire when the soul wants God. <!-- src: L15 ¶42-43 -->

¶13. Even gifts that stay are not arrival and the lectures call them "not a great deal", since a long way remains. Wonders prove nothing either. They come at the stage of spiritual disclosure and a great distance separates that stage from the end of the road. <!-- src: L07 ¶39 -->

## Section 3: Love, the gift the Imams asked for

**Point:** Among those gifts is love, which the Imams asked for, which carries the traveller, and which makes worship the worship of the free.

¶14. Among the gifts of this stage is love and it too cannot be made by us. No one reads a book and falls in love with God. After the soul has been purified, love is placed in the heart by God. Sheikh Bahmanpour explains how it comes. God "shows the face of Himself" to people and what they see is so lovely that they can think of nothing else. The gifts of adorning reach everyone, though only the purified catch them. This face is different. The lecture says it is shown only to those who deserve it. <!-- src: L07 ¶10; L12 ¶39-40 -->

¶15. The lecture offers a simple comparison. Two people fall in love because each has seen something in the other that draws them. In the same way, when God shows the heart something beautiful of Himself, the heart falls in love with Him. Once given, this love cannot be removed by anyone. How far it absorbs a person depends on their capacity and some can speak of nothing else. The capacity of the prophets is the highest and the Prophet ﷺ, though shown the best face of Allah, still lived like other people. The lectures do not cite it, but Surah al-Maʾida speaks of such a people. <!-- src: L12 ¶41-42 -->

[verse]

[box] Mahabba is the Arabic word for love, and the love of God is one of the two things ʿirfan seeks, beside knowing Him. The verse above uses verbs from the same root for both sides of the love: God "loves" them (yuhibbuhum), and they "love Him" (yuhibbunahu). The Munajat Shaʿbaniyyah uses the noun itself when it speaks of God awakening the one who prays "to Your love" (li-mahabbatika). The Lovers of the Whispered Prayer are the muhibbin, "those who love", from the same root again. ʿAllama Tabatabaʾi gives the word a plain sense, "Love means attraction, or in other words to be drawn by some person or some truth". On that sense, to love God is to be drawn to Him. Read with the Munajat's line, the verse suggests to this book that the drawing begins on His side.

¶16. Because love is given, the Imams (as) always asked for it. In effect they asked to be shown the face of God that would absorb them in His love. Imam ʿAli (as) taught Duʿa Kumayl to Kumayl ibn Ziyad and many of us recite it on Thursday evenings, as the Mafatih recommends. It treats separation from God as harder to bear than His punishment. <!-- src: L12 ¶41 -->

[quotation]

¶17. Punishment, the one praying supposes, might be endured, whereas separation from God could not. The duʿa goes on in the same voice as the Munajat line at the head of this chapter. If left with speech among the fire's inhabitants, the one praying will still lament and cry out to God, as the hopeful and the bereft do. Imam Zayn al-ʿAbidin (as) opens the Whispered Prayer of the Lovers with a question of the same kind.

[quotation]

¶18. A heart that has tasted that sweetness wants nothing in God's place and this love carries the traveller. The early mystics walked with fear (khawf), renunciation (zuhd) and love and each had all three. In the lectures' telling, love became in the end the victorious side of mysticism in Islam. They also call love the heart of mysticism. Lantern of the Path puts fear first and hope next, with love following when "the breeze of love stirs". Fear keeps its place too and the Kernel of the Kernels places perfection in "possessing perfect expansion while possessing perfect fear", which is "exclusive to the Immaculate Imams". <!-- src: L01 ¶27; L11 ¶61-63; L12 ¶56 -->

¶19. Worship that comes from love is a different kind of worship, the worship of the free (ʿibadat al-ahrar). Imam ʿAli (as) names three kinds in Nahj al-Balagha.

[quotation]

¶20. The merchant worships for a price and the slave from fear, whereas the free, in Imam ʿAli's saying, worship to give thanks. A report from Imam al-Sadiq (as), which ʿAllama Tabatabaʾi records, gives the free a second reason. They worship Allah because they love Him and their worship "is that of freemen". The lectures give a third reason, that the free worship God "because He deserves to be worshipped". A prayer of Imam ʿAli (as) that Tabatabaʾi quotes gives the same reason, since in it he worships God because he has found Him fit for worship. <!-- src: L12 ¶60 -->

¶21. One early mystic of Basra is remembered for this kind of worship. Rabiʿa al-ʿAdawiyya, "one of the wonders of her time", began in the austere, fearful way of the ascetics of her city and moved to the way of love. She taught that even repentance is a gift from God, since no one could repent unless God had first accepted them. The lectures note that much told of her is legend. Her sayings, poems and prayers are not. <!-- src: L12 ¶52-53, ¶60-61 -->

¶22. She held that fear and hope, good as they are, could become veils between the servant and God. The story is told that a group of Sufis once saw her hurrying along with water in one hand and a burning torch in the other. They asked her what she was doing. She wanted, she said, to throw fire into paradise and pour water onto hell. Then these two veils would disappear and it would be clear who worships God out of love and not from fear of hell or hope of paradise. The lectures set her beside the worship of the free that Imam ʿAli (as) describes. <!-- src: L11 ¶72-73; L12 ¶53-54, ¶60 -->

¶23. In Lantern of the Path, Imam ʿAli (as) is reported to describe this love as a fire.

[quotation]

¶24. The same page says what the fire burns. Once love of Allah takes possession of the servant's innermost being, "it empties him of every preoccupation except remembrance of Allah". In the stage before, the traveller cleared his heart by his own effort. Love, given by God, carries that clearing on. <!-- src: L07 ¶22 -->

## Section 4: Even love is left at the door

**Point:** Love too is a means, so at the Beloved even love is left behind.

¶25. Love carries the traveller a long way, but it remains a means. ʿAllama Tabatabaʾi describes what happens a few stages on: "In the beginning the spiritual traveler goes forward with the help of love, but after traversing a few stages he realizes that love is different from the beloved." The traveller then tries to give up the love that was his means of progress so far, since it "might prove a hindrance in his further advancement". From then on, "he concentrates all his attention on the Beloved Whom he worships as his Beloved only".

¶26. Love is the traveller's own state, whereas the Beloved is God. A traveller who keeps looking at his own love is still looking at something of his own and the love that carried him this far begins to stand between him and the One he loves. The love itself does not leave the heart, since no one can remove it. What the traveller leaves, as this book reads Tabatabaʾi, is his attention to his own love.

¶27. Murtada Mutahhari makes a related point about God's gifts with a story from Saʿdi's Bustan. Sultan Mahmud's treasure chest breaks open in a narrow pass. His riders fall on the pearls and corals, while his friend Iyaz rides on after the king. Asked what booty he has brought, Iyaz says he preferred the king's service to treasure. Saʿdi draws the lesson: "If you look to your friend for his favors, / You are tied to yourself not to your friend".

¶28. Mutahhari's point is that the ʿarif does not seek God for the sake of His gifts. If he did, the gifts would be what he wanted and God only the means to them. The real object of his desire would then be his own self, since the gifts serve its pleasure. A traveller who has stopped seeking the gifts in God's place is ready to take the same step with his own love.

[box] Imam Khomeini speaks to mystics, philosophers, ascetics, jurists, and believers alike. He tells them not to make much of their love for God or to think too well of themselves, and he puts to them a question that any traveller can ask: "Ask your heart whether it is in search of God or whether it is in love with itself". Think of one act of worship you look forward to. What are you asking of God in it?

¶29. Rabiʿa, who carried the torch, comes close to this in a prayer that Sheikh Bahmanpour reads. She asks God to give whatever He has destined for her in this world to His enemies and whatever He has destined for her in the next to His friends, "since you are more than enough for me". The lecture adds that she wants nothing from Him. <!-- src: L12 ¶59-60 -->

¶30. God therefore adorns the emptied heart. He gives it knowledge, a deeper understanding of the Qur'an and a light to walk by, in flashes at first and later to stay. Among His gifts is love, which the Imams asked for in their prayers. Love carries the traveller and makes worship the worship of the free. At the door even love is left behind, so that only the Beloved remains.

¶31. With even love left behind, what remains of the traveller before God?
