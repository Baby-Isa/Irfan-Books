# How AI-written does the book read? (2026-10-19)

User's question: the attached text (a Linus-style rant) scored 100% AI; score the book so far and suggest how to make it read less AI-written.

## Method
- No external detector is available here, so two in-house measures:
  1. **Blind judge** (Fable, fresh, saw only `packet.md`): 16 anonymised ~230-word passages, one from each of Ch 0–12 (a third of the way in), the user's anchor text, and two human controls (published translations of Mutahhari, `light` p. 30, and Tabatabaʾi, `kernel` p. 40). Scores 0–100 (100 = certainly AI). Key in `key.json`, answers in `answers.json`.
  2. **Tell counts** (`tells.py`): sentence-length variation, short sentences, short paragraph endings, "not X but Y", lists of three, therefore/thus, signposts, whereas, stock words.
- Caveat: the judge recognised the human controls partly by scan artefacts (ligatures, OCR breaks), so the gap between "human" (4) and "us" is overstated. The useful signal is the tells it names, and the comparison with the exemplar.

## The tells, and how each collides with a house rule
1. **Every paragraph opens on a thesis that restates the last paragraph's conclusion** ("Old habits return because the self fights back."). This is TONE 1 and the proof ladder doing their job, made too regular. Fix: let about one paragraph in three open on the concrete thing (a name, a quotation, a scene) and carry the link implicitly; cut "therefore" where the previous sentence already does the work.
2. **Mirrored antithesis to close a paragraph** ("The body tires… when the soul is after other pleasures, and it does not tire when the soul wants God."). Already against TONE 32 (no mic-drop); still slipping through. Fix: keep one half, or end on the source's own words.
3. **Short emphatic fragments** ("Reason has a limit." "Wonders prove nothing either."). A side effect of TONE 38 (plain short sentences). Fix: fold into the neighbouring sentence, or give it a concrete subject and object.
4. **Templated parallel paragraphs** ("The first door is the tongue, and silence shuts it." / "The second door is the stomach, and hunger shuts it."). Fix: vary the frame of the second item; start it on its example or source.
5. **Quote, then a one-line label** ("This is the polishing that gives the first stage its name."). TONE 6 (speak to every quote) done mechanically. Fix: put the point before the quotation and let the quotation have the last word, or speak to it in a fuller sentence.
6. **Signposting inside the prose** ("Taking them one at a time shows…"). This is TONE 40a's "tell the reader what is coming", done too often. Fix: keep it only before nested steps that would otherwise seem unconnected; elsewhere the heading does it.
7. **"Not X. It means Y." against an objection nobody raised.** Fix: state the positive meaning; keep the "not" only where a source or the lecturer raises the misreading.
8. **Uniform sentence length and total resolution.** Our sentence-length variation is 0.43–0.54 (anchor 0.67). Fix: allow one specific, slightly surplus detail per section (a place, a year, an odd phrase of the Sheikh's) and let the odd long sentence run.
9. **Brochure framing** (prologue: "a welcoming guide", "helpful context and short side notes"). Fix: plain facts about what the book contains.

## Round 3 (2026-10-20): a better test
- **Online detectors** (GPTZero, ZeroGPT, Sapling) and Hugging Face are blocked from this environment.
- **Statistical detector (GPT-2, GLTR-style; `det.py`, `run.py`, `results.json`).** GPT-2 was fetched from the ONNX model zoo on GitHub; features: perplexity, share of top-10 tokens, log-prob spread. Calibrated on 12 passages of the held human translations against 10 passages of the raw AI first drafts of Ch 8–12. Leave-one-out accuracy only 14/22: GPT-2 is too weak a model to tell modern AI prose from older translated prose, so it cannot grade the book. (Its chapter scores, 48–68, and the before/after difference, within a few points, are not meaningful.)
- **Pairwise blind test (`pairs.md`, `pairs-key.json`, `pairs-*.json`).** 34 edited passages (25 from Ch 1–7, 9 from Part Three), each shown as A and B in random order, before and after the edits, to three judges (Fable, Opus, Sonnet), who picked the more human-sounding version. The edited version was preferred by Fable 33/34, Opus 31/34, Sonnet 29/34; majority 32/34; unanimous for 28. No judge preferred an original with clear confidence. The one unanimous preference for the original was Ch 7's "…gnosis and ethics, and the box below tells how Akhund Hamadani was himself trained." This is the most reliable measure we have: the approved edits do make the prose read less AI-written.
