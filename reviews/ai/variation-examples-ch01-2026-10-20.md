# Chapter One: the five variation lessons in action (2026-10-20)

Examples only. Nothing is applied to `book2/chapters/01-seeking-not-powers.md` until the user approves.
Measures: `reviews/ai/variation.py` method, script in the session scratchpad. Lesson 5 is held to a high bar (see the end).

Lessons: (1) rhythm follows the thought; (2) the particular over the abstract; (3) fewer The/This/That/It openers; (4) mirrored "X; Y" and colon reveals rare; (5) a short quote from the Sheikh, high bar only.

## 1. Signs most people miss, ¶1 (lessons 1, 3)
Before: An invitation addressed to those who reflect, know, listen, and reason implies that many people do none of these things. This is not limited to the careless. Most pious people, even those careful about the shariʿa, do not find a sign in these verses. Looking at the variety of languages and colours, we are more likely to see a reason for division than a sign of God.
After: An invitation addressed to those who reflect, know, listen, and reason implies that many people do none of these things, and not only the careless. Most pious people, even those careful about the shariʿa, find no sign in these verses. Looking at the variety of languages and colours, we are more likely to see a reason for division than a sign of God.

## 2. Signs most people miss, ¶2 (lessons 5, 4); quote from L01 ¶11
Before: We have heard these verses many times, and we accept that there are signs in them because the Qur'an says so; yet accepting that a sign exists is not the same as perceiving it. The Qur'an describes the gap in one verse.
After: We have heard these verses many times and, as the Sheikh puts it, accept them out of obedience: "you say there are signs, so there are signs." Accepting that a sign exists is still not the same as perceiving it, and the Qur'an describes that gap in a single verse.

## 3. The fault is in the receiver, cattle ¶ (lessons 4, 1)
Before: Cattle see and hear perfectly well, so the comparison cannot be about the senses. It is about what lies behind the senses. Eyes and ears take in the surface of things, whereas inside a human being there should be something more: a faculty, which animals lack, that turns what the senses take in into understanding. In most of us, it is not working.
After: Cattle see and hear perfectly well, so the comparison must be about what lies behind the senses. Eyes and ears take in the surface of things, and behind them a human being should have a faculty animals lack, which turns what the senses bring in into understanding. In most of us it is not working.

## 4. The fault is in the receiver, machine ¶ (lessons 5, 3, 1); quote from L01 ¶11
Before: … That machine is the soul. It has to be repaired before it can catch the signals of God. This is the whole subject in one sentence: mysticism is about making the soul hear, see, and reflect. It is not easy, and it requires a great deal of training.
After: … That machine is the soul, and it has to be repaired before it can catch the signals of God. Making the soul hear, see, and reflect is, he says, "the summary of mysticism", and getting there takes a great deal of training.

## 5. Some do see, tawhid ¶ (lessons 4, 2)
Before: … Every Muslim knows that there is no god but Allah. Our knowledge of *tawhid*, God's oneness, is still not the knowledge of those who witness it. Tawhid is simple to say and hard to grasp; the intellect alone cannot reach its depths, and only the awakened soul can. …
After: … Every Muslim knows that there is no god but Allah and says so in every prayer, yet our knowledge of *tawhid*, God's oneness, is not the knowledge of those who witness it. Tawhid is simple to say and hard to grasp, and its depths are reached by the awakened soul rather than by the intellect alone. …

## 6. Some do see, friends of God ¶ (lessons 4, 3, 2, 1); L01 ¶26
Before: Other people look at the surface of the world; the friends of God look at what lies inside it. This does not mean turning away from the world. There is nothing wrong with pursuing material wealth in this life; the trouble is being solely occupied with it. The friends of God have families, friends, and a place in society, as we do. They think not about themselves but about God, and in their hearts they are cut off from everything else.
After: Where other people look at the surface of the world, the friends of God look at what lies inside it. None of this means turning away from the world. There is nothing wrong with being rich, so long as the wealth does not take up the heart, and the friends of God have families, friends, and a place in society, as we do. Their thoughts are on God rather than on themselves, and in their hearts they are cut off from everything else.

## 7. What they are given, closing ¶ (lessons 3, 2, 1)
Before: This is the case for pursuing ʿirfan. The Qur'an invites more than the minimum, and what stands in the way is a faculty in us that can be repaired. On the other side of that repair lie nearness to God, a good life, and freedom from fear and grief. That rank is open to any believer who obeys God and the Prophet and acts righteously.
After: Taken together, these verses make the case for pursuing ʿirfan. The Qur'an asks for more than the minimum of prayer, fasting, alms, and hajj, and what stands in the way is a rusted faculty in us that can be repaired. On the other side of that repair lie nearness to God, a good life, and freedom from fear and grief, and Surah al-Nisaʾ opens that rank to any believer who obeys God and the Prophet and acts righteously.

## Lesson 5: the bar, and what failed it
A direct quote goes in only if all four hold: it is short (about a dozen words or fewer); it reads cleanly with nothing more than added commas; it carries something a paraphrase loses (his voice, or the reader's own attitude); and it sits in a paragraph that is already about him or his reading. In Chapter One, two passages cleared it (examples 2 and 4). Ones that failed: "We are animals who have potential to become human beings" (L01 ¶24: harsh out of context, and ¶35 already quotes "we are not yet human beings"); "this is not of course a joke" (¶12: no jokes, and no asides); "god knows what type of signals you can see and hear" (¶14: needs rewording to be clear); the riches lines in ¶26 (they contradict each other as transcribed, so the paraphrase stays).
