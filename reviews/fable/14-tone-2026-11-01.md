## Verdict
The chapter is in the house voice: it opens on a duʿa the reader says, each section runs as the reader's chain of questions, the verses and quotations each raise or answer a question, the unity-of-being section answers "am I God, or nothing?" before anything else, and the critics are heard side by side (D20). The patterns that needed work were: sentences that restated their neighbour (two tidy closers), three callbacks that were inexact or missed the chance to carry content, the order of the "Praise is to me" paragraph (the excuse came before the saying), one explanatory sentence a newcomer could not follow ("denies all plurality and otherness"), the eight semicolons and one weak final comma `punct` flagged, and a hand-off clause that stated a fact no held page gives.

`voice.py profile`: PASS (0/14 chunks beyond the exemplar's 90th percentile; read-twice 1.2%; Sheikh named 7 times, 2.4 per 1k). `voice.py punct`: no TAIL-COMMA remains. Left as they were: one TAIL on "you can never rationalise this theory." (the full stop inside the quotation), two READ TWICE on sentences that read once aloud.

## Edits (before → after, with why)

### Opening
1. "Each Shaʿban, the Munajat Shaʿbaniyyah asks God for a place…" → "Each Shaʿban, in the Munajat Shaʿbaniyyah, we ask God for a place…". *We say the prayer; the prologue's "we" warmth.*
2. "Chapter Twelve followed the traveller into annihilation and back, and it kept to principles rather than names. Those whose names the tradition keeps did not all come back in the same way, and what came after the swoon divided them." → "…and back, and kept to principles rather than names. The tradition keeps the names, and what came after the swoon divided them." *The mirrored closer restated the hook; about 20% less.*
3. "This chapter sets out who stayed drunk and who came back sober, the theory the greatest of their heirs gave to what they saw, and the concerns of the scholars who have been wary of it all and the answers given to them." → "This chapter sets out who stayed drunk and who came back sober, and the theory their greatest heir made of what they saw. It then turns to the scholars who have been wary of it all, and to the answers given to their concerns." *Flagged READ TWICE; the double "and" tail.*

### §1 Lost in God, or back with God
4. "The first name the tradition gives to this experience" → "attaches to this experience". *A name is attached to an experience, not given to it.*
5. "sets the saying at the point where love itself is left at the door, when the traveller" → "at the point Chapter Eleven reached, where even love is left at the door and the traveller". *The ladder's Ch 11 callback, in a clause.*
6. "A man so emptied can lose hold of his tongue; in that intoxication some mystics spoke ecstatic sayings (*shath*), words drawn from what they had experienced…" → "A man so emptied can lose hold of his tongue. In that intoxication some mystics spoke ecstatic sayings (*shath*), words drawn from what they had seen…". *SEMI-WEIGHT; the short opener lands the step; "seen" matches §2's "what they saw".*
7. Same paragraph, reordered: the saying "Praise is to me" now comes before the lectures' judgement and the ʿurafaʾ's excuse ("he uttered these words…"), and the paragraph ends "Taken at its word, though, the saying still seems to say "I am God"." *Before, "these words" pointed at words the reader had not yet read; now the worry sits right before the paragraph that answers it (TONE 47).*
8. "is the question Sheikh Bahmanpour takes up next. He gives two readings" → "is the question Sheikh Bahmanpour then takes up, and he gives two readings". *Fold; "next" was lecture-sequence.*
9. "Bayazid had come to a station where he could no longer see himself and saw only God, and he spoke when the glory of God had removed him from his own sight. When he looked at himself he did not find himself there" → "Bayazid had come to a station where he saw only God, and he spoke when God's glory had removed him from his own sight. When he looked for himself he did not find himself there". *The same fact three times; "looked for himself" is what the lecture describes.*
10. "The tree did not become God when the words came from it; what went from Bayazid's sight was a self, while his soul remained and God remained God, as Chapter Twelve found." → "…came from it, and what went from Bayazid's sight was the false self Chapter Twelve described, while his soul remained and God remained God." *SEMI-WEIGHT; the callback now carries content instead of trailing.*
11. Hallaj: "The ʿurafaʾ excused his words as they excused Bayazid's, though they "accuse him…". Hafiz put that charge into a couplet: "…" The ʿurafaʾ remember him, even so, as a martyr." → "The ʿurafaʾ excused his words as they excused Bayazid's, and they remember him as a martyr, though they "accuse him of disclosing spiritual secrets". Hafiz put that charge into a couplet: "…"". *The couplet now ends the paragraph, so the next paragraph ("What the ʿurafaʾ blamed was the telling…") follows straight from it (TONE 47).*
12. "he would have taken him for an unbeliever, though both were companions of the Prophet ﷺ [@light, p. 23]. Abu Dharr, whose lonely death…, was no ordinary believer. The sober therefore spoke in allusion (*ishara*), a language outsiders could not follow, and it became a custom" → "…for an unbeliever [@light, p. 23]. Abu Dharr, whose lonely death…, was no ordinary believer. The sober therefore spoke in allusion (*ishara*), a language outsiders could not follow, which became a custom". *"though both were companions" said what the Abu Dharr sentence says better; "and it" → "which" (TONE 45 method 2).*
13. "He died in Baghdad in 298/910, more than thirty years after Bayazid, and he had been the pupil and nephew of…" → "…after Bayazid, having been the pupil and nephew of…". *Two facts joined by "and he had been"; participle.*
14. "For him intoxication is a passing experience at the first steps of the road; annihilation is not the goal. Beyond it lies remaining (*baqaʾ*), which is the sober return Chapter Twelve found loftier" → "…at the first steps of the road, and even annihilation is not the goal. Beyond it lies remaining (*baqaʾ*), the sober return Chapter Twelve found loftier". *SEMI-WEIGHT; "even" carries the surprise; "which is" cut.*
15. "Going further kept Junayd at his prayers." → "Across the river, Junayd kept to his prayers." *The image of the paragraph before, echoed rather than abstracted (TONE 47 imagery).*
16. "With every doubt gone and every sign behind him, what Junayd held on to was the night prayer" → "…Junayd held to the night prayer". *Plainer; no cleft.*
17. "a secret kept and a theory written seem hard to hold together" → "are hard to hold together". *State the tension (TONE 37) without the hedge.*

### §2 A theory of what they saw
18. "There is only one existence; all existing things are its manifestations, its states, and its particular forms." → "There is only one existence, and everything else is a way it shows itself: its manifestations, its states, and its particular forms." *SEMI-WEIGHT; "a way it shows itself" is the plain English a newcomer needs for "manifestation" before the verse uses "the Manifest".*
19. "It is what we mean, as Chapter Twelve found, when we say after the prayer that there is no power and no strength except through God." → "This is what we mean, as Chapter Twelve found, when we say after the prayer that there is no movement and no power except through God." *Chapter Twelve's wording of the phrase is "no movement and no power"; the callback must match it (LESSONS 30, consistency across chapters).*
20. "The verse of Surah al-Hadid and this oration held the truth as statements. In the lectures' account, it was Ibn ʿArabi who made a theory of them, and after him readers saw his theory in their words." → "The verse of Surah al-Hadid and this oration state the truth without building a theory on it. In the lectures' account, Ibn ʿArabi built the theory, and after him readers saw it in their words." *"Held the truth as statements" was abstract; plain contrast of statement and theory.*
21. "No argument fits the unity of being better than an image does." → "Argument does no better than the image." *The clever opener; the short sentence picks up Ayatullah Khomeini's "not perfect" from the paragraph before.*
22. "The mind is not built for it; it is understood "only through hard experience", which is the line Chapter Two drew between knowing about God and knowing Him." → "The mind is not built for it, and it is understood "only through hard experience", on the far side of the line Chapter Two drew between knowing about God and knowing Him." *SEMI-WEIGHT and a "which is" tail; the line becomes an image the sentence stands on.*
23. Cut: "The secret the sober kept and the theory their heir wrote are one thing seen from two sides." *Tidy aphorism (LINGO): the sentence before already resolves the puzzle, and the paragraph now ends on the concrete reason, "since without the experience no one can understand it".*
24. "he was amazed at its analysis, until he read Ibn ʿArabi's *al-Futuhat al-Makkiyya* … and found it all there" → "he was amazed at its analysis. Then he read Ibn ʿArabi's *al-Futuhat al-Makkiyya* … and found it all there." *"Until" said the amazement ended; it did not.*
25. "Ayatullah Hasanzadeh Amuli reports him saying that he had learned it all from him. Tabatabaʾi's own experiences "matched very much" what Ibn ʿArabi had experienced" → "reports Tabatabaʾi saying that he had learned it all from Ibn ʿArabi. His own experiences "matched very much" Ibn ʿArabi's". *"him … him" named nobody (TONE 13).*
26. "Many mystics found that the theory matched their own experience, and they "found no better language" for it… set Ibn ʿArabi's mystical theory out" → "Many mystics saw their own experience in the theory and "found no better language" for it… set the theory out". *"found … found"; "mystical theory" restated.*
27. "has drawn critics of its own" → "has drawn critics". *"Of its own" answered nothing.*

### §3 The critics' concerns, and the answers
28. "Their concerns come down to three" → "From both sides the concerns come down to three". *"Their" had two groups to point at, one of which welcomed ʿirfan.*
29. "The second concern has two halves. One is pantheism, which holds that the world is God. The other is the belief that God can come to dwell in a man." → "The second concern has two halves: pantheism, which holds that the world is God, and the belief that God can come to dwell in a man." *"One is… The other is" list labels (TONE 46 scaffolding); the count is kept in the sentence.*
30. "By contrast, the unity of being denies all plurality and otherness before God:" → "By contrast, the unity of being denies that anything stands beside God at all:". *Plain English for a newcomer; "plurality and otherness" is the Kernel's register, not ours.*
31. "The third concern is the one the prologue named as the chief reason for wariness, that mysticism sets the law aside, and the tradition judged its own ecstatics on this point." → "The third concern is the one the prologue named as the chief reason for wariness: that mysticism sets the law aside. The tradition judged its own ecstatics on this point." *An embedded appositive made the sentence read twice; the colon is a built-to reveal.*
32. "Chapter Twelve found unacceptable the claim that duties are lifted from one who has arrived, and such words give it no ground." → "…from one who has arrived. Such words give it no ground." *The short sentence lands the step.*
33. "Opinion on him has always been divided, since friends called him the Perfect Saint and enemies "the Killer of the Faith"" → "…has always been divided: friends called him…". *The names show the division; they did not cause it.*
34. "Visions, moreover, take the shape of the theology a person already holds; Ibn ʿArabi's visions may reflect his own." → "…already holds, so Ibn ʿArabi's visions may reflect his own." *SEMI-WEIGHT; "so" shows the step.*
35. "He found the Sunni case "much much more powerful", because "we shouldn't be unjust"." → "…"much much more powerful", and says so because "we shouldn't be unjust"." *"Because" had attached to the finding; the justice is in his saying it as a Shia.*
36. "Ecstatic words gave no licence, since the masters, drunk as well as sober, kept the law." → "Ecstatic words gave no licence, since drunk and sober alike kept the law." *The one real TAIL-COMMA.*
37. Hand-off: "ran back to ʿAbd al-Qadir al-Gilani, who headed one of the great Sufi orders." → "ran back to ʿAbd al-Qadir al-Gilani, and the Qadiriyya, one of the great Sufi orders, is attributed to him [@light, p. 34]." *"Headed" is in no held page; `light` p. 34 says the Qadiriyya "is attributed" to him, and Chapter Fifteen names it among the great orders, so the hand-off now carries the order the next chapter meets.*
