# Chapter Seven, "Finding a guide": Fable review (2026-10-16)

Reviewed against `reviews/fable/REVIEW-BRIEF.md`, TONE 1, 25–27, 38–44, LESSONS 22–26, the approved case (`docs/briefs/07-case.md`) and Chapter One as exemplar. The chapter was not edited.

## Verdict

Yes: the user would read this through. The opening hooks in two paragraphs, the ladder is readable on its own, and most sentences are plain. The single biggest remaining problem is §1 paragraphs 3–4, where the chapter's one nuanced disagreement (when the need for a guide begins) is delivered in a tangled paragraph that leaves the reader to infer the Sheikh's answer, and the next paragraph then appears to contradict it ("does not make a beginner wait for a guide" → "At the start, a teacher is needed"). Second: §2's heading and point use "beside" in two different senses, so the section's point reads two ways.

## Ladder edits

### Chapter point
- Before: "A guide who has walked the road helps the traveller, but a Shia guide stands under the Imams, so he is trusted rather than blindly obeyed and is tested by the law and long company, never by wonders." (38 words, three claims joined by "but… so… and")
- After: "A guide who has walked the road helps the traveller. He stands under the Imams, so he is trusted and not obeyed, and he is tested by the law and long company, never by wonders."

### §1 Why a traveller needs a companion
- Point. Before: "A traveller needs someone who has walked the road, because the soul meets states on it that it cannot read alone." After: "A traveller needs a companion who has walked the road, because the soul meets states it cannot read alone."
- Step 3. Before: "Sheikh Bahmanpour does not make a beginner wait for such a guide, because the first steps are the law." (describes what the Sheikh does; also a jump from steps 1–2, which never raise *when*). After: "That guide is not needed at the very start, because the first steps are the law."
- Step 4. Before: "At the start, a teacher is needed only to say which practices matter most." After: "What a beginner does need is narrower: a teacher who says which practices to begin with."
- Step 5. Before: "Therefore: early or late, every traveller needs a companion who has walked the road; Murtada Mutahhari asks for one at every stage." (conclusion and evidence in one step). After: "Others place the need at every stage. Therefore: whether it begins early or late, every traveller needs a companion who has walked the road."

### §2 A guide beside the Imams, not in their place
- Heading. Before: "A guide beside the Imams, not in their place". "Beside the Imams" reads as "on a par with the Imams"; the prose says the guide walks beside *the traveller*, under the Imams. After: "Under the Imams, not in their place".
- Point. Before: "A Shia guide holds no authority beside the Imams, so he is trusted and consulted, never blindly obeyed." ("beside" now means "apart from"; two senses in heading and point). After: "A Shia guide stands under the Imams, so he is trusted and consulted, never blindly obeyed."
- Step 2. Before: "Shia hold that this knowledge passed by inheritance to the Imams, which leaves no room for such a chain." After: "For Shia that knowledge passed by inheritance to the Imams, which leaves no room for such a chain."
- Step 3. Before: "Without that authority, a guide cannot ask for complete obedience; Shia look instead for a teacher they can trust." After: "So a Shia guide cannot ask for the obedience the orders ask for; he can only be trusted."
- Step 5. Before: "The general teacher is needed for a stretch of the road, whereas the special teacher is the Prophet and the Imams." After: "The general teacher is needed for a stretch of the road, whereas the special teacher, the Prophet and the Imams, is never left."
- Step 6. Before: "Therefore: the Imams' inner company never ends, so a guide walks beside the traveller under the Imams and never in their place." (lands the heading, not the point; "trusted, never obeyed" is landed back at step 3). After: "Therefore: the guide stands under the Imams, trusted and consulted for a stretch of the road, never obeyed in their place."

### §3 Teachers within living memory
- Point. Before: "A Shia traveller can name teachers of this road in our own time, all of them outside the Sufi orders." After: "Shia can name teachers of this road in the recent past, none of them in a Sufi order." (Hamadani died in the 1890s; "our own time" overclaims.)
- Steps 3 and 4 should swap, with the paragraphs (see Structural edits), so the section ends on the link to this book's road rather than on a new fact ("outside the orders") introduced as a "therefore". After:
  3. "They were scholars and jurists who belonged to no Sufi order."
  4. "They taught the road this book describes, knowing God through knowing the self. Therefore: Shia can name teachers of this very road, outside the orders, in the recent past."

### §4 How to test a guide
- Point: keep.
- Step 1. Before: "A line of true teachers does not make every claimant true, and learning cannot show that a teacher has walked the road." (two claims joined by "and"). After: "A guide's learning cannot show that he has walked the road." (The "must be tested" framing is the paragraph's opening link, not its step.)
- Step 2. Before: "Wonders cannot show it either, because such powers come early on the road, far from its goal." After: "Nor can wonders, which come early on the road, far from its goal."
- Step 3. Before: "The law can: anyone who works wonders while abandoning one command of God is lying." After: "The law can rule a guide out: anyone who works wonders while abandoning one command of God is lying." (Paragraph 4 opens "Keeping the law rules a false guide out", so the step should say so.)
- Steps 5–7 reorder to match the paragraph move below:
  5. "Where long company is impossible, his teaching is weighed against the Qur'an, the Sunna, and the practice of the Imams."
  6. "Both tests are needed because the road is full of pretenders."
  7. "A pretender turns people towards himself, whereas a true guide turns them towards God."
- Step 8. Before: "ʿIsa's answer gives the simplest form of the test." (a description). After: "The Prophet ʿIsa (as) gives this test in its simplest form: sit with one whose sight reminds you of God."
- Step 9. Before: "Therefore: any believer can test a guide by where his company turns them." (lands only the last third of the point). After: "Therefore: a true guide is known by the law, by long company, and by where he turns people, and any believer can check the last."

## Structural edits

**Opening (¶1–2):** keep. Hook lands in two paragraphs; the callback to the law is one sentence with no chapter named; "why finding a guide helps the traveller, and how to know a true guide" uses the title's words.

**§1 ¶3 (Sheikh / don't wait):** rewrite as one chain so the reader is told, not left to infer, that those who wait are mistaken, and so "he" in the last sentence cannot point at the beginner. Replace the paragraph with: "How early in the journey this need begins is where scholars differ. Many who want to set out look first for a teacher and, finding none, conclude that they cannot begin. Sheikh Bahmanpour does not make a beginner wait. In his account the first steps are the law: 'First you have to comply completely with whatever Sharia tells.' The guide who can read the soul's states is found after those steps, not before. The Sufi orders tell a beginner that anything done without their instruction will leave him lost, which he calls 'something of an exaggeration'."

**§1 ¶4 (narrower teacher):** the opener "At the start, a teacher is needed in a narrower sense" contradicts ¶3 for a reader. Replace the first two sentences with: "What a beginner does need is narrower: a teacher who says which of the practices handed down in the hadith matter most." Then define the Musabbihat before the anecdote: "The Musabbihat are the five surahs that open with God's glorification, which the Prophet ﷺ recited every night before sleeping. Mirza ʿAli Aqa Qadi, the teacher of ʿAllama Tabatabaʾi, had beginners recite them every night before sleep for a whole year, and only then gave them a second instruction."

**§1 ¶5 (Mutahhari):** the "so on either account" comes before the evidence (TONE 27). Open: "Murtada Mutahhari places the need earlier, at every stage." Move the conclusion to the end, after the couplet, and speak to the couplet (TONE 6): "The darkness is the soul's states this section began with, and the company asked for is a guide who has already passed them. On either account, then, a traveller needs a companion who has walked the road."

**§2 ¶2 (inheritance):** the Imam ʿAli sentence is a puzzle (why does inheritance mean he is "not counted as one companion among others"?). Replace with: "Imam ʿAli (as) received it as the Prophet's heir, not as one learned companion among others." Also fold the closing quote into the sentence so the paragraph does not end on a bare punchline (TONE 32): "For Shia that authority belongs to the Imams alone, and so, in his words, 'We don't have such a thing as tariqa in Shia.'"

**§2 ¶6 (company never ends):** land the section point, not only the heading. Replace "A guide therefore walks beside the traveller under the Imams, and never in their place." with: "A guide therefore walks beside the traveller under the Imams (as) and never in their place: consulted and trusted for a stretch of the road, never obeyed as the orders obey their masters." Trim the pointer: "Chapter Fifteen returns to the Sufi orders and how the Shia path differs from them."

**§3 ¶2 (Hamadani):** "He is not the Hamadani who found the law, the path, and the truth in a single prayer" is a negative callback the reader may not place (TONE 26, 29). Replace with: "He is not Mulla ʿAbd al-Samad Hamadani, who in the last chapter found the law, the path, and the truth in a single prayer, but another scholar of the same name."

**§3 ¶3 and ¶4:** swap them. New ¶3 (outside the orders) opens: "They were scholars, several of them jurists, and they stood outside the Sufi orders." Move the Sheikh's remark up into it: "Sheikh Bahmanpour calls Tabatabaʾi a symbol of Shia mysticism today, and Tabatabaʾi is plain that this mysticism owed nothing to the orders: 'we belong to none of these orders…' [@light, p. 105]." New ¶4 (the method) opens as now ("These teachers shared one method, and it ran through the self…") and closes on the section point: "So the teachers a Shia traveller can name taught the very road this book describes."

**§3 Story box (weaver):** fix the two "his/he" referents at the end (see Sentence rewrites). The box tells a story with a point and needs no added moral.

**§4 ¶7 (weighing against Book, Sunna, Imams):** move it to directly after ¶4 (long company) and before the Maliki Tabrizi box's successor paragraph, so the fallback test sits beside the test it replaces, and the pretender paragraphs run straight into ʿIsa's answer. Its present opener already links ("Not every traveller can keep long company with a teacher before trusting him…"). Then open the pretenders paragraph: "Both tests are needed because the road has many pretenders."

**§4 ¶6 (Khomeini):** fold the closing antithesis into the sentence before so it is not a balanced closing line (LESSONS 9): "ʿIrfan is sought for God alone, so a guide is tested by the same aim: a false guide leaves people looking at him, and a true guide turns them towards God."

**§4 ¶10 (recap):** keep (within the idle band), but vary the four staccato sentences (TONE 31): "Finding a guide therefore helps the traveller, as long as the guide is tested. A true guide has walked the road himself and walks beside the traveller under the Imams (as), never in their place. He is known by the law, by long company, and by where he turns people, and never by wonders."

**§4 Who's Who box:** cut "whom Chapter Four introduced" (a chapter tour inside a box). Otherwise keep; the fourteen years carry ¶4's point.

**Closing question:** keep as is.
