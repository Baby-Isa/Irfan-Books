## Changes (before → after)

1. **`book2/parts/4-fellow-travellers.md`** (callback and Ch 16). "some lost their footing in ecstasy while the sober reached the end" → "some stayed lost in ecstasy while the sober went further". Ch 14 says the sober went further (the book chain's own wording) and Ch 16 says that reaching the end belongs to the Prophet ﷺ and the Imams (as). A first wording with "came back and went further" tipped the page's `'and' per 1k` to OFF on a 105-word page, so the chain's wording is used.
2. **Ch 13, "The Qur'an, the companions and the Imams".** "One young companion told him after the dawn prayer that he could see the people of paradise and the people of hell, as we saw in Chapter Three." → "The young companion of Chapter Three told him after the dawn prayer that he could see the people of paradise and of hell [@light, p. 19]." Ch 3's box has neither the dawn prayer nor hell; both are in Mutahhari p. 19, so the detail is now cited rather than attributed to Ch 3.
3. **Ch 14, "The critics' concerns".** "Drawing on these reports and those below, this book groups the concerns as three:" → "This book groups the concerns as three:". Scaffolding ("those below") removed; Ch 14 was the most scaffolded of the six.
4. **Ch 15, "One source", point note 2a.** "through Maʿruf, said to have become a Muslim at Imam al-Rida's hands, to the Imam" → "through Maʿruf, whom Chapter Thirteen met, to Imam al-Rida".
5. **Ch 15, "One source", prose.** "Maʿruf is said to have become a Muslim at the hands of Imam al-Rida and to have learned much from him [@light, p. 25]." → "Maʿruf, as Chapter Thirteen told, became a Muslim at that Imam's hands." Told in full, with the same citation, in Ch 13.
6. **Ch 15, Who's Who box.** "Fayd Kashani, a pupil of Mulla Sadra who belonged to no Sufi order [@light, p. 40], praised" → "Fayd Kashani, a pupil of Mulla Sadra [@light, p. 40], praised". 7. **Ch 15, "One path, many channels".** "Ayatullah Hasanzadeh Amuli, whom Chapter Fourteen met" → "Ayatullah Hasanzadeh Amuli". He appears nowhere before Ch 15. `docs/GLOSSARY.md`, Sayyid Haydar Amuli entry: "(Ch 14)" → "(Ch 15)" to match.
8. **Ch 15, four places.** "Sayyid Haydar" → "Sayyid Haydar Amuli" (point note 4; "in Sayyid Haydar's words"; "What Sayyid Haydar reached"; "as Sayyid Haydar, Tabatabaʾi, and Ayatullah Khomeini followed theirs"). The glossary rules that the name is always written in full.
9. **Ch 16, opening.** "Kumayl ibn Ziyad, whose duʿa many of us hear on Thursday evenings [@mafatih, p. 129]" → "Kumayl ibn Ziyad, whose duʿa opened Chapter Three". Thursday evenings had been said in Ch 3 (twice), Ch 11 and Ch 13.

11. **Ch 16, "From ʿArafah to Karbala".** "This book reads the scene as both sides of the return in one moment: with his household…" → "Both sides of the return meet in that moment: with his household…". The section's first paragraph already declares everything drawn from ʿArafah and Karbala to be the book's own reflection; the point note (3) keeps "the book reads the scene".
12. **Ch 17, "Will, power, knowledge, existence".** "…replied, "My wish is not to wish at all" [@forty_hadith, p. 207]." → "…[@forty_hadith, p. 207], words Chapter Fourteen heard from Bayazid as well." The same saying (*urid an la urid*) was given in Ch 14 from *Kernel* p. 73 as Bayazid's fourth day; the book no longer tells it twice as two sayings. Point note 1 extended: "(Ayatullah Khomeini; the saying Chapter Fourteen heard from Bayazid)". Forty Hadith leaves the mystic unnamed, so the prose says only that Bayazid said the words too.

## Left for the user's judgement

- **Ch 14, "The critics' concerns", first sentence:** "No critic's own writing is among the books held for this one, so the concerns are heard here through those who answer them." An honest note, but the only place in the book that tells the reader what the shelf lacks. Keep as transparency, or cut and let the point note carry it.
- **Ch 14 vs Ch 12 on Bayazid and the river:** Ch 12 names Bayazid for ecstatic sayings and Junayd for sobriety; Ch 14 then has the Sheikh place Bayazid at the river and Mutahhari report him speaking of subsistence. The chapter leaves this open ("He may have reached that further shore"). An emphasis call: whether to let Bayazid stand as the type of the drunk, as the glossary does, or keep the hedge.
- **Ch 16, Karbala section:** the reading of ʿArafah as "both sides of the return" and of Karbala as the fourth journey walked "with them and for them" is the book's, as the section says. The user may want to weigh how far the chapter should carry Khomeini's "wielded the sword also" into the Karbala reflection.
- **Ch 17, "The return refused":** the lecture's "God doesn't want to revenge" is placed beside its "will deserve the wrath of God" with the book's gloss ("the wrath is what such a soul has made itself fit for, and not a change in God's mercy"). This is a theological join the chapter makes on the Sheikh's two sentences; it is marked as a reading, but the user may want to see it.
- **Words boxes repeat terms** defined in the previous chapter (*sukr*/*sahw*/*shath* in Ch 12 and Ch 14; *baqaʾ* in Ch 12 and Ch 16; *tawakkul* in Ch 13 and Ch 17). Left as is, since the boxes are per-chapter reference aids; a rule either way belongs in STYLE_GUIDE.
- **Thursday evenings** is still said in Ch 3 (twice), Ch 11 and Ch 13 (the last supporting Mutahhari's list of four prayers). Only the Ch 16 repeat was removed, since it was the fifth.
