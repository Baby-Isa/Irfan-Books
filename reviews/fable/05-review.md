# Review: Chapter Five, "The soul and its worlds" (2026-10-16)

Reviewer: Fable. Chapter not edited. Line numbers refer to `book2/chapters/05-the-soul-and-its-worlds.md` as reviewed.

## Verdict

**Yes, with line work.** The ladder is sound: a reader could read the four point notes alone and follow the argument from "the dead have gone deeper, not away" to "what the soul becomes is in our hands", and the prose delivers the steps in order. The single biggest remaining problem is the one that stopped the user in Chapter Four: about thirty sentences still stack two or three clauses (the eight profile hits plus some the metric misses, mostly in §1, §2 and the §4 close), so the metaphysics reads as denser than its argument is. Second, and the one logical gap: §1 step 4 asks the reader to go from "I can picture a face without matter" to "a whole world of images exists on its own" without using the premise (step 1) that licenses it.

Body: about 2,500 words (boxes about 450 more). Within the brief's range. Sheikh named five times: within TONE 23. No open markers seen. Closing question kept and plain.

## 1. The ladder (point notes read alone)

**Chapter point** (as given): "The soul lives in more than one world, its deeds give it a form there, and so what it finally becomes is in our hands." Three claims, but they are the three sections in order, so it works as a summary. Sharper: "The soul already lives in the worlds beyond death, and what it becomes there is in our hands."

### §1 One world at three depths
Point: good; repeatable as it stands.

| # | Before | After | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The soul is a small copy of creation, so each of our three ways of knowing gives us a sample of one world. | The soul is a copy of creation, so each of its three ways of knowing samples one world. | shorter; same claim |
| 4 | Our imagination is a sample of a world of images, which exists in its own right. | Because the soul is a copy of creation, our imagination is a sample of a world of images that exists on its own. | names the premise that closes the gap between 3 and 4 |
| 5 | The intellect grasps meanings without image or matter, and its world is the world of intellects. | The intellect grasps meanings with no image or matter, and these belong to a third world, the world of intellects. | one claim; the "and" joined two |

Steps 2, 3, 6, 7, 8: keep.

### §2 A soul that crosses them
Point: "The soul moves between the worlds, as far as the life it lives takes it." Keep.

| # | Before | After | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | The difference is one of degree: at death the soul lets go of the body completely. | At death the soul lets go of the body completely and lives in an image body with shape but no mass. | step 5 is where the image body is introduced; the note should say so, or 5 and 6 read as one step |
| 6 | At death the soul sets the body down and goes on in the image body it already had. | The image body was with us all along, so at death the soul sets the body down and goes on in it. | makes 6 depend on 5 |
| 8 | The angels each have a fixed station. | The angels, unlike the soul, each have a fixed station they cannot leave. | as written it is a jump; the contrast is the step |

Steps 1, 2, 3, 4, 7, 9: keep.
