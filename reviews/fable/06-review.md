# Review: Chapter Six, "No path outside the law" (Opus rebuild, 2026-10-16)

Reviewer: Fable. Chapter not edited. Line numbers refer to `book2/chapters/06-no-path-outside-the-law.md` as reviewed.

## Verdict

**Yes, with one structural fix.** The user would follow this and, I think, enjoy it: the ladder reads on its own, the car hooks the reader in paragraph two, paragraph four says in the title's words what the chapter establishes, and almost every paragraph delivers its step. The single biggest remaining problem is **Section Two, which carries fourteen steps and two separate points** (no wonder or experience stands in for the law; the first inner work is attention in prayer). Split it at line 98 into two headed sections. After that, sixteen sentences need a plain rewrite (listed below), and three quotations need to be spoken to or shortened.

Measured by hand (no shell available for `voice.py`): about 2,500 body words, at the low end of the 2,400–3,200 target, so the fixes below should tighten rather than cut. Idle paragraphs (restating or supporting without advancing) are roughly 7 of 37, about 19%, inside the 15–28% band. Sheikh Bahmanpour is named three times in the body plus once in each box: well under the cap. No `[QUERY]` markers; one `[VERIFY]` (Arabic of the Prophet's reply, line 139), carried from the brief.

## 1. Ladder edits

### Chapter point
- Before: "There is no path outside the law, because the law, the path and the truth are one religion at three depths, and each depth is reached only through the one before."
- After (optional, drops the "because" so the two halves read as one claim and its reason): "There is no path outside the law: the law, the path, and the truth are one religion at three depths, and each depth is reached only through the one before."

### Section 1, "Shell, core, and kernel" (lines 25–37)
- **Point.** Before: "The law, the path, and the truth are one religion at three depths, reached in order." (Two claims joined by a comma; TONE 40.) After: "The path and the truth are the inside of the law, so they are reached only through it."
- **Step 2.** Before: "We keep the law to draw near to God, by a path that leads to the truth." After: "We keep the law to draw near to God: the drawing near is the path, and what it reaches is the truth."
- **Step 3.** Before: "A jurist can judge only the outward form of an act." This is the paragraph's evidence, not its claim; the paragraph (line 43) opens on "The path is not a second set of acts beside the law." After: "The path is not a second set of acts: two identical prayers are equally valid to a jurist."
- Steps 1, 4–9: keep. (Step 7, the single salah, is a support step, not an advance; it is fine within the band.)

### Section 2, "The inner never cancels the outer" (lines 59–76)
- **Point.** Before: "Nothing inward can stand in for the law, so the inner work is done inside it, beginning with attention in prayer." Three clauses; needs a second reading. After: split the section (see Structural edits 1).
  - §2a "The inner never cancels the outer", point: "No wonder or experience stands in for the law." Steps 1–8.
  - §2b "The first inner work" (or "Attention in prayer"), point: "The inner work begins inside the law, with attention in prayer." Steps 9–14, renumbered 1–6.
- **Step 3.** Before: "The law cannot be bypassed, because it purifies the character that leads to knowing God." After: "The law comes first because it makes the character that can know God."
- **Step 4.** Before: "Knowing God comes through a purified self, not through study, so dropping the law breaks the chain at its first link." After: "Knowing God is not learned in a classroom, so without the law the chain never starts."
- **Step 12.** Before: "It does not, because presence needs knowledge and knowledge needs attentive practice: a circle." After: "It does not: the sense of God's presence comes from knowledge, and that knowledge comes only from attentive practice."
- Steps 1, 2, 5–11, 13, 14: keep.

### Section 3, "To the last breath" (lines 112–125)
- **Point.** Before: "The law binds the traveller to the end of the road, where keeping it becomes thanks to God." After: "The law holds to the last breath; only the reason for keeping it changes."
- **Step 1.** Before: "Some claim that an advanced traveller is released from the law; ʿAllama Tabatabaʾi calls this a lie." (A description of the paragraph, not a claim.) After: "No station on the road releases the traveller from the law."
- **Step 8.** Before: "The last stage of the road is lived among people." This is a jump from step 7 (thanks) and does not mention the law. After: "Even the return to creation at the end of the road is made under the law."
- Steps 2–7, 9: keep. Step 10 is the closing question and may stay as a question.

Read in order, the three section points now prove the chapter point: the path and truth are inside the law (§1); nothing inward replaces it and the inner work happens inside it (§2a, §2b); it holds to the end (§3).

## 2. Structural edits

1. **Split Section Two at line 98.** Insert a new `##` heading before "That lifelong keeping is also where the first inner work is done…" with the point and steps given above. Paragraph 98's first sentence already does the pivot, so keep it as the new section's opening: "That lifelong keeping is also where the first inner work is done, inside the prayers we already pray." The split departs from the approved brief's three sections (6.1–6.3); the argument and order are unchanged, so it is a sub-heading change, not a new case. If the sections must stay as three, then at minimum make the §2 point two sentences ("No wonder or experience stands in for the law. The inner work begins inside it, with attention in prayer.") and keep line 98's first sentence as the pivot.
2. **Line 88, cut the classroom aside.** "Theology can be learned in a classroom, where a student can set out how Muslim theology differs from Christian theology." The second clause is lecture detail that serves no step. Replace the sentence with: "Theology can be learned in a classroom, but the reality of God cannot."
3. **Line 104, cut the repeat.** The paragraph opens "The difficulty is a circle." and later says "The lectures call this a vicious circle, and…". Cut "The lectures call this a vicious circle, and" and fold the rest into the sentence before (rewrite given in §3 below).
4. **Line 145, add the link to the law.** Step 8 arrives from "thanks" with no bridge. Replace the first sentence with: "The end of the road is also lived among people, not above them, and the law goes with the traveller on the way back."
5. **Line 11, speak to the words displayed.** The prose paraphrases "always remember God", which is not in the displayed epigraph (§126–127). Either display §125 as well, or write: "The line before asks to be among those who always remember God. This line adds two more requests: never to fail to thank Him, and never to take His commands lightly." The paragraph's last sentence then stands.
