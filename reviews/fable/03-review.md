# Review: Chapter Three, "You already know these words" (2026-10-16)

Reviewer: Fable (structural and line edit after the Opus rebuild). Chapter not edited. Chapter point as given: "The prayers we already say on ordinary nights ask for what ʿirfan seeks, so its goal is already on our lips."

## Verdict

Yes: the user would enjoy this and follow it. The ladder is sound, the chapter is short, every paragraph has a step, and the sentences are mostly plain. The single biggest remaining problem is that the longest section ("To see, to love, to draw near") never lands its own point: its last paragraph repeats the sentence before it ("asks for a place among…") and stops on nearness, so the reader is not told that sight, love and nearness together are what ʿirfan seeks. Everything else is line-level.

## Is anything missing or too thin at 1,400 words?

No step is missing. The chain from severance → three requests → "why may we say such words" → "on nights we keep" → "so the goal is on our lips" is complete, and the cuts from the case brief (Abu Hamza §510 and §599, Mutahhari p. 20, Light p. 157) were right by TONE 43: each would have added a second voice to a point no reader disputes. The chapter should stay short. Two places are thin by one sentence each, not by a paragraph:

1. **§2 landing.** Paragraph 97 ends on nearness without closing the section (see Structural edit 10).
2. **§3, step 3 → 4.** "Even the turning towards God is His doing" (118) is followed by "The answer to the difficulty rests on the same humility" (120). The reader is not told why God's authorship of our turning answers the "primary school boy" problem. One link sentence does it (Structural edit 13).

Optional, not required: paragraph 41 asserts that the friends of God keep their families with no source in this chapter. Chapter One proved it from Nahj 3.403. A four-word attribution ("as Imam ʿAli (as) describes them") would carry the weight without re-quoting (Structural edit 5).

## Structural edits (by paragraph; line numbers from the current file)

1. **Opener, para 13.** Replace the first sentence and the "This chapter" sentence. Before: "Knowing God, and not only knowing about Him, is reached by purifying the heart. … This chapter shows that we already know these words, and sets out what they ask for." After: "Knowing God, rather than only knowing about Him, comes through purifying the heart. That leaves the question of where an ordinary believer already stands on this path. Duʿa Kumayl asks for the love that goes with that knowing, and other prayers we recite ask for the knowing itself. This chapter sets out what these words we already know ask for: the knowing and love that ʿirfan seeks." The callback stays at one sentence, the title words stay, and the chapter now says its point rather than its procedure.

2. **§1 para 31.** State the step first, callback second, quote last. After: "The Munajat Shaʿbaniyyah asks to be cut off from everything before it asks to see God. This book opened on its request for light in the eyes of the heart; in the prayer, that line follows a request for severance. Sheikh Bahmanpour reads the order as deliberate: those who seek God want to know Him and to look at Him, but 'they have to cut off from everything first'." (Cuts "reads the Munajat as a sequence", which tells the reader nothing until the end of the paragraph.)

3. **§1 para 37.** Fold the last sentence into the one before (TONE 32). After: "The Munajat asks for this severance (*inqitaʿ*) in full. Taken at its word, the request could sound like a call to leave family, work, and society behind, a reading the prayers themselves rule out."

4. **§1 para 39.** Display Abu Hamza §384 as a `::: {.quote ar="سَيِّدِي أَخْرِجْ حُبَّ ٱلدُّنْيَا مِنْ قَلْبِي" source="Duʿa Abu Hamza al-Thumali"}` block, like every other prayer line in the chapter, and speak to it in the paragraph after. The inline Arabic in brackets mid-sentence ("it asks ([Arabic])") breaks the sentence in two and is the only prayer line in the chapter set this way. Lead-in: "Duʿa Abu Hamza al-Thumali, recited on the nights of Ramadan, names exactly what is to be cut away." After the block: "The prayer asks to lose love for the world, not the world itself or the people and work in it."

5. **§1 para 41.** The first three sentences repeat Chapter One (L115) almost word for word ("They keep their families, their friends, and their place in society, as we do… cut off from everything"). Cut to one sentence and, optionally, attribute: "The friends of God, as Imam ʿAli (as) describes them, live out this severance: they keep their families and their place in society, and it is their hearts that are cut off from everything but God." Then the ʿArafah lead-in (rewritten in Sentence rewrite 4).

6. **§1 para 47.** "not in the household or the marketplace" → "not in our homes or our work" (matches para 37's words; TONE 27 needs the "therefore" to echo the premise).

7. **§2 para 67.** State the difficulty before saying the Sheikh raises it. After: "The first is sight. The Munajat asks God to light the eyes of the heart so that they look at Him and pass even through the veils of light. Yet the Qur'an says that sight cannot perceive God (Q 6:103), so some who meet these words take them for a mistake. Sheikh Bahmanpour raises this difficulty himself."

8. **§2 para 69.** "Later in the course" → "His answer, given in a later lecture, is that…" (TONE 21: never classroom language).

9. **§2 para 91.** Gloss *muqarrabun* here, at the first use of "brought near", and drop the gloss from para 110: "asks for a place among those who have already been brought near (*muqarrabun*)."

10. **§2 para 97.** Cut the last sentence ("The Whispered Prayer of the Lovers asks for a place among them"), which repeats para 91's last sentence, and land the section. After: "The difference nearness makes is felt in prayer. For some of us, calling on God can feel like calling into the sky to someone far away. Others feel Him present when they call, and feel His response. The Whispered Prayer of the Lovers asks to join the second group, and with that the prayers have asked for all that ʿirfan seeks: to see God, to love Him, and to be near Him."

11. **§3 para 110.** Three attributions in one paragraph ("states it himself", "he asks", "in his words"). After: "Requests this high raise a difficulty, which Sheikh Bahmanpour puts plainly. These words come from the peak of understanding and longing, from those brought near to God, yet ordinary believers are told to recite them. That, he says, is 'like [telling] a primary school boy to read books of university'."

12. **§3 para 112.** Cut "as a pupil would". A pupil reading above his level does not "speak from need"; the simile strains. "The prayers themselves speak from need. Duʿa ʿArafah…"

13. **§3 para 120.** Add a link sentence after the first. After: "The answer to the difficulty rests on the same humility. The words are not a claim to have reached their level; they are a plea from below it. Many of their meanings are above us, and not understanding them does not make them meaningless. We are honoured by the invitation to recite them. If the reciter is sincere, God may grant the request, as He granted the Imams what they asked."

14. **§4 para 139.** Fix the first sentence (TONE 33: the invitation is not God's "through books") and drop the second gloss of the Mafatih, which the Words box already gives. After: "The invitation to recite these words reaches us through a book most believers already own. Four of the prayers quoted in this chapter are in the Mafatih al-Jinan [@journey, p. 112], which sets each of them on a night or a day in the believer's year."

15. **§4 para 153.** Two mentions of the Sheikh in one paragraph (TONE 23). After: "Sheikh Bahmanpour still recommends the Munajat to every listener, to be recited in Shaʿban as often as they can. The two positions answer different questions: how far a reciter reaches, and who may begin. This chapter claims only the second, access to these words rather than attainment of what they ask for."

16. **§5 para 167.** Move the Appendix pointer out of the conclusion paragraph; the Try this box already says "(in the Appendix)". If a pointer is wanted in the body, put it at the end of para 141, where the Munajat is placed in Shaʿban. The paragraph then ends on the conclusion: "They ask in the voice of someone in need, on nights the ordinary believer keeps. The goal of ʿirfan is therefore not foreign to us: it is already on our lips."

17. **§5 para 169.** "Self" appears here for the first time; the chapter has said "heart" throughout. Link them in the question: "These prayers keep asking God to work on the heart, on the self within us. What is this self?" (Writer's call on wording; the join is what matters.)

18. **History box.** It is a side history lesson until its last sentence (LESSONS 17). Give it a point in its first sentence: "The prayers in this chapter come from two books that have been in ordinary Shia hands for centuries." Then the existing text.

19. **"As given in the Mafatih" (paras 41, 69, 112).** Three identical attributions read as a tic. Recommend to the orchestrator: keep the full phrase at the first use (41), where D41's reason can be carried by the footnote, and write "Duʿa ʿArafah" alone at 69 and 112. If D41 is read as requiring the phrase every time, vary it ("in the Mafatih's text of Duʿa ʿArafah").
