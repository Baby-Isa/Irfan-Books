### Is the chapter too thin?
About 1,600 words of running prose against the rewrite brief's 2,400–3,200. My view: do not pad it. The user's complaint was bloat and double reading, and every step of the argument in the case brief is present (Q 91, Q 59:19, Q 6:20, the saying with both attributions, Q 5:105 and the "window", Q 6:31, the Khomeini limit, the "in his presence" seed, Q 17:72, Maliki Tabrizi). Three places are worth a look, in order of weight:
1. **§2 ¶8, "the soul reflects its Maker"** is the hinge of the whole chapter (the saying holds because of it; §3 step 5 leans on it) and is carried by one assertion ("Yet there is a likeness") and Q 15:29. It is adequate, since the case brief deliberately cut the "in His image" hadith as unsourced. If L04 ¶21–25 gives the Sheikh's own reason for the likeness, one sentence of it belongs after "Yet there is a likeness"; if not, leave the paragraph as it stands. Nothing from memory.
2. **§2 ¶4, the stages of return,** is muddled rather than thin: two sentences assert stages the reader cannot order. Fix the sentences (S5); do not expand.
3. **The "how" of the opening promise** ("why we must know ourselves to know our Lord, and how") is answered only in outline: lift the veils, in this life. That is right for a chapter that points to Chapter Eight for the stages, and the pointer is already in §3 ¶2. No addition needed.
After the cuts below (about 130 words) the chapter will be nearer 1,500 words. I would rather hand the user a short chapter whose every paragraph has a step than one brought up to a word count.

### §1 The human story is the soul's story
Point and three steps are right. No change.

### §2 Forgotten together, known together
Point stays. Nine steps is long but each is short and the chain holds; the only step that needs a second reading is 7.
- Step 7 before: "Knowing runs both ways as well: whoever knows himself knows his Lord."
- Step 7 after: "Knowing is tied the same way as forgetting: whoever knows his soul knows his Lord."
- Step 3 before: "A forgotten soul also hides its faults, and what is not seen cannot be cured."
- Step 3 after: "A forgotten soul also hides its faults, and a fault not seen cannot be cured." (names the referent)

### §3 Near already: lifting the veils
The section point is landed by step 2. Steps 3–7 then argue what the lifted veil shows and does not show (nearness, never a grasp), and 8–9 argue that the lifting is for this life. The point note should name the "this life" half so the section carries no claim the note does not name (TONE 11); the "nearness, never a grasp" steps stay as the needed guard against misreading "meeting".
- Point before: "God is already near, so reaching Him means lifting the veils over our own soul."
- Point after: "God is already near, so reaching Him means lifting, in this life, the veils over our own soul."
- Step 3 before: "The Qur'an promises every soul a meeting with God." (jump from veils to meeting; the prose makes the link, the ladder does not)
- Step 3 after: "The veils come off for every soul at the meeting with God that the Qur'an promises."
- Step 4 before: "At that meeting the veils come off, and each soul sees itself as it is."
- Step 4 after: "At that meeting each soul sees itself as it is."
- Step 8 before: "What we perceive of God then depends on what we perceive of Him now."
- Step 8 after: "What a soul perceives of God at that meeting depends on what it perceives of Him in this life."

## 2. Structural edits (by paragraph)

1. **Opening ¶4 (knowledge by presence).** This is a callback to Chapter Two at 88 words with the hunger example re-taught (TONE 26: one or two sentences). Replace the paragraph with: "The soul is also the one thing we know from the inside. Everything else reaches us through an idea of it, and the idea can be wrong. Our own hunger we know with no idea in between, and the soul knows itself in the same direct way: this is knowledge by presence (*ʿilm al-huduri*). The soul is its clearest case, since here the one who perceives and the thing perceived are the same." (58 words; the new last sentence, from L04 ¶49, is kept because it is this chapter's own.)
2. **Opening ¶5.** Cut "ʿIrfan has a practice, the training by which the mystics come to see, and a theory that sets out what they have seen." (a digression the chapter never uses). Open the paragraph on both preceding paragraphs: "Because the soul is both the instrument of all knowing and the one thing we know directly, the theory of ʿirfan begins with it. Nothing else can be studied until the instrument of all study is understood." Then the "Knowing God…" sentence as rewritten in S3 below, then the title sentence unchanged. (TONE 1: "That is why" currently points only at ¶3, not at ¶4.)
3. **§1 ¶2.** Cut "Its power to tell good from evil is no small gift." It belongs to step 1, not step 2, and reads as an aphorism (LINGO). The paragraph becomes two sentences; that is enough for its step.
4. **§2 ¶3.** Cut "After death, we will wake to see how many such sins we committed." A new claim about the afterlife that step 3 does not need and nothing later uses. The istighfar sentence stands without it.
5. **§2 ¶4 (the cure).** The stages are asserted in an order the reader cannot follow (remembering God turns us to the soul; then first we turn to the self; then full knowledge comes through remembrance). Replace from "The mystics read the verse this way…" to the end with: "This is how the mystics read the verse. The return comes in stages: a person first turns back to the self and sees that it must have a Creator. Full knowledge of the soul comes later, as the remembrance of God deepens."
6. **§2 ¶9.** Cut the Shomali sentence: "Mohammad Ali Shomali reaches the same conclusion: "If one is determined to learn about one's Lord, then the best way to accomplish the task is to learn about oneself" [@self_knowledge, p. 9]." The point has just been argued from the Qur'an and the saying; Tabatabaʾi's "window" line lands it with an image, and Shomali's restates it in plain words. Shomali is already quoted in the Word Study box directly below (TONE 24, 43).
7. **§3 ¶2.** Cut "since He is not far from anyone" from "To be brought near to God is to have such a veil removed, since He is not far from anyone." ¶1 has just said God is already near (TONE 21).
8. **§3 ¶5.** "Yet the verse speaks of meeting Him, not of meeting our own soul." raises a puzzle and the next sentence moves on without resolving it (TONE 29). Replace that sentence and the one after it with the sentence in rewrite 17.
9. **§3 ¶6.** Cut the last sentence, "Some understanding of God through the soul is possible, whereas a complete knowledge of Him is not." It restates the paragraph's first sentence (TONE 14). The paragraph then ends on "who is without limit."
10. **§3 ¶8.** Cut "The limit of our perception of God in the next world is set by the limit of our perception of Him in this one." It repeats the sentence before it (TONE 14).
11. **Word Study box.** "The lectures accept that reading but prefer the other" names a reading the box has not given (TONE 28). Insert, after "so no one can know their Lord.", the sentence "Others take it as a promise: whoever comes to know his soul does come to know his Lord." and change the two sentences after it as in rewrite 22. (This keeps the user's approved TONE 38 wording in substance and only names "the other".)
12. **§2 ¶8 (the reflection).** See "Is the chapter too thin?" item 1: if L04 ¶21–25 gives the Sheikh's reason for the likeness between soul and Maker, add it in one sentence after "Yet there is a likeness, and through it God can be known by looking within."; otherwise leave the paragraph.
