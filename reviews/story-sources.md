**How to read the grades.** **Found** is used only where a held text (`sources/extracts/`) was read with its page. No web page could be opened this session: WebFetch failed on every host, and the proxy refused thaqalayn.net, hadith.net and archive.org. Web references therefore come from search results. Each one is graded **Likely**, and "(excerpt seen)" marks the cases where the search returned the page's own text under that reference. Check these on the page before printing. **What this means for the book.** The Sheikh almost never names a source. The exceptions are Ibn al-Jawzi for Shaqiq (#43), Ghazali's *Mishkat* for Ibn ʿAwf (#61), and Qushayri for a line on Fudayl; the first two check out. Research still traces most of the stories. Four can carry a printed citation from a held book now: Harithah (#18, `light` p. 19), Shaqiq (#43, `light` p. 24), Bayazid's four days (#46, `kernel` p. 73), and the "Praise be to me" saying (#47, `inner_secrets` p. 45, unattributed). Tell each in the held book's wording, where it differs from the Sheikh's. Ten more have a named classical source with volume and page from reliable secondary citations: #1, #3, #16, #28, #32, #40, #41, #42, #60 and #61. They could carry a citation once someone checks the page (thaqalayn.net and shiaonlinelibrary.com hold most of them) and adds the key to `bibliography.bib` under the SOURCES.md tiers. Until then they take a `[VERIFY]` marker or stay uncited. Two of these come with a warning: #28 rests on a transmitter Sunni critics reject, and #61's hadith is judged forged. Their chapters should present them as reported, not as sound. Three told stories have no source found: #5, #12 and #51, along with #26 (only a forum version) and #36 (only popular attributions). These stay "the Sheikh's account" or are left out, which matches their current status except for #36, which is planned for Ch 13.2. The four personal stories (#19, #29, #53, #58) are the Sheikh's own testimony and can only ever be "the Sheikh's account". #29's preacher is probably Hajj Ismaʿil Dulabi, but print the name only if the user agrees. #2 should keep to the meeting in `light` p. 29: the Sheikh's letter in which Ibn Sina asks to be taught has no support, and the one letter on record runs the other way.
