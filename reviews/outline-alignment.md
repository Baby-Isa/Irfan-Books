# Outline alignment review (OUTLINE v2 against held sources)

_Scope: each chapter's **Point** in `docs/OUTLINE.md`, checked against the ten source maps in `sources/maps/`, the duas.org extracts, and the "Thesis" sections of `notes/IrfanNN.md`. Keys are from `sources/bibliography.bib`. Pages are PDF `[p. N]` markers. For Nahj the format is Qutbuddin's `chapter.text`. Spot quotes were re-checked in `sources/extracts/`. Nothing here was edited outside this file._

**Verdict key.** SUPPORTED: the held sources back the point. NEEDS ADJUSTMENT: the sources nuance or contradict it. THIN: little support beyond the lectures.

## 1. Per-chapter verdicts

### Prologue: An invitation
- **Point:** ʿirfan is not magic, a cult, or a replacement for fiqh or taqlid. It has a history running from the Quran and the Imams to Isfahan, Najaf and Qom, and some scholars are wary of it.
- **Verdict: SUPPORTED.**
- **Anchors:**
  - `light` p. 9 (Mu): the ʿurafaʾ as a discipline versus the Sufis as a social group. This is the best held definition.
  - `light` pp. 13–14 (Mu): three views of the ʿurafaʾ (a ruse, an anti-Arab movement, sincere but fallible): "Of the three views, I prefer the third." This gives the "honest note".
  - `kernel` p. 56: the wayfarer learns the rules "from a jurist (faqih)". With `forty_hadith` p. 11 (fiqh connected "with all the other dimensions of Islam") and p. 35 (no path "unless one familiarizes himself with the apparent or overt form of Shari’ah").
  - `self_knowledge` p. 94: following a mujtahid "is advised by reason and approved by Islam" (taqlid).
  - `light` pp. 22–41 (Mu): a dated history of about 70 figures, enough for the two-page history. `kernel` pp. 87–88: the orders all trace to ʿAli. `kernel` pp. 8, 88–89 and `light` pp. 105–106: the Najaf–Qom line.
  - `inner_secrets` p. 160: Amuli blames "ignorant Sufis" for talk of licence.
- **Cautions:**
  - Every held Tier B author (Mutahhari, Tabatabaʾi, Khomeini, Amuli, Maliki) is a defender of ʿirfan. The "wary scholars" have no voice of their own in our holdings; they appear only as reported by Mu pp. 13–14 and by `forty_hadith` p. 345 and p. 402 ("We call Sadr al-Muta’allihin… a heretic (zindiq)"). See §2.
  - The `lantern` p. 3 publisher's note itself records jurists' doubts about that book. This is useful as a live example of the scrutiny.
- **D15:** L02 ¶22 "Jami in 255 AH" for the first "Sufi" conflicts with Mu (first half of the 2nd century; Abu Hashim, `light` p. 24). Use Mu's account and drop the 255 date.

### Part One: The Call

#### 1. Seeking, not powers
- **Point:** our inner receiver is broken. ʿIrfan repairs it, and its only aim is knowing and loving God.
- **Verdict: SUPPORTED.** The "receiver" image is the lecturer's, but every element of it has held backing.
- **Anchors:**
  - `nahj` 3.403, p. 826: the awliyāʾ "perceive the world's reality when others are deceived by her appearance". This is L01 ¶25–27's "sermon 432".
  - `nahj` 1.219.1, pp. 522–524: remembrance as "burnish" for hearts. They "hear after your earlier deafness, see after your earlier night-blindness". This is the receiver repaired, in ʿAli's words.
  - `kernel` pp. 28–29: the wayfarer "has no right to seek occult experiences and miraculous qualities" (Q 25:43). Also `light` p. 69 and p. 101 (Tb) and `forty_hadith` p. 61 ("his efforts are rendered invalid").
  - `kernel` p. 4: the religious urge is "part of the innate instincts of the human being". `forty_hadith` p. 176: "We are lovers of Absolute Perfection". The receiver is built in.
  - `inner_secrets` p. 50: "the elite of the elite, the intimate ones and those who go before", read through Q 3:18 and the Waqiʿa triads. This backs the muqarrabun / special-class section.
  - `self_knowledge` pp. 17–18: fiqh gives "the minimally necessary conditions" and akhlaq goes further. This answers "why go beyond the wajib" without the unsourced Bedouin hadith.
- **Cautions:**
  - `inner_secrets` pp. 138–139 says the saints "possess great power", and `self_knowledge` pp. 29–30 says a real ʿarif "can easily control supernatural phenomena". Neither may be cited for the anti-powers point. They are contrasts at most.
  - `self_knowledge` p. 12 (the soul-power seekers "In India") is a usable line but culturally blunt. Paraphrase it.
- **D15:**
  - L01 ¶30–31: already logged.
  - L01 ¶5: the Bedouin hadith (unidentified). Keep the question and drop the hadith.
  - L01 ¶17: Zayn al-Abidin as a reciter of the Munajat. See Ch 2.
  - L01 ¶26: Q 29:49 is low-confidence. Use it only if the context reads cleanly in Qaraʾi.
  - L02 ¶16: "7 layers, each 70, each 700" has no source. Rework to `light` pp. 130–133 (Kh, "70 or 70,000 layers") or omit.

#### 2. You already know these words
- **Point:** the duʿas we recite are the first textbook of ʿirfan.
- **Verdict: SUPPORTED.** This is very strong. The outline's "Needs: English translations" is now met by the duas.org extracts (Shaʿbaniyyah, Kumayl, Abu Hamza, ʿArafah; "Free to use with credit", SOURCES.md).
- **Anchors:**
  - `journey` pp. 42–43: the Imams' duʿas are "the Qur'an from Allah's most distinguished servants, ascending upward towards Him". This is the closest held statement of the point itself.
  - `light` p. 20 (Mu): Kumayl, Abu Hamza, the Sahifa and the Shaʿbaniyyah "all contain the most sublime spiritual ideas". p. 87 fn 2 (Tb): the duʿas "provide the best means of our moral and spiritual training".
  - `light` pp. 138, 156–157 (Kh): "all Imams recited" the Shaʿbaniyyah, and "Those who recited the Munajat Sha'biniya, wielded the sword also". Kh comments on it line by line.
  - `kernel` pp. 59–60 and `light` p. 87: the Imams' laments were real, not performance.
  - `sahifa` pp. 656–657 (the Lovers). `duas_munajat_shabaniyah` §1–134. `duas_dua_arafah_imam_husain` §781–799.
  - `forty_hadith` p. 47: read Abu Hamza "one third or one fourth of it every night with feeling". p. 401: do not explain such duʿas away.
- **Cautions:**
  - Attribution. Mafatih p. 243, the duas.org description and `light` p. 138 give the Shaʿbaniyyah to Imam ʿAli and all the Imams. None names Zayn al-Abidin alone (L01 ¶17).
  - `journey` never mentions the Shaʿbaniyyah. The duas.org description's claim about Maliki Tabrizi is the site's editorial, so mark it [VERIFY] if used.
  - The two English versions of the Munajat diverge in meaning at points (§75, §80; see §4).
  - ʿArafah: the textual status of the closing section needs a check before it is presented as Imam al-Husayn's own words. [VERIFY: textual history of the final part of Duʿa ʿArafah.]
- **D15:** rework L01 ¶17 to "Imam ʿAli and the Imams after him". `sahifa` pp. 655–657 already corrects the L01 lines to the Lovers (Munajat 9).

#### 3. Two kinds of knowing
- **Point:** knowing *about* God is not knowing God. The second kind is light placed in a purified heart, so the path is purification, not study.
- **Verdict: NEEDS ADJUSTMENT** (wording). The sources support the first two clauses strongly. They resist "not study".
- **Anchors:**
  - `lantern` Ch 6, p. 10: "Knowledge is a light, which Allah casts into the heart of whomsoever He wills." Also `self_knowledge` p. 90.
  - `light` pp. 41–42 (Mu): "The hakim seeks certain knowledge ('ilm al-yaqin), while the 'arif seeks the certainty of direct vision ('ayn al-yaqin)". Also p. 12.
  - `forty_hadith` p. 542: "There is an evident difference between the terms ‘irfan and ‘ilm"; the ʿarif "knows God through direct witnessing".
  - `nahj` 3.323, p. 800: knowledge is innate or acquired, and the acquired is of no use without the innate.
  - `kernel` pp. 6–8: reasoning "does not satisfy the heart"; Sadra's "syllogisms were barren". This suits the Mulla Sadra box.
  - `light` p. 145 (Kh): the Ibn Sina / Abu Saʿid "blind man… with his stick" story, and p. 29 (Abu Saʿid). Material for the boxes.
- **Cautions:**
  - `forty_hadith` p. 403: "The sciences are seeds of (spiritual) experience". `light` p. 146 (Kh): "Intellect discovers the truth and teaches it to the heart". `kernel` pp. 56–57: knowledge and action beget each other. `inner_secrets` p. 53: the law is intellect from outside, and intellect is the law from inside.
  - "Positive knowledge of God as ʿirfan's subject" (L03 ¶38) sits against the strong apophatic line in `nahj` 1.1.1, `sahifa` p. 669 ("no way to know Thee… save incapacity to know Thee!") and `forty_hadith` pp. 469–470. Present it as the lecturer's claim, with these as the boundary.

#### Part One hand-off: no issues.

### Part Two: The Terrain

#### 4. Know yourself, know your Lord
- **Point:** the soul is the doorway. Forgetting God and forgetting ourselves are one illness.
- **Verdict: NEEDS ADJUSTMENT** (attribution and sourcing only; the point stands).
- **Anchors:**
  - `forty_hadith` p. 201: Shahabadi had pupils recite Q 59:18–24 after prayer.
  - `self_knowledge` p. 8: "forgetting Him causes us to forget ourselves in turn".
  - `light` p. 7 (Tb): "see Allah through the window of his own soul", with the hadith and fn 11. p. 105: Hamadani's method rests on "knowledge of self invariably leads to knowledge of Allah".
  - `kernel` pp. 87, 89: gnosis of the soul is "essential to the gnosis of the Lord".
  - `lantern` Ch 5, p. 9, and Ch 80, p. 79: the self is the darkest veil.
  - `inner_secrets` pp. 114, 155: the hadith applied to microcosm and macrocosm.
- **Cautions:**
  - "Whoever knows himself knows his Lord": the `lantern` (Ch 5, p. 9) gives it to **the Prophet**. `self_knowledge` p. 9 calls it "a tradition" (Ghurar, not held). `light` p. 7 fn 11 has it cited by Sunni and Shia gnostics. L04 ¶21 says **Imam ʿAli**. `nahj` does not contain it (`maps/nahj.md`).
  - The outline's "Nahj themes 'self-knowledge and nafs'" anchors are good (1.220.1, 1.155.2, 3.192), but they are not the hadith.
  - Q 6:20 (L04 ¶18) is not in `self_knowledge`. Check it in Qaraʾi.
- **D15:** present the hadith as "a famous tradition", citing the Lantern's ascription to the Prophet. Note that the Lantern itself is only "attributed to" al-Sadiq (`lantern` p. 3). Omit "Imam ʿAli said" unless Ghurar is obtained, and log it in OMISSIONS. The L04 ¶37–44 Q&A on how close the Prophet came conflicts with `self_knowledge` p. 23. Condense it and do not adjudicate.

#### 5. The soul and its worlds
- **Point:** reality has layers, and so do we. Our deeds shape an inner form that we will meet.
- **Verdict: NEEDS ADJUSTMENT** (detail; the point is well supported).
- **Anchors (the image body and the meeting of deeds):**
  - `forty_hadith` pp. 41–42: "another form and a different face of him"; p. 46: hell "of one’s actions"; pp. 463–465: "the shell is the image of the kernel".
  - `light` p. 90 (Tb): every deed "has a corresponding truth". p. 30 (Abu Saʿid): "You shall rise in the form of your qualities."
  - `journey` pp. 113–115: deeds take form in the hidden world and meet their owners.
  - `self_knowledge` p. 21 and p. 77 (tajassum al-aʿmal).
  - `kernel` p. 5 (body, imagination, spirit all travel) and pp. 65–66 (acts have a living inner reality).
  - `forty_hadith` p. 440: "Read and ascend a degree." This is the source for "recite and ascend".
- **Cautions:**
  - **Cosmology ladder.** Mulk, malakut and jabarut are held (`inner_secrets` pp. 41, 47; `light` p. 77; `kernel` p. 60). The lecturer's "kursi, ʿarsh, jabarut" sequence (L04 ¶84–87) has **no held support**. Keep it short and attribute it to the lecturer.
  - **Layers of the ruh.** `light` p. 58 (Mu) gives nafs, qalb, ruh, sirr, khafi, akhfa, and `forty_hadith` p. 32 counts the levels as seven, four, three or two. The lecturer's list (L05 ¶31–47) differs. The "seven layers" box should say the mystics count differently.
  - **The three nafs.** `self_knowledge` p. 41 calls them "types", while the lecturer says "states" of one nafs (L05 ¶47). `inner_secrets` p. 192 lists them as states. Use Amuli.
  - **Resurrection body.** `self_knowledge` p. 76 says it is physical-spiritual and "like the body in its present situation". L05 ¶12 says it is different. Fix the ruh/nafs GLOSSARY entry first (`maps/self_knowledge.md` §3.5).

#### 6. The ship, the sea and the pearl
- **Point:** no pearl without the ship. Shariʿa is never left behind.
- **Verdict: SUPPORTED** (strongest consensus in the book).
- **Anchors:**
  - `kernel` p. 30: release from duties is "a big lie and slander"; p. 57: austerities on an unclean heart.
  - `light` p. 70 (Tb): the traveller "must abide by the Islamic injunctions from the very beginning… to the end". pp. 14–15 (Mu): "the Shari'ah is the means to, or the shell of the Tariqah"; shell, kernel, kernel of the kernel.
  - `inner_secrets` p. 48: "shari`ah is possible without tariqah although tariqah is not possible without shari`ah". p. 32: "shari`ah is my words, tariqah my actions, haqiqah my states" (= L06 ¶13). p. 32: the almond image.
  - `forty_hadith` p. 35: "Without adhering to the apparent teachings of Islam it is not possible that the light of the Divine Knowledge could descend". p. 375: concentration is "the quintessence and kernel of ‘ibadah".
  - `lantern` Ch 52, p. 55: "I wonder at a person who seeks something extra while he abandons an obligation". Ch 41, p. 46: attention in prayer.
  - `light` pp. 49–50 (Mu) and `inner_secrets` p. 140: the four journeys, for the closing sketch.
- **Cautions:**
  - **Khomeini on outward forms.** `forty_hadith` p. 35 rules out any inner perfection that cancels the outward forms. L06 ¶20 says, tentatively, that a karama-worker "probably" could neglect the shariʿa. That is a substantive point: keep a `[QUERY]` (D15 scope) or drop the "probably".
  - **The image itself.** The ship/sea/pearl comes only from the slides, in Kubra's name (Fawaʾih [VERIFY]). The held near-parallel, ʿAli in `inner_secrets` p. 50 ("the philosophers… dive for pearls in the sea… the gnostics… ride in boats to safety"), uses the image **differently**: the pearl-divers are the philosophers. Do not merge the two.
  - **Hierarchy.** Amuli ranks haqiqa "higher" (p. 48). That is compatible if the chapter says "higher, but never without".
  - **Which Hamadani** (L06 ¶23; `maps/kernel.md` §3.6): [QUERY].

### Part Three: The Travellers

#### 7. Roots and the first seekers
- **Point:** ʿirfan grew from the Quran and the Imams, not from monks or yogis.
- **Verdict: NEEDS ADJUSTMENT.** The point is well supported, but the lecturer's main authority for it (Schimmel) is not held, and two blocks of content are lecture-only.
- **Anchors:**
  - `light` p. 16 (Mu): weighs the four outside-source theories and concludes "'irfan has derived its basic sources of inspiration from Islam itself". p. 21: Nicholson and Massignon concede the Quran has "the raw materials". p. 20: Jurdaq's pitcher.
  - `light` pp. 4–5 (Tb): ʿAli as the source; the chain "Salman Farsi, Uways Qarani, Kumayl… Hasan Basri".
  - `light` p. 19: the Harithah hadith (= L11 ¶42–43).
  - `light` pp. 23–27: dated portraits (Hasan al-Basri, Ibrahim b. Adham, Rabiʿa p. 24, Shaqiq and Imam Musa, Maʿruf and al-Rida, Fudayl, Muhasibi, Dhu'l-Nun).
  - `kernel` pp. 87–88 and `inner_secrets` pp. 12–13: the chains converge on ʿAli and al-Rida.
  - Tawakkul: `forty_hadith` pp. 203–209; `lantern` Ch 78, p. 77, and Ch 11, p. 15 (Abu Dharr's household).
- **Cautions:**
  - The **Schimmel** thesis on Imam al-Sadiq has no held text. Either obtain Schimmel or carry the argument with Mu and Tb and name Schimmel as the lecturer's reference.
  - The **Ahl al-Suffa** appear in no held source.
  - **Rabiʿa** has only a short paragraph (`light` p. 24, "amazing spiritual states") and no sayings.
  - **Hasan al-Basri:** Tb (`light` pp. 4–5) and Amuli (p. 13) put him in ʿAli's chain. The lecturer calls him "ambivalent" (L11 ¶64–70). Show both.
- **D15:** "conceal zuhd" and "Nahj is full of love" (logged). For L12 ¶15 keep `nahj` 3.221 (the pig's bone, found) and 3.409 for renunciation.

#### 8. Drunk and sober
- **Point:** two ways of being lost in God, and the most debated figure in the field.
- **Verdict: NEEDS ADJUSTMENT.** The sukr/sahw half is supported. The "honest Shia debate" is **one-sided** in our holdings, and Hakim Tirmidhi is lecture-only.
- **Anchors:**
  - `light` p. 57 (Mu): dhawq, shurb, sukr; mahw and mahq; "loftier… is called sahw". pp. 25–27: Bayazid, Junayd as "Sayyid al-Ta'ifah", Hallaj "beside themselves".
  - `kernel` p. 73 (Bistami) and pp. 19–20 (the sober wayfarer "finds unity while dwelling in plurality"). `lantern` Ch 91, p. 88: "with the people, while his heart is with Allah".
  - `light` pp. 34–35 (Mu): Ibn ʿArabi "certainly the greatest mystic of Islam", with "wildly divergent views"; "Mumit al-Din… Mahi al-Din" (= L13 ¶53).
  - `inner_secrets` pp. 98–99: Amuli reads the Seal of the Saints as ʿAli. This is the Shia appropriation.
  - `light` p. 144 (Kh): "the sea is not the waves, although the waves are the sea". pp. 152–158: "Make peace with the gnostics".
  - `forty_hadith` p. 402: Sadra called "a heretic (zindiq)". p. 345: do not "blindly accuse them of unbelief".
- **Cautions:**
  - **The critics' side is not held.** Mu p. 13 summarises it in one line. Without a Tier B critic the "honest debate" will read as advocacy.
  - **Ibn ʿArabi's affiliation.** Mu p. 34 gives his chain through Abu Madyan to Gilani (Sunni), while Amuli makes him a witness to ʿAli. Present both.
  - **Unity of being.** The lecture has four theories plus Sadra's tashkik (L13 ¶55–67). `light` p. 12 has two. Keep the four in a box, sourced to the lecture.

#### 9. Sufis and Shia
- **Point:** what the orders really are, what we share, and where we differ: no silsila and no blind obedience.
- **Verdict: NEEDS ADJUSTMENT.** The held Shia gnostics **all used** shaykh, bayʿa or khirqa forms, so "no tariqa" needs nuancing.
- **Anchors:**
  - `light` p. 105 (Tb): about 100 orders, 25 principal, "Only two or three of them are Shi'ite… But we belong to none of these orders".
  - `kernel` p. 88: "our tariqah is that of the marhum Akhund which does not end in any of these silsilahs". This is probably the "one Shia tariqa" promised at L06 ¶15 and L14 ¶25.
  - `light` pp. 5–6 (Tb): the Sufi system's "total obedience, liturgy, special robes, music and ecstasy" were invented; the Qutb "does not actually exist anywhere outside the Shi’ite world".
  - `inner_secrets` p. 29: Amuli compares the sincere Sufis and the Imamiyya. p. 13: the Junayd and Suhrawardi chains to al-Rida (= L14 ¶36–37).
  - `light` pp. 31–34, 40–41: Ghazali, Gilani, Kubra, Suhrawardi; Sadra and Fayd outside the orders. `kernel` pp. 86–87: the Naqshbandi staring technique, barred.
- **Contradictions:**
  - **Amuli** took the Sufi khirqa (pp. 8, 14) and says the wayfarer must not travel "without a shaykh of perfection" (p. 192), with obedience "without any further argument".
  - The **Kernel** expects bayʿa (p. 60) and loyalty (p. 64).
  - **Khomeini** was Shahabadi's "murid with his murshid" (`forty_hadith` p. 8).
  - All three are Shia. The defensible claim is "no silsila *of authority* beside the Imam", with the Imam as the inner teacher (`kernel` pp. 80–81; `forty_hadith` p. 464).
- **D15:** the **Khoja/pir box** (Akhtar) has no held source. Drop it until held. Samaʿ appears only in Tb's single line ("music and ecstasy"); keep it brief.

### Part Four: The Road

#### 10. Finding a guide
- **Point:** the living Shia chain, and what a trustworthy guide is today.
- **Verdict: NEEDS ADJUSTMENT.** The **chain as listed is not what the sources give.** The guide/charlatan half is very strong.
- **Anchors:**
  - `kernel` pp. 87–89 and `light` pp. 105–106: an unnamed weaver, then Sayyid ʿAli Shushtari, then Akhund Mulla Husayn-Quli Hamadani. His pupils were Maliki Tabrizi, Sayyid Ahmad Karbalaʾi, Bahari and Hubbubi. Then Qadi, then Tabatabaʾi.
  - `light` pp. 100–102 (Tb): general versus special preceptor; test over time; powers prove nothing; "compare… with those of the Holy Prophet and the Imams".
  - `kernel` pp. 80–83: the Imam is the inner teacher; "A thousand trap lies concealed in this wilderness".
  - `forty_hadith` pp. 102–103, 237, 465: the false murshids. `journey` pp. 131–133: the sincerity test, "why does he like and desire to be the leader".
  - `lantern` Ch 8, p. 13 ("one the sight of whom reminds you of Allah"); Ch 72, p. 70 (five qualities of an adviser).
  - `inner_secrets` pp. 51–52: the shaykh as doctor.
- **Cautions:**
  - **Mulla Sadra is not a link** in either held chain. He is a precursor, outside the orders (`light` pp. 40–41).
  - **Bahjat and Hasanzadeh Amoli** appear in no held source.
  - **Khomeini's** line is via Shahabadi (`forty_hadith` p. 8), not the Qadi circle.
  - The L07 ¶14 "no teacher at the start" view differs from `kernel` p. 80 (the general teacher is needed "only at the beginning"). Present this as a nuance.

#### 11. The stages of the first journey
- **Point:** the road has stages: polishing, emptying, adorning, and losing oneself in God.
- **Verdict: NEEDS ADJUSTMENT.** The fourfold scheme as named is the lecturer's. The held "ladder" is Ibn Sina's, via Mu.
- **Anchors:**
  - `light` pp. 41–50 (Mu, *Isharat* IX): iradah (pp. 45–47), riyada (pp. 47–48), flashes (p. 48), the mirror (p. 49), union; Tusi counts nine stages (p. 50). This is the source for the one-page table.
  - `inner_secrets` pp. 108–110: takhliya and tahliya as seven vices exchanged for seven virtues. p. 195: a stage list ending in fana.
  - `lantern` Ch 58, p. 59: the heart "polished by the burnish of repentance" (tajliya in substance). Ch 56, p. 58: fear, hope and love as a ladder.
  - `forty_hadith` pp. 35–37: contemplation, resolve (citing Ibn Sina's iradah), musharata, muraqaba.
  - `kernel` pp. 33–45 and pp. 90–92: the twelve realms and the four unities, a parallel Shia route.
- **Cautions:**
  - "Tajliyah, takhliyah, tahliyah" do not occur together in any held source. Present the triad as the lecturer's framing, with Amuli and the Lantern for the substance.
  - The thunder, mirror-frame and host's-house analogies are **absent** from Mu's Ibn Sina. Mark them in the prose as the lecturer's explanations, not Ibn Sina's.
  - Isaac of Nineveh has no held source. One paragraph, attributed to the lecture, or cut.

#### 12. Setting out
- **Point:** anyone can begin, at any age and after any sin, but slowly. Good character is the first condition.
- **Verdict: NEEDS ADJUSTMENT.** "Any age" and "relapse" are softer in the lecture than in the sources.
- **Anchors:**
  - `light` pp. 83–84 (the Zanjan engineer: "Have a bath for repentance, and don't do such a thing again"). p. 89: rule 3, "Too much enthusiasm in the beginning leads to too little interest in the end". p. 70: character.
  - `kernel` pp. 62–64: "Do not force worship on yourself"; Imam al-Sadiq's "lift him up gently".
  - `forty_hadith` pp. 249–251: tawba is no "mere verbal exercise". p. 504: the hadith of those God loves.
  - `nahj` 1.191, pp. 476–480 (Hammam). `lantern` Ch 44, p. 49, and Ch 77, p. 76.
  - `mafatih` p. 356: the Dhu'l-Qaʿdah rite.
  - `journey` pp. 160–161: do not drop a small good deed. pp. 173–175: Imam al-Sajjad's forgiveness diary.
- **Cautions:**
  - **Age.** `forty_hadith` p. 118 and p. 250 ("rarely attainable"; youth is the time) and `light` pp. 126–127 (Kh: "do not postpone… to old age") push harder than L08 ¶13–22.
  - **Relapse.** Tb rule 4 (`light` p. 89) and `kernel` p. 64 (wafa) say a sin repented "must not be committed again".
  - Keep the lecturer's mercy, and quote the harder voices so readers are not misled.
- **D15:**
  - The rite follows Mafatih p. 356: **100** istighfar, **Falaq and Nas**, no "in twos".
  - Drop "the Prophet came on a Sunday" (L08 ¶29).
  - Drop the attribution of the rite to al-Muraqabat. The held `journey` is Ramadan-only and lacks it.
  - **Kubra's voluntary journey** comes from the slides only (`light` pp. 32–33 is a bare portrait). Keep it as the lecturer's citation or cut it.
  - **Hadith of the Miʿraj:** use `forty_hadith` p. 504 and `self_knowledge` p. 90 (which gives it as a Miʿraj report, *Usul al-Kafi* 2:352–3) for "those God loves". The full Miʿraj text is unheld (★★).

#### 13. The five disciplines
- **Point:** silence, hunger, night prayer, seclusion and remembrance, each in measured doses.
- **Verdict: NEEDS ADJUSTMENT** (seclusion, and two unsourced lecture texts). The fivefold itself is well supported.
- **Anchors:**
  - `lantern` Ch 54, p. 56: silence, retreat, hunger and "wakefulness, which illuminates your heart" grouped together. This is the best single anchor.
  - `light` pp. 97–98 (Tb, rules 13–16) and `kernel` pp. 75–78: the same set, plus dhikr (`kernel` pp. 57–58, 83–85; awrad "like medicines").
  - `journey` pp. 15–22: the fullest treatment of hunger. p. 16: the Miʿraj chain from silence to wisdom (= L08 ¶65–75).
  - `nahj` 1.191, p. 478: the night vigil (Hammam). `mafatih` p. 848; `duas_salat_al_layl_tahajjud_prayer`; `sahifa` Supplication 32.
  - `mafatih` p. 802: the tasbih of al-Zahra. `sahifa` Munajat 13, pp. 674–675.
  - Measure: `lantern` Ch 52, p. 55 ("nearest to security"); `light` p. 89 (rule 3); `kernel` pp. 61–64.
- **Contradictions:**
  - **Seclusion.** The Kernel calls special seclusion, "distancing oneself from all people", "considered essential by the masters" (p. 77). The `lantern` Ch 45, p. 50 says no prophet or wasi "has not chosen retreat". Amuli did a Najaf seclusion "of an intensity and severity never before achieved" (pp. 8, 10). Khomeini: "in seclusion pray to God" (`forty_hadith` p. 36). The lecturer (L10 ¶41–45) is wary and prefers the nightly hour as seclusion of the heart. `journey` pp. 75 and 159 back the lecturer's form.
  - **Tabatabaʾi "against extreme feats"**: the forty-day quote is **not in the Kernel**, which in fact approves the forty-day hadith (pp. 7, 23).
- **D15:**
  - **ʿUnwan al-Basri**: not in the Lantern and not held anywhere. Remove the named hadith frame, or obtain *Bihar*. Carry its counsels with the Lantern ("no worse vessel than his belly", Ch 34, p. 40) without citing the Lantern for the hadith.
  - "Small constant deed" (logged): rework with `journey` pp. 160–161.
  - Tabatabaʾi's forty-day quote: keep the lecturer's caution, attributed to the lecturer, with `kernel` pp. 28–29, 63–64 and `light` p. 101 as support. Drop "Tabatabaʾi said".

#### 14. Love, the engine
- **Point:** fear and renunciation begin the path; love carries it.
- **Verdict: SUPPORTED**, with one doctrinal caution.
- **Anchors:**
  - `sahifa` pp. 656–657 (the Lovers) and pp. 669–670 (the Knowers: "how pleasant the taste of Thy love").
  - `nahj` 3.222, p. 760: worship of merchants, slaves and the free (= L12 ¶60). `kernel` pp. 70–72: love is "the worship of freemen".
  - `duas_dua_arafah_imam_husain` §783–785: "It is You Who have removed love for others from the hearts of Your lovers so that they have loved none save You". §798: "O He Who has made His lovers the sweet taste of entertainment".
  - `inner_secrets` p. 197: "As for gnosis, it is preceded by love".
  - `lantern` Ch 92–94, pp. 88–90. `forty_hadith` p. 176.
  - `light` pp. 44–45: Mahmud and Ayaz.
- **Cautions:**
  - `kernel` pp. 72–73 and `light` p. 95 (Tb): even love is a means, to be left at the end ("love is something other than the beloved"). "Carries it" is fine; "is the goal" is not.
  - `forty_hadith` p. 293 calls fear- or reward-driven worship "the idol of one’s carnal desire" (accepted by leniency). This is harsher than the lecturer's view that fear is a legitimate beginning.
  - `forty_hadith` p. 89 warns against loud claims of love.
  - Nahj has few love passages, as the outline notes (1.224, p. 532 is the best).

#### 15. Losing the self, finding God
- **Point:** fana is losing the false sense of self, not merging with God. Baqa is returning transformed.
- **Verdict: SUPPORTED**, with cautions on imagery and counting.
- **Anchors:**
  - `inner_secrets` p. 115: "a gnostic annihilation and not an actual experiential annihilation". This backs the lecturer's epistemological reading (L09 ¶3–8).
  - `inner_secrets` p. 116: "Beware of merging". `kernel` p. 84: hulul and ittihad "are kufr and shirk". `forty_hadith` pp. 516–517 (Baha'i's warning).
  - `inner_secrets` pp. 113–117: **three degrees (action, attribute, essence)**, exactly the lecturer's count. pp. 45–46: the Prophet's fana did not stop him "eating, drinking or getting married".
  - `light` pp. 102–103 (Tb): the dangers after fana, "that Allah has dwelt in him" and licence. `forty_hadith` p. 158 and pp. 492–494.
  - `light` p. 57 (Mu): mahw and mahq as fana, then baqa and sahw. p. 63 (Tb): not nirvana, "sees unity in plurality".
- **Cautions:**
  - Tb counts **four** unities (adds names: `kernel` pp. 90–91; `light` pp. 107–108).
  - Mu and Kh use the **drop in the sea** (`light` pp. 42, 136, though Kh adds "the simile does not represent the position fully"). Amuli uses charcoal and fire: "the one becomes the other" (p. 174). Khomeini sounds ontological (`forty_hadith` p. 515). Phrase the point as "not *becoming* God" rather than "not merging", because the sources do use union imagery.
  - **Knysh and Baldick** are not held. Cite them only once obtained.
  - Rumi: `light` p. 47 (the reed) and `journey` pp. 182–183.

### Part Five: The Return

#### 16. The four journeys and the perfect human
- **Point:** the end of the road is a return to people, with God.
- **Verdict: NEEDS ADJUSTMENT.** The point is exactly what the sources say, but it **collides with the lecturer's own restriction**.

#### 17. Reading the Quran with the heart, and the return to God
- **Point:** the inner meaning never cancels the outer. The return is the surrender, or the refusal, of will, power, knowledge and existence.
- **Verdict: SUPPORTED.** Tusi turns out to be held.

  - `journey` pp. 53–54: limiting the Quran "to their apparent" closes the path.
  - `lantern` Ch 12, p. 17: the outer limits are never dropped.
  - `forty_hadith` p. 39 ("Jahannam al-firaq") and p. 238.
- **Cautions:**
  - Khomeini on outward forms: `forty_hadith` p. 365 criticises reading paradise and hell as mere perceptions (aimed at Sadra), which is close to the lecturer's "opposed will" reading (L16 ¶42–48). p. 137 keeps the "fire of Divine Wrath".   - Qadi al-Nuʿman is not held. Use him as a one-line example attributed to the lecture.
