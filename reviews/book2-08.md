## Argument (D22) against the brief

- Three headings map one-to-one onto 8.1, 8.2 and 8.3, in the brief's order. The boxes (road at a glance, philosopher's ladder), Try this, and the hand-off are present. No story, as the brief decided.
- Section 1: premises 1-3 and 5 appear in order. Premise 4 (two kinds of veil) is sourced and in order. The step from 4 to 5 and the conclusion fails as written: premise 5 ("the veils lie on our side") is false for the buffer veils the chapter has just described (the medium finding on l.58). Fix that, and "the journey removes veils inside us" follows.
- Section 2: premises 1a-1d and 2 appear in order, each with a source, except the dark-veil mapping for 1a (book's reading), the "last veil" language for 1d, and the "always followed by a sober return" addition (high). The table is the stated tool for premise 2. Step 2 uses "dark veils" and "outer veils of light" from section 1 only. It does not rely on anything later (the table's love and return cells point forward to Chapters Eleven and Twelve without citing them).
- Section 3: premise 1 (order) and 3 (law first) are well sourced. Premise 2 (none dropped) is sourced for polishing only. Premise 4 (four journeys) now sits in the closing recap. Premise 5 (Ibn Sina's order is the same) rests on a synthesis sentence with no source. The conclusion "four stages, in order, two ours and two God's, none dropped" follows only for the first and last claims, until those two are repaired.
- The hand-off question is the approved one. The Try this box comes after it and gives a concrete step, which is acceptable.
- Tone: no jokes, no rhetorical-question chains, no condescension. No banned words from LINGO found (grep for the banned list, intensifiers and wordy constructions is clean; no sentence opens with So/Well/Look/Now). Two throat-clearing sentences and one "Not X. Y." are in the low items.
