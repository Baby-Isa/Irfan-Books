# Source map: Mafatih al-Jinan (sources/extracts/mafatih.md)

Page numbers are PDF pages (`[p. N]` markers). In this PDF the page numbers in the table of contents match the PDF pages.

**Key limitation.** This PDF is titled "Arabic-English", but in the extract the supplication texts themselves are **Arabic only**. English exists only for the connecting prose: introductions, merits, rubrics and narrations. I checked Kumayl, Jawshan, Abu Hamza, Arafah, Ashura and the Munajat. The English runs under about 15 lines per page (rubrics) and contains no translated petitions. The Arabic text is also badly broken by extraction: letters are split and ordering is unreliable. So:

- No Mafatih supplication can be quoted in English from this file.
- Quotes below are from the English prose only.
- The book must obtain translations elsewhere. Mark them `[VERIFY]` and do not translate from memory.

## 2. Theme passages

Because only the prose is in English, these entries are **what the English of Mafatih says about practices and merits**. They are not what the supplications say. For several themes the closest material is a supplication that is available only in Arabic. Those are marked "Arabic only: location" with no content claim, so a human reader can supply the translation.

### Love and yearning
- **p. 240** (Shaʿban merits). Fasting in Shaʿban is urged "as a sign of your love for your Prophet and as a sign of seeking nearness to your Lord". Whoever fasts for the Prophet's sake and for nearness, "Almighty Allah will love him, draw him near". Closest English statement of love as the reason for practice.
- **p. 245** (the Munajat's closing note). It is ascribed to the Holy Imams, "comprises excellent meanings", and is "advisable to say... with presence of heart all the time". This is the Mafatih compiler's own testimony that the text is meant to be said with the heart present.
- **p. 244 / 194** (Arabic only). Munajat Shaʿbaniyyah, and the Whispered Prayer of the Lovers (Munajat al-Muhibbin, TOC p. 194).
- **p. 257.** On the 15th night of Shaʿban: "exert all efforts to seek nearness to Him at this night", because He will not reject any asker who does not ask for disobedience.

### Severance and the veils
The English prose offers almost nothing on this theme.
- **p. 244** (Arabic only). The Shaʿbaniyyah text has the stems for cutting off (inqitaʿ), the veils (hujub), light (nur), the source of grandeur, being suspended (muʿallaqa) by the glory of sanctity, and "those who dwell in" the guardianship (ahl wilayatik), as the L01 lines require. See section 3. This is the main source for the theme.
- **pp. 401, 403.** On Ashura the believer abandons worldly business, stores nothing at home and "devotes themselves to weeping". The rite is detachment from the ordinary, but it is a practice of mourning rather than mysticism, and should be so labelled.
- **p. 617.** The Ashura day is "doomed", so no need is to be settled on it. This is a rite of suspension of worldly aims.

### Repentance
- **p. 356** (Dhu al-Qaʿdah Sundays). The Prophet is reported to have promised that those who pray on these Sundays will have "their repentance... accepted, their sins forgiven". Dhu al-Qaʿdah is "the month of responding to the prayers in hardships" (Ibn Tawus).
- **p. 204** (Rajab). The Prophet is quoted: "Rajab is the month of imploring for forgiveness for my people". Repeat istighfar "much more than you do in other months".
- **p. 242** (Shaʿban general aamal). The best words in Shaʿban are istighfar. English on this page mentions a reward comparing it to "seventy thousand times a day in the other months". The sentence is broken in the extract, so verify it before use. `[VERIFY]`
- **p. 264** (Ramadan sermon). "Repent from your sins." Souls are "dependent upon your deeds", so free them "by means of imploring for forgiveness". Long prostration lightens the "burdens on your backs".
- **p. 839** (between sunset and bedtime). Engage "in uttering expressions of glorification of Almighty Allah and implorations for His forgiveness". Dusk is as honourable as the pre-sunrise hour.

### Self-knowledge
- No English passage in this extract addresses self-knowledge directly. The nearest cases are Jawshan Kabir (151-162) and the Fifteen Munajat of the ʿArifeen (p. 197), which are Arabic only.
- **p. 881** (story from al-Qutb al-Rawandi). A worshipper learns that his good deeds have no merit because he does them for repute. He is told to "purchase yourself from Him" with a daily act of remembrance. The story is about sincerity and the self, and should not be oversold as irfan.

### Remembrance
- **p. 802** (Tasbih of Fatima). Imam al-Baqir: no "adoration through which Almighty Allah is worshipped can be better than the doxology of Fatimah". "Misery will never find a way" to its reciter. The counts are 34, 33 and 33, and the 802-803 passage reconciles the two orders in the traditions. For the book: the Imam's own ranking of this dhikr.
- **p. 881.** The Prophet repeated a doxology 360 times a day, one for each vein. Also: praising God 100 times a day is "better than manumitting one hundred slaves".
- **p. 839.** Dusk is a time for glorification and istighfar.
- **p. 810.** Merits of the Four Tasbihat. Not read in detail; check before use. `[VERIFY]`

### Night vigil
- **p. 848** (Merits of the Night Prayer). The Infallibles called it "the honor of the true faithful believers". It "erases the sins committed in days, saves from the loneliness in graves". Eight rakʿas plus one at the last hours of the night is "the ornament of the Hereafter". Wealth and sons are the ornament of this world. One who prays at night and goes hungry is a liar, since "the night prayer guarantees the sustenance of the next day".
- **p. 848.** The Prophet repeats "Abide by the night prayer" three times in a hadith to Anas ibn Malik (the text is partly lost in the extract). `[VERIFY]`
- **p. 320** (Qadr nights). Worship on the Night of Qadr "is equal to the acts of one thousand nights". The angels descend "to visit the Imam of the Age".
- **p. 400** (Ashura night). Spending the whole night in worship is "equal to seventy-year worship".
- **p. 257.** The 15th night of Shaʿban is described as "the most favorable night after the Qadr Night".

### Nearness to God
- **p. 240.** See "Love and yearning".
- **p. 257.** See "Love and yearning".
- **p. 80** (Fridays). Do not claim rights wrongly but "seek nearness to Almighty Allah by means of righteous deeds and abandonment of all forbidden acts".
- **pp. 796, 836.** Rubrics speak of "seeking nearness to Allah the Exalted" in the Dawn Prayer and the noon rakʿas.
- **p. 485.** At the Ziyarah site: "the place you are in is the zone of granting requests". The scene is one of earnest supplication on the cheek-to-ground prostration.

### The Imams and wilaya
- **p. 386** (Ghadir). Eid al-Ghadir is "the Greatest feast day of Almighty Allah", marking the Prophet's appointment of Imam ʿAli as successor. The Imam al-Sadiq is asked what to do on it; the answer is fasting, worship and mention of the Household.
- **p. 320.** The angels at Qadr bring destinies "before him", meaning the Imam of the Age.
- **p. 257.** The 15th night of Shaʿban is the birth night of the Mahdi, and was "chosen for us, the Ahl al-Bayt".
- **p. 502.** Abu Hamza al-Thumali narrates meeting Imam Zayn al-Abidin at Kufa. The Imam is overheard making "the famous supplicatory prayer", and Abu Hamza is called "one of the chiefs and ascetic people of al-Kufah". This links the Imam, the Kufa mosque and the Abu Hamza tradition.
- **pp. 404-405.** The compiler says forged traditions about Ashura were invented. He quotes Maytham al-Tammar as saying Imam ʿAli foretold that the Umayyads would take the day as blessed. This shows how the Shia tradition polices what counts as authentic.

### The return to God
Very little in the English. The material is about death and the Resurrection, not the mystical return.
- **p. 204-205.** Fasting the last days of Rajab secures one "against the agonies of death, the horror of the resurrection, and the grave chastisement".
- **p. 264.** In Ramadan: "remember the hunger and thirst of the Resurrection Day".
- **p. 848.** Night prayer "saves from the loneliness in graves".
- **p. 149.** An English note on "deviation at the hour of death" in connection with Jawshan. Context incomplete in the extract. `[VERIFY]`

Pages: heading and intro p. 243; Arabic body p. 244; closing note p. 245.

The lecture notes (`notes/Irfan01.md`, section 2.7, ¶17-24) say:
- The Munajat was recited by all the Imams. The notes say it is "reported from Imam Ali (as) and Imam Ali Zayn al-Abidin (as)" (¶17).
- Eight quoted lines, given in the notes in transliteration.

**What Mafatih says.** The intro on p. 243 says the Munajat was "narrated by Ibn Khalawayh" who said it is "the confidential prayer of Imam Ali Ameer al-Momineen and the Holy Imams (a.s.) who used to say it in the month of Sha'ban". This agrees with "all the Imams" and with Imam ʿAli. **Imam Zayn al-Abidin is not named** in Mafatih, so ¶17 is a mismatch to flag. `[QUERY: the lecturer's attribution to Zayn al-Abidin is not in Mafatih's introduction; the lecturer may have another source.]`
