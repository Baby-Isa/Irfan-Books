# Source map: Kernel of the Kernels (Lubb al-Lubab)

**Source:** `sources/extracts/kernel.md`. Tabataba'i's 1949–50 Qum lectures on practical ʿirfan, written up by his pupil Sayyid Muhammad Husayn Husayni Tihrani (finished 24 June 1950, pp. 93–94) and translated by Ali Quli Qara'i. Page numbers are the extract's PDF pages. Exact quotes are in “curly marks”; ligatures and non-breaking hyphens in the extract are written plainly. Spellings follow the source, not yet the GLOSSARY. The pp. 4–10 preface and the anecdotes are a disciple's devotional testimony.

## 2. Chapter map

### Prologue
- p. 5: the framing balance. Quote: “combine within themselves the outward and the inward”. Both pure outwardness and pure inwardness fail.
- p. 5: the shariʿa-only are “satisfied with the crest, foregoing the kernel and the essence”. The only shell-and-kernel play in the text.
- p. 56: the wayfarer learns the rules “from a jurist (faqih)”. ʿIrfan does not replace fiqh.
- p. 28–29: no seeking miraculous powers. Useful for the section on what ʿirfan is not.
- p. 87–88: ʿirfan traced to ʿAli; over a hundred orders, about twenty-five principal, all ending in him. Material for the two-page history.

### Part One

**1. Seeking, not powers**
- p. 4: the religious urge is innate, “part of the innate instincts of the human being”, a pull from the Beloved.
- p. 12: the jadhba comes in gusts. Quote: “you will come across wafts of breeze from your Lord” (a hadith, given without source; [VERIFY]).
- p. 28–29: powers are barred. The wayfarer has “no right to seek occult experiences and miraculous qualities”; seeking them is worshipping desire (Q 25:43).
- p. 25–27: the mukhlasin, safe from Satan, rewarded beyond their works (Q 38:83, 37:40, 50:35).

**2. You already know these words**
- p. 5–6, fn. 2 on p. 10: a supplication attributed to ʿAli: “illumine my outward being with obedience to Thee and my inner being with Thy Love”. [VERIFY against the held duʿa texts.]
- p. 59–60: the Imams' laments were “neither a pretence nor for the purpose of others' training and guidance”. Supports reading duʿas as the Imams' own states.
- p. 72: an unattributed duʿa: “I found You worthy of worship and so I worshipped You”. [VERIFY.]

**3. Two kinds of knowing**
- p. 6: reasoning “does not satisfy the heart and the spirit”; philosophy is the noblest contemplative science but leaves the heart without food.
- p. 7–8: Mulla Sadra (Asfar introduction): “their syllogisms were barren”.
- p. 8: Tabataba'i after Isharat, Shifa and Asfar “settle down at the sacred threshold of the Qur'an”.
- p. 43: Iman a'zam passes “beyond knowledge and belief” to direct vision.
- p. 56–57: “knowledge gives birth to action and action in turn brings knowledge”; saying the sujud dhikr teaches majesty.
- p. 21–22: language fails for higher worlds: “does not go beyond hints”.

### Part Two

**4. Know yourself, know your Lord**
- p. 87, 89: Hamadani's method was gnosis of the soul, “which is essential to the gnosis of the Lord”.
- p. 17: first sighting of the soul's immateriality: “he might see himself standing before himself”. Qadi's mole anecdote follows.
- p. 41: “The (lower) soul is the biggest idol”; “Your staunchest enemy is your (lower) soul”.
- p. 28: all austerity hides self-love: “he has an extreme attachment to his own being”.

**5. The soul and its worlds**
- p. 5: body (tabʿ), imagination (barzakh), spirit: all three must travel.
- p. 13: barzakh thoughts as a settled pool: “all the impurities and dirt that it contains settle down at the bottom”.
- p. 17–18: lights belong to barzakh; Karbala'i's boundless “nur al-isfahbod” vision.
- p. 22: this world is “the darkest of the divine worlds”.
- p. 65–66: acts have living inner forms: a mosque “has a reality that is living, conscious, and perceptive”. The earth that wailed at a traveller's departure.
- p. 26, 40, 45: lesser, middle and greatest “resurrection of the souls” as inner stages.

**6. The ship, the sea and the pearl**
- p. 30: shariʿa to the last breath. The claim of release from duties is “a big lie and slander”; the dropper is a hypocrite.
- p. 30: the Prophet to ʿAʾisha: “Don't you want me to be God's grateful servant?” (Q 48:2).
- p. 57: “the neglect of obligatory duties and the commission of unlawful acts is contrary to Godward wayfaring”. Austerities on an unclean heart are gold on filth.
- p. 34–36: Islam, Iman, ihsan as stages. Islam is “surrender (taslim), and surrender means conviction (yaqin)”. Ihsan is made an enduring habit (malika).
- p. 5, 81: balance, and powers are no proof.
- The four journeys are not laid out as a scheme. The Asfar is cited only as Sadra's book (pp. 8, 10). The route is the twelve realms.

### Part Three

**7. Roots and the first seekers**
- p. 87–88: ʿirfan from ʿAli; some orders reach Imam al-Rida through Maʿruf al-Karkhi; two or three of the twenty-five are Shia.
- p. 53–54: Idris in a vision.
- p. 77–78: Salman and the house “to the extent of your body”.
- p. 73: Bistami. No entry for Uways, the Suffa, Hasan al-Basri, Rabiʿa or Dhu'l-Nun.

**8. Drunk and sober**
- p. 73: Bistami's “I want not to want anything!” and the four renunciations; wine imagery (pp. 16, 73).
- p. 19–20: sobriety: the wayfarer “finds unity while dwelling in plurality”; the strong one attends both worlds.
- p. 81: Ibn ʿArabi and two masters: “He invited you to the companion (rajiq), and I invited you to the Way (tariq).”
- p. 84: hulul and ittihad “are kufr and shirk”; true unity of being “totally negates plurality”. A Shia gnostic's line against incarnation.
- p. 21: Jili and Waliullah Dehlawi cited. Hakim Tirmidhi and Junayd: none.

**9. Sufis and Shia**
- p. 60, 64: bayʿa with an aware shaykh (the extract's typo: “shayhh”); loyalty to one's pledges to him.
- p. 83: but one “must not surrender oneself to everyone that exhibits his merchandise”; test the teacher against the Book, Sunna and Imams.
- p. 86–87: the Naqshbandi staring technique is described, then barred: “to concentrate on a stone or piece of wood” is not permitted.
- p. 88: “our tariqah is that of the marhum Akhund which does not end in any of these silsilahs”.
- p. 80–81: the Imam is the inner “special teacher” of every wayfarer.
- p. 67: grace reaches “Muslims, Jews, Christians, Zoroastrians” alike, though people receive it differently.

### Part Four

**10. Finding a guide** (details in 3 and 4 below)
- p. 80–83: the two kinds of teacher, the signs of a real one, the Imam as inner teacher.
- p. 60: the guide is “a wali of God” who “has passed beyond the station of fana' and reached the station of baqa' billah”.
- p. 81: not proofs of attainment: “extraordinary feats, knowledge of hidden things, of private thoughts of persons”.
- p. 83: “A thousand trap lies concealed in this wilderness”.
- p. 55: the engineer story. A sayyid reaches a lost man unasked, rebukes a gambling sin, then sends him to scholars and shrines.
- p. 88–89: the chain (4 below).

**11. The stages of the first journey**
- p. 33–45: twelve realms. Lesser to greatest Islam, Iman, hijra, jihad.
- p. 19: the hal becomes a maqam and then voluntary. p. 31: “the smallest lapse in wayfaring and spiritual jihad would bring him down again to the lower world”.
- p. 17–19: the sequence of lights, soul-vision, Attributes, Names, Essence.
- p. 90–92: four unities (acts, attributes, names, essence).
- No tajliya/takhliya/tahliya triad; no Ibn Sina ladder (the Isharat is named only as a text, p. 8); no Isaac of Nineveh.

**12. Setting out**
- p. 53: the first duty is inquiry: “sufficient rational grounds for whatever beliefs he holds”; failing that, tears and pleading.
- p. 55: the engineer is told “Take a bath of repentance”.
- p. 62–64: gentleness. Stop “when there is still interest left in him”. Imam al-Sadiq to Qaratisi: “lift him up gently to yourself”; “Do not force worship on yourself”.
- p. 64: wafa: not returning to a sin after tawba. p. 68: muhasaba with istighfar.
- p. 37–38: hijra from disobedient company and from customs. No Kubra, Hammam or Dhu'l-Qaʿdah rite.

**13. The five disciplines**
- Silence, pp. 75–76: “Silence is the motto of the lovers”; “our Shi'ah are the mute ones”. General and special forms.
- Hunger, pp. 76–77: “Hunger is the condiment of the believer”. The Miʿraj hadith is cited (Irshad al-qulub; Bihar 15). Qadi's guest story: God found no “better food for him than hunger”.
- Night, p. 78: sahar “to the extent permitted by the wayfarer's constitution”. Long surahs in the night prayer; Surat Sad in the Friday witr (pp. 18–19).
- Khalwa, pp. 77–78 (see 3 below).
- Dhikr, pp. 57–58, 83–85: “Dhikr means the heart's remembrance of the Names and Attributes of the Supreme Creator.” Awrad are “like medicines” and dosed by the teacher.
- Muraqaba, pp. 15–19, 67–68, 86–87: “the wayfarer should not be devoid of muraqabah”. Four degrees (pp. 86–87), from avoiding the haram to seeing God present with a glimpse of beauty. Without it, dhikr is fruitless (p. 68).
- Thought control, pp. 84–89: “the greatest of the purging agents of the inner spirit”; Hamadani's way is the soul, not a stone.
- Secrecy, pp. 78–80: Imam al-Sadiq tells Abu Basir not to repeat a saying because “the people cannot understand its meaning”.

**14. Love, the engine**
- p. 16: muraqaba unveils innate love: “the love of perfect beauty and perfection is innate in man”.
- p. 70–72: three worships. Fear is the slaves', greed the merchants', love “the worship of freemen”.
- p. 72–73: love too is a means to be left: “love is something other than the beloved”.
- p. 59: the bereaved image: “An ocean of longing surges in his heart”.
- p. 69: affection for creatures; a Layla poem. p. 76: silence as the lovers' motto.
- p. 92–93: the “valley of love” and the Simurgh.

**15. Losing the self, finding God**
- p. 14–15: Q 28:88, 55:26–27, 3:169 read as fana and baqa; the Imams' “We are the Names of God” as fana in the Essence.
- p. 19: baqa bi'l-maʿbud follows total fana; the wayfarer lives among people unseen.
- p. 28: “as long as the wayfarer does not transcend himself, he will not reach God”.
- p. 74–75: Qadi's “incineration”: helplessness to remove desire burns its roots.
- p. 84: against hulul. p. 83: Satan despairs once the Essence is revealed.
- p. 90–91: fana in act, attribute, name, essence; “it is not he who acts but God”. Four degrees, not three.
- Rumi, nirvana, shatahat: none.

### Part Five

**16. The four journeys and the perfect human**
- p. 20: wilaya, fana in the Essence, “is not at all exclusive”. Prophethood and Imamate are the exclusive offices.
- p. 20: the Prophet summoned his ummah to his own station (Q 33:21).
- p. 45–48: earlier prophets reached only Names and Attributes and took recourse in the wilaya of the Prophet, ʿAli, Fatima and the Imams (the “greatest spiritual authority”, p. 46).
- p. 59–60: after fana the Imams hold unity and plurality together.
- p. 45: “Whoever wishes to see a dead man walking about should look at Ali Ibn Abi Talib.”
- p. 89: tawassul to Imam al-Husayn lifts veils. p. 55: the engineer weeps at the Iraq border. Karbala itself is not discussed.
- p. 60: the “greatest journey” is from malakut to jabarut and lahut.

**17. Reading the Quran with the heart, and the return to God**
- p. 15: a worked reading: wajh as the Names (55:26–27 with 28:88).
- p. 7: Sadra submits without seeking rational justification (59:7).
- p. 65–66: the shariʿa's acts have inner life; the inner does not cancel the outer.
- p. 18, 89: the reciter finds God is the reciter.
- p. 34: rida as “renunciation of all complaint and objection”. p. 62: tawakkul (3:160, 14:12).
- p. 44–45, 74–75: Q 89:27–30 and 2:156 as the soul's return.
- p. 67: ill “derive solely from ourselves”. The Light Verse, Yasin, Tusi and Qadi al-Nuʿman: none.

### Epilogue
- p. 63: “Do not force worship on yourself”; p. 83: the test against charlatans; p. 92–93: closing petition for “all the lovers”.

## 3. Agreements and tensions with the lecturer

**Seclusion (L10 ¶44–45), the specific check.** The lecturer says Tabataba'i held that forty days of seclusion and extreme asceticism can boost the soul to extraordinary feats, but “it is possible, but it's not desirable”. **The Kernel does not contain this statement.** It supports its spirit and diverges on a detail:
- Agreement: powers are not an aim (pp. 28–29); feats prove nothing (p. 81); overzealous worship breaks the soul (pp. 63–64); moderation (p. 61).
- Divergence: the Kernel prescribes special seclusion, “distancing oneself from all people”, “considered essential by the masters (mashayikh) of the Way” (p. 77), in a bare cell for one. It approves the forty-day hadith (pp. 7, 23). L10 ¶41–42 says Shia mysticism does not do seclusion. The Kernel is warmer to khalwa than the lecturer.
- **[QUERY: the lecturer's Tabataba'i quote on forty days (also L06 ¶66–69, riyadha) is not in the Kernel. Ask for the source.]**

**Agreements**
- Shariʿa first (L06): pp. 30, 56–57.
- Gradual, light dosing (L07 ¶16–17; L08): pp. 62–64, 66–67.
- Dhikr is prescribed, not improvised (L10 ¶58–59): p. 83.
- The five practices (L08 ¶55): all in the Kernel's list (pp. 75–78, 84–85).
- Fana is graded (L09 ¶52–64): pp. 90–92.
- Worship from love, not fear or greed (L12): pp. 70–72.

**Tensions**
1. **Teacher timing.** L07 ¶14: no teacher is needed at the start; follow the shariʿa. Kernel: the general teacher is needed “only at the beginning” (p. 80), the Imam always (p. 81), and bayʿa is expected (p. 60). A nuance for Ch 10, not an error.
2. **Obedience.** The Kernel's loyalty clause (p. 64) sits against "no blind obedience" (L14 and the outline). Its own safeguard (p. 83) narrows the gap.
3. **Fana counted in three or four.** L09: action, attributes, essence. Kernel adds names. [QUERY: does the lecturer fold names into attributes?]
4. **Danger after fana.** L09 ¶20–23: even the near can fall; mukhlasin are safe from Satan but not from the self. Kernel: Satan has no power over the mukhlasin (p. 25) and despairs at the Essence (p. 83), yet the “greatest kufr” strikes before that (pp. 32, 41).
5. **Wilaya.** L09 ¶87–89: the fourth journey is for the Prophet and Imams alone. Kernel: wilaya, fana in the Essence, “is not at all exclusive” (p. 20). Matters for Ch 16.
6. **Which Hamadani.** The Kernel's is Akhund Mulla Husayn-Quli (Dargazini) Hamadani (pp. 8, 89). L06 ¶23 names Mulla ʿAbd al-Samad Hamadani (d. 1787), the shell/core/kernel source, probably a different man. **[QUERY: confirm which Hamadani is meant.]**
7. **Chain.** The outline starts Ch 10 at Mulla Sadra; the Kernel's line begins with an unnamed weaver (pp. 88–89).

## 4. The chain of teachers (Ch 10)

1. **An unnamed weaver.** Appears to Shushtari three nights (“The way is not what you are pursuing”), sends him to Najaf, meets him at Wadi al-Salam (p. 88). Who he was and where he got the teaching: “nothing is known” (p. 89).
2. **Sayyid ʿAli Shushtari.** A judge and marjaʿ-type authority. In Najaf he attended Shaykh Murtada Ansari's fiqh lectures; Ansari attended his akhlaq lessons. He took over Ansari's chair and died six months later (pp. 88–89). He redirected Hamadani.
3. **Akhund Mulla Husayn-Quli (Dargazini) Hamadani.** A pupil of Ansari. “at the head of the previous century” (p. 8); “the master of gnosis and tawhid” (p. 17). Method: muraqaba and concentration on the soul (pp. 87, 89).
4. **His pupils** (p. 89): Hajj Mirza Aqa Maliki Tabrizi (fourteen years with Hamadani, p. 17; fear predominant, p. 70), Aqa Sayyid Ahmad Karbala'i Tehrani, Aqa Sayyid Muhammad Saʿid Hubbubi, Hajj Shaykh Muhammad Bahari (affection predominant, p. 70).
5. **Sayyid Ahmad Karbala'i Tehrani.** His “commanding light” vision (p. 18). Qadi belonged to his circle (p. 89).
6. **Hajj Mirza ʿAli Aqa Qadi Tabrizi.** “the pride of the legists and the pearl of the gnostics” (p. 8). Taught Tihrani in Najaf (p. 53). Stories: the mole (p. 17), incineration (pp. 74–75), hunger (pp. 76–77). Method: half an hour or more a day on the soul (p. 89). His father **Sayyid Husayn Aqa Qadi** was taught by **Hajj Imam-Quli Nakhjawani**, who trained under **Sayyid Quraysh Qazwini** (p. 43).
7. **ʿAllama Tabataba'i.** Traversed “the path, both in philosophy as well as in gnosis, under the tutelage of marhum Qadi” (p. 8). Gave the lectures in Qum, 1949–50 (pp. 9, 93–94).
8. **Tihrani**, the author. Studied under Tabataba'i and Qadi. His own teacher Sayyid Hashim Haddad is quoted in a translator's note (pp. 32–33, citing *Rah al-mujarrad*). A friend of **Murtada Mutahhari** for over thirty-five years; Mutahhari's late turn inward is told on pp. 8–9. A “brother” Hajj Sayyid Muhammad Hasan Tabataba'i appears at p. 53 (literal or spiritual? [VERIFY]).

**Others:** Sayyid Mahdi Bahr al-ʿUlum (twelve-realms treatise, pp. 33, 85; twenty-five years against riyaʾ, pp. 29–30); Baba Faraj Majdhub of Tabriz (p. 19).

**Not in the Kernel:** Mulla Sadra as a link in the line; Bahjat; Khomeini; Hasanzadeh Amoli; Kubra. The outline's wider chain needs another source.
