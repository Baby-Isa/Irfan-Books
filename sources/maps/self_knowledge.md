# Source map: Shomali, Self-Knowledge (2nd ed., 2006)

Extract: `sources/extracts/self_knowledge.md`. Pages are the PDF `[p. N]` markers. Quotes are exact (checked by script), including the source's own spelling slips. Arabic lines in the extract are ignored and none is reproduced. SOURCES.md rates the book Tier B. It is a clear English textbook from Qom, written for a mixed readership. It is not a work of ʿirfan. Its Quranic English is Shomali's own, leaning on Shakir, so verses should be cited by reference and not quoted from this book.

## 1. Structure

**Which chapters are richest.** The brief guessed chapters 4 and 5. In fact chapter 4 is anthropology (humans and angels) and chapter 5 is a list of verses, though 5.C, 5.E, 5.I, 5.L and 5.M are useful. For ʿirfan, the richest are chapters 1, 3, 12 and 13, then 7 and 14. The book has no hunger, silence or dhikr discipline, no Ibn ʿArabi, no Sufi history, no ruh layers and no kursi, ʿarsh or jabarut.

## 3. Tensions with the lecturer (notes 04 and 05)

1. **Who said "whoever knows himself".** Shomali gives only "a tradition" (p9) and the footnote on p14 points to Ghurar al-Hikam. L04 ¶21 says Imam Ali. The Lantern (Ch 5, p9) gives it to the Prophet. [QUERY: the attribution differs.]
2. **The limits of the hadith.** Shomali reads it plainly (p9). L04 ¶29–33 allows an "impossibility" reading. Use Shomali only for the plain reading.
3. **How close the Prophet came.** Shomali p23: "only two stages beyond seeing the Lord of the universe in all His Majesty". L04 ¶37–38 says even the Prophet was "limited" at higher levels. L04 ¶40–44 says "meeting" God is seeing a reflection. [QUERY.]
4. **Powers and spiritualism.** Shomali p29: "It has been proved that some of these stories disclosed by the medium have been found to be authentic." L05 ¶9 says those who claim to take the soul out of the body "mostly lie", and Outline Chapter 1 sets power-seeking apart from ʿirfan. Do not cite pp29–30 as support. Use p12, p30 and p32 (footnote 3).
5. **Ruh and nafs.** Shomali p82 (footnote 1): "we understand that the soul (spirit) is equal to the self". L05 ¶31–47 separates the great ruh, with its layers, from the nafs as the whole. The two vocabularies clash, so fix the GLOSSARY entry before drafting Chapter 5.
6. **Three souls.** Shomali p41 calls them "types". L05 ¶47 says "we don't have different types of nufus", so the lecturer treats them as states of one nafs. Shomali names only the tranquil soul.
7. **Resurrection body.** Shomali p76: "we believe in the physical-spiritual resurrection", with a body "like the body in its present situation". L05 ¶12 has a body "different from this misaali body", and L05 ¶6 has "deeper and deeper". Shomali also admits the topic is "very controversial". The kursi, ʿarsh and jabarut ladder (L04 ¶84–87) has no support in this book.
8. **Worlds as places.** Shomali p72 says death is "a gate to another universe". L05 ¶4 says the worlds are "another dimension of the same world".
9. **Eternal soul.** L04 ¶29 says "eternal". Shomali p72 says the spiritual universe is "also endless" and p34 calls the spirit "another creation". This probably settles the L04 [QUERY] as "everlasting".
10. **Theoretical ʿirfan.** L04 ¶3 describes it as the mystics' theory of existence and the soul, "more like philosophy". Shomali p31 gives God as its subject. Outline Chapter 3 follows Shomali.

**Agreements worth using.** The spirit of God is symbolic (p38, with L04 ¶23 and L05 ¶32). Deformed forms appear in Q 78:18 (p21, with L05 ¶11). Heaven and hell exist now (p76, with L04 ¶62). The faithful see natures in this life (p22). Quote: "enabling them to understand people's true natures and characters in this life". Fiqh and akhlaq are two levels (p17, with L07 ¶17).
