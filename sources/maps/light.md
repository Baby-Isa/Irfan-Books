# Source map: *Light Within Me* (Mutahhari, Tabatabaʾi, Khomeini)

Extract: `sources/extracts/light.md` (al-islam.org edition of the 1991 Karachi book, trans. M. A. Ansari; Part 1 has a different translator). Pages are PDF pages. **Mu** = Mutahhari, **Tb** = Tabatabaʾi, **Kh** = Khomeini. Held, full text. Quotes are exact; a few cross a line break at a hyphen.

**Cautions.**
- The cover blurb (p. 3) describes ethics, theology and spiritual insight. It does not match the contents.
- Part 2 is credited to Tb, but the narrator calls Tb "my brother" (p. 83). [VERIFY: authorship of Parts 2.1 to 2.3 before citing as Tabatabaʾi.]
- Mu's text is a chapter of his *Ashnai ba ulum-e Islami* (p. 8) and reads like the lecturer's own genre, a lecture series.

---

## 1. Structure

- **Preface** (Tb, pp. 4–8): gnosis in all religions and in Islam; Sufis versus Shiʿa; notes p. 8.
- **1. An Introduction to ʿIrfan** (Mu, pp. 8–58): viewpoints, practical and theoretical ʿirfan (8–13); three views of the ʿurafaʾ, shariʿa/tariqa/haqiqa (13–15); origins (15–21); brief history, about 70 figures to the 9th century AH (22–41); maqamat from Ibn Sina's *Isharat* IX and the four journeys (41–50); 20 terms and notes (50–58).
- **2.1 Stages of Spiritual Journey** (Tb, pp. 59–72); **2.2 The Twelve Worlds** (Tb, 72–87); **2.3 Rules of Attaining Spiritual Perfection** (Tb, 87–109): 25 rules, preceptor, the Hamadani–Qazi chain, four unities.
- **3.1 Interpretation of Surah al-Hamd** (Kh, 109–128); **3.2 Relationship between Allah and His Creation** (Kh, 128–141); **3.3 Allah and His Glory** (Kh, 141–162).
- **Appendix: Invocation of Shabaniyah** (translator unnamed, 163–166): English only; Arabic block p. 166; links p. 167.

The 25 rules (2.3): 1 customs, 2 determination, 3 moderation, 4 steadiness, 5 continuance, 6 meditation, 7 checking, 8 censuring, 9 hastening, 10 faith and reliance, 11 veneration, 12 intention, 13 silence, 14 hunger, 15 solitude, 16 vigil, 17 cleanliness, 18 humility, 19 plain food, 20 secrecy, 21 preceptor, 22 liturgies, 23–25 remembrance and evil thoughts. They run pp. 88–105.

## 2. Chapter map

### Prologue
- **p. 9, Mu.** The best ʿirfan-versus-tasawwuf definition held. Gnostics "are called 'urafa' and when referred to as a social group are generally called" Sufis. He keeps to the discipline.
- **p. 9, Mu.** "It is these who are the real gnostics; not those who have invented for themselves hundreds of special mannerisms".
- **pp. 13–14, Mu.** Three views of the ʿurafaʾ: a "ruse" (jurists), an anti-Arab movement (modernists), or sincere but fallible. "Of the three views, I prefer the third." (Also Ch 8.)
- **p. 4, Tb.** "Gnosis is a perfect way of worship, based on love, not on fear or hope."
- **p. 70, Tb.** On law: "It is a big lie and calumny to hold that the Islamic code of law may be disregarded by a wali". See L06 ¶12–22.
- **p. 159, Kh.** "Islam is not the name of the rules of law only."

### Ch 1: Seeking, not powers
- **p. 7, Tb.** The world as sign: "this world in relation to Allah is a nonentity". Close to the Surah Rum signs of L01.
- **p. 69, Tb.** "That is why he does not seek any miraculous power for himself."
- **p. 101, Tb.** Powers "are not a sign of anybody's being a favorite of Allah".
- **pp. 43–44, Mu (Ibn Sina).** The ʿarif's worship is not "out of desire (for rewards) or fear (of chastisement)"; cites ʿAli: "My God, I do not worship You in fear of Your Fire, nor in desire for Your Paradise".
- **pp. 66–68, Tb.** The special class, the sincere (Q 37:128): "Satan can no longer gain control over them."

### Ch 2: You already know these words
- **p. 20, Mu.** Kumayl, Abu Hamza, the Sahifa and the Shaʿbaniyyah "all contain the most sublime spiritual ideas".
- **p. 87 fn 2, Tb.** Supplications "provide the best means of our moral and spiritual training" (Mujir, Kumayl, Abu Hamza, ʿArafah).
- **p. 87, Tb.** "It is below the dignity of the Imams to say anything unrealistic": their weeping was real.
- **p. 138, Kh.** "Those who recited the Munajat Sha'biniya, wielded the sword also." All Imams recited it, as L01 ¶17 says.
- **pp. 156–157, Kh.** Line-by-line comment on the Munajat (§3).
- **pp. 139–141, 161, Kh.** Defends the duʿa books (Q 25:77, 40:60): "Just as the Holy Prophet and the Holy Qur'an are not separate from each other".

### Ch 3: Two kinds of knowing
- **p. 12, Mu.** Philosophy reasons; ʿirfan argues "like studying something that has been translated from some other language".
- **pp. 41–42, Mu.** Compact ʿilm/maʿrifa: "The hakim seeks certain knowledge ('ilm al-yaqin), while the 'arif seeks the certainty of direct vision ('ayn al-yaqin)." Also "perfection in reaching rather than in understanding" (cf. L02 ¶37).
- **p. 57, Mu.** Tasting: "mere conceptual knowledge of anything has no attraction" (Ibn Sina's example).
- **p. 145, Kh.** Ibn Sina box: "Wherever I went, this blind man also arrived there with his stick."
- **p. 146, Kh.** "Intellect discovers the truth and teaches it to the heart."
- **p. 29, Mu.** Abu Saʿid box: tasawwuf is to "give up whatever is on your mind, give away whatever is in your hand".

### Ch 4: Know yourself, know your Lord
- **p. 7, Tb.** Q 5:105 read as self-knowledge: "He must see Allah through the window of his own soul". The hadith "He who has known himself, has known Allah" (fn 11: cited by Sunni and Shiʿa gnostics).
- **p. 105, Tb.** Hamadani's method rests on the claim that "knowledge of self invariably leads to knowledge of Allah". Half an hour a day (p. 107).
- **p. 123, Kh.** "Your worst enemy is your lower self that is within you."
- **p. 58, Mu.** Soul by state: "When the human soul is dominated and ruled by desires and passions they call it nafs." Then qalb, ruh, sirr, khafi, akhfa (box for Ch 5).

### Ch 5: The soul and its worlds
- **pp. 59–62, Tb.** Barzakh as the traveller's own desires; lights; self-stripping. Qazi "suddenly saw himself standing quietly beside himself" (p. 62).
- **p. 77, Tb.** "moving from the angelic world ('Alam Malakut) enters the souled world ('Alam Jabarut)".
- **p. 90, Tb.** Every deed "has a corresponding truth transcending this material and physical world" (the image body; acts of worship take form, p. 91).
- **p. 30, Mu (Abu Saʿid).** "You shall rise in the form of your qualities."

### Ch 6: The ship, the sea and the pearl
- **pp. 14–15, Mu.** The core: "the Shari'ah is the means to, or the shell of the Tariqah". Also "One is outward shell, another is inward kernel, and the third is the kernel of the kernel."
- **p. 70, Tb.** Never leave it: "must abide by the Islamic injunctions from the very beginning of his embarking on the path of gnosis to the end of it".
- **p. 58, Mu.** An inner voice is satanic "if its command or prohibition is contrary to the dicta of the Shari'ah".
- **p. 85, Tb.** "It is no use to perform commendable deeds and spiritual exercises if the heart and soul are polluted".
- **p. 41, Mu.** Post-10th-century ʿurafaʾ show "a perfect loyalty to the shari'ah and a harmony between the rites of the path of progression and the rites of jurisprudence".
- **pp. 71–73, Tb.** Islam, faith, ihsan in degrees: "That you worship Allah as though you see him."
- **p. 135, Kh.** Attention in prayer: "How is it that the study of a book often absorbs man's entire attention, but prayer does not?"
- **p. 102, Tb.** Warning against pretenders: "it is not safe for a devotee to submit himself to any Tom, Dick or Harry."
- **pp. 49–50, Mu.** The four journeys in outline (closing sketch).

### Ch 7: Roots and the first seekers
- **pp. 4–5, Tb.** ʿAli is the source. The chain runs through "Salman Farsi, Uways Qarani, Kumayl bin Ziyad, Rashid Hujari, Mitham Tammar, Rabi’ bin Khaytham and Hasan Basri"; later ascetics "did not talk openly of gnosis".
- **p. 16, Mu.** Outside-source claims (Christian monks, Persian reaction, Neoplatonism, Buddhism), parallel to L11. His verdict: "'irfan has derived its basic sources of inspiration from Islam itself and from nowhere else".
- **p. 19, Mu.** The Harithah hadith of L11 ¶42–43: "This is a man whose heart has been illuminated with the light of faith by God".
- **p. 20, Mu.** Jurdaq's pitcher: critics seek a pool for "someone standing at the side of a sea or river with a pitcher of water in his hands". A story for the chapter.
- **p. 21, Mu.** Nicholson and Massignon concede: "the Qur'an contains the raw materials of both". No Schimmel here.
- **pp. 23–27, Mu.** Short portraits with dates: Hasan al-Basri (23), Ibrahim Adham, "resembles that of Buddha" (24), Shaqiq and Imam Musa (24), Maʿruf and Imam al-Rida (25), Fudayl (25), Sari and the shop fire (26), Muhasibi (23, 26), Dhu'l-Nun (27).
- **p. 84, Tb.** Tawakkul in practice: one sure of "Allah's absolute providence" will not "desperately try to earn money at all costs".
- **Gaps.** No Ahl al-Suffa; Uways only a name.

### Ch 8: Drunk and sober
- **p. 25, Mu.** Bayazid: "I came forth from Bayazid-ness as a snake from its skin." Mu rejects the water-carrier tale.
- **p. 57, Mu.** Dhawq, shurb, sukr, riyy; mahw, mahq; "This state, loftier even than mahw and mahq, is called sahw."
- **pp. 26–27, Mu.** Junayd is "Sayyid al-Ta'ifah, just as the Shi'ah jurisprudents call al-Shaykh al-Tusi Shaykh al-Ta'ifah" (as L13 ¶28). "Importance lies only in the (inward) glow."
- **p. 27, Mu.** Hallaj: the statements of Hallaj and Bayazid "were made when they were beside themselves". Hafiz: "His crime was that he used to reveal secrets."
- **p. 93, Tb.** Temperaments: "he who is dominated by cheerfulness is called a "drunkard"".
- **pp. 34–35, Mu.** Ibn ʿArabi "is certainly the greatest mystic of Islam"; "wildly divergent views about him"; "Mumit al-Din (the Killer of the Faith) or Mahi al-Din (the Effacer of the Faith)" (as L13 ¶53). Sadra rated him above Ibn Sina. Qunawi explained him (pp. 35–36).
- **p. 12, Mu.** Unity of being: "all things are names, qualities, and manifestations of God, not existents alongside Him". Critics' charge (p. 17): "existentialist monism".
- **p. 144, Kh.** "the sea is not the waves, although the waves are the sea" (cf. L13 ¶62). Also "All that exists is Allah" (p. 154), yet no particular thing is God (p. 155).
- **pp. 152–158, Kh.** The Shia defence: "Make peace with the gnostics." "Try to understand what they mean and why they do not use the diction commonly used by other people." Grapes story (p. 152).
- **pp. 50–52, Mu.** Malamatiyya: "an inverted form of ostentation (riya' ma'kus)"; "This issue is one of those over which the fuqaha' have found fault with the 'urafa'." (cf. L12 ¶5–8).
- **Gaps.** No Hakim Tirmidhi; no Shia-or-Sunni argument over Ibn ʿArabi.

### Ch 9: Sufis and Shia
- **pp. 5–6, Tb.** The Sufi system as invention ("invented by the mystics themselves"): "total obedience, liturgy, special robes, music and ecstasy"; some orders "joined hands with the Batinites".
- **p. 6, Tb.** "the Qutb or the perfect man conceived by the mystics does not actually exist anywhere outside the Shi’ite world". The Shia need no silsila.
- **p. 105, Tb.** About 100 orders, 25 principal, mostly Sunni; "Only two or three of them are Shi'ite"; "But we belong to none of these orders". Maʿruf's golden chain (pp. 25, 39).
- **p. 104, Tb.** Naqshbandi technique "derived from the method followed by the Naqshbandi", set aside.
- **pp. 40–41, Mu.** After the 10th century formal Sufism "lost itself in customs, outward aspects"; Sadra, Fayd Kashani and Qadi Saʿid stood outside the orders.

### Ch 10: Finding a guide
- **p. 10, Mu.** The master is needed: without one, the traveller "is in danger of going astray". "Leave not this stage without the company of Khidr,".
- **p. 87, Tb.** Rule 1 of the guide: appoint a righteous preceptor who has passed away from self.
- **pp. 100–102, Tb.** General versus special preceptor; test him over time; powers prove nothing; "compare the proposed preceptor's teachings with those of the Holy Prophet and the Imams". The charlatan check.
- **p. 106, Tb.** "My preceptor was the late Haji Mirza Ali Agha Qazi who was a pupil of Agha Sayyid Ahmad Karbalai." Chain: an unnamed weaver, Shushtari, Hamadani (pp. 105–106), then Malaki, Karbalaʾi, Habbubi, Bahari. Sadra is not in it.
- **pp. 83–84, Tb.** The Zanjan engineer: "anybody seeking guidance from Allah earnestly is bound to succeed in his objective".
- **Gaps.** No Bahjat, Hasanzadeh or Khomeini line.

### Ch 11: The stages of the first journey
- **pp. 41–50, Mu (Ibn Sina, *Isharat* IX).** The best held ladder: iradah (45–47), riyada (47–48), flashes "appearing and instantly vanishing" (48), the stable state, the mirror (49), union. Tusi counts nine stages (p. 50). Thunder and host's-house analogies are absent.
- **p. 45, Mu.** "The ends are the return to the beginnings." Rumi's reed is iradah: "Listen to the reed as its story it relates" (p. 47).
- **p. 47, Mu.** "The original meaning of this Arabic word is 'to exercise', or 'to break in a colt.'"
- **pp. 59–64, Tb.** Another route: barzakh, lights, stripping, attributes, names, fana, baqa. "the station of baqa (abiding in the everlasting Will of Allah)" (p. 60).
- **pp. 72–87, Tb.** Twelve worlds. Tajliyah, takhliyah and tahliyah do not occur in the extract.

### Ch 12: Setting out
- **p. 136, Kh.** Q 34:46, "that you rise for Allah"; gnostics take it as the first stage. "This is the starting point."
- **pp. 126–127, Kh.** Start young: "do not postpone the task of reform to old age." (Tension, §4.)
- **p. 89, Tb.** Rule 3: "Too much enthusiasm in the beginning leads to too little interest in the end."
- **pp. 83–84, Tb.** Any sinner may begin: "Have a bath for repentance, and don't do such a thing again".
- **p. 70, Tb.** Character: "It is not possible to acquire the position of the virtuous and the pure without acquiring their qualities."
- **p. 93, Tb.** Rule 10: "A lover of Allah shows kindness to all men and animals."

### Ch 13: The five disciplines
- **p. 97, Tb.** Silence: "Silence is the way of the lovers of Allah because Allah likes it."
- **pp. 97–98, Tb.** Hunger: "The believer enjoys hunger." Cites the Miʿraj reports (Daylami's *Irshad*, *Bihar* vol. 2). Qazi: "there was no food better than hunger".
- **pp. 63, 98, Tb.** Night: Q 51:18; prostration surahs and Surah Sad on Thursday night.
- **pp. 92, 104–105, Tb.** Muraqaba as "the foundation stone of spiritual journey", with four stages. Liturgies "are just like a medicine which may suit some and may not suit others" (p. 102); "Mere verbal repetition is of no use."
- **p. 92, Tb.** "He who does not take account of himself once every day is not one of us."
- **Notes.** Rule 20, secrecy (equivocation, p. 99), is an extra. The outline's Tabatabaʾi on extreme feats has only weak counterparts: rule 3 and the warning on p. 61.

### Ch 14: Love, the engine
- **p. 4, Tb.** The worshipper who acts without hoping for "any reward or" fearing any punishment. Imam al-Sadiq's slaves, wage-earners, freemen (p. 94; p. 8 fn 2).
- **p. 61, Tb.** "it is an inborn instinct of man to love the Absolute Beauty and Perfection".
- **p. 86, Tb.** The lover as bereaved: "The pain of separation keeps him restless".
- **p. 95, Tb.** The challenge: "he realizes that love is different from the beloved" and must leave even love behind.
- **pp. 44–45, Mu.** Mahmud and Ayaz: "If you look to your friend for his favors, You are tied to yourself not to your friend".
- **p. 93, Tb.** "Allah, I seek of You Your love and the love of him who loves You."
- **p. 126, Kh.** "Paradise and Hell are alike for him who has annihilated himself."
- **p. 165.** The Munajat: "place me among Your friends in the position of one who hopes for an increase in Your love".

### Ch 15: Losing the self, finding God
- **p. 57, Mu.** "Mahw and mahq mean fana' (annihilation)"; return as baqa and sahw: "it is possible for an 'arif to return from the state of fana' to the state of baqa'".
- **pp. 107–108, Tb.** Four unities (actions, attributes, names, being): "While passing through each of these worlds the spiritual traveler annihilates a part of his self".
- **p. 63, Tb.** Not nirvana: "the devotee sees unity in plurality". Baba Farajullah: "I have not seen it since I was born." (p. 64)
- **pp. 102–103, Tb.** Dangers: "to believe (God forbid) that Allah has dwelt in him"; "denial of being obligated to abide by religious injunctions and regarding everything lawful".
- **pp. 95–96, Tb.** Bayazid: "I want that I do not want." Qazi's "burning" "will also burn the roots of all desire".
- **p. 42, Mu; p. 136, Kh.** Drop in the sea: "He seeks to reach reality like a drop of water in the search of the sea." Kh adds "the simile does not represent the position fully".
- **p. 131, Kh.** Moses: "the mountain here might have meant the remnant of egoism still left in Moses".

### Ch 16: The four journeys and the perfect human
- **pp. 49–50, Mu.** The four journeys in full: "The fourth journey is amongst the people while still united with God."
- **p. 64, Tb.** The strong traveller "maintains well his relations to the world of unity and to the world of plurality at the same time". Wilaya "is not exclusively reserved for the Prophets and the Imams".
- **p. 61, Tb.** "We are the Names of Allah." Kh calls the Prophet the "exalted name" (p. 148).
- **pp. 78–80, Tb.** Earlier prophets reached names and attributes and invoked the guardianship of the Prophet and Imams.
- **p. 138, Kh.** For D16: the Prophets "used to set up justice and fair play"; "Imam Husayn also did the same thing. Just see his Supplication of the Day of Arafah and ponder over it."

### Ch 17: Reading the Quran with the heart; the return
- **p. 110, Kh.** "Islam does not allow anybody to interpret the Qur'an according to his personal opinion or private judgment." He warns materialists and those who "give some spiritual meaning"; his reading is "only a possible interpretation." (p. 109: gnostic commentaries "not the exegesis".)
- **pp. 130–133, Kh.** "revealed in 70 or 70,000 layers" (cf. L02 ¶16); "Only he to whom it is addressed, knows the Qur'an."
- **pp. 113–114, Kh.** Light Verse: "It has not said that the heavens and the earth are illuminated by the light of Allah."
- **p. 101, Tb.** "there is no knowing, living or powerful being except Allah" (with pp. 107–108: the surrender of action, attributes, names, being).
- **pp. 45–46, Mu.** Mabdaʾ and maʿad. **p. 77, Tb:** Q 89:27–30.
- **pp. 126, 164.** Paradise and hell: "If You haul me into the hell, I will tell its inmates that I love You."
- **Gaps.** No Yasin chains, Qadi al-Nuʿman or Tusi.

### Epilogue: First steps
- **p. 107, Tb.** A graded plan: "should concentrate his attention on self for half an hour or a little more". Add rule 7 (daily check), rule 3 (moderation).
- **pp. 101–102, Tb.** Guide-versus-charlatan list (Ch 10).
- **p. 166.** The final petition (§3).

## 3. Appendix: Munajat Shaʿbaniyyah

**What it holds.** Pages 163–166 give a continuous English translation headed "The Invocation Of Shabaniyah". There is no commentary, no numbering and no facing Arabic. The Arabic block on p. 166 is garbled and seems to be a run of blessing-formulae mentioning Shaʿban, not the Munajat. Do not use it. Epigraph Arabic comes from Mafatih p. 244 (D13).

**Where the commentary is.** Kh quotes key lines in his own wording on pp. 156–157 and asks about each:
- "complete withdrawal" (why do the Imams pray for it?);
- "the eyes of our heart";
- "tear off the curtains of light" and the "source of majesty";
- "souls get suspended";
- those called who respond and are dumbfounded, linked to Moses falling senseless;
- the plea to "pray in the style of the Shabaniyah Supplication" (p. 157).

**Order of the translation.** (1) respond to my call, all gain and loss is in Your hand (p. 163); (2) confession, forgiveness, do not expose me on the Day (pp. 163–164); (3) apology and hope; if You haul me into hell I will say I love You (p. 164); (4) wasted youth "intoxicated with keeping myself away from You", awakened to Your love (p. 164); (5) a heart, tongue and vision near You (p. 165); (6) friends, increase of love, passionate remembrance; (7) severance, eyes of the heart, veils, suspended souls; (8) those called, "thunderstruck by Your majesty"; (9) hope against despair, closing petition and blessing (p. 166).

**Lines matching L01 (¶18–24).** L01 lines 1–5 are in items 7 above, line 6 in item 8 and lines 7–8 in item 6 (all p. 165). The wording differs from Kh's own gloss ("penetrate the veils of light" against "tear off the curtains of light"; "thunderstruck" against "dumbfounded"). [VERIFY: choose the English against Mafatih p. 244 and the al-islam.org edition.]

**Candidate epigraphs (D13).**
- **Prologue / Part One, The Call.** "My Lord, grant me complete severance of my relations with everything else and total submission to You." (p. 165). Or "I was not strong enough to move away from Your disobedience, except when You awakened me to Your love." (p. 164)
- **Part Two, The Terrain.** "Enlighten the eyes of our hearts with the light of their looking at You" (p. 165), continuing "penetrate the veils of light and reach the Source of Grandeur".
- **Part Three, The Travellers.** "My Lord, make me one of those whom You call and they respond" (p. 165).
- **Part Four, The Road.** "My Lord, inspire me with a passionate love of remembering You so that I may keep on remembering You" (p. 165). Or "provide me with a heart, the passion of which may bring it near You".
- **Part Five, The Return.** "You whisper to them secretly and they work for You openly." (p. 165). It suits the return to people, and Kh's remark that its reciters "wielded the sword also".
- **Epilogue.** "My Lord, let me be attached to the Light of Your Majestic Glory, so that I may know You alone" (p. 166).

## 4. Agreements and tensions with the lecturer

**Definitions.**
- *Agree.* Sufism and ʿurafaʾ: L02 ¶22–24 treats Sufism as another term for ʿurafaʾ. Mu is sharper: discipline versus social group (p. 9).
- *Agree.* The machine and hear/see/reflect (L01–L02), and acquired versus presential knowledge (L02 ¶29–37), match Mu's philosopher versus ʿarif (pp. 12–13, 41–42) and Kh (p. 146). L01 ¶6 (jurists' objection) matches Mu's view (a), p. 13.
- *Terminology.* The L02 transcript of ʿilm huduri is garbled. The Light never uses the term; it gives kashf (p. 12), dhawq (p. 57), ʿilm and ʿayn al-yaqin (p. 41).
- *Tension.* L02 ¶6 says mysticism has no end; Mu and Tb give a goal, tawhid and fana (p. 10). L01 ¶5–6 asks why strive beyond the wajib; Tb answers that deeds perfect faith (pp. 84–85).

**History.**
- *Agree.* The name Sufi (Abu Hashim al-Kufi, wool): L02 ¶22, Mu p. 22. Mu dates it to the first half of the 2nd century. L02 ¶22 has Jami using it in 255 AH [QUERY: Mu has Qushayri dating the name before 200 AH].
- *Agree.* Ibrahim Adham–Buddha (L11 ¶78, Mu p. 24); Fudayl, Shaqiq and Imam Kazim (L12, Mu pp. 24–25); Maʿruf (Mu p. 25); Sari, Junayd (L13 ¶27–28, Mu pp. 26–27); Hallaj (L13 ¶31, Mu p. 27); Ibn ʿArabi's titles (L13 ¶53, Mu p. 35); the Harithah hadith (L11 ¶42–43, Mu p. 19); Abu Dharr and Salman (L13 ¶30, Mu p. 23).
- *Tension.* Hasan al-Basri. L11 ¶64 sees him as ambivalent, a mystic of fear. Mu makes him the start of the history (p. 23); a footnote (p. 58) corrects Mu's attribution of *Riʿaya* to Muhasibi.
- *Tension.* Origins. L11 cites Schimmel; Mu cites Nicholson and Massignon. Neither uses the other's authorities.
- *Tension.* Ibn ʿArabi's affiliation. L13 ¶47–55 debates Shia or Sunni. Mu gives his chain through Abu Madyan to Gilani (p. 34); Tb implies most such leaders were Sunni (p. 5). Kh counts him among commentators who "exposed some aspects" (p. 109).
- *Tension.* Fana. L13 and the outline say it is not merging; Mu and Kh use the drop in the sea (pp. 42, 136).
- *Tension.* Wahdat al-wujud. L13 ¶55–67 has four views and Sadra's tashkik; the Light has only philosophers versus ʿurafaʾ (Mu p. 12), and Kh's sea and waves.
- *Tension.* Relapse and age. L08 ¶13–20 says repentance may recur and age makes it harder. Tb rule 4 says a sin repented "must not be committed again" (p. 89); Kh says the young reform more easily (p. 126).
- *Tension.* Chain and stages. The outline's chain has Sadra and Bahjat; Tb's starts at a weaver (pp. 105–106). The outline has three levels of fana; Tb has four (pp. 107–108).

**Names to reconcile with the glossary.** Malaki/Maliki, Karbalaʾi, Hamadani/Hamdani, Shushtari, Qazi/Qadi, Habbubi, Shabaniyah/Shaʿbaniyyah, riyada/riyazat.
