# Source map: Khomeini, *Forty Hadith: An Exposition*

**Source:** `sources/extracts/forty_hadith.md` (bib key `forty_hadith`, Tier B). Ruhullah Khomeini, written 1939; Ansariyan 2nd revised edition, trans. Ali Quli and Mahliqa Qara'i.

**Conventions:**
- `p.` is the PDF `[p. N]` marker, not the printed page. Cite as `[@forty_hadith, p. N]`. `H#` is the hadith number.
- Quotes are exact copies from the extract, ligatures included. Duʿa lines are Khomeini's own wording, so check them against `maps/mafatih.md`.
- **Read in whole or large part:** H1, 6, 10–14, 16–18, 20, 22, 28, 30, 31, 33, 34, 37, 38. The rest were reached by targeted search.
- **Not in the extract:** the hadith "whoever knows himself", the four journeys as such, silence, hunger and Muzzammil as disciplines, and the early Sufi figures (Hasan al-Basri, Rabiʿa and others).

## 2. Chapter map

### Prologue: An invitation
- p. 8, biography: Shahabadi taught him the *Fusus* and Ansari's *Manazil*; "his relationship with him was that of a murid with his murshid".
- p. 11, biography: he taught fiqh while linking it to the gnostic dimension. "connect ﬁqh with all the other dimensions of Islam" (supports L06 ¶12–22).
- p. 35, H1: the sharia comes first. "no path for the realization of divine existence can be undertaken unless one familiarizes himself with the apparent or overt form of Shari’ah".
- p. 345, H24: scholars of one science must not revile another. "why did you blindly accuse them of unbelief and insult them?".

### 1. Seeking, not powers
- p. 61, H2: powers sought for worldly ends void the path. "spiritual exercises are meant for acquiring powers for worldly ends, his efforts are rendered invalid".
- p. 298, H20: even seeking mystic knowledge fails if it serves the ego. "self-seeking and God-seeking cannot go together".
- p. 168, H10: even "practitioners of ʿirfan" can be blocked from God. "who have no other purpose except His vision and His vicinity, yet whose vision is obstructed in a different way".
- p. 176, H11: all humans love absolute perfection, so the "receiver" is built in but misaimed. "We are lovers of Absolute Perfection; we are enamored to Absolute Beauty and Majesty".
- p. 199, H12: ranks of taqwa, from ordinary to the elect, the sincere and the faniyun. "for ordinary men taqwa is with respect to the forbidden things; for the elect it is vis-a-vis sensual pleasures".

### 2. You already know these words
- p. 380, H27: the Munajat Shaʿbaniyyah in his wording. "illuminate the vision of our hearts with the light of Thy Vision". p. 399, H28 repeats it as proof of liqaʾ.
- p. 47, H1: Abu Hamza should be read slowly. "Try to read one third or one fourth of it every night with feeling, and meditate upon its words.".
- pp. 170, 247, H10 and H16: Kumayl. p. 400, H28: ʿAli's "how shall I bear Thy separation?" shows the awliyaʾ weep for God, not for houris.
- p. 401, H28: warns against explaining such duʿas away. "we would open the door of ta’wil and interpretation and, in a word, block the door to the knowledge (marifah) of God".

### 3. Two kinds of knowing
- p. 542, H37: "There is an evident difference between the terms ‘irfan and ‘ilm"; the ʿarif "an ‘arif billah (a gnostic of God) is someone who knows God through direct witnessing".
- p. 126, H6: "his heart does not accept the ﬁndings of his reason if they have not entered the heart itself". See also p. 320, H22.
- p. 403, H28: "a learning that does not bring faith with it is the greatest of veils", yet "The sciences are seeds of (spiritual) experience.". p. 460, H30: "one may acquire complete mastery in expounding the meaning and essence of the heart".
- p. 469, H31: "God, the Almighty and the Glorious, cannot be described". p. 470: "Description (tawsif) itself is a kind of measurement of the object described" (pairs with Nahj 1.1).

### 4. Know yourself, know your Lord
- p. 201, H12: Shahabadi told his pupils to recite Q 59:18–24 after prayer. "And be not ye as those who forgot God, and He caused them to forget their souls." (copied from this extract).
- pp. 32–33, H1: "man is a marvel possessing two lives and two worlds within one existence"; "human existence serves as the battleground of these two bands".
- pp. 553–556, H38: Adam as the complete manifestation of the Names. "It is a form that was originated and created.".
- pp. 396–398, H28: liqaʾ Allah. "if anyone loves meeting God (liqa’ Allah,) God too loves to meet him". p. 57, H2: "whosoever is desirous of meeting his Creator, he should perform virtuous deeds". p. 407: "man beholds that which had been in him and of which he was unaware".

### 5. The soul and its worlds
- pp. 41–42, H1: the inner form follows the dominant faculty. "there is another form and a different face of him, which is heavenly and invisible".
- p. 46, H1: hell is "is actually the hell of one’s actions". p. 137, H7: "no ﬁre is more painful than the ﬁre of Divine Wrath".
- pp. 463–465, H30: resurrection form follows the heart; "the shell is the image of the kernel".
- p. 32, H1: levels of the soul counted as seven, four, three or two. p. 36: musharatah, muraqabah. p. 440, H29: "Read and ascend a degree.".

### 6. The ship, the sea and the pearl
- p. 35, H1: "Without adhering to the apparent teachings of Islam it is not possible that the light of the Divine Knowledge could descend upon his heart".
- p. 375, H27: "concentration of the heart, which makes up the quintessence and kernel of ‘ibadah". p. 293, H20: legal validity is not acceptance.
- p. 358, H25: waswasa misses the prayer's soul; "salat, which is the spiritual ascension (mi’raj) of the faithful".
- p. 542, H37: ascent is "the reality of spiritual ascent is the curvilinear spiritual movement with which is completed the circle of existence" (hook for the route sketch).

### 7. Roots and the first seekers
- p. 319, H22: Abu Dharr on why we hate death. "he is like someone returning to his family after a (long) absence".
- pp. 203–209, H13: tawakkul in degrees, defined via Ansari. "My wish is not to wish at all".
- p. 393, H27: Najm al-Din Kubra. "The Fire is better than dishonor.". p. 296, H20: Ibn ʿArabi cited.
- p. 210, H14: fear and hope. "the light of fear and the light of hope".

### 8. Drunk and sober
- p. 402, H28: "We call Sadr al-Muta’allihin (Mullah Sadra), who is the foremost of the adherents of tawhid, a heretic (zindiq)".
- p. 296, H20: he cites Ibn ʿArabi on pure sincerity. "as long as there is a worshipper and the worshipped one, worship, sincerity, and din, there remain the taints of otherness and egoism".
- p. 515, H34: after obliteration comes "the state of awakening (sahw)" (Junayd's sobriety). p. 199: the majdhubun versus the steady.

### 9. Sufis and Shia
- pp. 102–103, H4: false murshids. "They have distracted the attention of the creatures of God from Him, attracting them towards themselves". p. 237, H16: "Those (pseudo mystic masters) who are in pursuit of disciples and claim to guide others".
- p. 465, H30: "towards some coquettish person (shukh) or guru (shaykh)". p. 464: "They don’t take any step by themselves and do not allow their intellect to meddle" (the Imam, not a silsila).
- p. 8: his own bond with Shahabadi was "his relationship with him was that of a murid with his murshid".

### 10. Finding a guide
- p. 403, H28: learn "from the adept (mashayikh)", but also "the remembrance of the Beloved and by preoccupying his heart and mind with that Sacred Essence".
- pp. 168, 513: Shahabadi's ten stages per step. p. 201: his practical rules.
- pp. 102–103, 237, 465: marks of a charlatan. Nothing on Qadi, Tabataʾi or Bahjat.

### 11. The stages of the first journey
- pp. 35–37, H1: contemplation, resolve (Ibn Sina's iradah cited, p. 35), musharatah, muraqabah, tadhakkur. "I will not violate the Law of God today.".
- pp. 374–375, H27: tafarrugh is emptying the heart. p. 265, H18: dhikr as polish.
- pp. 198–199, H12: taqwa ranks. p. 399, H28: annihilation in the Names, then the Essence.

### 12. Setting out
- pp. 249–250, H17: "return from (physical) nature (tabi’ah) to the soul’s spirituality"; "One who repents from sin is like one who has not sinned.".
- p. 250: success is "rarely attainable". p. 251: "Do you think that tawbah is a mere verbal exercise?". p. 118, H5: "it is better to take an action against moral vices when one’s youth".
- p. 202, H12: the early effort eases. "discomfort will change into comfort and hardship into leisure". p. 227, H15: "a man moving with a lamp in darkness".
- p. 504, H34: the Miʿraj hadith of those God loves. "I become the hearing with which he hears, the sight wherewith he sees".

### 13. The five disciplines
- pp. 194–196, H12: night prayer. "Observe the midnight prayer.".
- p. 260, H18: "I am the companion of one who remembers me.". p. 262: "the desirability of remembrance within the heart and secretly can be inferred". p. 265: "so that, God willing, the heart itself takes on the form of dhikr".
- p. 36, H1: "Join the community of the men of religion and in seclusion pray to God". p. 299, H20: "the power and elegance of the spirit of their action".

### 14. Love, the engine
- p. 176, H11: "We are lovers of Absolute Perfection; we are enamored to Absolute Beauty and Majesty". p. 177: "Look again into the book of your nature".
- p. 263, H18: "removes all the veils and affords the unobstructed vision of the Beloved". p. 293, H20: "But as to the ‘ibadah of free men (ahrar), performed for the love of God Almighty".
- p. 400, H28: "we don’t worship God for the fear of hell, nor for the desire of paradise". p. 393, H27: "the Master of the house will reveal Himself therein".
- p. 89, H3: "Ask your heart whether it is in search of God or whether it is in love with itself." (a caution against loud claims of love).

### 15. Losing the self, finding God
- p. 515, H34: "the ultimate of nearness through the nawaﬁl is total fana’"; "plurality is a veil for us that hides the Divine Face, while for them it is a mirror of epiphany".
- pp. 516–517: Baha'i warns that these words can mislead into hulul and ittihad.
- p. 400, H28: "This direct experience of extinction (fana’) is not contrary to the impossibility of fathoming and encompassing". p. 147, H8: "he annihilates his own will in the Will".
- p. 158, H9: "claiming mystic knowledge, ecstasies of love, states of annihilation in God and subsistence through Him" are claims anyone can make. p. 492, H33: "then do whatever you want"; p. 494 refutes the licence: "do not neglect works for reliance upon us".

### 16. The four journeys and the perfect human
- p. 464, H30: the Perfect Man is the Straight Path. "They don’t take any step by themselves and do not allow their intellect to meddle".
- pp. 515–516, H34: the Perfect Man is "on the same plane as the Absolute Will". p. 302, H21: "the unchanging essence (‘ayn al-thabit) of the Perfect Man".
- pp. 492–503, H33: wilayah is the condition for accepted works.
- p. 167, H10: the Imams "whose women and children were taken captive for the sake of your guidance and your deliverance" (implicitly Karbala). p. 13, biography: on ʿAshura 1963 he "drew parallels between the Umayyad caliph Yazid and the Shah".

### 17. Reading the Quran with the heart, and the return to God
- p. 39, H1: "Bihisht al-liqa, the heaven of beatiﬁc vision, and the Jahannam al-ﬁraq, the hell of separation from Him". p. 238, H16: "the Hereafterly form of this very servitude and subjugation to the domination of Passion and Anger". p. 246: "the Paradise of character and the criterion in the Paradise of character is the strength and perfection of will".
- p. 364, H26: "the way to a path is also part of the path". p. 365 criticises reading paradise and hell as perceptions.
- p. 370, H26: the Light Verse. "knowledge is same as light".
- pp. 207–208, H13: tawakkul, rida and tafwid. "the devotee should see no power and capacity in himself".

## 3. Tensions with the lecturer

1. **Hell.** L16 ¶42–48 says hell is opposed will, not revenge. Khomeini also locates hell in one's deeds (pp. 46, 238), but dwells on the "no ﬁre is more painful than the ﬁre of Divine Wrath" (p. 137). Use his tone only as a cited contrast.
2. **Paradise and hell as perceptions.** p. 365 criticises Sadra for a view close to Ghazali's. L16 draws on Tusi via Sadra. For the lecturer: how does this fit ¶15, ¶51?
3. **Age and tawba.** L08 ¶22: "difficult... but not impossible". Khomeini: sound tawba is "rarely attainable" and youth is the time (p. 250). Same facts, harsher emphasis.
4. **Powers and sharia.** L06 ¶20 is tentative that a karama-worker "probably" could neglect sharia. Khomeini (p. 35) rejects any claim that inner perfection cancels outward forms. Flag for the lecturer.
5. **Fana.** L09 ¶3–8 reads it as epistemological, "not nirvana". Khomeini (pp. 515–516) sounds more ontological ("God hears by him"), yet cites Baha'i against incarnation (pp. 516–517).
6. **Fear-driven worship.** L08 and L12 treat fear as a legitimate start. p. 293 says worship from fear of hell or desire for paradise is "the idol of one’s carnal desire", though God accepts it by leniency.
7. **Guides.** L14 ¶22–25: Shia seek a trustworthy guide, with no silsila. Khomeini was Shahabadi's murid (p. 8) but warns against shaykhs and guide-hunters (pp. 102–103, 237, 465), much more harshly.
8. **Adam's image.** L04 ¶8–9 reads it as shared qualities. H38 (p. 550) says the form was "It is a form that was originated and created."; pp. 553–556 go on to the Greatest Name. Keep that in a box.
9. **Wahdat al-wujud.** Khomeini is a partisan (pp. 345, 402). For Ch. 8's honest debate he is one voice only.
10. **Agreements.** p. 89 puts the Imams' prayer beyond any mystic's (L12 ¶37). p. 398 matches L03 on the limit of knowing God.
