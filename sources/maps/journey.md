# Source map: The Spiritual Journey of the Mystics (Maliki Tabrizi)

**Source:** `sources/extracts/journey.md` (SOURCES.md tier B). Page numbers are the extract's PDF pages, not printed pages. Exact quotes are in “curly marks”. Spellings follow the source, not yet the GLOSSARY. Hadith and duʿa come without full chains, so every one needs [VERIFY] before reuse as scripture.

## 1. Identification

**What it is.** The English translation (Sayyid Hussein Alamdar, Tehran, 27 Ramadan 1418, p. 6) of the Persian *Suluk-e ʿArifan*, itself a translation of the Arabic *al-Muraqabat fi ʿAmal al-Sunnah* by Mirza Javad Agha Maliki Tabrizi (p. 5). This is the *al-Muraqabat* the lecturer cites at L08 ¶29.

**Scope: Ramadan only, as translated.** The translator describes the "deeds and etiquette" of Ramadan (p. 5). The original is wider: footnote 18 (p. 41) says the Rajab etiquette "has not been translated into English as yet", and the Shaʿbaniyyah sermon appears as already given (p. 127). So the Arabic work evidently covers other months too. This extract does not hold them. The Dhu'l-Qaʿdah tawba rite the lecturer attributes to Maliki is **not** in it (searched), so Mafatih p. 356 stays the anchor for Ch 12.

**Author (pp. 8–11).** Born in Tabriz. Studied in Najaf. Spent 14 years with Mulla Husayn-Quli Hamadani (p. 8). Later taught fiqh and akhlaq in Qum. Died 11 Dhu'l-Hijjah 1343. Anecdotes: he thanked a student whose night prayer had brought him grace (p. 9); he was calm at his son's drowning (pp. 9–10); he wept at night prayer (p. 10); he died as he said the takbir (p. 10).

## 2. Chapter map

### Prologue
- p. 5: the book takes the reader past “the apparent rituals of not eating and drinking”. A frame for "ʿirfan is the inside of what you already do".
- p. 8: a jurist and a mystic together, pupil of Hamadani. Fits L06 ¶12–22 (no replacement for fiqh).
- pp. 5–6: the age's sickness is dry eyes; the translator tells the reader to go to spiritual physicians. A scene-opener.

### Part One

**1. Seeking, not powers**
- p. 19: fasting should aim at nearness, not Hell or Paradise. Quote: “this deed would make us closer to Allah”.
- pp. 24–25: five ranks of intention, from fear of people up to nearness alone.
- pp. 99–100: the gnostic asks God only for God. Saʿdi (fn 31): ask nothing of the beloved but the beloved.
- pp. 26–29: Maliki rejects worship with no aim at all, resting on ʿAli's prayer (p. 28).

**2. You already know these words** (the book's richest part for this chapter)
- pp. 42–43: the Imams' duʿas as the Quran's echo. Quote: “the Qur'an from Allah's most distinguished servants, ascending upward towards Him”. In sermons they spoke to people; in duʿa they spoke to God.
- p. 42: compose your own duʿa without copying, then compare it with theirs.
- p. 57: duʿa is humble asking; it needs a present heart, need, hope and firm faith.
- pp. 58–61: a refusal can be the real answer (the father and the harmful medicine, p. 60; Khidr, p. 59). Quote: “the grant of his prayer would be to not to grant his prayer”.
- pp. 64–66: hope in God alone; the hadith qudsi “Was there anyone who trusted Me and was betrayed?”; the servant who looks back from the road to Hell.
- p. 67: good expectation must not turn into slackness.
- pp. 69–70: leave oppression and haram first. Quote: “Allah does not grant the prayer of a sleeping and negligent heart”.
- p. 72: tears. Quote: “except those who shed tears out of fear for Allah's wrath”.
- p. 73: praise and apology first; ʿAli's formula “nearer to me than my jugular vein”.
- pp. 74–77: pause and insist, three times (p. 74); pray in private (p. 75); in company when the danger is common (p. 76, fn 23); with “a broken heart” (p. 77).
- pp. 78–79: salawat at both ends, so the middle is not left behind.
- pp. 82–86: pray in good times (p. 82); for others first (p. 83); the angel's thousandfold reply (p. 84).
- p. 87: Imam al-Sadiq's five hand positions. pp. 93–97: best times (Friday, last third of the night, dawn, after the wajib prayers, ʿArafah).
- p. 103: understand what you say. Quote: “should not treat Allah (the Glorious, the Exalted), as a plaything”. pp. 105–106 test a first-night duʿa line by line.
- p. 111: Abu Hamza, Iftitah and Sahar are named for Ramadan; Kumayl in fn 4 (p. 7); ʿArafah in fn 28 (p. 97, pointing to Chittick). The Munajat Shaʿbaniyyah is **not** mentioned.
- pp. 156–157: attentionless recital is like the magicians' empty words. Quote: “Their prayers ascend upwards”.

**3. Two kinds of knowing**
- p. 47: the Quran's reality lies behind the written words, a light from God's own illumination.
- pp. 49–50: pondering the water verse until it becomes shuhud. Quote: “I never saw a thing but saw Allah, before, after, and with it”.
- p. 55: the reciter sees the Speaker in the speech. Quote: “Allah has manifested himself to his servants through His words”.
- p. 17: hunger sharpens the heart; fullness works like drunkenness.

### Part Two

**4. Know yourself, know your Lord**
- p. 125: Quote: “Whoever has identified his self in reality has recognised his Creator”. The text links khud-shinasi and khuda-shinasi.
- p. 49: the human being as the smaller world, with creation miniatured within.

**5. The soul and its worlds**
- pp. 124–125: spirits, barzakh/mithal, bodies; everything first exists in "hidden divine treasures" (Q 15:21).
- pp. 113–115: deeds take form in the hidden world and meet their owners.
- pp. 165–166: the world as a House of Deception (the spoon in the tea).
- pp. 181–183: lifeless things live in other worlds; Rumi, *Mathnavi*.
- p. 52: looking in the mirror for a face blackened by sin.

**6. The ship, the sea and the pearl**
- pp. 23, 34: a fast can be valid in fiqh yet not accepted by God. Quote: “fasting does not mean only abstinence from eating and drinking” (p. 33).
- pp. 113–114: ablution and ghusl have unseen effects; obey even without understanding.
- pp. 110–111: do not drop worship for lack of mood.
- pp. 136–139: the preacher must not repeat hadith that make the faith look foolish.

### Part Three

**7. Roots and the first seekers**
- p. 20: Imam al-Sadiq's conditions of a true fast.
- p. 118: Baba Tahir, Saʿdi, Hafiz on seeing God in the world.
- pp. 182–183 (fn 39): Rumi, *Mathnavi* 3.1008.
- pp. 173–175: Imam al-Sajjad (see Ch 12).

**8. Drunk and sober**
- p. 54: the soul “intoxicated with the joy of attaining spiritual contemplation (shahud)”.
- pp. 26–29: the dispute over disinterested worship.
- p. 117: “I see Your manifestation in everything”: manifestation, not union.
- Not found: Ibn ʿArabi, Bayazid, Junayd.

**9. Sufis and Shia**
- Thin. Maliki is wholly Imami: tawassul (pp. 107–109, 150), a day for each Imam (fn 33, p. 112), the Imam as seer of Qadr (p. 124).
- p. 88: a Najaf "shaykh of the mystics" teaches one practice. A Shia shaykh, not a silsila.

### Part Four

**10. Finding a guide**
- p. 8: fourteen years with Hamadani. p. 9: the Shahrud dream and the meeting in Qum.
- pp. 137–138: the preacher should treat listeners as a careful physician treats patients.
- pp. 131–133: the sincerity test (see §3).
- p. 146: if you cannot judge your own mood, consult God by istikhara.

**11. The stages of the first journey**
- p. 16: the Miʿraj chain. Quote: “The result of silence is wisdom; the result of wisdom is enlightenment”. It runs on to certainty, contentment, then thanks, dhikr and love undivided.
- p. 27: God trains prophets by alternating manifestations of beauty and glory.
- pp. 178–179: the audit lists stations: fear, hope, patience, asceticism, remembrance, love, reliance, surrender, consent, tawhid.
- p. 88: prostration as annihilation.

**12. Setting out**
- p. 36: the Prophet's sermon. Quote: “by means of repentance make yourself free”.
- p. 101: tawba first, then purified wealth (khums) before the month begins.
- pp. 104–106: the first-night duʿa on God's veiling and patience.
- p. 61: God may allow a small sin to keep a servant from arrogance.
- pp. 153–154: the hopeful Qadr duʿa against Satan's despair.
- pp. 160–161: do not quit a small good deed. Quote: “lest he renounces a good deed because he is not able to perform it”.
- p. 163: even a drunkard's cry is answered.
- p. 170: ten rakʿat and “Astaghfirullah” a thousand times on the last night. Maliki admits the chain is weak (p. 172).
- pp. 173–175: Imam al-Sajjad's diary of servants' faults, read aloud and forgiven. Maliki adapts it for ordinary people (p. 175).
- pp. 178–179: the audit; Yunus's cry (21:87).
- pp. 187–188: the farewell apology; “perfect repentance with truth and sincerity”.
- Character: pp. 83–86 (Hussein b. Abi al-Ala and the shamed pilgrims); p. 36 (“Whoever makes people immune from his mischief”).
- Not here: relapse and age (L08 ¶13–22); the Dhu'l-Qaʿdah rite.

**13. The five disciplines**
- *Hunger.* pp. 15–22 are the longest treatment. Quote: “Keep your stomachs hungry and thirsty” (p. 15). Six benefits (pp. 17–18): purity of heart, humility, weaker passion, less sleep, time for worship, money for charity. p. 39: “Stop the path of Satanic influence upon your self through hunger.” Eaters by type: pp. 30–31; iftar intention: pp. 116–117.
- *Silence.* p. 16 (less eating and less talking); p. 33 (limbs fast too); p. 133 (speech is better than silence, yet some chose silence).
- *Night vigil.* pp. 93–94 (last third of the night); p. 120; pp. 143–154 (a full programme for Qadr); p. 17 (hunger reduces sleep and opens the night).
- *Seclusion.* p. 75 (a private prayer is worth seventy public ones); p. 159 (a set time of “private union”). No extended khalwa.
- *Remembrance.* p. 16 (dhikr “not mixed with forgetfulness”, with the translator's definition in fn 9); p. 148 (think first of the meaning of each act of prayer).
- *Measure.* pp. 109–111, 160–161 (choose acts by mood; do not quit; do not boast). Quote: “select the proper program which is suitable for him” (p. 109).
- Only the hunger, silence and love part of the Hadith of Miʿraj is quoted (pp. 15–17).

**14. Love, the engine**
- p. 16: Quote: “love not mixed with the love of others”.
- p. 27: prophets sometimes lose themselves in God's beauty and speak as lovers.
- p. 31: the overfed faster is a lover who “amuses himself with something else” before the beloved.
- pp. 105–106: the first-night duʿa on thirst for friendship, tested against the heart.
- pp. 183–184: farewell to Ramadan as to a friend. p. 118: Hafiz and Saʿdi on the world loved because it is “from Him”.

**15. Losing the self, finding God**
- p. 88: prostration as annihilation. p. 52 (fn 21): shuhud is witnessing without awareness of self.
- pp. 20–21: the fast of the favourites. p. 55: Quote: “he will forget himself, his Qur'anic recital”.
- Not found: baqa, shatahat.

### Part Five

**16. The four journeys and the perfect human**
- pp. 124–126: the Imam of the age sees the descent of destinies; Ibrahim's malakut (6:75).
- pp. 37, 98, 157: ʿAli's foretold death at prayer in Ramadan; the head of Husayn; his ziyarat on Qadr. Raw material only.

**17. Reading the Quran with the heart**
- pp. 46–48: pondering first. Quote: “regards them as though all of them have been revealed especially for him”. p. 47: “I am the speaking Qur'an”.
- pp. 49–52: a worked method: the water verse, the prophets' trials, the punishments, the lost nations.
- pp. 53–54: obstacles to understanding. Quote: “Limiting the meanings and aims of the Holy Qur'an to their apparent”. Denying tawil closes the path.

### Epilogue: First steps
Every practice in §3 serves here. Strongest sources: grading by intention (pp. 24–25); graded fasts (p. 23); attention in prayer (p. 148); the audit (pp. 178–179); the farewell apology (pp. 187–188); and the sincerity test (pp. 131–133) as a checklist for telling a sincere teacher from a showman. Quote: “why does he like and desire to be the leader” (p. 131).

9. Ponder one verse from many sides (pp. 49–52).
10. Name your intention before worship (pp. 24–25).
11. Do the small deed even without the mood (pp. 110–111, 160).
12. Add God's nearness to a worldly request (pp. 99–100).

**Intermediate (this month)**
13. Compose your own duʿa, then compare it with an Imam's (p. 42).
14. A daily prolonged prostration with Q 21:87 (pp. 88–89). The Najaf shaykh's circle said it a thousand times; start far shorter.
15. Rise in the last third of the night, even briefly (pp. 93–94).
16. An hour of private, free talk with God (p. 159).
17. Write down others' wrongs against you, forgive them, and ask God to do the same (pp. 173–175).
18. A month-end audit of faith, character and tongue (pp. 178–179).
19. A farewell apology for time wasted (pp. 187–188).
20. Think of the meaning before each act of prayer (p. 148).
21. Test your motives for leading or speaking (pp. 131–133).
22. Two rakʿat on a Qadr night with Ikhlas seventy times (p. 155) (W).

**Advanced (this year)**
23. The fast of the elite, giving up even lawful distractions (pp. 23, 116–117).
24. The fast of the heart (p. 34).
25. A whole-night vigil, with the Judgement-Day exercise (pp. 151–152). It uses dirt and a tied neck, which readers may not wish to copy.
26. One hundred rakʿat on Qadr, or Ramadan's thousand (pp. 109, 155).
27. Ten rakʿat and a thousand istighfar on the last night (p. 170) (W).
28. Admit no other thought, even a good one, into prayer (p. 148).

## 4. Agreements and tensions with the lecturer

**Agreements**
- *Hunger and wisdom.* L08 ¶65, 71, 75 use the same Miʿraj hadith as p. 16. The lecturer's chain (hunger, wisdom, maʿrifa, yaqin, indifference to ease) is the chain on p. 16.
- *Full stomach.* L08 ¶72–73: a heavy stomach clouds remembrance. Maliki p. 17 says the same, as drunkenness.
- *Hunger is not enough.* L08 ¶66–67: hunger does not always bring wisdom. Maliki p. 147 puts contemplation where there are no “pains of hunger”.
- *Silence.* L08 ¶55, 59: silence from what does not concern you, not for life. Maliki p. 133 keeps speech above silence.
- *Night vigil.* L10 ¶8–12, 19–21: it prepares the soul. Maliki pp. 93–94, 140 give the same call.
- *Privacy.* L10 ¶44–47: the nightly hour is the true seclusion. Maliki pp. 75, 159 agree.
- *Quran.* L10 ¶12, 16: learning joined to heart knowledge. Maliki pp. 49–54 asks for pondering and defends tawil.

**Tensions**
- *Dosage.* The lecturer starts small (L10 ¶23–27: half an hour, one practice for a year; L08 ¶26: the danger of rushing). Maliki's programme is heavy (pp. 109, 155). He softens it: choose by mood (p. 109) and do what you can (pp. 160–161). Use the lecturer's pace in "Try this" and Maliki as the ceiling.
- *Extreme hunger.* Tabatabaʾi's feats are "not desirable" in the lecturer's words (L10 ¶44–45), and ʿAli is "level 1000" (L08 ¶82–83). Maliki quotes the Prophet's “Keep your stomachs hungry and thirsty” (p. 15) and ends Ramadan “hungry and prostrating” (p. 189). His hunger is the Ramadan fast, not extra fasting.
- *Seclusion.* The lecturer's "seclusion of the heart" (L10 ¶42) has no direct basis in Maliki, and no conflict.
- *Remembrance.* The lecturer builds dhikr kathir on Q 33:41–42 and the tasbih of al-Zahra (L10 ¶54–55); Maliki's dhikr is the Ramadan duʿa and istighfar.
- *Weak hadith.* Maliki accepts weak chains for recommended deeds (p. 172) and calls one Qadr narration questionable (p. 122). Mark these (W).
- *Extreme gestures.* Maliki asks for dirt, a tied neck and weeping (pp. 100, 151). Offer them as description only.
- *The Dhu'l-Qaʿdah rite.* L08 ¶29–33 cannot be checked here. Log a [GAP]. Maliki's istighfar count here is the last-night 1,000 (p. 170), not the lecturer's 70.
- *Duʿa as proof.* Maliki says the duʿas prove the Imamate (p. 42). The lecturer uses them as a textbook of ʿirfan (L01 ¶17–31). Attribute the claim to Maliki.
