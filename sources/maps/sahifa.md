# Source map: Sahifa Sajjadiyya (Chittick translation)

Source: `sources/extracts/sahifa.md`. Pages are the PDF page from the `[p. N]` markers. A heading often sits a page or more before its English text, because the Arabic runs first. Where that matters, the English start is given. Titles are shortened.

## 1. Overview

The front matter (pp. 1-48) holds the introduction and the contents list (pp. 45-47). The introduction says the main body is 54 supplications, and that the addenda and the fifteen munajat are later additions that "suggest the hand of an editor". The body (pp. 49-615) is followed by the Fifteen Whispered Prayers (pp. 616-685), notes, and the Treatise on Rights (about pp. 690 onward; the right of the self is on p. 691).

## 2. Theme map

### Love of God
- **Munajat 9, Lovers, pp. 656-657.** The whole prayer. The lovers are those "purified through Thy affection and Thy love," and the speaker asks for "love for those who love Thee".
- **Munajat 8, Devotees, p. 651.** Love as the only aim: "Toward Thee is my yearning,".
- **Munajat 12, Knowers, pp. 669-670.** Hearts "seized by the ardour of Thy love", and the taste: "how pleasant the taste of Thy love,".
- **Munajat 3, Fearful, p. 627.** Love as the ground of hope: "Dost Thou seal hearts which harbour Thy love?"
- **Munajat 15, Abstainers, p. 683.** "plant in our hearts the trees of Thy love," and "dislodge the love of this world from our spirits,".
- **Supplication 38, p. 342.** Repentance that makes God's "love for me obligatory".

### Yearning and intimacy
- **Munajat 13, Rememberers, p. 674.** "love-mad hearts are enraptured by Thee,"; the plea "Make us intimate with silent remembrance,".
- **Munajat 8, p. 651.** "Be my intimate in my loneliness,".
- **Munajat 11, Utterly Poor, p. 664.** Only the vision satisfies: "damp my yearning but gazing upon Thy face,".
- **Munajat 9, p. 657.** "O Furthest Wish of the hearts of the yearners!"
- **Supplication 40, p. 353.** Even death is a meeting: "our familiar place toward which we yearn,".
- **Supplication 47, p. 493.** "make me yearn for the meeting with Thee,".

### Severance from all but God
- **Munajat 9, p. 656.** The summit: "and from whom Thou hast cut off all things" / "which cut him off from Thee!"
- **Supplication 28, Fleeing to God, p. 275.** The whole prayer; it opens "I showed sincerity by cutting myself off from everything but Thee."
- **Munajat 8, p. 651.** "my aspiration has been cut off from everything but Thee".
- **Supplication 31, p. 295.** The repentant's state: "His longing was devoid of every object of longing but Thee,".
- **Supplication 47, p. 490.** "Root out from my heart the love of this vile world,".
- **Supplication 54, p. 559.** "cut off my need for this world,".

### The veils
- **Supplication 55, p. 569.** God "veiled Himself from eyes through might,".
- **Supplication 50, p. 532.** "Thy splendour masked by the veils!"
- **Munajat 7, Obedient, p. 647.** "uncover from our hearts the wrappings of doubt and the veil,".
- **Munajat 4, Hopeful, p. 633.** "and lift from my insight the veils of blindness!"
- **Munajat 12, p. 669.** The result: "The covering has been lifted from their eyes,".
- The note on p. 77 (to Supplication 3) cites the hadith of seventy veils of light and darkness.

### Repentance
- **Supplication 31, pp. 283-300.** The main text; p. 295 is the portrait of the penitent; p. 298: "everything opposed to Thy will or far from Thy love".
- **Munajat 1, Repenters, pp. 617-618.** "My dreadful crimes have deadened my heart," then "so bring it to life by a repentance from Thee!"; p. 618: "does the runaway servant go back but to his Master?"
- **Supplication 9, p. 111.** "make us go to the repentance that Thou lovest".
- **Supplication 45, p. 423.** "place us with Thee among the repenters,".
- **Supplication 47, p. 493.** "repent with an unswerving repentance".
- Long confessions: Supplications 12 (p. 121) and 16 (p. 152).

### Self-knowledge and the nafs
- **Supplication 9, p. 112.** The self left alone errs: "for they choose falsehood". A note cites 12:53.
- **Supplication 22, p. 223.** "if Thou entrustest me to myself," and "I will be incapable before myself".
- **Supplication 16, p. 155.** "How marvellous the witness I bear against my own soul".
- **Supplication 20, pp. 197-198.** Against "self-admiration" and for "an inward abasement in myself to the same measure!"
- **Supplication 53, p. 551.** "What delusion with which I have deluded myself!"
- **Treatise on Rights, p. 691.** "The Right of your self": the nafs is to be employed in obeying God.

### Remembrance (dhikr)
- **Munajat 13, pp. 674-675.** The main text: "I remember Thee in my measure, not in Thy measure,"; it quotes "Remember Me, and I will remember you" and ends "Rememberer of the rememberers!"
- **Supplication 11, p. 121.** "divert our hearts through remembering Thee".
- **Munajat 4, p. 632.** God remembers first: "while Thou never ceasest remembering me?"
- **Munajat 9, p. 656.** "inspired with Thy remembrance,".
- **Supplication 21, p. 207.** "occupy it with remembering Thee,".

### Night prayer
- **Supplication 32, heading p. 300, English from p. 313.** A confession after the night prayer. p. 315: "nor can I seek sanctuary in night because of my vigil;".
- **Supplication 47, pp. 490, 492.** "Embellish for me solitude" in whispered prayer "by night and by day!"; also "solitude with vigil for Thee,".
- **Munajat 9, p. 656.** The lovers' "eyes wakeful in Thy service,".
- **Supplication 42, p. 372.** The Qur'an as "an intimate in the shadows of nights".
- The extract gives the spirit of night prayer, not its practice.

### Fear and hope
- **Munajat 3, Fearful, pp. 627-628.** Fear of being cast out by the One one loves.
- **Munajat 4, Hopeful, pp. 632-633.** "How should I have hope in other than Thee,".
- **Supplication 32, p. 315.** Both together: "hesitating between longing for Thee and fear of Thee."
- **Supplication 45, p. 423.** "fear of the threatened punishment" and "yearning for the promised reward".
- **Supplication 21, p. 207.** "animate it with fear of Thee and quaking before Thee,".

### Knowledge of God, and the limits of describing Him
- **Munajat 12, Knowers, p. 669.** The key text: "no way to know Thee" / "save incapacity to know Thee!"
- **Supplication 1, p. 57.** "describers' imaginations are not able to depict Him."
- **Supplication 32, p. 313.** "Attributes go astray in Thee,".
- **Supplication 46, p. 432.** "and attributes fall apart without reaching Thy description."
- **Supplication 47, p. 434.** "and the most I can do is admit to inability,".
- **Munajat 13, p. 674.** "I would declare Thee far too exalted for me to remember Thee,".

### Annihilation and nearness ("brought near")
- The translation has no word for annihilation (fana). The nearest language is captivation and nearness.
- **Munajat 9, p. 656.** "Who can have become intimate with Thy nearness," and "whose heart Thou hast captivated with Thy will,".
- **Munajat 12, p. 670.** "how delightful the drink of Thy nearness!"
- **Munajat 7, p. 647.** "let us taste the sweetness of Thy affection and nearness,".
- **Supplication 31, p. 283.** The world's goods as "a link to Thy nearness,".
- **Supplications 3 (p. 69) and 45 (p. 392).** "brought nigh" names the highest angels.

### Wilaya, the Imams and the Ahl al-Bayt
- **Supplication 47, pp. 481-484.** On p. 481 the best of the Household are "the guardians of Thy religion," and "Thy vicegerents in Thy earth,". On p. 484 God is asked to bless "the adherents to their guardianship,". Note 27 (p. 496) glosses wilaya as "friendship, authority, guardianship, rule".
- **Supplication 48, p. 512.** "this station belongs to Thy vicegerents, Thy chosen,"; the Imam laments that they were "forcibly stripped".
- **Supplication 57, pp. 571-572.** The addendum on the Household: "appointed them the heirs to the prophets,".
- **Supplication 2, p. 62 onward.** Blessing on Muhammad and his Household.
- **Munajat 9, p. 656.** "for Thy nearness and Thy friendship," (the Arabic is wilaya; see the lecture check).
- **Munajat 10, p. 661.** The Prophet's intercession as the speaker's mediation.

### Good character
- **Supplication 20, pp. 197-204.** The main text. "Give me the highest moral traits" and "Preserve me from vainglory!" (p. 197). Pages 198-200 turn each hostility into its opposite, and include "lowering the wing," (p. 199) and "speaking the truth, though it be painful," (p. 200).
- **Supplication 22, p. 225.** "provide me with a breast safe from envy,".
- **Supplication 35, p. 325.** "Lest I envy Thy creatures".
- **Treatise on Rights, pp. 691-692.** The rights of the self, tongue, hearing and sight.

### Contentment with the decree (rida) and reliance (tawakkul)
- **Supplication 35, pp. 325-326.** "delight my soul through Thy decree," and "expand my breast through the instances of Thy decision,".
- **Supplication 33, p. 321.** "Make us love what we dislike in Thy decree".
- **Munajat 9, p. 656.** "made pleased with Thy decree,".
- **Supplication 22, p. 225.** "repose in satisfaction".
- **Supplication 54, p. 560.** "and Thou art my trust and my hope in all affairs,".
- **Munajat 14, p. 679.** The prayer of clinging: "It is not right for him who holds fast to Thy cord".

### The return to God, paradise and hell
- **Munajat 1, p. 618.** The return as the runaway servant's coming back (see above).
- **Supplication 40, p. 353.** Death as a "homecoming to Thee" that the soul should crave.
- **Supplication 41, p. 357.** "set me down in the midst of Thy Garden,".
- **Munajat 10, p. 661.** The "selected friends" "in the midst of Thy Garden,".
- **Munajat 3, pp. 627-628.** Hell as the fear of the lover.
- **Supplication 50, p. 532.** The Fire set against God's mercy.
- **Munajat 15, p. 683.** The world is a trap; the end sought is "the vision of Thee".

### The Quran
- **Supplication 42, pp. 358-376 (English from 370).** The main text. "and make the Qur'an our mediation to the noblest stations of Thy honour," (p. 371); "an intimate in the shadows of nights" (p. 372); "the understanding of the Qur'an's wonders" (p. 372). A note on p. 375 links the Qur'an to the Household.
- **Supplication 45, p. 418.** Ramadan is honoured "through the Qur'an and the Light which Thou sent down within it,".
- **Supplication 44, p. 391.** Ramadan as the month "in which the Qur'an was sent down as guidance".
- **Munajat 13, p. 675.** Two Quranic verses on remembrance are quoted within the prayer.

**Mismatches and queries**
1. ¶31, "heart burn with the love of seeking you", is in no Whispered Prayer in this translation. The nearest are "whose heart Thou hast captivated with Thy will," (p. 656), "seized by the ardour of Thy love" (Knowers, p. 669) and "love-mad hearts are enraptured by Thee," (Rememberers, p. 674). Flag as `[QUERY]`: is this a loose paraphrase or a different line?
2. The angels "burnt in the love of Allah" and struck dumb (¶30-31) do not appear in the Lovers. Supplication 3 (pp. 69-70) has angels "never heedless of passionate love for Thee", and nothing about being struck dumb. Treat it as the lecturer's own teaching.
3. In ¶28 the lecturer's "love" in "nearness, love and friendship" is slightly more than the translation has: Chittick has nearness and friendship, and love follows in the next clause.
4. The notes name the source "Munajat Khamsa Ashar", which is the whole set; the lines are all from the ninth.

### Irfan11 ¶58-59 (notes line 106)
The lecturer says Rabiʿa's poems cannot be compared with "Munajatil Muhibeena of Imam Sajjad(as)" and that the ulama recite the Sahifa's prayers when speaking of love. No line is quoted, so there is nothing to verify beyond the identity: Munajat 9, pp. 655-657.

### Extra: Irfan03 ¶30
The lecturer says that in Munajat-e-Muhibeen "give me the favour of looking at you". This matches "Oblige me by allowing me to gaze upon Thee," (p. 657) and "granted gazing upon Thy face," (p. 656).
