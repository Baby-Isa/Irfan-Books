## 1. Identification and structure

**The work.** Sayyid Haydar Amuli, a Shia gnostic of the 8th century AH, wrote a handbook that explains each doctrine and rite three times, for the people of shari`ah, of tariqah and of haqiqah. The Arabic title is *Asrar al-Shari`ah* (variants at p. 24 n.16). Amuli also commented on Ibn ʿArabi's *Fusus* (*Nass al-Nusus*, 782 AH, aged 63; p. 5) and wrote a Quran commentary (p. 11).

**Provenance.** The English is "Translated from the Original Arabic by Asadullah ad-Dhaakir Yate" (p. 1). The extract has no copyright page, so publisher and date are [VERIFY]. pp. 2-27 are a modern Iranian editor's introduction, not Amuli. It was dated 1 Muharram 1403 (p. 21) and tells how a rare manuscript was found and collated (pp. 3-4). The colophon date 950 AH (p. 203) is treated by n.30 as a copy date, so Amuli's own date is not given [VERIFY]. p. 5 says "born in 819 AH", which contradicts 782 AH at age 63 (about 719) [QUERY: editor's slip].

## 2. Chapter map

### Prologue
- **D** pp. 29-30: aim is to show shari`ah, tariqah and haqiqah are "synonyms for one truth, albeit in different terms". A frame for what ʿirfan is not.
- **S** p. 160: ʿirfan is not licence; Amuli blames "the ignorance Sufis talking about unrestricted license and the neglect of religious duties".
- **S** pp. 21, 31: on wary critics. "will be killed by the sword of those expressions".
- **D** pp. 7-9: Amuli's own story (court office, a dream of his shrouded body, Najaf seclusion) as a human opening.

### 1. Seeking, not powers
- **S** p. 58: "absolute perfection, it is found in gnosis of Allah and in the performance of the different acts of worship".
- **S** p. 50: "they are classed as the elite of the elite, the intimate ones and those who go before" (muqarrabun in Quranic triads).
- **D** p. 7: leaving court life, "all my passion and desire was to leave this world and its pleasures".
- **C** pp. 138-139: affirms saints' powers: "the prophets, saints, perfected ones and spiritual poles possess great power in both the earthly and spiritual worlds". Contrast with the lecturer's anti-power stance.

### 2. You already know these words
- **D** pp. 198-199: Part 4 holds long du`a-like prayers of "one of the masters of the spiritual path" (not a held Mafatih text; paraphrase only).
- **D** p. 12: the dhikr chain passes through "Kamil ibn Ziyad Nakh'i" (Kumayl) to the Commander of the Faithful.
- **D** pp. 111, 125, 155: the qudsi hadith "I become his hearing and his sight" as the grammar of nearness.

### 3. Two kinds of knowing
- **S** p. 44: "I have known my Lord by my Lord" (also p. 45).
- **S** p. 139: Ali: "I have come to know Allah by abandoning thought."
- **D** p. 111: Ibn ʿArabi: "The ways leading to knowledge of Allah are two, not three in number" (unveiling, reflection).
- **D** p. 53: balance with reason: "the divine code is the intellect from without and the intellect is the divine code from within".

### 4. Know yourself, know your Lord
- **S** pp. 114, 155: "Whoever knows himself knows his Lord" applied to soul and body, God and world.
- **D** p. 93: microcosm: "Glory be to the One who made the whole in one of the parts."
- **D** p. 141: "The heart is the abode or the station of Allah".

### 5. The soul and its worlds
- **D** pp. 41, 47: mulk, malakut, jabarut matched to shari`ah, tariqah, haqiqah; in the Light Verse "The niche refers to the world of the mulk (the phenomenal world) and it corresponds to the shari'ah".
- **D** pp. 106-108: four deaths (red, white, green, black). "Die before you die".
- **D** p. 192: three nafs states, "commanding to evil", "reproachful", "inspired", then at rest.
- **D** pp. 145-146: prayer postures mapped on plant, animal and rational souls.
- **D** p. 170: imagination as "the thickest veil which blinds the self from its own essence".

### 6. The ship, the sea and the pearl  (priority)
- **S** p. 50: **Ali's image, the closest parallel to ship, sea and pearl:** "as for the philosophers, they dive for pearls in the sea; as for the gnostics, they ride in boats to safety" (shari`ah a river, haqiqa a sea).
- **S** p. 32: "of tariqah that you attain His presence, and of haqiqah that you witness Him"; the Prophet's "shari`ah is my words, tariqah my actions, haqiqah my states" (same saying as Irfan06 ¶13).
- **S** pp. 32, 19: almond: "the almond as a whole is the shari`ah, the kernel is the tariqah and the oil the haqiqah"; compare Irfan06 ¶23.
- **S** p. 42: "Whoever does not protect his spiritual state and path by the shari`ah will destroy that state".
- **S** p. 48: "shari`ah is possible without tariqah although tariqah is not possible without shari`ah".
- **D** pp. 32, 152: prayer as service, nearness, arrival (the lecturer's example, Irfan06 ¶24).
- **D** pp. 33, 41: islam, iman, certainty as prerequisites of the three levels; ʿilm/ʿayn/haqq al-yaqin (p. 32).
- **D** p. 162: Junayd: "the only thing of benefit to us is the prayer we perform in the depth of the night".
- **C** p. 48: ranks haqiqa "higher"; keep the lecturer's "never left behind".

### 7. Roots and the first seekers
- **D** pp. 12-13, 23: dhikr chain from Ali via Hasan al-Basri, Habib al-ʿAjami, Maʿruf al-Karkhi to Junayd; khirqa via Maʿruf al-Karkhi from Imam al-Rida; Ibrahim b. Adham and Uways al-Qarani as majdhubs.
- **S** p. 81: "Surely the Garden yearns more strongly for Salman than Salman for the Garden".
- **C** p. 162: Bayazid as Imam al-Sadiq's cup-bearer [VERIFY chronology].

### 8. Drunk and sober  (priority)
- **D** pp. 98-99: **Shia reading of Ibn ʿArabi's Seal of the Saints.** From the *Fusus* and *Futuhat*, Amuli identifies him as Ali: "the absolute Seal of the Saints, is the Commander of the Faithful". Core of the Shia debate.
- **D** p. 6: Ibn ʿArabi's poem, "I have drawn close to the religion of love and passion", taken by Amuli as true to the Imams.
- **D** p. 171: Amuli corrects Shahruzi and notes Ibn ʿArabi disagrees; he is no uncritical follower.
- **D** p. 14: "I was a majdhub - a person intoxicated by divine attraction - amongst spiritual travelers".
- **S** p. 45: Ali's wine of the awliya; "when they join, there is no difference between them and their Beloved". Shatahat defended pp. 45, 174.
- **D** pp. 114, 190: Bayazid's "There is no morning as far as I am concerned"; "The destruction of what is imaginary comes with the sobriety of the Known".
- **D** p. 115: unity of being: "There is nothing in existence but Allah, His Names and His Actions; all is Him, by Him, from Him and to Him."

### 9. Sufis and Shia  (priority; see section 3)
- **D** pp. 5-6, 24 n.15, 29, 97-99, 160, 192.
- **S** pp. 8, 14: Amuli's own khirqa: "until he gave me the real patched garment of the Sufis to wear".
- **S** p. 13: "Most of the chains of transmission of the kharqah come through Junayd al-Baghdadi"; Suhrawardi chain to Ali al-Rida (confirms Irfan14 ¶36-37).
- **C** p. 192: "The Shaykhs are agreed that the spiritual wayfarer should be prevented from traveling the path on his own without a shaykh of perfection."

### 10. Finding a guide  (priority)
- **D** p. 51: "The spiritual doctor is the shaykh, the person who has gnosis of the treatment"; the patient must drink the bitter medicine (pp. 51-52).
- **C** p. 192: obedience to "the Prophet, the Imam or the shaykh without any further argument".
- **D** pp. 8-11: his teachers: Nur al-Din Tihrani, al-Muqaddasi in Najaf, the Hilli jurist Fakhr al-Muhaqqiqin ("Zayn al-`Abidin the Second"). Jurist and mystic together.
- **S** pp. 29, 163: "after seeing the door to spiritual travel closed"; real awliya "are few in number and vast in rank".
- **D** p. 198: "the daily awrad (the program of dhikr or remembrance given by a shaykh)".

### 11. The stages of the first journey
- **D** p. 195: **stage list** from murid, will, hope, fear, doing without, patience, gratitude, trust, contentment, gnosis, love, to ecstasy, intoxication, unveiling: "the station of annihilation, is the last of the levels and the highest of the stations of the path".
- **D** pp. 108-110: takhliyah and tahliyah: seven base qualities (vanity, arrogance, miserliness, envy, cupidity, sexual excess, anger) exchanged for seven fine ones (gnosis, wisdom, forbearance, humility, generosity, chastity, courage).
- **D** p. 45: "It is not everyone who sets out who arrives".

### 12. Setting out
- **S** p. 7: his conversion: "It became clear to me that I was following a way of perversity".
- **S** p. 201: "Knowledge does not stagnate with a people such that it no longer flows to others".
- **D** pp. 107, 108: "Death is turning (for forgiveness to Him)" (Imam al-Sadiq); "I have been sent to perfect good behaviour".

### 13. The five disciplines
- **D** pp. 107, 166: hunger: "gluttony kills sagacity"; "curb his coursing by means of hunger".
- **D** p. 167: silence as the greater fast (Mary).
- **D** pp. 160-161, 198: night prayer: "Abstain from indulging in your sensual desires and pray in the darkness of the night."
- **D** pp. 8, 10: seclusion in Najaf, "of an intensity and severity never before achieved" (compare Tabatabaʾi's caution).

### 14. Love, the engine
- **S** p. 197: "As for gnosis, it is preceded by love".
- **S** p. 61: "Original love is the love of the essence itself for itself"; answers philosophers who deny that God can love.
- **D** p. 108: "All from the Beloved is beloved". Also pp. 6, 20, 137.

### 15. Losing the self, finding God  (priority)
- **D** pp. 113-117: **three degrees of fana: action, attribute, essence**, each with its "garden". p. 115: "This annihilation is a gnostic annihilation and not an actual experiential annihilation".
- **D** pp. 45-46, 96: baqa. "annihilation of the Prophet in Allah did not prevent him from eating, drinking or getting married"; wilayah as "living among created beings after annihilation in the Real and subsistence in Him".
- **S** p. 116: **against merging.** "Beware of merging and separation for the first causes heresy and the negation of one's Islam".
- **S** p. 163: rejects charges of "incarnation, union with the divine and the ascription of human characteristics to Allah".
- **S** p. 161: "The final point of arrival at Reality is abandoning any perception of action", not abandoning action.
- **D** pp. 43, 174: "annihilation of the knower in the known"; charcoal and fire, "Thus the one becomes the other."

### 16. The four journeys and the perfect human  (priority)
- **S** p. 140: **the four travels**: to Allah, in Allah, ascent to the source of gatheredness, and "travel `by' Allah `from' Allah in order to achieve perfection". "There are no further ways of travel or spiritual destinations beyond these four." The fourth is "the return from the Presence of the Real to creation".
- **S** p. 189: return in the hajj, Arafat to Mina; Junayd: "It is a return to the beginning."
- **D** pp. 96-99, 6: Perfect Man and wilayah; "Only wilayah continues among men until eternity"; the Imams as "the teachers of the shari`ah and the leaders of the tariqah and the poles of the haqiqah".
- **C** p. 8: visits Najaf and the shrines; the extract has no Karbala reflection or `Arafah du`a.

### 17. Reading the Quran with the heart, and the return to God
- **D** p. 188: "Truly the Qur'an has an outward and an inward dimension"; seven knowledges (p. 196 n.29).
- **D** p. 47: Light Verse on three levels (niche, glass, lamp).
- **D** p. 100: "Most of these arguments have been taken from the works of Khawaja Nasir al-Din al-Tusi" (ma`ad).
- **D** pp. 109-110: hell's seven gates as the base qualities, which become gates of paradise when exchanged.
- **D** pp. 106, 113-117: return as surrender of will ("death of the will"), action, attributes, essence.
- **C** p. 43: dismisses the child-Abraham reading as "heresy", a firm ta`wil to compare with the lecturer's idea of legitimate ta`wil.

- **D** p. 198: "Above all, you must equip yourself with fear of Allah and sincerity in your intention".

## 3. Amuli on Shiʿism and Sufism (for Chapter 9)

**Thesis.** The true Shia and the true Sufi are one. Amuli's plan is "comparing the way of the sincere Sufis and the way of the Imamiyah sect" at the three levels (p. 29). His formation joined "the outer shari`ah and the inner haqiqah" (p. 5): Imami fiqh for the outer, the Sufis and masters of tawhid for the inner. The Sufis' deepest teaching is the Imams' inheritance: "there is not a single form of knowledge but that the Imams are the source of that knowledge" (p. 6). A commentator reports on his *Risalat al-Faqr*: "any Shi'ah who is not of the people of poverty and spiritual journeying is not of the Shi'ah" (p. 24 n.15; editor's report, not Amuli's own words).

**Reconciliation.**
1. *Three names, one reality* (pp. 29-33): apparent conflict between jurist and mystic is a failure to see levels. "it is not permitted to reject any of the people of the shar'iah, tariqah or haqiqah" (p. 41).
2. *Imamate in three tiers* (pp. 93-99): shari`ah defends the infallible Imam (pp. 94-95); tariqa makes the Imam the "dependent wali" (p. 96); haqiqa makes him the Pole, with Ibn ʿArabi's Seal read as Ali (pp. 98-99). The Sufi's central concept becomes an Imami one.
3. *Sufi chains converge on Ali and the Imams* (pp. 12-14, 23), and Amuli's own initiations were Sufi (pp. 8, 14).
4. *Defence against heresy charges* (pp. 160-163): rebuts hulul and the antinomian excuse.

**Criticisms of Sufis (and others).**
- Most Sufis are ignorant of the Imams: "Because of their extreme ignorance, the majority of Sufis think that the Imams were devoid of the excellencies and superior insights of Sufism" (p. 6). Many Shia likewise limit the Imams' knowledge to law (p. 6).
- Ignorant Sufis give licence a bad name and provoke the exoteric scholars' rejection (p. 160).
- A Shia who neglects poverty and travel is not truly Shia (p. 24 n.15).
- Sufi language is hard and misread; the warning of the "nine hundred and ninety nine" is aimed at deniers, not at Sufis (pp. 20-21, 31).
- He corrects Shahruzi (p. 171), calls Ash`arite predestinarians veiled (p. 50) and says Mu`tazilite and Ash`arite disputers make words a "commerce" (p. 201).

**Caveat.** The extract never states "Shia and Sufi are one" as a slogan. It is built from the autobiography (pp. 5-8), the faqr note (p. 24), Part 1 and the imamate section. The fuller statement is in *Jami` al-Asrar*, which is quoted here (pp. 5-6) but not held [VERIFY before presenting it as a stand-alone Amuli thesis].

## 4. Tensions with the lecturer

1. **Tariqa and silsila (Irfan06 ¶15-16; Irfan14 ¶6, ¶22-25).** The lecturer says Shia have no tariqa because Imamah rules out a silsila. Amuli took the Sufi khirqa from two shaykhs (pp. 8, 14), passed on a dhikr chain to Ali (pp. 12-13) and requires a shaykh (p. 192). Present Amuli as a historical Shia mystic who used Sufi forms and anchored them in the Imams. Irfan06 ¶15 and Irfan14 ¶25 promise "one" Shia tariqa [QUERY: which].
2. **Submission (Irfan14 ¶20-21, corpse and washer; ¶25).** Amuli's doctor image (pp. 51-52) is close, but p. 192 puts the Imam above the shaykh. The lecturer calls the tariqa claim that independent practice misleads "something of an exaggeration" (¶25); Amuli says no travel without a shaykh (p. 192).
3. **Sufism as "a branch of Shiaism" (Irfan14 ¶10; Irfan06 ¶11, Corbin; Irfan11 ¶45-61).** Amuli's argument is theological, not historical. Corbin appears only in a note (p. 26 n.22). Do not cite him as support for a historical-origins thesis.
4. **Karamat (Irfan06 ¶17-20).** The lecturer is wary; Amuli says the perfected can do "anything he wishes" (p. 138). Keep the lecturer's frame and use Amuli as contrast.
5. **Hierarchy (Irfan06 ¶12).** The lecturer: irfan begins with sharia. Amuli: haqiqa is "higher" (p. 48) though impossible without the lower. Compatible if worded carefully.
6. **Early mystics (Irfan11 ¶64-72).** Amuli places Hasan al-Basri in his dhikr chain (p. 13); the lecturer calls him "ambivalent" and notes Imam Sajjad disagreed (¶69-70). The Bayazid and al-Sadiq link (p. 162) matches the lecturer's discipleship claim but is chronologically doubtful [VERIFY].
7. **Ibn ʿArabi (Irfan11 ¶16-17).** The lecturer will not call the theory absolutely true; Amuli extends it (pp. 98-99, 115). Present Amuli as one Shia voice, not the lecturer's.
8. **Exclusivity and method (Irfan14 ¶65).** The lecturer: no single method. Amuli: anyone off the three-level path is astray (p. 42). Hold both: methods vary, the three levels do not.
