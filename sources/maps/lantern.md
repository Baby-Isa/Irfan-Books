# Source map: Lantern of the Path (Misbah al-Shariʿa, trans. Haeri)

Extract: `sources/extracts/lantern.md`. Pages are the PDF `[p. N]` markers. Quotes are exact (checked by script). Arabic lines in the extract are ignored and none is reproduced. "Ch" numbers below count the chapters in order of appearance: 99 in all, which matches the publisher's note (p. 3). Ch 2 is "More on Bondage", which agrees with Shomali's citation "Misbah al-Shari'ah, chapter 2" (`maps/self_knowledge.md`).

**Standing caveat (Tier A/B).** The publisher's note (p. 3) records that Majlisi and Hurr al-Amili doubted that the Imam wrote the book. The attribution is therefore "attributed to". Book 2 should say so on first use.

## 2. Chapter map

Strength: the Lantern is a book of conduct, not theory. It is richest for chapters 6, 12, 13 and 14, and thin for 7 to 10, 15 and 16. Rabiʿa, Junayd, Ibn ʿArabi and the Sufi orders are absent. Rabiʿ ibn Khuthaym and Uways al-Qarani appear.

**Prologue**
- p3. The book is attributed to Imam al-Sadiq, with the authorship doubted. Quote: "there is some controversy whether the book was penned by the Imam".
- p4. The translator's introduction states the outer-inner principle. Quote: "If there is an inner attribute there must be a corresponding outer expression".

**1. Seeking, not powers**
- Ch 92, p88. Love of God empties the lover of every other concern. Quote: "it empties him of every preoccupation except remembrance of Allah".
- Ch 5, p9. The only road is knowledge, and it is joined to action. Quote: "There is no way to Allah except via knowledge."

**2. You already know these words**
- Ch 62, p63. Supplication is surrender, not request. Quote: "a melting of the heart in contemplation of its Lord".
- Ch 62, p64. The one who is answered forgets the need. Quote: "I called on Allah once and He answered me. I forgot the need".

**3. Two kinds of knowing**
- Ch 6, p10. Knowledge is a gift of light. Quote: "Knowledge is a light, which Allah casts into the heart of whomsoever He wills."
- Ch 5, p9. A true knower is known by conduct, not talk. Quote: "He who truly knows is the one in whom sound actions, pure supplications, truthfulness and precaution speak out".
- Ch 53, p56. Reflection needs gnosis. Quote: "singled out for the light of gnosis".

**4. Know yourself, know your Lord**
- Ch 5, p9. The hadith is given here to the Prophet. The lecture gives it to Imam Ali (see section 3 below). Quote: "Whoever knows his own self knows his Lord".
- Ch 9, p13. "Knowledge" is glossed as self-knowledge. Quote: "that is, knowledge of the self".
- Ch 80, p79. The self is the thickest veil. Quote: "There is no veil between the bondsman and Allah which is darker or more desolate than that of self and passion".

**5. The soul and its worlds**
- Ch 20, p24. Sleep is a rehearsal of death. Quote: "Know that sleep is the brother of death."
- Ch 28, p32. The pre-creation light of the Prophet, in Salman's report. Quote: "Allah created me from the quintessence of light, and called me, so I obeyed Him."
- Ch 74, p72. The spirit precedes the body. Quote: "when the light of time was mixed with that of eternity".
- Ch 80, p79. The commanding self (ammara). Quote: "the self which commands evil".
- Ch 21, p26. The hajj rites rehearse the afterlife. Quote: "preparation for, and an indication of, death, the grave, the resurrection".

**6. The ship, the sea and the pearl**
- Ch 52, p55. The obligatory comes first. Quote: "I wonder at a person who seeks something extra while he abandons an obligation".
- Ch 50, p52. Outer and inner move together. Quote: "The corruption of the outward being comes from the corruption of the inward."
- Ch 55, p57. Islam and iman rest on certainty. Quote: "Certainty is the earth of Islam and the heaven of iman."
- Ch 84, p82. Miracles are a matter of certainty. Quote: "If he had had more certainty, he could have walked on air."
- Ch 41, p46. Attention in prayer. Quote: "If someone's heart is attached to something other than Allah in prayer, he is near to that thing".
- Ch 56, p58. Three nested precincts. Quote: "like the sanctuary, the mosque, and the Ka'bah".

**7. Roots and the first seekers**
- Ch 46, p50. Rabiʿ ibn Khuthaym on silence. Quote: "Oh! The silent have indeed been saved!"
- Ch 79, p78. Uways al-Qarani on mortality. Quote: "How is a man in the morning when he does not know if he will be alive in the evening".
- Ch 11, p15. Abu Dharr's household on tawakkul. Quote: "When he returns is in the hands of someone else".
- Ch 78, p77. Trust. Quote: "Trust (tawakkul) is a cup sealed with Allah".

**8. Drunk and sober**
- Ch 91, p88. The sober gnostic lives among people. Quote: "The person of the gnostic ('arif) is with the people, while his heart is with Allah."
- Ch 86, p84. Annihilation of choice. Quote: "He who is content is annihilated to all his choices".
- Ch 51, p54. A warning against saying "I". Quote: "If anyone tells you, 'I', say 'you'."
- p3. The objection of the jurists is recorded in the publisher's note. Quote: "division into ninety-nine chapters and its emphasis on inner meaning".

**9. Sufis and Shia**
- Ch 74, p73. Following a model. Quote: "If the faith of Allah had had a path straighter than following a model".
- Ch 28, pp32–34. The twelve Imams are named in a hadith of Salman. This is a Shia anchor, and it does not name any silsila.

**10. Finding a guide**
- Ch 8, p13. Sign of a good companion. Quote: "With one the sight of whom reminds you of Allah".
- Ch 72, p70. Five qualities of an adviser. Quote: "intellect, knowledge, experience, good counsel, and precaution".
- Ch 76, p75. The false preacher. Quote: "How he leads people astray with his words!"

**11. The stages of the first journey**
- Ch 56, p58. The ladder of fear, hope and love. Quote: "Fear is the branch of knowledge; hope is the branch of certainty, and love is the branch of gnosis".
- Ch 16, p20. A ladder of those who perish. Quote: "Those who act will perish, except for those who worship".
- Ch 58, p59. Polishing the heart (tajliyah). Quote: "it is polished by the burnish of repentance".
- Ch 83, p82. Fana in the letters of tafwid. Quote: "the fa of the annihilation (fana) of every aspiration other than Allah".

**12. Setting out**
- Ch 44, p49. Repentance for all, even the lapsed. Quote: "his continued weeping and regret for what he has missed is in itself an act of worship".
- Ch 77, p76. Character first. Quote: "Never get angry, for anger contains opposition to your Lord".
- Ch 32, p37. Humility. Quote: "Whoever is humble to Allah, Allah will honour over many of His bondsmen".

**13. The five disciplines**
- Ch 34, p40. Hunger. Quote: "Hunger is a condiment for believers, nourishment for the spirit, food for the heart".
- Ch 54, p56. Silence, retreat, hunger and wakefulness are grouped together. Quote: "wakefulness, which illuminates your heart".
- Ch 45, p50. Retreat. Quote: "There is no prophet nor regent (wasi) who has not chosen retreat in his lifetime".
- Ch 24, p29. Remembrance without limit. Quote: "The Messenger of Allah did not set any limit on remembering Allah".
- Ch 52, p55. Measured doses. Quote: "The best form of worship is that which comes nearest to security."

**14. Love, the engine**
- Ch 93, p89. Love is freedom from everything else. Quote: "the root of love is being free of everything except the Beloved".
- Ch 56, p58. Love arrives with gnosis. Quote: "the breeze of love stirs, he settles in the shadow of the Beloved".
- Ch 80, p79. Taste. Quote: "If you were to experience the sweetness of worshipping Allah".

**15. Losing the self, finding God**
- Ch 2, p6. Servanthood's inner nature is lordship. Quote: "Bondage is an essence, the inner nature of which is lordship".
- Ch 41, p46. Quote: "disappearing from all things and being veiled from all that the eyes see".
- Ch 16, p20. Tanzih as the aim. Quote: "purifying the meanings of disassociation (tanzih) in His unity".

**16. The four journeys and the perfect human**
- Ch 27, p31. The prophets as the means of access. Quote: "the means for all creatures to come to Him".
- Ch 28, p33. Wilaya and the Imams. Quote: "He will return to where we return, and he will be where we are!"
- Ch 28, p33. The twelfth Imam. Quote: "the announcer who establishes the right of Allah".

**17. Reading the Quran with the heart, and the return**
- Ch 12, p16. Direct address. Quote: "intimate conversation with the Lord, without any intermediary".
- Ch 12, p17. The outer is never dropped. Quote: "while failing to observe the legal limits contained therein".
- Ch 6, p10. Qualifications for interpretation. Quote: "Do you have a command of the intentions of Allah in the parables of the Qur'an?"
- Ch 98, p92. Will surrendered. Quote: "does not recognize the reality of causes".

**Epilogue**
- Ch 77, p76. Quote: "into one single characteristic and that is Precaution (taqwa)".
- Ch 56, p58. A self-test. Quote: "content to meet death".

## 3. Lecture check (L07, L08, L10)

The transcripts do not name the Lantern. `notes/Irfan07.md` §10 and `notes/Irfan08.md` §10 guess "Bihar al-Anwar, Misbah al-Shariʿa" as the source. The transcripts at L07 ¶18–19 and L08 ¶77–88 mention only "the Hadith of Unwan Basri".

**Result: the ʿUnwan al-Basri hadith is NOT in the Lantern.** A search of the extract finds no "Basri", "Unwan", "Malik" or "Anas". The story (the old man, the first rejection, the sending to Malik) is absent. So the advice in L08 ¶78–88 cannot be cited to the Lantern. Whether the full hadith sits in a longer Misbah text or in Bihar al-Anwar is [VERIFY], because no held source reproduces it.

**Use.** For Chapters 12 and 13 the Lantern supports the themes (hunger, silence, retreat, wakefulness, anger), not the lecture's wording. Book 2 must not present the Lantern as the source of the ʿUnwan hadith. Ch 54 (p56) is the best anchor for the five disciplines.
